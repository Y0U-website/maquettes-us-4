What to Do When a Dental Emergency Occurs

If you are experiencing a dental emergency in Fayetteville, North Carolina, contact our office at 910-864-2944 right away. Describing your symptoms clearly will help us prepare for your visit and provide guidance on managing discomfort in the meantime. You may receive instructions to keep the area clean, avoid chewing on the affected side, or use cold compresses to reduce swelling.

Some emergencies involve trauma, such as a knocked-out tooth or injuries to the lips, cheeks or gums. Others may be caused by infections, decay, or damage to existing dental work. Common signs of a dental emergency in Fayetteville, NC, include sharp pain, persistent bleeding, sensitivity to temperature, swelling in the face or jaw, and visible damage to the teeth or mouth. If you notice any of these symptoms, do not delay in seeking care.

When to Seek Treatment at an Emergency Room

While our Fayetteville emergency dentists can handle many urgent dental issues, some situations require medical attention before dental treatment can begin. If you experience severe trauma to the head or neck, heavy bleeding, or symptoms of a serious infection such as difficulty breathing or swallowing, go to the emergency room or call emergency services. Once your condition is stabilized, our team will be ready to assist with any necessary dental care.

Frequently Asked Questions About Emergency Dentistry

What qualifies as a dental emergency?

A dental emergency typically involves sudden pain, visible damage or symptoms that disrupt normal oral function. Examples include intense sensitivity, swelling near a tooth, broken dental appliances, or unexplained bleeding. If something feels urgent or unusual, it is always best to contact a dental office for guidance and evaluation.

How quickly should I seek help for dental trauma?

Timing is critical when dealing with dental trauma. Teeth or restorations that are loose, fractured or dislodged benefit from immediate attention. The sooner our dentists can examine the area, the better the chances of preserving your dental health and avoiding complications. Contact a professional as soon as the injury occurs.

Can I manage dental pain at home temporarily?

Over-the-counter pain relief and cold compresses may help reduce discomfort, but they are not substitutes for professional care. Home remedies can mask symptoms without addressing the cause. If the pain persists or worsens, it is essential to schedule an appointment to prevent further damage or infection from developing.

What should I do if a filling or crown falls out?

If a restoration comes loose, try to keep it clean and store it in a safe place. Avoid chewing on that side of your mouth and rinse gently with water. Although it might not cause immediate pain, exposed tooth surfaces are vulnerable and should be evaluated as soon as possible.
