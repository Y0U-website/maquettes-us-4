What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are advanced dental solutions designed to replace missing teeth with a permanent and stable option. These implants use titanium posts that are surgically inserted into the jawbone to mimic the structure of real tooth roots. As the implants and bone meld together over time, a strong base is formed that can hold prosthetic teeth like crowns, bridges , or dentures. This integration ensures a durable and natural-looking restoration, providing a secure and long-lasting alternative to traditional dentures and bridges.

Am I a good candidate for implants?

At Abadin Dental, dental implants can be an excellent solution for replacing missing teeth. Nonetheless, a number of reasons will determine if you are a suitable fit for this treatment. We will thoroughly evaluate your case during your appointment, paying particular attention to the following crucial elements:

  • Overall Health: Overall health is a prerequisite for a successful implant procedure. To make sure you are in the greatest possible condition for the treatment, we will go over your medical history.
  • Jawbone Density: Sufficient density of the jawbone is essential for the proper positioning and support of dental implants. In order to improve bone volume and support if your jawbone is not strong enough, doctors could recommend bone grafting.
  • Gum Health: Gum health is critical to the long-term viability of dental implants. We will assess the state of your gums to make sure there are no gum diseases or infections that could compromise the effectiveness of your therapy.
  • Impact of Smoking: Smoking can hinder the healing process and reduce the success rate of implants. We will discuss how smoking affects your dental health and offer support and tools if you want to stop.

Dental implant cost can differ significantly depending on a number of variables, including location, materials, and insurance coverage. In Florida, the price for a single-tooth implant typically ranges between $3,000 and $5,000. In Coral Gables, you can expect similar pricing for single-tooth implants, though costs may vary.

For multiple tooth implants, the overall cost usually falls between $6,000 and $10,000 or more, depending on the specifics of your case. If you’re considering All-on-4 implants, which provide a complete solution for full arch restoration, you should anticipate a cost ranging from $20,000 to $40,000 or more per arch. To choose the best course of action for your needs and financial situation, it’s critical to talk over your alternatives with your dentist.

FAQ’s

How many days does it take for a dental implant to heal? After your dental implant procedure, it’s normal to experience some soreness, pain, and mild bleeding, particularly within the first 24 hours. If you had only one or two implants placed, recovery can typically occur within a few days.Most patients discover that they may easily return to their regular eating routines after seven to ten days.

Do I get temporary teeth while waiting for implants? Think of temporary teeth as stand-ins for your natural teeth. They’re not intended to be a long-term solution, but they play a crucial role while you wait for your dental implant. Essentially, they act as a placeholder for the missing tooth.

Who should avoid dental implants? If you frequently grind your teeth, have uncontrolled diabetes, suffer from gum disease, or have undergone jaw irradiation due to cancer treatment, these factors can impact the success of dental implants. To achieve the best possible results, it’s crucial to address and manage these conditions before proceeding with implant restoration.

Are dental implants safe, and what’s the success rate? Implants are highly safe when placed by trained specialists. Initial success rates are around 90–98 % , with long-term prosthetic survival 52–76 % over 16–20 years. Risks include infection, nerve injury, or peri‑implantitis.

How long do dental implants last? The titanium implant implant can last a lifetime with good oral hygiene and regular check-ups. Crowns or prosthetics may need replacement after 10–15 years due to wear.

Is the surgery painful? Placement is done with anesthesia—most patients feel pressure but not pain. Mild swelling or soreness afterward can be managed with OTC pain relievers, ice, or prescribed medication.

Can implants fail? What risks exist? Failure may stem from:

  • Poor osseointegration
  • Infection or peri‑implantitis (gum/bone infection)
  • Overloading, smoking, uncontrolled diabetes, poor oral hygiene Peri-implantitis requires special care—regular cleanings every 3‑6 months help prevent it.
