FAQ’s

How do I know what I am experiencing is a dental emergency? If you are experiencing severe pain, uncontrolled bleeding, a knocked-out tooth, or signs of infection such as swelling or fever, it may be a dental emergency. Other indicators include broken teeth or painful swelling in your gums. In general, if your symptoms disrupt your daily activities or cause significant discomfort, it’s best to seek immediate care. When in doubt, contact your dentist for guidance.

How do I know if I have a dental abscess? A dental abscess often presents with symptoms such as severe tooth pain, swelling in the gums, and a persistent bad taste in your mouth. You may also notice a lump on your gums or experience fever and general discomfort. If you have persistent pain and swelling that doesn’t improve, it’s important to see your dentist for evaluation. Early treatment is crucial to prevent further complications.

What should I do if I can’t get to my dentist right away? If you can’t reach your dentist immediately, try to manage your symptoms at home. For bleeding, apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth or gauze for 10-15 minutes. Use a cold compress to reduce swelling or pain, and over-the-counter pain relievers can help manage discomfort. Avoid eating hard or hot foods that may exacerbate the issue. However, seek professional dental care as soon as possible to address the underlying problem.
