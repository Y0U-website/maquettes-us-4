Why Airway Health Matters

Breathing should be easy, quiet, and mostly through the nose. When a child or adult is not breathing well during sleep, the body may not fully rest. Over time, poor airway function can affect energy, mood, concentration, growth, and overall wellness.

In children, airway concerns are not always obvious. Some kids do not simply appear “tired.” Instead, they may seem hyperactive, irritable, restless, or have trouble paying attention in school. Parents may notice mouth breathing, snoring, dark circles under the eyes, bedwetting, teeth grinding, or frequent waking during the night.

Common Signs of Airway Issues in Children Some of the most common signs parents notice include snoring, noisy breathing, sleeping with the mouth open, restless sleep, teeth grinding, frequent waking, bedwetting, daytime fatigue, trouble focusing, hyperactivity, and chronic mouth breathing.

Other signs may include crowded teeth, a narrow upper jaw, a crossbite, open bite, forward head posture, or difficulty keeping the lips closed at rest. These concerns do not always mean there is a serious airway issue, but they are worth evaluating early.

Frequently Asked Questions About Airway Orthodontics

Can orthodontics help with mouth breathing?

Orthodontics may help when mouth breathing is related to narrow arches, jaw development, crowding, or bite issues. Dr. Franco can evaluate whether orthodontic treatment may support better oral posture and nasal breathing.

Does airway orthodontics cure sleep apnea?

Airway orthodontics is not a guaranteed cure for sleep apnea. However, it may help improve structural issues that contribute to restricted airflow, especially in growing children. Some patients may need care from an ENT or sleep physician as part of a complete treatment plan.

What age should my child be evaluated?

A first orthodontic evaluation is recommended around age 7. If your child snores, mouth breathes, grinds their teeth, or has restless sleep, it may be worth scheduling sooner.

Is airway orthodontic treatment painful?

Most airway-focused orthodontic treatments, such as expanders or braces, are well tolerated. Patients may feel pressure or mild soreness as the mouth adjusts, but treatment is carefully monitored for comfort.
