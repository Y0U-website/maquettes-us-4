The details that matter

Retrofit your current denture

When possible, we upgrade the denture you already own rather than making a new one. Same stability, lower cost.

Two to four implants per arch

For most overdenture cases, two to four implants deliver excellent stability without the cost of a full six-implant bridge.

Surgery and restoration in one office

No referrals out. The same team plans the case, places the implants, and delivers the final prosthesis.

Long-term maintenance

Attachment inserts are inexpensive to replace as they wear. We set realistic expectations about upkeep from the start.

The process

  • 01 Consultation and scan Cone-beam 3D imaging and evaluation of your existing denture if you have one. Treatment plan and cost review.
  • 02 Implant placement Two to four implants are placed under local anesthesia. Your existing denture is adjusted to wear during healing.
  • 03 Integration Three to four months of healing. The implants fuse to bone while you continue wearing your denture.
  • 04 Attachments Locator or bar attachments are connected to the implants and the denture is fitted to snap in place. Immediate improvement in stability.

Who is this for

Implant-supported dentures are designed for patients who already wear a conventional denture and want dramatically better stability, as well as patients facing the loss of all teeth in an arch who want a prosthesis with predictable retention from day one. The treatment is especially transformative for lower dentures, which often struggle for suction on the narrow lower ridge. Most adults in reasonable health qualify, and the consultation confirms feasibility for your specific anatomy.

Good candidates typically have

  • Most or all teeth missing in an arch, or a current denture they want to upgrade for better stability
  • Adequate bone in the anterior jaw to support two to four implants (less bone is needed than for fixed full-arch designs)
  • Controlled medical conditions, particularly stable A1C for diabetic patients
  • Healthy gum tissue and any periodontal infection resolved before surgery
  • Non-smoker preferred, smoking through integration measurably increases failure rates
  • Willingness to maintain a daily cleaning routine, including taking the snap-in denture out at night
  • Realistic expectations about the 3 to 4 month integration period before final attachment of the prosthesis
  • Manual dexterity to remove and replace a snap-in overdenture, or interest in the fixed hybrid option if dexterity is limited

When we recommend a different approach

Some patients are better served by other paths. If you have several healthy remaining teeth, a partial denture or a combination of crowns and bridges may be the more conservative option. If you want the closest possible feel to natural teeth and your case allows it, a fixed full-arch All-on-4 or full-mouth implant reconstruction may be a better fit despite the higher investment. Dr. Brar will be candid about which approach gives you the best long-term result for your anatomy and lifestyle.

Aftercare and longevity

Implant-supported denture cases have implant survival rates above 95 percent at ten years when planned and maintained well. The denture itself has its own service cycle, and the small attachment inserts that retain the snap-in design are routine wear items that are replaced inexpensively. Daily care is simpler than caring for a fixed prosthesis in some ways (the denture comes out for cleaning) and similar in others (the implant abutments need attention where they meet the gums).

Daily care

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush, including around each implant abutment in the mouth
  • Take out snap-in overdentures at night to give the gums a rest and allow proper cleaning
  • Clean the denture with a denture brush and non-abrasive cleaner after removal
  • Soak the denture overnight in a denture solution to maintain cleanliness and shape
  • Use a water flosser around the implant abutments daily for the most effective cleaning
  • Avoid sticky or extremely hard foods that can damage the prosthesis or stress the attachments
  • Wear a nightguard on the opposing arch if you grind, to protect the prosthesis

What to expect over time

The implants themselves typically last decades when bone health is maintained. The denture and attachment hardware have predictable service cycles that should be planned for from day one.

For patients still weighing implant treatment against a conventional approach, the full vs. partial dentures comparison lays out the non-implant paths.

  • Locator attachment nylon inserts wear and are replaced annually or every couple of years, a quick, inexpensive in-office procedure
  • The denture acrylic typically lasts 5 to 8 years before showing wear that warrants a reline or remake
  • Fixed hybrid bridges (on four or more implants) typically last 10 to 15 years before refurbishment
  • Six-month professional cleanings include checking each implant, the attachments, and the prosthesis fit
  • Annual recalls include x-rays to monitor bone levels around each implant
  • Smoking dramatically reduces long-term implant survival, quitting is the single best investment in case longevity
  • Bone loss around implants (peri-implantitis) is preventable with consistent home care and recall visits

Overdenture questions

Two to four implants per arch is typical for a removable overdenture. Four to six implants support a fixed, non-removable hybrid bridge. The right count depends on your bone volume, bite forces, and whether you want the prosthesis to be removable or fixed in place. Dr. Brar will explain the options and make a recommendation based on what produces the best long-term outcome for your anatomy.
