The best tooth replacement is the one that acts like a real tooth.

Dental implants replace the entire missing tooth, root included. That distinction matters more than most patients realize. The root of a natural tooth is what keeps the surrounding jawbone alive. Lose the root, and within twelve months the bone in that area begins to shrink, the gum line drops, and the face starts to subtly collapse around the gap. A bridge or a traditional denture only restores the visible portion of the tooth. The bone underneath continues to disappear.

An implant prevents that. A precisely placed titanium post takes over the root’s job: it stimulates the bone every time you bite or chew, and the bone responds by holding its shape for decades. That is why implants, done well, feel and function like a tooth you were born with. They do not click, do not slip, do not require special adhesives, and do not change how food tastes.

At Skyline Dental Care, implant dentistry is a substantial part of our practice. Dr. Brar plans and restores every case in-house and collaborates with Dr. Sang Park, DDS, MS , a periodontist, for the surgical placement of every implant. Every case begins with a cone-beam 3D scan and a digital surgical guide, so we know exactly where, how deep, and at what angle each implant will be placed before any incision is made. The result is the kind of predictable outcome that has made implants the standard of care for tooth replacement worldwide.

How a dental implant actually works

A complete dental implant restoration has three components:

The reason implants succeed so consistently is osseointegration itself. Once the bone has grown into and around the titanium surface, the implant is functionally part of the jaw. It is stable in every direction. That is why a single implant can support a crown that bites with full chewing force, and why four implants can anchor an entire arch of teeth as in All-on-4 .

For deeper background on what happens over the course of treatment, our single tooth implant timeline and full mouth implant timeline walk through every phase.

  • The implant. A small titanium post, typically 8 to 13 mm long, the width of a pencil lead, that is placed into the jawbone where the root used to be. Titanium is biocompatible: bone naturally grows to fuse with it, a process called osseointegration .
  • The abutment. A small connector that sits at the gum line and links the implant beneath to the restoration on top. Most abutments are titanium or zirconia, custom-milled for the individual case.
  • The restoration. The visible tooth, a crown for a single implant, a bridge for several teeth in a row, or a denture-style prosthesis for a full arch. The restoration is custom-shaded to match the surrounding teeth.

Am I a candidate for implants?

Most healthy adults are candidates for dental implants, but the precise answer comes from the cone-beam scan and exam at your consultation, not from a questionnaire. Four factors carry the most weight:

For full-arch candidacy specifically, which has its own bone-volume math because of the angled implant placement, see our All-on-4 candidacy guide .

  • Jawbone volume and density. The implant needs enough bone to sit in. If a tooth has been missing for years, the bone in that spot has almost certainly receded. The cone-beam image tells us exactly how much you have. When bone is insufficient, grafting or a sinus lift rebuilds it, typically with a three- to six-month healing period before implant placement. Most patients do not need significant grafting. Some do, and finding out at the planning stage prevents surprises mid-treatment.
  • Gum health. Active gum disease must be controlled before implants can be placed predictably. We treat this first if needed; once gum tissue is healthy, implants succeed at essentially the same rate as in patients who never had periodontal disease.
  • Medical history. Most medical conditions are compatible with implant treatment. The handful that require extra planning include uncontrolled diabetes, certain bone-affecting medications, head and neck radiation history, and active immunosuppression. We review your full medical history at consultation and coordinate with your physician when appropriate.
  • Habits. Smoking is the single biggest controllable risk factor for implant failure, not because it makes implants impossible, but because it slows healing. We discuss this candidly and, when relevant, offer a structured timeline to reduce smoking around the surgical phase.

Caring for implants is simpler than you might think

Once your final restoration is in place, the daily routine for implant care looks essentially identical to caring for natural teeth: brush twice a day, floss or use interdental brushes daily, and keep up with routine professional cleanings twice a year. Implant-supported dentures have a slightly different cleaning routine because of the attachment hardware, we walk through that in detail in caring for implant-supported dentures .

The short-term recovery from implant surgery itself is the part most patients worry about most. The reality is more manageable than the anticipation, most patients describe the first 48 to 72 hours as "uncomfortable but not painful," and are back to normal eating within a week. The day-by-day breakdown of what is normal (and what to call us about) is in recovering from implant surgery .

Long-term, the single biggest predictor of implant success is consistent home care plus consistent recall appointments. Implants do not get cavities, but the gum tissue and bone around them can develop peri-implantitis, the implant equivalent of gum disease , if plaque accumulates. The good news: this is entirely preventable with the same habits that keep natural teeth healthy.

Implants vs. bridges vs. dentures

Implants are not always the right answer. For some patients, particularly those whose general medical situation makes surgery inadvisable, or those whose preference is the lowest possible upfront cost, a bridge or a traditional denture is the better choice. Here is how the three approaches actually differ:

We walk through every viable option at the consultation and give a candid recommendation rather than upselling a more complex plan. The right answer depends on your specific anatomy, goals, and budget, not on what we have available to sell.

  • Single tooth gap. An implant replaces the missing tooth without touching the neighbors. A dental bridge spans the gap by attaching to the neighboring teeth, which means those teeth must be filed down to anchor the bridge, permanently changing their structure. The implant preserves the surrounding bone; the bridge does not. The detailed comparison is in implant vs. bridge for a single missing tooth .
  • Several adjacent teeth missing. Options multiply here. An implant-supported bridge (two implants anchoring three or four teeth) is often the most bone-preserving choice. A traditional bridge is faster but relies on the neighboring teeth. A partial denture is the most economical but the least stable.
  • Full arch or near-full arch missing. The traditional option is a complete denture , which sits on the gums and is held in place by suction and (sometimes) adhesive. The implant alternatives are All-on-4 (a fixed bridge on four implants, the most cost-effective implant option for a full arch) or full mouth dental implants (six to eight implants per arch, the most stable long-term solution). For the side-by-side comparison see full mouth implants vs. All-on-4 .
  • Existing denture wearers who want more stability. Implant-supported dentures dramatically improve denture stability without the cost of a fully fixed prosthesis. The detailed comparison of removable vs. fixed implant dentures is in snap-in vs. fixed implant dentures .

The implant process , step by step

Five phases over four to nine months. Most of that time is your body quietly healing, not appointments. Here is exactly what each phase involves.

  • 01 Consultation and 3D imaging We take a cone-beam CT scan, review your medical history, evaluate jawbone density, and walk through your goals. You leave with a written treatment plan and an itemized cost estimate, with and without insurance.
  • 02 Bone grafting (only if needed) If the cone-beam scan shows insufficient bone for safe placement, we perform a targeted graft. Most patients do not need one. When grafting is required, healing typically takes three to six months before implant surgery.
  • 03 Implant placement Using a 3D-printed surgical guide, the titanium implant is placed under local anesthesia. The visit usually runs 60 to 90 minutes for a single implant. Most patients return to normal activity in two to three days.
  • 04 Healing and osseointegration Over three to six months the implant fuses with the surrounding bone. You wear a temporary restoration during this period. We monitor healing at brief check-in visits, no major appointments during this stretch.
  • 05 Abutment and final restoration Once the implant has integrated, we attach the abutment (the connector) and seat the final crown, bridge, or implant-supported denture. The restoration is custom-shaded to blend with your surrounding teeth.

Implant questions

Most healthy adults with adequate jawbone are candidates. The determining factors are bone density, gum health, general medical status, and habits like smoking. A cone-beam 3D scan at consultation tells us exactly what is possible. If bone is currently insufficient, bone grafting or alternative implant solutions are usually available. For full-arch candidacy specifically, see our All-on-4 candidacy guide .

A straightforward single implant typically spans four to six months from placement to final crown, most of that time is waiting for the implant to integrate with the jawbone. For All-on-4 full-arch cases, we can often deliver a functional temporary bridge the same day as surgery, with the final fixed bridge placed several months later. The detailed phase-by-phase walkthrough lives in our single tooth and full mouth timeline articles.

The procedure itself is performed under local anesthesia, and most patients report less discomfort than expected. Post-operative soreness typically lasts two to three days and is well-managed with over-the-counter pain relief. The most common comment we hear at the one-week check is "I expected this to be worse." A complete day-by-day breakdown is in recovering from implant surgery .

Pricing depends on the number of implants, whether bone grafting is needed, and the restoration type (single crown, bridge, or full-arch). After the consultation and 3D scan, you receive a complete written estimate. We work with CareCredit and Cherry financing and offer an in-house dental plan to make larger cases manageable. See the cost and financing section on this page for more detail.

The implant post itself (titanium, anchored in bone) has excellent long-term success rates and often lasts a lifetime with proper care. The crown or bridge on top typically lasts 10 to 20 years before requiring replacement, similar to natural dentition under normal wear. The variable that matters most is daily home care plus consistent professional cleanings .

Most PPO dental plans cover a portion of implant treatment, typically the crown or denture portion and sometimes part of the surgical fee. Coverage caps vary widely. We verify benefits before treatment and explain what your specific plan covers in writing. For plans without implant coverage, our in-house dental plan and financing through CareCredit or Cherry keep monthly payments manageable.
