The process

  • 01 Comprehensive planning 3D imaging, scans, photos, and a thorough conversation about goals, timeline, and investment.
  • 02 Surgical phase Any remaining teeth are removed, grafting is performed as needed, and implants are placed under local anesthesia. Provisional teeth are placed same day when indicated.
  • 03 Healing and integration Three to six months of bone integration. Regular short check-ins confirm healing.
  • 04 Final prosthesis Your definitive bridges or overdentures are designed, tried-in, and seated. Recall schedule begins.

Who is this for

Full mouth implant reconstruction is for patients facing the loss of most or all of their teeth, whether from advanced gum disease, repeated restoration failure, severe wear from decades of grinding, or a combination of factors. It is also the right path for long-term denture wearers ready to upgrade to a stable, implant-anchored solution. Because the case spans surgical, restorative, and prosthetic phases, the consultation focuses as much on long-term feasibility as on the immediate clinical picture.

Good candidates typically have

  • Most or all teeth missing, or remaining teeth that cannot be predictably saved with root canals or other conservative treatment
  • Sufficient bone in at least one arch, or willingness to undergo grafting where needed
  • Controlled systemic health, particularly stable A1C for diabetic patients
  • No active uncontrolled periodontal disease , infections must be brought under control first
  • Non-smoker, or willingness to stop through integration, smoking dramatically reduces implant survival rates
  • Comfort with longer surgical appointments, which we plan with breaks built in
  • A clear understanding that the timeline runs 6 to 12 months from consultation to final teeth
  • Financial planning in place, with realistic expectations about the multi-phase investment

Aftercare and longevity

Full mouth implant cases enjoy implant survival rates above 95 percent at ten years when planned and maintained well. The prosthesis sitting on the implants, whether a fixed zirconia bridge, hybrid acrylic prosthesis, or removable overdenture, is the serviceable component, with its own predictable maintenance cycle. Daily home care is simpler than living with a conventional denture and similar to caring for natural teeth, with focused attention on cleaning where the prosthesis meets the gums.

Daily care

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush, paying close attention to the gum line around each implant abutment
  • Use a water flosser daily, this is the most effective tool for cleaning under fixed full-arch prostheses and around overdenture attachments
  • Use floss threaders or superfloss for areas the water flosser misses, particularly between implant abutments
  • Take out removable overdentures at night for gum and prosthesis cleaning
  • Avoid extremely hard or sticky foods that can fracture acrylic teeth or stress the implants
  • Wear a nightguard if you grind, parafunctional forces are the leading cause of premature prosthetic wear

What to expect over time

The implants themselves are designed to last decades when bone health is maintained. The prosthesis components have predictable service lives that should be planned for from the beginning, not encountered as a surprise later.

Our full mouth implant timeline walks through the multi-phase treatment in detail, the recovering from implant surgery resource covers the post-op weeks, and the bone grafting and sinus lifts guide addresses the preliminary procedures that apply to many full-mouth cases. The snap-in vs. fixed implant dentures comparison lays out the prosthetic options side by side.

  • Acrylic-wrapped hybrid prostheses typically last 10 to 15 years before refurbishment or remake
  • Monolithic zirconia full-arch bridges typically last 15 to 20 years
  • Overdenture attachment inserts wear and need replacement annually or every couple of years (an inexpensive routine procedure)
  • Six-month professional cleanings include checking each implant, the prosthesis fit, and bone levels by x-ray
  • Smoking dramatically accelerates bone loss around implants and shortens case lifespan
  • Screw-retained designs allow us to remove the prosthesis in the office every one to two years for thorough cleaning underneath
  • Tooth chips or small fractures on acrylic prostheses are usually repaired without remaking the whole appliance
