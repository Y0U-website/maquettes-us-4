Who is this for

A single tooth implant is the conservative, long-term solution when one tooth is missing or non-restorable and the neighboring teeth are healthy. It is also the right call when a failed root canal, deep fracture, or severe decay means a tooth has to come out, and you want a replacement that does not borrow support from the teeth on either side. Most adults with reasonable health and adequate bone are candidates, but the consultation is where we confirm it for your specific anatomy.

Good candidates typically have

  • Adequate bone volume at the site, or a willingness to graft if needed
  • Healthy gums and any periodontal disease already controlled
  • Controlled medical conditions (diabetes with stable A1C, no recent bone-modifying medications, no recent radiation therapy to the jaw)
  • A stable bite, with a nightguard if grinding or clenching is a factor
  • Non-smoker preferred, smoking roughly doubles the failure rate during osseointegration
  • Realistic expectations about the 3 to 6 month timeline from placement to final crown
  • Completion of any pending fillings or other active dental work before surgery

When we recommend a different approach

Not every missing tooth needs an implant. If the teeth on either side of the gap already need full-coverage crowns , a traditional bridge can be the more conservative choice because the crowns would be done anyway. If multiple adjacent teeth are missing, a longer implant-supported bridge or even implant-supported dentures may make more sense than several individual implants. And in select cases where bone loss is significant and grafting is not desired, a well-made partial denture remains a valid option. Dr. Brar walks through these trade-offs honestly at the consultation.

Cost, insurance, and financing

A single tooth implant in our area typically runs $3,500 to $6,000 for the complete treatment, which includes the titanium implant fixture, the abutment that connects to it, and the final crown. The wide range reflects real differences in case complexity: a straightforward back-tooth replacement falls toward the lower end, while front-tooth cases requiring custom shading, soft-tissue work, or premium ceramic crowns sit higher. Any preliminary work such as extraction, socket preservation grafting, or sinus lifting is quoted separately and discussed up front.

Our financial page covers payment options in detail, and our front desk is happy to walk you through a personalized estimate before you commit. We would rather have a candid conversation about cost early than have it become a barrier later in treatment.

  • Insurance: Most PPO plans contribute toward implants, abutments, and crowns, though policies vary widely. We verify your benefits before treatment begins and provide a written estimate so there are no surprises at the seating appointment.
  • CareCredit financing: Six, twelve, and twenty-four month no-interest plans are available for qualifying applicants, which spreads the investment across the natural treatment timeline rather than asking for everything up front.
  • Cherry financing: A second option to compare, with monthly payment plans and 0 % APR for qualifying applicants, and an application that uses a soft credit check rather than a hard pull.
  • In-house dental plan: Our membership plan includes meaningful percentage discounts on implant treatment for patients without insurance, and pays for itself in most multi-phase cases.
  • HSA and FSA: Implant treatment is generally an eligible expense for health savings and flexible spending accounts. We can provide documentation for reimbursement.
  • Phased payment plans: Because implant treatment is delivered in distinct phases over several months, payment is naturally spread across surgery, healing, and restoration. We do not require the full amount at consultation.

Aftercare and longevity

A well-placed single tooth implant has documented success rates above 95 percent at ten years, and many function for decades without intervention. The aftercare protocol is straightforward and largely indistinguishable from caring for a natural tooth, which is one of the strongest arguments for an implant over a bridge or removable prosthesis. The first few weeks after placement matter most, then care settles into a simple daily routine.

Daily care

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush, paying attention to the gum line around the crown
  • Floss daily, implants floss exactly like natural teeth, no threader needed once the final crown is seated
  • Use a water flosser as a useful supplement, especially if you have a history of gum disease
  • Avoid abrasive whitening toothpastes on the ceramic crown surface
  • Skip ice chewing, popcorn kernels, and other foods that can chip even very strong ceramics
  • Wear your nightguard if you grind or clench, grinding forces are the single most common cause of long-term crown damage

What to expect over time

With proper care, the implant fixture itself is designed to last a lifetime. The visible crown sitting on top is the wear component and typically needs replacement at the fifteen to twenty year mark, depending on bite forces, material, and habits. Replacement is straightforward because the implant and abutment remain in place, we simply fabricate a new crown.

Implant questions

From the day the implant is placed to the day your final crown is seated, expect three to six months. That timeline reflects osseointegration, the period during which bone fuses to the titanium implant surface. We use a comfortable temporary during healing so your smile stays intact. For straightforward cases in the front of the mouth, shorter timelines are sometimes possible.

The surgery itself is comfortable under local anesthesia; most patients compare it favorably to a routine extraction. Mild soreness and some swelling for a few days afterward are normal and managed with over-the-counter pain relievers. Serious postoperative pain is uncommon with a well-placed implant.

Bone loss is common after a tooth has been missing for a while. In many cases we can graft additional bone at the time of extraction or during implant placement. For more significant bone loss, a dedicated bone graft appointment or a sinus lift (for upper back teeth) may be needed first. Dr. Brar uses 3D cone-beam imaging to plan carefully, and will tell you honestly whether an implant is a good fit or whether a bridge is the better path.
