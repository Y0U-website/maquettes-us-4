The details that matter

3D guided surgery

Cone-beam imaging plus a custom surgical guide means implants are placed to pre-planned positions, not by eye.

Same-day teeth when possible

For qualifying patients, a provisional fixed bridge is attached the day of surgery. You do not leave without a smile.

Grafting minimized

The angled posterior implants often engage existing bone without a sinus lift, which shortens treatment and reduces cost.

One team, start to finish

Surgery and restoration are performed by the same team in our office. There is no handoff between offices.

The process

  • 01 Consultation and 3D scan Cone-beam imaging, digital impressions, and a detailed treatment plan covering timeline and cost.
  • 02 Surgery day Any remaining teeth are extracted, four implants are placed, and in most cases a temporary fixed bridge is attached the same day.
  • 03 Healing Three to six months of integration. You wear the fixed temporary and eat softer foods while the implants fuse to bone.
  • 04 Final restoration Your definitive zirconia or hybrid bridge is fabricated and seated. Regular recalls maintain it long-term.

Who is this for

All-on-4 is designed for patients who are missing all or most of the teeth in one or both arches, or whose remaining teeth are failing due to advanced gum disease, multiple fractures, or repeated restoration failures. It is also a strong option for long-term denture wearers who are tired of the rocking, slipping, and dietary restrictions that come with a conventional prosthesis. The angled posterior implant design means many patients with moderate bone loss qualify without needing extensive grafting first.

Good candidates typically have

  • Most or all teeth missing in an arch, or teeth that cannot be predictably saved
  • Adequate bone in the anterior jaw, enough to support four implants engaging dense, stable bone
  • Controlled medical conditions, including stable A1C for diabetic patients and no recent bone-modifying medications
  • Healthy soft tissue or willingness to address any active periodontal infection before surgery
  • Non-smoker preferred, smoking through the integration period significantly increases failure risk
  • A clear understanding that surgery day involves several hours and that final teeth arrive 3 to 6 months later
  • Comfort with a longer surgical appointment performed under local anesthesia

When we recommend a different approach

Not every patient is best served by All-on-4. If you have only a few missing teeth, individual implants or a smaller implant-supported bridge may preserve more healthy structure. If bone loss is severe and grafting is undesired, a snap-in implant-supported overdenture on two or three implants can deliver strong stability at a lower investment. And for patients who simply are not ready for surgery, a well-made set of conventional dentures remains a respectable option. Our candidate guide walks through these considerations in more detail.

Aftercare and longevity

Well-planned All-on-4 cases have documented implant survival rates above 95 percent at ten years, and many patients function on their original prosthesis for fifteen years or longer. Daily care is simpler than caring for a conventional denture and similar to caring for a fixed bridge, with one important addition: cleaning under the prosthesis where it meets the gums. The transition from a removable denture to a fixed bridge is usually described as a meaningful upgrade in daily life within the first month.

Daily care

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled brush, including the underside of the bridge where it meets the gum tissue
  • Use a water flosser daily, this is the single most effective tool for cleaning under a fixed full-arch prosthesis
  • Floss threaders and superfloss reach areas the water flosser misses
  • Avoid extremely hard foods (whole ice cubes, hard candies, popcorn kernels) that can chip the prosthetic teeth
  • Skip abrasive whitening toothpastes that can dull the acrylic or zirconia surface over time
  • Wear a nightguard if you grind, heavy parafunctional forces are the single most common cause of prosthetic wear or fracture

What to expect over time

The four titanium implants themselves are designed to last decades. The prosthesis sitting on top is the serviceable component: acrylic teeth on a titanium framework may show wear at the ten to fifteen year mark and can be refurbished or replaced without disturbing the implants. Monolithic zirconia bridges typically last fifteen to twenty years before considering replacement.

Our life after All-on-4 guide covers what daily living actually looks like, and the recovering from implant surgery resource walks through the surgical recovery period. For patients comparing All-on-4 with alternative full-arch designs, the snap-in vs. fixed implant dentures comparison lays out the trade-offs in detail.

  • Six-month professional cleanings include checking the prosthesis fit, the implant abutments, and surrounding bone
  • Annual recalls include x-rays to monitor bone levels around each implant
  • Smoking dramatically increases the rate of bone loss around implants, which can shorten the life of the case
  • On screw-retained designs, we can remove the bridge in the office for thorough cleaning underneath every one to two years
  • Acrylic tooth chips or fractures can typically be repaired chairside or in the lab without replacing the whole prosthesis

All-on-4 questions

All-on-4 is a specific full-arch technique that uses four strategically angled implants to support an entire arch of replacement teeth. The angled rear implants allow the restoration to engage dense bone without needing a sinus lift for most upper cases. Individual implants for every tooth would cost significantly more and typically require more grafting. All-on-4 is designed to deliver a full, fixed set of teeth efficiently.
