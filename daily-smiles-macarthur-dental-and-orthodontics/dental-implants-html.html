What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are essentially designed to mimic both the root and crown portions of teeth, something that cannot be said for dental bridges and dentures. They consist of a titanium post that integrates with the jawbone tissue, a restoration that sits on top, and an abutment that connects these components to ensure a sturdy and long-lasting fit.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

To replace teeth with dental implants, multiple phases of care must be completed. Learning how dental implants work in Irving can better prepare you for what lies ahead on your journey to a new and improved smile. From the initial consultation to the final placement of your dental restoration, you can count on us to ensure optimal results that will last a lifetime.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The first phase is to schedule an initial consultation with a member of our dental team. During this visit, you and our implant dentist in Irving will discuss your smile goals as well as your oral and overall health, and your jawbone density. These factors will determine if you’re a candidate for treatment and if you require preliminary care before moving forward with dental implant placement.

Dental Implant Surgery

Once you’re cleared for dental implant surgery, Dr. Rafiq can complete this procedure right here in-office for maximum convenience! This procedure involves one or more incisions in the gum tissue, followed by the precise placement of one or more dental implant posts. Positioning it to ensure maximum support, our team will place a protective healing cap over the area.

Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment

After your surgery is complete, you will return home to begin the recovery phase of treatment. This includes spending 3-6 months allowing osseointegration to occur. This is the process of bone fusing with the dental implant(s). This is necessary to ensure a stable and solid foundation for your new teeth. Once you’re fully healed from surgery, you’ll return to our dental office in Irving to receive your metal abutments. These serve as a connector piece between the restoration and implant post.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

The final phase of dental implant placement is receiving your customized restoration, which will be secured on top of your dental implants in Irving. This can include a dental crown, bridge, or denture depending on the number of teeth you are having replaced. Made of lifelike materials, the restoration will blend into your existing smile, looking and functioning like normal. You’ll receive instructions regarding how to properly take care of your new teeth, ensuring they last 30 years or even a lifetime.

Missing One Tooth

To replace one missing tooth, we can surgically place a single dental implant. After it has fused with the jawbone, we will secure a metal connector piece atop the implant called an abutment. Missing Multiple Teeth

If you’re missing several teeth throughout your arch or in a row, we may use a combination of placing implant bridges and implant crowns. For multiple consecutive teeth, we can secure a dental bridge to two dental implants placed at either side of the gap in your arch. Missing All of Your Teeth

To restore one or both rows of missing teeth, anywhere from four to eight dental implants can be secured beneath your gumline to hold a denture. Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants have the potential to last the rest of your life, but that will only happen if you give them the right kind of care. As durable as implants are, they can still be damaged, and they can fail if the tissues holding them in place become infected. Fortunately, implant maintenance is quite simple; in fact, it’s not much different than taking care of your natural teeth. Follow the steps below to keep your newly rebuilt smile in good shape for the long term!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While you don’t have to worry about your dental implants getting cavities, you still need to keep them clean with regular brushing and flossing. Any bacteria that accumulate around your implants might eventually lead to gum disease, which is a major cause of dental implant failure. As such, you need to pay extra close attention to the area around your implants while brushing. Don’t neglect the spot where the implant restoration meets the gums.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants don’t come with any diet restrictions, meaning you’re free to enjoy all of your favorite foods. But when it comes to foods that are high in sugar, you’ll want to exercise caution. Consuming sugar too often can increase your risk for gum disease, leading to problems with your implants. You should instead focus on foods with calcium and vitamin C that keep the jawbones strong and the gum tissues healthy.

Break Bad Habits

Dental implants can break or fail if you’re not careful with them. You need to stay away from any habits that might be putting your implant posts in danger, such as:

As soon as you’re aware of any habits that could be putting your implants at risk, you should take steps to break them.

  • Using your teeth as tools to open packaging.
  • Crunching ice, peppermints, and other overly hard foods.
  • Chewing on fingernails, pen tips, and other hard, inedible objects.
  • Smoking or chewing tobacco, which increases the risk of infection around your implants.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Dental implants may be stronger and sturdier than other forms of tooth replacement, but they are not indestructible. When playing sports that carry a risk of getting hit in the mouth, you should wear a mouthguard at all times to keep your implants safe. It’s also a good idea to get a nightguard to wear while you’re sleeping; this will protect your implants from any involuntary clenching or grinding that you might do during the night.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Every six months, you should visit our office for a regular checkup and cleaning. While we’re looking at your natural teeth, we’ll also check your dental implants to see if any problems need to be taken care of. Addressing small issues can help you avoid bigger problems like implant failure.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Directly following your dental implant surgery, which Dr. Hirji completes in-office, it’s important to preserve the blood clot that will form around your dental implant, just like after a tooth extraction. In order to do this, don’t touch or disturb the surgical site or gums. It’s also important that you avoid drinking from a straw and don’t use any tobacco products, including vaping. This can help prevent an infection from developing.

Common Side-Effects After Dental Implant Placement

It’s not uncommon for patients to experience side-effects after their surgery, but knowing which ones are considered normal can help decrease your anxiety and help you focus on your recovery. After your procedure, you’ll likely experience some soreness, mild discomfort, and even bleeding for the first one to three days, all of which should become less present with time. If your pain worsens or you have uncommon side-effects, give our office a call . Some instances in which you would call our team include:

  • Your pain progresses with time and can’t be relieved with prescribed or recommended pain medication.
  • Your gums remain swollen or bleeding for longer than a week.
  • Bleeding near the surgical site persists for over a week.

Eat a Soft Diet After Implant Surgery

For the following several days after your surgery, it’s best to maintain a soft diet and avoid foods that are hot. Before your procedure, stock your pantry with the following:

  • Pudding
  • Smoothies
  • Yogurt
  • Ice cream
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Oatmeal
  • Mashed potatoes
  • Pasta
  • Protein drinks

Stick to a Good Oral Hygiene Routine

One of the leading causes of implant failure is an infection called peri-implantitis. This can occur dure to a build-up of harmful oral bacteria around the implant, causing soft and hard tissues to erode. One great way to avoid this is by maintaining a good oral hygiene routine. You can do this by:

  • Brushing your teeth with ADA-accepted toothpaste twice a day for two minutes each time. Be gentle when cleaning near your surgical site.
  • Rinse with salt water two to three times a day, especially after you eat snacks or meals. Don’t spit the salt water out of your mouth, as this can dislodge the blood clot. Instead, let it flow out of your mouth into the sink.
  • Use alcohol-free mouthwash.

Caring for Your Restorations After They’re Attached to Your Implants

One of the best benefits of getting dental implants is that once the procedure is complete, you care for your teeth like you normally would. This means that you won’t have to worry about going out and buying special products. Simply brush your teeth twice a day, floss daily between your natural teeth and prosthetic, and rinse your mouth daily. It’s also important to visit our team every six months for routine checkups and cleanings .
