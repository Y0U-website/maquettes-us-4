What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a titanium cylinder that forms the new “root” of the tooth. It is surgically inserted into the jaw, where it bonds with the tissue and bone via the natural process of osseointegration. This merging is what gives dental implants the ability to last for decades if not a lifetime. Following osseointegration, we can affix a metal abutment on top of the implant, allowing us to secure a custom crown, bridge, or denture into place and complete your smile.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Throughout the healing process, your existing bone mass will fuse (called osseointegration) with the dental implant to create a solid foundation. Once this phase is completed, another sturdy titanium piece can be added: the abutment. Its function is to allow a final porcelain restoration to be securely attached.

The biggest benefit of this is that patients don’t have to worry about a set of prosthetic teeth falling out of their mouth. Better yet, there won’t be any shifting or sliding. With a reliable and sturdy abutment acting as the anchor, the final result will be a smile that looks and feels like you were born with it.

Placement of Dental Implant Restorations

The dental implant procedure has four main parts: the consultation, the implant insertion, the abutment placement, and the addition of the permanent restoration. The latter is what the world will see, but little will they know that beneath what appears to be a natural tooth, tedious work has gone into restoring your smile and oral health to normal.

If you’re only missing one tooth, then a single dental crown will be added. For patients with two or more missing teeth, there is the option of receiving an implant-retained bridge. The apparatus consists of a series of porcelain teeth that are affixed to the single structure, and it’s held in place via the abutment.

If you’re missing a full set of teeth, then you’ll need a set of implant dentures. After a series of strategically placed implants have healed and fused properly, the abutments can be added. They will attach to special snaps at the bottom of the dentures to create a secure, natural, and appealing smile.

Day-to-Day Benefits

  • Increased confidence. Dental implant restorations are custom-made to blend seamlessly with your remaining teeth. Since they’ll complement your facial aesthetics perfectly, you can flash your beautiful new smile with confidence.

Long-Term Benefits

Dental implants can certainly benefit you right now. However, they can also provide many advantages for years to come, such as:

  • High success rate. Implants have an impressive success rate of about 95 percent even 20 years after they’ve been placed.
  • Natural-looking appearance. Because implants stimulate blood flow in the jaw, they prevent deterioration and the sunken, aged look that often accompanies tooth loss.
  • Long-lasting results. Unlike dentures and dental bridges, implants can last decades without needing replacement.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Dental implants are an incredibly versatile solution. In fact, almost any otherwise healthy adult who has lost one or more teeth is eligible for this treatment. Depending on your unique circumstances, we may rebuild your smile with dental implants in one of the following ways:

Missing One Tooth

If we are rebuilding a singular tooth, an implant-retained dental crown will be the best choice for replacement. This crown will be designed as an ideal complement to the rest of your smile in look and feel; better yet, it will be able to fit right in alongside natural teeth without requiring any significant reduction of their shapes, which is much better for your long-term oral health. After some time has passed, it will feel like you never lost a tooth in the first place!

Missing Multiple Teeth

If you are missing multiple teeth in a row, there is the option to have an implant-retained dental bridge put into place. This unique restoration eliminates the need to alter healthy tooth structures that are otherwise necessary with traditional fixed bridges. Instead, you can enjoy a more durable and permanent fixture that lasts decades and restores function to your smile.

Missing All Teeth

When missing all of your teeth along the top or bottom row, an implant denture can be put into place using between four and eight dental implants. Depending on your case, this number may be higher or lower, as it cannot be determined until we meet for a scheduled consultation. With an implant denture, you can enjoy a restored bite, a more structurally sound jawbone, and visually appealing aesthetics.

For those who lack the necessary jawbone density to receive traditional implant dentures, there is the option of choosing All-On-4. This process uses only four titanium posts, which are placed in specific areas of the jawbone (the densest portions), allowing for a stronger and more supportive base.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

Other factors that will affect the total cost of your dental implant treatment include the following:

  • Number of dental implants: One dental implant to replace one missing tooth will cost less than four to six implants for an implant denture, but an implant denture will be more affordable than replacing each tooth individually.
  • Type of Restoration: The type of dental implant used will also affect the cost. Crowns, bridges, and implant dentures each cost different amounts. The smaller the prosthetic, the less expensive.
  • Size or material : The size and material of the implant post will also affect the price. These can be made longer or shorter, depending on their placement in your jawbone. Furthermore, some are made of zirconia instead of titanium, which will also influence the cost.
  • Brand of dental implant : Much like a car or cell phone brand, different dental implants have different manufacturers to cater to the unique needs of a variety of patients. What Should You Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery?

  • You should never drive for at least 24 hours following your surgery until any sedation has completely worn off.
  • Keep your tongue away from your surgery site.
  • Avoid exercising and get plenty of rest.
  • Take any prescriptions provided to you by your implant dentist in Baytown.
  • Avoid alcohol and smoking.
  • Place an ice pack on your mouth for 10-minute increments if you experience swelling.
  • Don’t rinse your mouth on the day of your surgery.
  • Do not drink carbonated beverages or use straws.

Common Side Effects

Certain side effects are completely common after your oral surgery, including some swelling, bleeding, and general discomfort. As time goes on, you should notice that they begin to go away. If they seem to be getting worse, please contact our office. It will also take a few hours for any numbness to wear off after your surgery, so keep that in mind as you navigate the rest of your day.

Diet

After your surgery, you’ll need to make sure that you stay hydrated and eat soft foods for the first few days to protect your blood clots. Here are some examples of foods that we suggest our patients eat after their procedure:

  • Eggs
  • Pasta
  • Soup (not too hot)
  • Macaroni and cheese
  • Pancakes
  • Yogurt
  • Ice cream
  • Cooked vegetables
