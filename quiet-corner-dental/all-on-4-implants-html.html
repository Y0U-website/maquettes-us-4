How Do All-On-4 Implants Work?

While dental implants are designed to serve as new tooth roots in the jawbone for single restorations, they can also be utilized to support prosthetics like dentures. With All-On-4, your dentist will use four metal posts and place them in the thickest parts of your jawbone and at specific angles to optimize their stability for your new smile. After having your implants placed, you’ll typically wear a temporary prosthetic until you can have your permanent one attached.

Am I a Candidate for All-On-4 Implants?

Here are some of the qualifying factors that can make a patient eligible for All-On-4 implant treatment:

Even if you aren’t sure if you’re a good candidate for All-On-4, you can speak with our team about it. We can help determine if you’ll require any preliminary treatments to help you prepare for the procedure.

  • Has lost the majority or all of the teeth in a dental arch
  • No active gum disease or other oral health problems that can risk failure
  • Possesses adequate jawbone density (without needing a bone graft)
  • Committed to practicing good oral hygiene habits for the longevity of their smile
  • Has great overall health for a smoother recovery after the surgery

Benefits of All-On-4 Implants

By choosing to undergo All-On-4 dental implant treatment, you can generally expect to appreciate a wide range of benefits, including:

  • A stronger biting force that allows you to enjoy a greater range of food options compared to what dentures allow
  • Customized All-On-4 dentures that look completely natural with your unique smile
  • Simpler maintenance that allows you to clean your new teeth like normal ones
  • Dental implants stimulate the jawbone and prevent deterioration of the facial structure, helping you maintain a stronger mouth and a more youthful appearance

All-On-4 vs. Traditional Implants

For those who don’t have a lot of jawbone volume, they can still appreciate the advantages of All-On-4 implants, as this option can make the most of their current facial structure. Other than using a minimal number of metal posts, this solution also only requires one surgery most of the time, meaning less time spent recovering. That said, if you need more strength or stability for a longer-lasting tooth replacement, then you may require traditional dental implants instead. Our team will walk you through the details of both options so that you know which is best for you.
