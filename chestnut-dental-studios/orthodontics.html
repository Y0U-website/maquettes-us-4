Orthodontics Explained

Orthodontics aligns teeth and corrects bite problems such as crowding, spacing, overbite, underbite, and crossbite. Treatment can help children whose jaws are still developing and adults who want to improve function and appearance. Many patients ask, “What is orthodontics?” In simple terms, it uses gentle, controlled forces to move teeth into more ideal positions over time.

Modern options include traditional metal braces, low-profile ceramic braces, and clear aligners such as Invisalign and ClearCorrect. Each method can address different needs and lifestyles. An early orthodontic evaluation around age 7 can identify growth patterns and guide timely care. Adult orthodontics is also common and can be effective at nearly any age with healthy teeth and gums.

Benefits of Orthodontic Care

  • Improved bite function for chewing and speaking.
  • Easier cleaning due to reduced crowding and overlapping.
  • Lower risk of uneven tooth wear and jaw strain.
  • Enhanced smile esthetics and facial balance.
  • Support for long-term oral health and stability.
  • Customized plans for children, teens, and adults.

How Orthodontic Treatment Works

Orthodontic treatment begins with a comprehensive exam, digital scans or impressions, photographs, and X-rays. These records allow precise diagnosis of tooth positions and bite relationships. A tailored plan outlines recommended appliances, estimated timeline, and goals.

Braces

Braces use brackets and an archwire to move teeth. Gentle adjustments at regular intervals activate the wire and guide teeth into place. Options include metal for strength and ceramic for a more discreet look.

Clear Aligners

Clear aligners are a series of removable trays that gradually shift teeth. Patients wear each set for one to two weeks, 20 to 22 hours per day. They are removed for eating and brushing, making home care simple. Clear aligners vs braces is a common comparison; your plan considers bite complexity, esthetics, and daily habits.

Retention

After active treatment, retainers hold teeth steady while surrounding tissues adapt. Retention is essential to maintain results, whether you wore braces or clear aligners. Your plan may include fixed or removable retainers and a wear schedule tailored to your needs.

What to Expect During Care

Most cases take 6 to 24 months, depending on bite complexity and appliance type. Expect brief adjustment visits or periodic aligner check-ins to track progress. Mild pressure or soreness can occur after changes and typically improves within a few days. Good home care is important: brush twice daily, clean between teeth, and follow any dietary guidance for braces. Clear aligner users should wear trays as directed and keep them clean to support steady progress.
