Bone Grafting and Sinus Lift Augmentation

If imaging shows you don’t have enough bone volume, that doesn’t rule out implants; it just means we build the implant site first. Bone grafting adds material to weak or thin areas of the jaw to build a stronger foundation. In the upper jaw, where the sinus cavity sits close to the tooth roots, a sinus lift (sinus augmentation) may be needed to create enough vertical bone height for a successful implant.

These preparatory procedures add time to treatment, but they meaningfully improve long-term outcomes. Rushing an implant into insufficient bone is one of the more common reasons implants fail elsewhere.

Please note that we currently provide bone grafting treatment.

Titanium Implants and Full Arch Solutions

Titanium remains the gold standard implant material because of its strength, biocompatibility, and decades-long track record of predictable osseointegration.

For patients missing all or most of their teeth in an arch, full-arch implant solutions, sometimes placed with as few as four strategically positioned implants, can support a fixed, non-removable prosthesis rather than a traditional full denture.

This has become one of the fastest-growing areas of implant dentistry, and it’s worth discussing during your consultation if removable dentures haven’t worked well for you in the past.

Dental Implant Procedure

  • Consultation and Imaging : We review your goals, take 3D imaging, and build a treatment plan specific to your bone structure and the number of teeth being replaced.
  • Implant Placement : The titanium post is placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia (sedation options are available for anxious patients).
  • Healing (Osseointegration) : Over the following months, the bone fuses to the implant. This phase typically takes longer in the upper jaw than the lower jaw.
  • Final Restoration : Once healing is complete, an abutment and custom crown, bridge, or implant-supported denture are attached and matched to your natural teeth in shape and shade.

Mild swelling or soreness for a few days after surgery is normal and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief. Most patients return to normal activities within a day or two, while the bone-to-implant healing process itself continues quietly in the background for several months.

How Much Do Dental Implants Cost? Insurance and Financing

Implant cost depends on how many teeth you’re replacing, whether you need bone grafting or a sinus lift, and which final restoration (crown, bridge, or full arch prosthesis) you choose—so we build out real numbers during your consultation rather than quoting a one-size-fits-all price online.

Dental insurance sometimes covers part of the process, particularly the restorative crown, and we’re happy to help you understand your specific plan’s coverage. For costs insurance doesn’t cover, we also offer financing options so you can phase treatment in a way that fits your budget.
