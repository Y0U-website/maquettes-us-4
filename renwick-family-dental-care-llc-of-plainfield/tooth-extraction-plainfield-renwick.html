Tooth Extraction

We take a conservative approach and only recommend extractions when truly needed. Our promise is simple: we’ll always explain the "why" and ensure you’re fully informed before moving forward.

  • Broken or fractured teeth below the gumline
  • Advanced periodontal (gum) disease
  • Overcrowding , especially before orthodontic treatment
  • Impacted wisdom teeth
  • Teeth causing infection or pain

What to Expect During a Tooth Extraction

Many patients are surprised at how quick and comfortable the process can be. Here’s what you can typically expect:

Step 1 Consultation and Imaging First, we’ll examine your tooth and take digital X-rays to assess the root structure and surrounding bone. We’ll review your medical history and any medications you’re taking to avoid complications. If the tooth can be saved, we’ll present restorative alternatives first.

Step 3 The Extraction Itself Using gentle, precise movements, Dr. Que will carefully loosen and remove the tooth. The area is then cleaned, and gauze is applied to control bleeding. Stitches are sometimes placed, especially for surgical extractions, but in many cases, they’re not needed.

The entire procedure typically takes less than 30 minutes for a single tooth.

Aftercare and Recovery: What You Need to Know

The healing process is just as important as the procedure itself. We’ll send you home with personalized instructions to promote healing and prevent complications like dry socket .

Your Recovery May Include: Biting on gauze for 30 60 minutes post-procedure Icing the area for the first 24 hours to reduce swelling Soft foods for a few days (soups, smoothies, yogurt) Avoiding straws, smoking, or vigorous rinsing for at least 48 hours Mild discomfort or swelling , which can be managed with over-the-counter medication Most patients recover fully within 7 10 days, and many return to work or school within a day or two.

Have questions about healing? You can always call our office we’re here for you, even after the visit.

What Happens After a Tooth Is Removed?

Once healing is complete, we’ll help you decide if and how to replace your missing tooth. Depending on its location and function, options may include:

Replacing missing teeth helps preserve your jawbone, maintain your bite alignment, and restore your confidence when smiling. We’ll guide you through these choices based on your health, lifestyle, and budget.

  • Dental implants
  • Fixed bridges
  • Partial dentures
