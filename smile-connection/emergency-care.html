What to Do in a Dental Emergency

Knowing what to do in a dental emergency can make a significant difference in the outcome. Here’s what you should do in common dental emergencies:

  • Toothache: Rinse your mouth with warm water and use dental floss to remove any trapped food. If the pain persists, contact us immediately.
  • Knocked-Out Tooth: Handle the tooth by the crown (avoid touching the root), rinse it gently with water, and try to place it back in the socket if possible. If not, store it in a container of milk or saline solution and bring it to our office right away.
  • Broken Tooth: Rinse your mouth with warm water and apply a cold compress to reduce swelling. Save any broken pieces and bring them to your appointment.
  • Lost Filling or Crown: Keep the area clean and avoid chewing on that side of your mouth. Use dental cement or a piece of sugarless gum to temporarily fill the gap until you can see us.
  • Abscess: Rinse your mouth with salt water several times a day to reduce discomfort and draw the infection out. Seek immediate dental care to prevent the spread of infection.

Preventing Dental Emergencies

While accidents happen, there are steps you can take to reduce your risk of a dental emergency:

  • Maintain Good Oral Hygiene: Brush and floss regularly to prevent decay and gum disease.
  • Wear a Mouthguard: If you play contact sports, wear a mouthguard to protect your teeth from injury.
  • Avoid Hard Foods: Refrain from chewing on hard foods or objects that can crack or break your teeth.
  • Regular Checkups: Visit us regularly for checkups and cleanings to catch potential issues before they become emergencies.

FAQs About Emergency Dental Care

Q: What should I do if I have a dental emergency outside of office hours? A: If you experience a dental emergency outside of our regular office hours, please call our emergency number. We will do our best to accommodate you as soon as possible.

Q: How can I relieve dental pain until I can see a dentist? A: Over-the-counter pain relievers, cold compresses, and rinsing your mouth with salt water can help manage pain until you can get professional care.

Q: Is a lost filling or crown considered a dental emergency? A: Yes, a lost filling or crown should be treated as soon as possible to prevent further damage or infection.
