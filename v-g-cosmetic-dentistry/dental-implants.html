Dental Implants

Restore with confidence: the foundation of a lasting smile

Dental implants are a state-of-the-art tooth replacement solution made from durable materials like titanium. Unlike dentures or bridges, dental implants are designed to integrate with your jawbone, offering a permanent solution for missing teeth. Their strength and stability make them an ideal choice for restoring your smile and chewing function.

How Dental Implants Work

The process begins with your dentist or oral surgeon placing the implant post directly into your jawbone, where your missing tooth once was. Over time, this post fuses with the bone in a process known as osseointegration, creating a solid foundation for the replacement tooth. Once the implant has integrated with the jawbone, a custom-made crown that looks and feels like a natural tooth is attached to the implant post.

Why Choose Dental Implants?

Looks and Feels Like Natural Teeth One of the most significant advantages of dental implants is their natural appearance and feeling. Unlike other tooth replacement options, implants are designed to look, feel, and function like your natural teeth. This can boost your confidence and allow you to smile, eat, and speak with ease.

Permanent Solution for Tooth Loss Dental implants provide a long-lasting solution to tooth loss. With proper care, implants can last a lifetime, unlike dentures or bridges that may need to be replaced every few years. This permanence can offer peace of mind and reduce long-term dental costs.

Protects Bone Health and Prevents Bone Loss When you lose a tooth, the bone that supported the tooth can begin to deteriorate. Dental implants are the only tooth replacement option that stimulates bone growth and prevents bone loss, helping to maintain your facial structure and oral health.

No Damage to Adjacent Teeth Traditional bridges require grinding down adjacent teeth to support the bridge. Dental implants eliminate the need to alter surrounding teeth, preserving your natural tooth structure and overall oral health.

AFTER REMOVABLE DENTURE DELIVERY

After the delivery of a removable denture, there are specific post-operative instructions you should follow to ensure proper adjustment, comfort, and maintenance of your new prosthesis. Here’s a general guide:

Initial Adjustment Period: It’s normal to experience a period of adjustment as your mouth gets used to the new denture. This includes minor discomfort, increased saliva production, and temporary changes in speech and chewing.

Wearing Your Denture: Follow your dentist’s advice on how long to wear your denture each day. Initially, you may be advised to wear it most of the time, including while sleeping, to identify areas that may need adjustment. Later, you may be advised to remove it at night to allow your gums to rest.

Eating: Start with soft foods cut into small pieces. Chew slowly using both sides of your mouth at the same time to prevent the denture from moving. Gradually introduce more varied textures as you become accustomed to your denture.

Speaking: Practice speaking aloud to quickly adjust to your denture. If you notice clicking sounds while talking, try speaking more slowly. Reading aloud can also speed up the adjustment to speech patterns.

Cleaning Your Denture: Clean your denture daily using a denture brush and a cleaning solution recommended by your dentist. Avoid using regular toothpaste as it can be abrasive to the denture. Rinse your denture after every meal.

Oral Hygiene: Maintain good oral hygiene by brushing your gums, tongue, and palate every morning with a soft-bristled brush before inserting your dentures. This stimulates circulation in your tissues and helps remove plaque.

Denture Soaking: When not wearing your denture, soak it in a cleaning solution or water to prevent it from drying out and losing its shape. Follow your dentist’s recommendations for soaking solutions.

Avoid Certain Products: Avoid using bleach or boiling water to clean your denture, as these can damage the material and alter its fit.

Regular Dental Checkups: Visit your dentist regularly for checkups to ensure the health of your oral tissues and the proper fit of your denture. Your denture may need to be adjusted or relined over time to ensure a comfortable fit.

Handling with Care: When handling your denture, do so over a folded towel or a sink filled with water to prevent damage if dropped.

Report Problems: If you experience persistent discomfort, irritation, or if your denture becomes loose, contact your dentist for an adjustment. Do not attempt to adjust or repair your denture yourself. Remember, adapting to your new denture takes time, and patience is key to a successful transition. Always follow the specific instructions and recommendations provided by your dentist.
