Because children are still growing, airway issues may not always be obvious. A narrow jaw, low tongue posture, or subtle facial imbalance can signal airway stress long before sleep problems or orthodontic issues become severe. Early assessment allows us to intervene conservatively and strategically.

  • Tongue posture and mobility

  • Breathing patterns (nasal vs. mouth breathing)

Advanced Technology and Hands-On Evaluation

Our airway assessments combine advanced imaging with careful clinical observation . When appropriate, we use 3D cone-beam imaging to evaluate jaw structure and airway anatomy. This technology allows us to visualize airway space, jaw relationships, and areas of potential restriction that cannot be seen on traditional 2D X-rays.

Equally important is the hands-on component of the exam. We take intraoral and extraoral photographs, evaluate facial symmetry, and measure functional movements such as how far the mouth opens. We observe how the tongue rests, how the lips seal, and how the muscles of the face and mouth function during swallowing and speech.

We intentionally schedule extended appointments for new patients—up to two hours if needed —because a true airway assessment cannot be rushed. Our focus is quality and thoroughness, not volume.

Why Seeing a Pediatric Airway Dentist Matters

While some general dentists may offer airway screenings, pediatric airway assessment requires specialized training and experience . Children are not small adults —their airways, jaws, and facial structures are actively developing, and interpreting these patterns requires pediatric-specific expertise.

As a pediatric dentist with two additional years of hospital-based specialty training at Children&#x27;s Mercy Hospital, one of the nation&#x27;s leading children&#x27;s hospitals, we are trained to evaluate growth, development, and function in infants, children, and adolescents. This background allows us to recognize early signs of airway compromise that may be missed in a general dental setting.

General dentists who practice holistic or airway-adjacent dentistry may focus on adult anatomy or tooth alignment. An airway-focused pediatric dentist understands when growth can be guided, when habits should be addressed, and when collaboration or referral is appropriate . This distinction is critical for achieving better outcomes in children.
