Implant treatment is a proven way to restore a complete dental arch, along with normal chewing and speech. Research consistently reports success in the range of ninety-six to ninety-eight percent.

An implant is placed when one or more teeth are missing entirely. It offers something the alternatives cannot. A dental implant restores the full function of a natural tooth, and it does so without any cost to the health of the teeth beside it.

If you are missing most or all of the teeth in one jaw, full-arch treatment on four or six implants is a separate approach, and we cover it on its own page.

Types of Implant Treatment

Two-stage treatment

Single-stage treatment

A temporary crown goes onto the implant the day it is placed, and is replaced with the permanent one after two to four months. This is the option that works best in the visible zone, at the front of the mouth.

Immediate placement

In a single visit the damaged tooth is removed, the socket is prepared, and the implant is placed directly into it. A healing abutment goes on in the back of the mouth, or a temporary crown at the front. The permanent crown follows after three to four months.

About the Implants Themselves

Modern implants are made from titanium, a material with excellent biocompatibility and strength that rarely provokes an allergic response. Manufacturers differ in how the surface is treated, in thread design, in shape and in dimensions, and each publishes its own expected service life. Part of planning is choosing an implant suited to the bone you actually have.

Patients sometimes ask about zirconia, usually because they would rather avoid metal. Zirconia is a ceramic and has a shorter track record in implant dentistry than titanium, so there is less long-term data behind it. It is a fair question to raise at your consultation, and you will get a straight answer about what each material can do in your case.

What an Implant Costs

Implant treatment is divided into stages with meaningful gaps between them, which is one reason it turns out more manageable than people expect.

The surgical stage covers the titanium root and the healing abutment. The restorative stage comes later and covers the abutment and the crown, and is handled by the prosthodontist as a separate piece of work.

An implant has three parts: the titanium root, the abutment, and the crown.

If more than three teeth are missing, the surgeon and the prosthodontist plan the case together from the start, since the sequence matters as much as the individual steps. The goal of implant treatment is a fully restored mouth, not a set of separate procedures.

After your consultation and scan you receive a written treatment plan with an itemized estimate, stage by stage. Many dental plans contribute toward implant treatment and coverage varies, so we will review your benefits with you beforehand. Implant treatment is generally an eligible HSA and FSA expense, and financing is available.

What Needs to Be in Place First

The mouth is prepared before surgery. Any decay is treated, teeth beyond saving are removed, and you have a professional cleaning, all of which keeps the risk of infection to a minimum.

Placing an implant also calls for healthy gums and enough jawbone to support it. Because implant treatment usually involves one or more preparatory steps, you will have a CT scan first, along with any lab work the surgeon asks for.

The scan shows the width and height of the bone where the implant is going. That tells us which implant fits, and whether any of the following is needed beforehand.

Bone grafting, to rebuild volume that has been lost.

A sinus lift, which raises the floor of the maxillary sinus and fills the space with graft material that the body gradually converts into its own bone.

A ridge split, which widens a jaw that has become too narrow.

Membranes, either titanium or collagen, used when more than a couple of millimetres of bone volume is being rebuilt.

The Stages, Visit by Visit

Surgical stage

Three months later we take an X-ray to confirm everything has integrated, and the healing abutment goes on. It stays for two to three weeks, and then the restorative stage begins.

  • First visit, reviewing the CT scan, selecting the implant, planning the surgery and the restoration together.
  • Second visit, placing the implant.
  • Third visit, a check two or three days afterward.
  • Fourth visit, suture removal, two weeks after surgery.

Restorative stage

The last step is placing the abutment and the crown, in either porcelain-fused-to-metal or all-ceramic, and this is done by the prosthodontist.

  • First visit, impressions.
  • Second visit, trying in the restoration.
  • Third visit, fitting the permanent restoration.

After Your Implant Is Placed

Eat once the anesthetic has fully worn off.

Chew on the opposite side, and set aside hard, spicy and very hot food for now.

Skip saunas, hot baths and strenuous exercise until the sutures come out, about two weeks.

Keep the mouth clean. Brush thoroughly, use the rinse we recommend, and treat the sutured area with the antiseptic gel we give you.

Smoking is not an outright barrier to treatment, but it roughly doubles the risk of an implant failing to integrate. Cutting back around the healing period makes a real difference, and we are glad to help you plan that.

When Can an Implant Go In After an Extraction?

Immediately, in the same visit, with the implant placed straight into the socket. This is possible when there is no inflammation behind the root, the tooth is not painful or troublesome, and the surrounding gum is healthy.

After about three months, which gives any inflammation time to settle and the bone in the socket time to form and mature.

Why it is worth not putting it off

There is no need to wait a year or two. The longer a gap is left, the more the bone in that area recedes, and the more likely it becomes that rebuilding the volume will need to happen first, which makes treatment longer and more involved. Coming back within two or three months of an extraction generally avoids all of that. Every case is different, and some of these stages can be combined when conditions allow. Our dentists will go through the options with you and settle on the plan that suits your situation best.
