What a Prosthodontist Does That a General Dentist Doesn’t

Prosthodontics is one of the dental specialties recognized by the American Dental Association. It takes three years of full-time training after dental school, spent entirely on restoring and replacing teeth.

A general dentist restores individual teeth. A prosthodontist is trained to plan the whole bite how the teeth meet, what the jaw joint tolerates, how a restoration will hold up in five and ten years, and in what order the work has to happen when several problems overlap.

Worth stating plainly: Dr. Yoon is a prosthodontist, not an oral and maxillofacial surgeon. He performs the surgery that supports a restoration extractions, grafting, implant placement. Cases that belong with an oral surgeon, wisdom teeth included , are referred out and coordinated.

  • Complex implant work or full-arch reconstruction
  • Dentistry that has already failed once
  • Severe wear, erosion or a collapsed bite
  • Full-mouth rehabilitation
  • Aesthetic cases where the margin for error is small

Precision First, Because the Second Attempt Costs More

The work here is planned for durability rather than speed. Many patients arriving for reconstruction have had something done before that didn’t last.

Every case starts with records a scan, imaging, and an assessment of how the bite actually functions. The restoration is designed from that, not from an impression of a single tooth. Where implants are involved, the position is planned from a 3D scan against the crown or denture that will eventually sit on it.

You get the options, the costs and the expected outcome in writing before treatment begins. Where a case falls outside what we should be doing, we say so and point you to the person who should.
