Dental Implants: Our Process

First, you’ll see Dr. Bracho or Dr. Keller for a comprehensive oral exam. This will include reviewing your medical history to make sure you’re a good candidate for dental implants, as well as taking x-rays and a CBCT scan. The CBCT scan is a critical component as it offers a 3-dimensional look at your mouth, jaw, and nasal cavities that informs precise implant placement. With the placement of your implant determined, it will be time to schedule your surgery.

Your surgery will be performed in our office by Dr. Bracho or Dr. Keller, so you won’t have to deal with an unknown doctor at an unfamiliar location. During surgery, your dentist will place your implant precisely in its previously determined location. We use the state-of-the-art X-Guide system, which functions like a GPS to ensure that implants are placed with precision and accuracy.

After surgery, we’ll wait a few months to allow the bone to fuse to the implant. Then, we’ll take digital impressions using intraoral photogrammetry. These will give us a 3D computer model of your mouth with the new implant so that Dr. Bracho or Dr. Keller can design your permanent crown. They’ll send the crown specifications to our local dental lab for fabrication. When your crown is ready, Dr. Bracho or Dr. Keller will attach it to your implant, and your smile will be as good as new!

Dental Implant FAQs

How long is the implant procedure?

For a single implant, the entire procedure typically lasts 1-2 hours.

Is getting a dental implant painful?

The procedure is not painful, as we use local anesthesia to numb the surgical area. For your comfort, we also offer oral and IV sedation. (Visit our page on sedation dentistry to learn more.) After the procedure, it is common to experience moderate pain and discomfort. These can be treated with over-the-counter pain relievers and typically subside within a week.

Are implants better than dentures?

Implant-supported dentures, which are attached via dental implants, are a secure, comfortable alternative to traditional dentures. They also promote the health of your jaw and prevent premature aging by inhibiting bone resorption and resultant facial collapse. To learn more, visit our page on implant overdentures .
