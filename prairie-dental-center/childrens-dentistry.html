Children’s Dentistry

Gentle Children’s Dentistry for Growing Smiles

About Our Children’s Dentistry Services

At Prairie Dental Center , we believe that a lifetime of healthy smiles starts with a positive experience in childhood. We are proud to provide general dentistry for the whole family , including children of all ages. Our goal is to create a fun, safe, and comfortable environment where your child feels at ease in the dental chair.

We focus heavily on education and prevention, teaching both parents and children proper brushing and flossing techniques to prevent cavities before they start. Whether it is their very first check-up, routine sealants, or fillings, we treat your children with the same care and patience we would treat our own. As a family-focused general practice, we look forward to watching your children’s smiles grow with us through every stage of their development.

What We Offer for Our Youngest Patients

  • Routine Cleanings & Exams: Gentle cleanings to remove plaque and thorough exams to monitor the development of your child’s jaw and teeth.
  • Protective Sealants: A thin, protective coating applied to the chewing surfaces of the back teeth to prevent "sugar bugs" from causing cavities.
  • Fluoride Treatments: A simple, quick application that strengthens tooth enamel and makes it more resistant to decay.
  • Tooth-Colored Fillings: If a cavity does develop, we use gentle, metal-free fillings that blend in perfectly with the natural tooth.

Frequently Asked Questions

We recommend scheduling a first visit by your child’s first birthday or within six months of their first tooth appearing. These early visits are more about comfort and education than intensive dental work.

Our friendly team will gently clean your child’s teeth, demonstrate proper brushing habits, and perform a quick exam. We take our time to explain every tool we use so your child feels brave and informed.

You should replace their toothbrush every three months, or sooner if the bristles look frayed. It is also a good idea to get a new toothbrush after your child has been sick to avoid reintroducing bacteria.

Signs can include tooth sensitivity to cold or sweets, visible white or brown spots on the teeth, or your child avoiding chewing on one side. However, some cavities have no symptoms, which is why regular check-ups are so important.
