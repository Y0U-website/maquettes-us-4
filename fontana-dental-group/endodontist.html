What Is Endodontics?

Endodontics focuses on diagnosing and treating problems involving the inside of the tooth, including the dental pulp and root canal system. The pulp can become inflamed or infected because of deep decay, a crack, repeated dental treatment, trauma or another condition that allows bacteria to reach the inner tooth.

Root canal therapy removes inflamed or infected tissue, cleans and seals the inside of the tooth, and is intended to preserve the natural tooth whenever it can be predictably restored. Our team explains the findings, treatment options and recommended next steps so you can make an informed decision about your care.

When Should You Call About Tooth Pain?

Not every toothache requires a root canal, but persistent or worsening symptoms should be evaluated. Dental examination and X-rays help determine what is causing the problem and which treatment may be appropriate.

Pain that does not improve or repeatedly returns should be evaluated.

Prolonged sensitivity may indicate inflammation inside the tooth.

Pressure or biting discomfort can occur with infection, cracks or inflammation.

Swelling, tenderness, drainage or a recurring bump on the gum needs attention.

A tooth that becomes noticeably darker after trauma or damage may require evaluation.

Cracks can allow bacteria to reach the inside of a tooth and cause symptoms.

Why Saving a Natural Tooth Matters

When a tooth is restorable, root canal treatment may allow it to remain in place rather than being removed. Keeping a natural tooth can help maintain normal chewing, support your existing bite and reduce the need for tooth-replacement treatment.

The long-term outlook depends on the amount of healthy tooth structure remaining, periodontal support, the quality of the final restoration and other individual factors. We explain what we find and what treatment may provide the most predictable result.
