Emergency Dental Problems We Evaluate

Different dental emergencies require different treatment. A focused examination and, when needed, digital X-rays help determine the cause of the problem and the appropriate next step.

Severe Tooth Pain

Tooth pain can be caused by decay, inflammation, infection, a fracture or a damaged restoration. Persistent or intense pain should be evaluated rather than ignored.

Broken or Chipped Teeth

A cracked, chipped or broken tooth can expose vulnerable tooth structure and may worsen with chewing. Prompt evaluation can help determine whether the tooth can be repaired.

Abscess or Swelling

Swelling around a tooth or the gums may be associated with infection. Dental infections should be evaluated promptly to identify the source and appropriate treatment.

Lost Fillings or Crowns

A missing filling or loose crown can leave a tooth sensitive and vulnerable. Save a dislodged crown if possible and contact our office for an evaluation.

Knocked-Out or Loose Teeth

A knocked-out permanent tooth is time-sensitive. Call immediately for instructions and urgent dental care. Do not attempt to reinsert a knocked-out baby tooth.

Emergency Root Canal Evaluation

Severe pain or infection inside a tooth may require root canal treatment. An examination and X-rays help determine whether endodontic treatment may help preserve the tooth.

What to Do During a Dental Emergency

A few simple steps can help protect the injured area until you are able to receive professional care.

Tell us what happened, where the pain or injury is located and when the problem started.

Rinse gently with warm water and avoid chewing on a painful or damaged tooth.

Bring any broken tooth fragments or a dislodged crown with you when possible.

Handle it by the crown, not the root. If it cannot be gently replaced, keep it moist in milk and seek care immediately.

What to Expect During an Emergency Dental Appointment

We focus first on the painful or damaged area so we can understand the immediate concern and explain your treatment choices clearly.

  • Digital X-rays when clinically appropriate
