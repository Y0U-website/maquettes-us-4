Frequently Asked Questions

What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium or other biocompatible post that is placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Once the implant has healed, it can support a custom dental crown, bridge, or denture to restore the appearance and function of your smile.

K L Who is a good candidate for dental implants?

Many healthy adults with one or more missing teeth may be candidates for dental implants. Successful treatment depends on factors such as your overall health, gum health, available jawbone, bite, and ability to maintain good oral hygiene. A dental implant consultation can determine whether implants are appropriate for you.

K L Can I get dental implants if I have bone loss?

Bone loss does not necessarily prevent you from getting dental implants. If there is not enough healthy bone to securely support an implant, a bone graft or other preparatory treatment may sometimes be recommended before implant placement. Your dental team can evaluate your jawbone and discuss your available options.

Dental implants are designed as a long-term tooth replacement option and can remain functional for many years with proper care. The implant itself may last for decades, while the crown or other restoration attached to it may eventually need repair or replacement due to normal wear.

K L What is the difference between a dental implant and a dental bridge?

A dental implant replaces the missing tooth root and supports a crown independently, while a traditional dental bridge uses neighboring teeth for support. An implant can avoid placing a crown on otherwise healthy neighboring teeth, while a bridge may be completed more quickly and without implant surgery. Your dentist can help determine which option best fits your needs.

K L What happens if I don’t replace a missing tooth?

Leaving a missing tooth untreated can allow neighboring teeth to shift, affect your bite, and make chewing more difficult. Bone loss can also occur in the area of the missing tooth. Replacing the tooth with an implant, bridge, denture, or another appropriate restoration can help restore function and maintain a healthier smile.

K L Can dental implants prevent jawbone loss?

When a natural tooth is lost, the jawbone in that area no longer receives the same stimulation from the tooth root and may gradually deteriorate. Because an implant is anchored in the jawbone, it can help maintain bone in the area surrounding the implant. However, individual results vary, and existing bone loss may require treatment before implant placement.

Dental implants should be cared for with the same commitment to oral hygiene as your natural teeth. Brush twice daily, clean between your teeth and around the implant restoration, and maintain regular dental examinations and professional cleanings. Your dentist may recommend specific tools or techniques depending on your restoration.

K L Can I eat normally with dental implants?

Once your implant has fully healed and your final restoration has been placed, you should be able to eat a wide variety of foods. Your dentist will provide specific instructions during the healing period and may recommend a temporary diet while the implant and surrounding tissues recover.

K L How long does the dental implant process take?

The implant process can take several months because the implant needs time to heal and integrate with the surrounding jawbone before the final restoration is placed. The exact timeline varies depending on the number of teeth being replaced, your oral health, whether bone grafting is necessary, and how your body heals.

Dental implant placement is performed with anesthesia to keep you comfortable during the procedure. Afterward, it is normal to experience some soreness, swelling, or tenderness as the area heals. Your dental team will provide aftercare instructions to help support a comfortable recovery.

K L How long does it take for a dental implant to heal?

The implant typically needs several months to integrate with the jawbone, a process called osseointegration. The timeline varies from patient to patient and depends on factors such as bone quality, the location of the implant, overall health, and whether additional procedures were required.

K L What is the difference between dental implants and dentures?

Traditional dentures are removable appliances that replace multiple missing teeth, while dental implants are anchored into the jawbone. Implants can support individual crowns, bridges, or implant-supported dentures, providing additional stability. Depending on your situation, implant-supported dentures may be an option if you’re missing many or all of your teeth.

K L Can dental implants replace multiple missing teeth?

Yes. Dental implants can be used to replace multiple missing teeth in several ways. Depending on your needs, treatment may involve individual implants and crowns, an implant-supported bridge, or implants used to stabilize a denture. Your treatment plan will depend on the number and location of your missing teeth.

K L Can dental implants support dentures?

Yes. Dental implants can be used to stabilize removable dentures and provide a more secure foundation than traditional dentures alone. Implant-supported dentures can improve stability during speaking and chewing and may be an option for patients who have experienced difficulty with loose or uncomfortable dentures.

Smoking and tobacco use can interfere with healing and increase the risk of complications following implant treatment. If you smoke, discuss your tobacco use with your dentist before beginning treatment. Your dental team can help you understand how it may affect implant healing and long-term success.

K L How do I know if dental implants are right for me?

The best way to determine whether dental implants are appropriate is through a comprehensive dental evaluation. Your dentist will consider your missing teeth, gum health, jawbone, bite, medical history, lifestyle, and treatment goals before recommending implants or alternative tooth replacement options.
