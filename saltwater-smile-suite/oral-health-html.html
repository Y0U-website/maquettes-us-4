Myofunctional Therapy

Myofunctional therapy treats orofacial myofunctional disorders (OMDs) by strengthening the tongue, lip, and facial muscles to improve breathing, swallowing, and chewing. Through personalized exercises, habit awareness, and guided education, this therapy supports healthier oral and airway function.

It may help support proper tongue posture, nasal breathing, balanced swallowing patterns, lip seal, and improved muscle coordination. Myofunctional therapy can also be a supportive approach for concerns related to mouth breathing, tongue thrust, clenching, orthodontic relapse, snoring, sleep quality, and overall oral wellness.

Chronic mouth breathing, nasal obstruction, and sleep-disordered breathing including snoring and mild to moderate sleep apnea.

Low resting tongue posture, tongue thrust (improper swallowing), thumb sucking, and tongue-tie.

Temporomandibular joint (TMJ/TMD) dysfunction, face and neck pain, and teeth grinding or clenching.

Orthodontic relapse, crowding, high or arched palate, and poor facial growth patterns.

Difficulty with speech clarity and muscular coordination of the mouth and face.

This therapy is often recommended for children with improper oral habits and adults seeking relief from jaw tension, snoring, or sleep concerns. Each therapy plan is tailored to the individual, with a focus on building long-term healthy habits that support both the smile and the whole body.
