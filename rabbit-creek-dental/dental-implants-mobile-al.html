Am I a candidate?

Most adults in good general health are candidates for dental implants. The main requirements are adequate bone volume to support the post and healthy gum tissue. Patients with bone loss from long-term tooth absence may need grafting first, and our overview of healing after a bone graft explains what that recovery looks like. Uncontrolled diabetes, active gum disease, and smoking increase risk but don’t automatically disqualify you — these are conversations we have at your consultation after reviewing your 3D imaging.

How long does treatment take?

Most cases run 3–6 months from placement to final crown. The longest phase is osseointegration — the 3–6 month healing period while the implant fuses with the bone. Appointments in between are short. If bone grafting is needed first, add 3–4 months before placement.

Brush and floss around your implant exactly as you would a natural tooth. Implants don’t decay, but the gum tissue around them can still get infected if plaque builds up. Regular cleanings every 6 months are essential for catching early signs of inflammation before they become a problem.

Implant failure is uncommon — success rates are above 95 % at 10 years. When failure does occur it’s usually due to infection, smoking, uncontrolled diabetes, or insufficient bone integration. Early-stage failure typically shows as implant mobility or pain, which is why we schedule follow-up visits after placement. Most failed implants can be replaced after the site heals.
