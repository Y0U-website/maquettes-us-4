The implant process

Most implant treatment happens over several visits. A typical path looks like this:

If you are comparing implants with a dental bridge , the video at the top of this page walks through the difference. The better option depends on the teeth next to the space and the bone that remains.

  • Consult and records. We review your goals, take images, and look at your bite and bone.
  • Plan. You hear which teeth would be replaced, what other care might come first, and what to expect between visits.
  • Placement. The implant post is placed in the jaw. You leave with clear home-care instructions.
  • Healing. The bone needs time to bond to the implant (osseointegration). This step is why treatment is not finished in a single visit for most people.
  • Abutment and crown. Once the implant is stable, we attach the connector and a crown matched to your smile.

Recovery basics

After placement, some swelling, tenderness, or a change in what you eat for a few days is common. Soft foods and the medicines or rinses your dentist recommends help while the area settles. Avoid chewing hard foods on that side until you are told it is okay.

Healing time varies from person to person. Call the office if pain increases, swelling worsens, or something feels loose. Regular checkups after the crown is placed help us watch the implant, the gum, and your bite.

Dental implant FAQs

How much do dental implants cost in Norwalk?

Implant cost varies with the number of teeth, whether other treatment is needed first, and what your insurance may contribute. We do not publish a one-size price. Ask for an estimate at your consult so it reflects your mouth and your plan.

Does dental insurance cover implants?

Some PPO plans help with part of implant treatment; others do not. Coverage depends on your policy. Bring your insurance card to your visit and we will review benefits with you. Dental Care of Norwalk accepts many PPO plans.

How long does the dental implant process take?

Most people need several visits over a period of months. After the implant is placed, the bone typically needs time to bond to it before the final crown is attached. Your dentist will outline a timeline after examining you.

Do dental implants hurt?

The placement visit is done with local anesthetic so the area is numb. Some soreness or swelling afterward is common. Your dentist will review comfort options and home-care instructions at your appointment.

How long do dental implants last?

Implants are meant to be a long-term way to replace a tooth when you brush, floss, and keep regular dental visits. How long yours lasts depends on your health, bite, and home care. That is something to discuss at your consult — not a guarantee.

What if I am missing bone where a tooth used to be?

Some people need additional treatment before an implant can be placed; others do not. Only an exam and images can tell. Your dentist will explain the options that apply to you rather than a generic plan.
