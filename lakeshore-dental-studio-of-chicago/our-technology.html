Your Entire Mouth In A Single Scan

Dental cone beam computed tomography (CT) is a special type of x-ray equipment used when regular dental or facial x-rays are not sufficient. Dr. Kella may use this technology to produce three dimensional (3-D) images of your teeth, soft tissues, nerve pathways and bone in a single scan. We use this technology to:‍

  • Plan surgical extractions for impacted teeth
  • Diagnose temporomandibular joint disorder (TMJ)
  • Ensure that implant placement is completely accurate
  • Evaluate your jaw, sinuses, nerve canals, and nasal cavity
  • Determine bone structure and tooth orientation

Improving Every Aspect Of Care

Intraoral cameras can save time, improve accuracy, promote communication, and enhance positive patient experiences. With intraoral cameras, we are able to capture detailed images of a patient’s mouth. This technology has many benefits, including:

  • Enhanced communication with patients: You’ll see an accurate picture of your oral health on the monitor during your exam. Dr. Kella can go over everything you see to keep you informed and educated.
  • Powerful magnifying capabilities: This means that Dr. Kella can examine your mouth in extreme detail to make an accurate, thorough diagnosis.
  • Visual proof for coverage: If you want coverage for a treatment, your insurance provider often needs visual proof of your condition. Just as the high-resolution images show you exactly what’s happening in your mouth, they do the same for your provider, bolstering your treatment case.

Detailed Imaging

Digital radiography (known as digital x-ray) uses an electronic sensor instead of traditional x-ray film to capture images of your teeth, jaw structure, and gums. The digital images are then easily stored directly to a computer in our office. The benefits of digital radiography include:

  • Minimal radiation
  • Images available immediately
  • Increased visibility of hard-to-see oral issues

Intraoral Scanner

Our state of the art intraoral scanner is used to capture a direct optical impression. Patients will no longer need to have impression material used on them. With this technology, patients will have their oral impressions taken virtually . The scanner projects a light source onto the area. The images are captured by imaging sensors and are processed by scanning software, which then produces a 3D surface model. Patients will be able to view what their new smile will look like immediately with a smile simulation!
