Recovery and What to Expect

Most patients are back to normal life within 48 to 72 hours of implant placement.

For the full week-by-week timeline, see our dental implant recovery guide .

  • Day 1–3: Mild soreness, manageable with ibuprofen. Soft-food diet.
  • Day 4–7: Most patients return to a normal diet, avoiding the implant side. Back to work usually within 1 to 2 days of the procedure.
  • Weeks 2–12: Bone fuses to implant (osseointegration). Life is normal. Short check-ins every few weeks.
  • 3–6 months: Final crown is placed in a 60-minute appointment. Done.

Frequently Asked Questions

Real questions we hear in single-tooth implant consultations.

How long does the whole process take from start to finish? Plan on 3 to 6 months from your first consultation to the final crown. The active appointment time is short: a 60-90 minute placement surgery, a few brief check-ins during healing, and a 60-minute crown attachment at the end. The rest of the time is bone integration happening invisibly while you live your normal life.

Is the surgery painful? Less than most patients expect. We use local anesthetic, with optional sedation if you prefer. Post-op discomfort is typically mild and managed well with over-the-counter ibuprofen for 24 to 48 hours. Most patients describe the experience as easier than a tooth extraction.

Will my insurance cover a single-tooth implant? It depends on your plan. Many dental plans now cover a portion of implant treatment, sometimes the crown but not the implant itself, sometimes both. We review your specific benefits at your consultation and tell you exactly what is covered. FSA, HSA, and Cherry patient financing fill in the rest.

What if I have been missing the tooth for a long time? Often still possible. Bone loss progresses over time, but most patients still have enough bone for an implant even after several years. If your scan shows the bone has receded too much, a small bone graft can rebuild what is needed. See our bone grafting guide for the full picture.

Can I get a single implant if I have other crowns or bridges nearby? Yes. The implant is placed independently of any existing dental work. It does not affect crowns, bridges, or fillings on neighboring teeth. This is the key advantage of an implant over a traditional bridge.

How long will a single-tooth implant last? The titanium post is designed to last a lifetime, and most do. The crown on top typically lasts 15 to 20 years before needing replacement, similar to any high-quality dental crown. For the longer answer, see our implant longevity guide .

What happens if I do nothing about a missing tooth? The jawbone in that spot starts to recede (about 25 % of width lost in the first year). The teeth on either side gradually shift into the gap. The opposing tooth above or below starts to over-erupt because nothing is pushing back. Your bite changes. Doing nothing is the most expensive option long-term, not the cheapest.

Is a single implant better than a bridge? For most patients with healthy neighboring teeth, yes. A bridge requires us to grind down the two healthy teeth on either side of the gap. An implant leaves them completely untouched. The implant also lasts much longer and preserves jawbone. The bridge can be the right answer if the neighboring teeth already need crowns anyway.
