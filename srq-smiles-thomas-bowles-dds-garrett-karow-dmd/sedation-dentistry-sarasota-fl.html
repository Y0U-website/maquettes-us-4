What Is Sedation Dentistry?

Sedation dentistry uses safe, carefully controlled medication to help you feel relaxed and comfortable during your dental visit.

There are different levels of sedation, ranging from mild relaxation to moderate conscious sedation where you enter a deeper state of relaxation.

During your initial visit with Dr. Bowles and Dr. Karow, you’ll work together to decide which option is best for your dental needs and level of anxiety.

Types of Sedation We Offer

We offer different types of sedation, and during your initial visit, you’ll work with Dr. Bowles and Dr. Karow to choose the option that best fits your needs and comfort level.

Nitrous Oxide (Laughing Gas)

A mild form of sedation delivered through a small mask. You remain awake and aware, and the effects wear off quickly so you can return to your day as normal.

Oral Sedation

Taken as a pill, oral sedation helps you reach a deeper level of relaxation. You may feel drowsy during your visit, and you’ll need someone to drive you to and from your appointment.

IV Sedation

Administered through a small IV, this is the deepest level of sedation we offer. It allows you to remain deeply relaxed throughout your visit, while being safely monitored by our team.

Sedation FAQs Will I Be Asleep During Sedation Dentistry? Most forms of sedation help you feel deeply relaxed, but you’ll remain awake and able to respond. With deeper sedation, you may feel very drowsy and remember little of the visit afterward.

How Long Does Sedation Last?

The effects depend on the type of sedation used. Nitrous oxide wears off quickly, while oral or IV sedation may leave you feeling drowsy for a few hours after your visit.

Can I Drive Myself Home?

If you receive nitrous oxide, you can usually drive yourself home. For oral or IV sedation, you’ll need someone to drive you to and from your appointment.

Are There Any Side Effects?

Most patients tolerate sedation well, with minimal side effects. You may feel drowsy or slightly groggy afterward, depending on the type of sedation used.

How Is Sedation Different From General Anesthesia And Local Anesthetic?

Sedation helps you relax during your visit, while local anesthetic numbs the area being treated. General anesthesia is a deeper level that puts you fully asleep and is typically used in hospital settings.
