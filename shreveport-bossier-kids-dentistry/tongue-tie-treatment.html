Tongue-Tie Treatment

  • Tongue-tie can make breastfeeding difficult, sometimes causing latch problems for infants and pain for mothers.
  • A tongue-tie release, called a frenectomy, can be performed with a laser or a traditional technique.
  • Left untreated, tongue-tie can sometimes affect speech clarity, particularly with sounds like "t," "d," "l," "r," and "s."
  • Stretching exercises after the procedure are often recommended to help prevent the tissue from reattaching during healing.

What Is Tongue-Tie (Ankyloglossia)?

Tongue-tie is present from birth and varies quite a bit in severity. Some cases are mild and cause no issues at all, while others noticeably limit tongue mobility in ways that affect feeding, speech, or oral hygiene.

What About Lip-Tie?

A related condition called lip-tie involves the band of tissue connecting the upper lip to the gums. Lip-tie can sometimes contribute to feeding difficulties as well, and our dentist will check for both conditions during an evaluation.

Signs Your Child May Have a Tongue-Tie

In Infants

Signs in babies often show up around feeding, including trouble latching, clicking sounds while nursing, slow weight gain, or nipple pain for breastfeeding mothers.

Not every feeding difficulty is caused by tongue-tie, so a proper evaluation is the best way to know for sure what’s going on.

In Older Kids

As kids grow, tongue-tie can sometimes show up as difficulty pronouncing certain sounds, trouble licking their lips or an ice cream cone, or difficulty sweeping food debris away from teeth, which can affect oral hygiene over time.

Some kids also report irritation or discomfort when their tongue is stretched to its limit, such as when eating certain foods or trying to stick out their tongue fully.

How Tongue-Tie Is Treated: The Frenectomy Procedure

Laser Frenectomy

A laser frenectomy uses a dental laser to gently release the tight tissue. This method typically involves minimal bleeding, often doesn’t require stitches, and tends to allow for faster healing.

Traditional Frenectomy

A traditional frenectomy uses scissors or a scalpel to release the tissue. Our dentist will discuss which approach makes the most sense for your child’s specific situation.

Why Early Evaluation Matters

Catching a tongue-tie early, especially for infants struggling with feeding, can make a meaningful difference in those first weeks and months. Feeding difficulties can affect both an infant’s growth and a breastfeeding parent’s comfort and confidence.

For older kids, addressing tongue-tie before speech patterns become deeply ingrained can sometimes make speech therapy, if needed, more effective. That said, it’s never too late to have tongue-tie evaluated and treated at any age.

What Happens During a Tongue-Tie Release Appointment

A frenectomy is a brief, straightforward procedure. Here’s the exact mission we follow to release the tether and get your child moving freely.

Evaluate & Confirm Our dentist examines the frenulum and confirms whether a release is the right next step.

Comfort & Numb We apply numbing as needed to keep your child comfortable throughout the procedure.

Quick Release The tight tissue is carefully released using a laser or traditional technique, often in just a few minutes.

Aftercare Guidance We walk you through healing expectations and any stretching exercises to do at home.

Aftercare and Stretching Exercises

After a frenectomy, the tissue can sometimes start to reattach or scar as it heals. To help prevent this, our team will show you specific stretches to do with your child at home and explain how often to do them.

Healing is generally quick, and most kids return to normal eating and activity within a day or so. We’ll always let you know what to expect and how to reach us if anything seems off during recovery.

Consistency with any recommended stretches makes a real difference in how well the tissue heals, so our team will make sure you feel confident doing them correctly before you leave.

Is Tongue-Tie Release Right for My Child?

Tongue-tie can be treated at almost any age, from newborns experiencing feeding difficulty to older kids working through speech or hygiene challenges. Every child’s situation is evaluated individually.

Our dentist will assess the severity of the tongue-tie, how it’s affecting your child, and whether a release is likely to help before recommending treatment. If this is your child’s first visit with us, our team will make sure the evaluation feels calm and unhurried.
