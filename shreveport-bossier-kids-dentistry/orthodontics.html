Orthodontics

  • About 75 % of 12-year-olds need orthodontic treatment, yet 90 % of facial development has already occurred by that age.
  • Early functional appliance treatment can address up to 80 % of a bite issue before all adult teeth arrive.
  • Roughly 55 % of kids needing treatment for a bad bite have an underdeveloped or overdeveloped jaw.

Why Early Orthodontic Evaluation Matters

This approach isn’t about rushing into treatment. It’s about having the information early enough to make the best decision for your child, whether that means starting Step One now or simply monitoring growth at regular visits.

Signs Your Child May Benefit From Early Orthodontic Treatment

A few specific signs often point toward benefiting from an early orthodontic evaluation, even before any teeth look obviously crooked.

Habits Like Thumb Sucking or Tongue Thrusting

Prolonged habits like thumb sucking or tongue thrusting can affect how the jaws and teeth develop over time.

Breathing and Airway Concerns

Mouth breathing, snoring, or a constricted airway from swollen adenoids or tonsils can all influence facial and jaw growth.

Bite and Jaw Development

A visibly bad bite, or jaws that appear narrow or underdeveloped, are common reasons to consider early treatment. Space maintenance needs, such as when a tooth is missing or was removed early, can also call for early orthodontic planning, and pairs closely with the space maintainers discussed on our Extractions page .

None of these signs mean something is definitely wrong. They simply mean it’s worth having a professional take a look while your child’s jaws and teeth are still developing.

Step One: Functional Appliances and Limited Braces (Ages 5-12)

In this first step, our focus is on the position and symmetry of your child’s jaws, future growth patterns, tooth spacing, breathing, and any oral habits that could lead to abnormal development over time.

Treatment started at this stage is often very effective, and in some cases can reduce or even eliminate the need for more extensive orthodontic treatment later.

Functional appliances used during this step work with your child’s own growth, gently guiding jaw development rather than forcing teeth into position the way traditional braces do.

Step Two: Braces (Ages 12-14)

In the second step, we focus on how your child’s teeth and jaws fit and function together. This is when teeth are straightened and the bite is properly aligned, with attention given to the jaw joint, facial profile, and gum tissue health.

Completing Step One first usually means less time is needed in full braces during this second phase.

By this stage, most of your child’s adult teeth have arrived, making it the right time to fine-tune alignment and bite for the long term.

What If My Child Doesn’t Need Early Treatment?

Not every child who has an early evaluation ends up needing Step One treatment, and that’s a perfectly good outcome. Sometimes an evaluation simply confirms that growth and development are on track.

Either way, having a professional check early means any issues that do exist get caught while they’re easiest to address, and you’ll have a clear picture of what to expect as your child continues to grow.

What Happens During an Orthodontic Evaluation

An orthodontic evaluation is a straightforward, non-invasive appointment. Here’s the exact mission we follow to map out your child’s growth and development.

Scan & Assess Our dentist examines jaw position, tooth spacing, and bite, often with photos or X-rays as needed.

Growth Mapping We evaluate how your child’s face and jaws are developing and identify any early warning signs.

Appliance Planning If early treatment is recommended, we explain which functional appliance or approach fits your child best.

Ongoing Monitoring We schedule regular check-ins to track growth and adjust the plan as your child develops.

Why Timing Matters: Cooperation and Treatment Length

Younger kids, especially between ages 8 and 11, tend to be more cooperative with early orthodontic appliances than kids in the 12 to 14 age range. That cooperation makes early treatment more effective and often more pleasant for the whole family.

Starting early doesn’t just address problems sooner. It frequently means your child spends less overall time in braces once Step Two begins, since much of the foundational work has already been done.

It’s also simply easier on families. Shorter phases, better cooperation, and a clear plan mapped out well in advance all make the process feel more manageable from start to finish.
