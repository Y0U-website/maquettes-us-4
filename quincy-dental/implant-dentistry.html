Implant Dentistry

Studies show that 178 million people across the United States are missing at least one tooth, and 35 million are completely edentulous (missing all their teeth).

Dental implants represent the most advanced system for the replacement of missing teeth. They are small, biocompatible surgical posts that behave in much the same way as the roots of natural teeth.

Types include single-tooth implants, implant-supported fixed bridges, full arch replacement with four or six implants, and implant-retained dentures.

Benefits include replicating the look, feel, and function of natural teeth; lasting for many years with proper care; providing continued stimulation to the underlying bone to prevent bone loss; no slipping or dislodging; and no decay or cavities.

According to clinical studies, dental implants have a demonstrated long-term success rate of well over 95 %. However, long-term stability depends upon the presence of sufficient bone at the time of placement.

Surgery to place dental implants is typically considered a minor surgical procedure and performed on an outpatient basis. It takes a few months for implants to fully integrate with the surrounding bone.
