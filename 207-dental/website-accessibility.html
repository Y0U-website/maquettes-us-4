The following tips can help improve your browsing experience and give you more access to the assistance you are looking for. A . Visual Assistance If you have difficulty viewing web pages, these options may help: Use your computer&#x27;s built-in text-to-speech functionality
  • Navigate the page using your keyboard instead of a mouse
  • Increase text size using your browser&#x27;s zoom or accessibility settings
  • Use screen magnification software
  • Adjust color schemes — change background and text colors to improve contrast
  • Enhance mouse pointer visibility (available in Windows accessibility settings)

B . Audio Assistance — Closed Captioning

Closed captioning displays spoken words as on-screen text, allowing you to follow along with any video content without relying on audio. This is beneficial for individuals who are deaf or hard of hearing, or anyone watching in a noisy environment.

Most modern browsers and devices support closed captioning for compatible video content.

C . Audio Assistance — Volume Controls

You can adjust volume at both the device level (via your operating system&#x27;s sound settings) and within individual media players. Most video and audio players include an on-screen volume slider for convenience.

D . Keyboard and Mouse Alternatives

Speech recognition software — such as Dragon NaturallySpeaking — allows you to navigate websites and dictate text using only your voice. This is especially helpful for users who are unable to use a traditional keyboard or mouse.

Most operating systems also include built-in voice control features in their accessibility settings.
