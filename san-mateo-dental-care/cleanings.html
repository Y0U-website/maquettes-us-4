Initial Consultation

We begin with a thorough examination of your oral health, including checking for signs of gum disease or other concerns. Our team reviews your medical history and current oral hygiene routine to understand your needs. We discuss any specific concerns or goals you have for your oral health.

Customized Treatment Plan

Based on your oral health assessment, we create a personalized cleaning and maintenance plan. We determine the appropriate type and frequency of cleanings needed to maintain your oral health. Our team explains all recommended treatments and provides cost information upfront.

Implementation of Treatment

Our hygienist performs a thorough cleaning using specialized instruments to remove plaque and tartar. We clean both above and below the gum line to ensure comprehensive care. Special attention is given to areas where bacteria commonly accumulate.

Aftercare & Maintenance Tips

We provide detailed instructions for maintaining optimal oral hygiene between visits. Our team demonstrates proper brushing and flossing techniques specific to your needs. We recommend appropriate oral care products and tools for your home care routine.

Follow-Up

Regular follow-up appointments help maintain your oral health and monitor any areas of concern. We adjust cleaning frequency based on your individual needs and response to treatment. Our team provides ongoing support for your oral hygiene efforts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is oral hygiene important?

Oral hygiene plays a crucial role in maintaining both dental and overall health by preventing tooth decay, gum disease, and other oral health issues. Good oral hygiene habits help remove harmful bacteria that can lead to cavities and periodontal disease, while also preventing bad breath and maintaining a bright smile. Regular brushing, flossing, and professional cleanings work together to protect your oral health and contribute to your general well-being.

What can poor oral hygiene result in?

Poor oral hygiene can lead to a cascade of dental and health problems, including cavities, gum disease, tooth loss, and chronic bad breath. Research has shown connections between poor oral hygiene and serious systemic conditions like heart disease, diabetes, and respiratory infections. Beyond the physical impact, untreated oral health issues can affect your self-confidence, social interactions, and quality of life.

How to improve oral hygiene?

Improving oral hygiene involves developing consistent daily habits and maintaining regular professional care. Brush your teeth twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, floss at least once daily, and use an antimicrobial mouthwash as recommended by your dentist. Schedule professional cleanings every six months, or more frequently if advised, and use appropriate tools like soft-bristled toothbrushes and interdental cleaners.

How long does a dental cleaning take?

A routine dental cleaning typically takes between 45 to 60 minutes, depending on the amount of buildup present and your overall oral health. More extensive cleanings for patients with gum disease may require additional time to ensure thorough treatment. The appointment includes not only the cleaning but also an examination, oral cancer screening, and personalized oral hygiene instruction.

How long after a dental cleaning can I eat?

You can eat immediately after a routine dental cleaning, though it’s recommended to wait 30 minutes if fluoride treatment was applied. For the first few hours after your cleaning, avoid extremely hot or cold foods if you experience sensitivity. It’s best to stick with softer foods for the first meal after your cleaning, especially if your gums feel tender.
