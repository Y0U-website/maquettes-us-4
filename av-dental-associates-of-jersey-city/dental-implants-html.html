What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are durable, natural-looking prostheses that replace lost teeth. An implant is a small titanium post that is compatible with the oral tissues and bones that integrates into the jaw, and it acts as the missing root while supporting an artificial crown.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Since dental implants need to be surgically placed, the process of getting them can take several months. That might seem like quite a while, but you’ll be able to see that it was time well-spent once you experience the benefits of your new smile! There are four main phases of getting dental implants. To help you familiarize yourself with the process, here is a sum-up of what you can expect to happen during each step.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

Are dental implants right for you? Can you have them placed right away, or will you need a preparatory procedure like a bone graft first? The only way to have these questions answered is to schedule a dental implant consultation. Our team will determine the number of teeth that must be replaced, check the bone density levels in your jaw, and identify any oral health issues. Once the necessary information has been gathered, we can start planning the rest of the dental implant process.

Dental Implant Surgery

You won’t have to drive to an unfamiliar practice to receive dental implants from a specialist you barely know; our team at AV Dental Associates of Jersey City can perform the procedure right here. The surgery involves opening your gums and inserting the implant posts into your jawbone. The procedure will be very carefully planned; we’ll know ahead of time exactly where your dental implants need to be placed and at what angle.

Once we’re sure that your implants are in the proper positions, we will suture your gums shut. A small cap will be attached to each post to keep them safe.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Dental implants can fuse with your jawbone, but the process that allows them to do so also known as osseointegration can take as long as six months. You’ll receive instructions for taking care of your smile until the healing process is complete.

Successful osseointegration is followed up by abutment placement. With dental implants, the term abutment refers to a connector piece that allows us to anchor a prosthesis to the post. Abutments are placed via surgery, so your mouth will need a few weeks to recover afterward.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

An impression of your mouth is captured once the implant posts have received their abutments. It normally takes a few weeks for a dental lab to create your prosthesis, which will be a crown, a bridge, or a denture. Great care will be taken to design your replacement teeth in a way that makes them practically indistinguishable from your real teeth.

When we get ready to attach the prosthesis to your dental implants, we’ll double-check to see if any adjustments need to be made. Then it’s a simple matter of anchoring it to the posts and putting the finishing touches on your dazzling new smile!

Dental Implant Failure & Salvage

Dental implants are successful in the vast majority of cases. However, due to infection or other sad circumstances, they can fail in rare instances. If you ever believe something is wrong with one of your implants, do not hesitate to contact our team. We will examine your mouth, identify the cause of the problem, and do all we can to get the health of your restored smile back on track in a conservative and efficient manner.

Benefits of Dental Implants

If you don’t already know, dental implants are pretty popular as tooth replacements. It’s to the point that dentists nationwide place 500,000 of them yearly. As for why they’re so well-liked, much of the reason lies in their amazing upsides. These prosthetics have special features due to their fusing directly with your jawbone. In fact, we at AV Dental Associates can tell you about them. So, just keep reading to learn the benefits of dental implants in Jersey City.

Day-to-Day Benefits

Perhaps obviously, the most noticeable benefits of implants are the day-to-day ones. Such perks include the following:

  • A Beautiful, Full Smile Because implants are capped with dental crowns, they look gorgeous and blend seamlessly with surrounding teeth. In other words, they both restore your full smile and help it look prettier.
  • Easy Eating Since implants fuse with your jawbone, they restore much of your bite force. Consequently, they give your jaw the strength to chew all your favorite tough foods.
  • High Confidence By removing your unseemly smile gaps, implants boost your confidence and help you feel less insecure. That change in attitude can then strengthen your relationships.
  • Low-Maintenance Results Overall, you can maintain implants as you would natural teeth. Namely, simply brush them twice daily, floss between them once daily, and rinse them often with mouthwash. So long as you do so, they will last a while.

Health Benefits

Of course, implants’ advantages cover more than simply day-to-day living. They also take the form of solid health benefits. In particular, these are:

  • Improved Oral Health Truthfully, gaps between teeth are breeding grounds for harmful germs. As implants fill these gaps, they thus reduce your odds of oral health problems.
  • A Strong Jawbone If you don’t treat tooth loss quickly, your jaw will erode and weaken. However, implants can reverse this process by stimulating your jawbone.
  • No Sensitivity Compared to dentures or bridges, implants don’t rub against other teeth or gum tissue. Consequently, they don’t irritate your mouth or cause pain.

Long-term Benefits

Perhaps most crucial to implants are their various long-term benefits. Among them, the most noteworthy ones are perks like:

  • Low Failure Risk Assuming placement is successful, implants don’t often fail early. Their success rate ten years after the initial surgery is over 95 %!
  • Long Treatment Lifespan On average, a dental implant will last roughly 15-20 years. A properly maintained one, however, can have a lifespan of 30 years or more. That means these restorations can potentially last a patient a lifetime.
  • Cost-Effective Care So long as you treat them well, implants save you money in the long run. Their long lifespan (see above) means they don’t incur high replacement or repair fees. Dentures and bridges, meanwhile, often need pricey follow-up care.

Almost anyone can be a candidate for dental implants as long as they are missing at least one tooth. After completing an examination of your mouth, our team can determine whether you’re a candidate for dental implant surgery and (if you are) start planning the treatment so that you can regain a complete smile. You can count on us to figure out how many dental implant posts you need as well as the type of restoration that they’ll have to support.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

One of the most important qualities of a viable candidate for dental implant surgery is having good overall health. Surgery is required to place dental implants in your jaw, so it’s important to confirm that your body is healthy enough for a smooth recovery process.

The current state of your mouth is also critical. Gum disease and other oral health issues can cause serious complications for your dental implants. As such, certain patients might need gum disease treatment or other preliminary procedures before they can have their dental implant surgery performed.

One last thing to keep in mind is that jawbone density can make a difference. Dental implant posts need a strong jawbone to give them support. A lack of bone can therefore be a significant obstacle to having dental implants placed, which is why bone grafting is often needed beforehand.

Missing One Tooth

In some cases, you may only have one tooth that needs to be replaced. An implant post can be inserted into the gap in your smile, and after a certain point, it will be ready to support a dental crown. Not only will the crown blend in with your other teeth, but the dental implant post itself will feel like a natural part of your smile.

Missing Multiple Teeth

It’s easy to guess that only one dental implant post is needed to replace a single tooth. But how many are required when multiple teeth are missing? In cases where three or more consecutive teeth have been lost, it may be possible to re-complete your grin with as few as two dental implant posts. Said posts can be used to support a lifelike dental bridge. Unlike the traditional type of dental bridge, an implant bridge can be placed without making permanent alterations to your remaining teeth.

Missing All Teeth

Combining dentures with dental implants allows you to replace all of your missing teeth at once without having to worry about the inconveniences that often come with a prosthesis that can potentially slip out of place. The exact number of implant posts needed to support a denture can vary, but oftentimes around 4 to 6 are used. Many implant dentures are fixed in place, which can simplify the process of caring for them since you will be able to brush them as if they were real teeth.

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

After you’ve received dental implants, your mouth will need time to recover. Naturally, you can count on our team to help you make sure that this process goes as smoothly as possible. We’ll provide you with a set of instructions regarding what you will need to do after your surgery. Below is a brief summary of some of the guidelines that you will be given; please let us know right away if you have any questions.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

You will require time to rest after receiving your dental implants, so plan on heading home after the procedure. During the initial phase of the healing process, you will need to focus on protecting the blood clots that have formed at the surgical sites. This means avoiding anything that might dislodge the clots, such as drinking through a straw, touching the implant site, and spitting.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

There are a number of side effects that you can typically expect after dental implant surgery, such as:

These symptoms should go away on their own as your mouth heals. If they persist or seem to grow worse, give our office a call immediately.

  • Discomfort that can be controlled with medication (which might be prescribed but can also be bought over the counter).
  • Swelling that can be reduced by periodically holding a cold compress over the area.
  • Intermittent bleeding that can be stopped by putting pressure on the area with a clean piece of gauze.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

Following your dental implant surgery, you’ll need to refrain from eating certain foods. Specifically, it’s best to avoid anything that’s particularly hard or crunchy. You’ll eventually be able to reintroduce more solid foods into your diet, but until then, you’ll need to stick to meals consisting of soft or liquid foods. Some examples of things that you can eat after dental implant surgery include:

  • Scrambled eggs
  • Mashed potatoes
  • Yogurt
  • Pasta
  • Soups and broths that aren’t too hot

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Following your dental implant surgery, it’s important to keep your mouth clean in order to minimize your risk for infection. On the day after your procedure, you will be able to brush your teeth like normal, but don’t forget to be careful around the areas where the dental implant posts were placed in the jaw. Additionally, you should get in the habit of rinsing with salt water at least two or three times a day. Remember to pour the water out of your mouth instead of spitting once you’re finished.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

The final crown, bridge, or denture will be attached to the implant posts after the mouth has finished healing. At this point, there may be some mild sensitivity, but you shouldn’t experience any swelling or bleeding. If unexpected symptoms do occur, don’t hesitate to call our team.
