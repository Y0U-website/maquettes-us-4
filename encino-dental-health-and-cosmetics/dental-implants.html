Dental Implants

Dental implants are an effective restoration treatment for the replacement of missing teeth. You may lose one or more teeth to disease, infection, or accidents. To prevent further damage and restore your oral function, dental implants are applied to replace the missing teeth. Dental implants consist of a titanium post anchored with a porcelain crown.

Encino Dental Health and Cosmetics has helped hundreds of patients with effective restorative dentistry treatments. Our team of certified oral surgeons carefully place dental implants that last a lifetime.

Advantages of Dental Implants

When you lose a tooth, your gums are exposed to bacteria and infection. Due to poor stimulation and blood circulation in the gums, they start receding, causing damage to surrounding teeth. Dental implants fill the gap between missing teeth and trigger jawbone stimulation for the healthy function of the gums and teeth. Implant-supported dentures maintain the shape and integrity of the mouth and prevent deterioration of gums in patients who are suffering from multiple tooth losses.

The Process of Dental Implants at Encino Dental Health and Cosmetics

Dental implants are applied in two appointments.

The dentist will put a titanium post in your jawbone during the first appointment and cover it with a temporary crown. The post is allowed to integrate with the jawbone and roots for a few weeks. Once it is effectively fused with the roots, we proceed with the final phase of dental implant application. In the second appointment, we assess how effectively the implant is responding. If all looks good, we replace the temporary crown with a durable and permanent porcelain crown.

Frequently Asked Questions

Dental implants are typically made from medical-grade titanium, a biocompatible material that integrates with natural bone. Titanium provides strength, stability, and long-term durability while minimising the risk of rejection or allergic reaction.

Single implant placement usually takes one to two hours, depending on complexity. Surgical time varies based on bone condition, number of implants, and whether additional procedures are required.

Healing typically takes three to six months. During this period, the implant bonds securely with the jawbone, creating a stable foundation for the final crown or prosthetic restoration.

Osseointegration is the biological process where the implant fuses directly with the surrounding jawbone. This integration creates a strong, stable base capable of supporting normal biting and chewing forces.

Smokers may qualify for implants, but smoking increases the risk of healing complications and implant failure. A detailed consultation helps evaluate individual risk and determine suitability.

Implant failure is uncommon but may occur due to infection or inadequate integration. In many cases, the implant can be removed, the site treated, and replacement considered after healing.
