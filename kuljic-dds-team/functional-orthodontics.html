A Different Kind of Orthodontic Planning

Straight teeth are the result of this process, not the starting point. Before any appliance is chosen, we need to understand why the teeth ended up where they are.

Evaluate the Whole System The evaluation looks at jaw development, tongue posture, breathing patterns, and how the bite comes together. Sometimes the crowding is a simple space problem. More often, it’s the visible part of something larger.

Guide Growth and Create Room Instead of extracting teeth to relieve crowding, functional orthodontics develops the arches so the teeth, tongue, and airway all have the space they need. In children, this means guiding growth while it’s still happening. In adults, appliances like MARPE make expansion possible without surgery in many cases.

Unique to our practice Retrain the Muscles That Shaped the Problem This is the step most orthodontic treatment skips. If the tongue keeps resting low and the mouth stays open, the same forces that caused the problem keep working against the result. Our in-house myofunctional therapy program retrains tongue posture, swallowing, and breathing habits, so the new space is used and kept.

Who Functional Orthodontics Helps

For Children (Ages 5–12) The best window for functional orthodontics is while the jaws are still growing. At this stage, growth can be guided instead of corrected: arches develop the room they need, breathing and tongue habits are addressed early, and many children avoid extractions or years of braces later.

If your child shows signs like mouth breathing, snoring, or early crowding, an evaluation now can change the entire course of their development. Appliances like Myobrace work with growth, not against it.

For Adults Most adults assume that once growth stops, so do their options. That’s no longer true. MARPE makes palatal expansion possible for adults, in many cases without surgery, creating room for the tongue and opening the nasal airway.

Adult patients rarely come to us for straighter teeth. They come because of jaw tension, grinding, poor sleep, or teeth that shifted back after braces years ago. Functional treatment addresses what’s behind those symptoms.

Appliances & Approaches We Use

There’s no single appliance for every patient. The choice follows the evaluation: what the jaws need, what stage of growth the patient is in, and what the airway requires.

MSE / MARPE

Miniscrew-assisted palatal expansion for teenagers and adults. MARPE widens the upper jaw without surgery in many cases, creating room for the teeth and tongue and improving nasal breathing.

Myobrace

A removable appliance system for children that guides jaw development and corrects the habits behind crowding: mouth breathing, low tongue posture, and reverse swallowing.

NiTi Palatal Expander

A nickel-titanium expander that widens a child’s arch with light, continuous pressure instead of the sharp turns of a traditional screw expander.

Bent Wire System (BWS)

A fixed, low-profile appliance that develops the arches gradually. The BWS works well for patients who need steady expansion over time, and it stays in place, so results don’t depend on remembering to wear it.

Can adults benefit from palatal expansion?

Yes. This is one of the biggest changes in orthodontics over the past decade. With MARPE, the upper jaw can be expanded in adults, in many cases without surgery. Adult patients typically seek expansion for breathing, sleep, and jaw comfort as much as for their smile.

How is functional orthodontics different from braces?

Braces move teeth into alignment within the jaw as it is. Functional orthodontics develops the jaw itself, addresses the breathing and tongue habits behind the crowding, and then aligns the teeth in the room that’s been created. For traditional braces and clear aligners, which still have their place, see our orthodontics page.

Will my child still need braces later?

Sometimes, yes. Functional treatment doesn’t always eliminate the need for braces, but when they are needed afterward, treatment is usually shorter and simpler because the jaws already have room. What functional treatment aims to prevent is the harder path: extractions, prolonged treatment, and results that relapse.

How long does treatment take?

It depends on the phase and the patient. Expansion takes months, while guiding growth and retraining habits is measured in phases over a longer period. After your evaluation, you’ll get a realistic timeline for your specific case, not a generic estimate.
