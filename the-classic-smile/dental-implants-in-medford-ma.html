Single Tooth Implants

A single tooth implant replaces one missing tooth from root to crown. A small titanium post is placed in the jaw where the root once sat. As it heals, the bone fuses to the implant surface, creating a stable base that can handle normal biting pressure.

An abutment connects the post to a custom ceramic crown shaped and shaded to match neighboring teeth. Because the implant stands independently, nearby teeth remain untouched.

Full Mouth Implants

Full mouth implants address extensive tooth loss using a carefully planned set of implants placed at key support points in the jaw. The implants anchor a full arch of fixed replacement teeth that stay in place at all times.

The number and position of implants depend on bone volume, bite alignment, and facial structure. Full mouth implants restore broad chewing surfaces, improve speech clarity, and support facial contours that can flatten after tooth loss.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Implant-supported dentures are designed for patients who want added security without a fully fixed restoration. A small number of implants are placed in the jaw and fitted with connectors that allow a custom denture to attach firmly.

The denture can still be removed for cleaning, yet it remains stable during eating and speaking. Because support comes from implants rather than gum pressure alone, patients typically experience less shifting, fewer sore spots, and improved chewing efficiency.

Benefits of Dental Implants Compared to Other Dental Restorations

Structural Support and Stability

Dental implants are anchored directly into the jawbone, where they function like artificial tooth roots. Their foundation keeps restorations firmly in place during chewing and speaking.

Removable options rely on gum support, and traditional bridges depend on neighboring teeth for stability. Implant anchorage allows biting forces to distribute more naturally across the jaw.

Preservation of Natural Teeth

Traditional bridges often involve reshaping healthy teeth to hold crowns that support the replacement tooth. Implants stand independently and do not require adjacent teeth for support.

Preserving natural enamel helps maintain long-term tooth strength and reduces additional restorative work later.

Jawbone Health

When a tooth is lost, the surrounding bone gradually shrinks due to lack of stimulation. Implants transmit chewing pressure into the bone, helping maintain bone volume and facial structure. Other restorations replace visible teeth but do not stimulate the underlying bone in the same way.

Longevity and Maintenance

Implants are designed for long-term performance with routine oral care and professional maintenance. Some traditional restorations may require replacement sooner due to wear, fit changes, or strain on supporting teeth.

Implant restorations are also cleaned similarly to natural teeth, which simplifies daily hygiene.

Function and Comfort

Removable restorations may shift slightly during use and can create pressure points along the gums. Because implants are fixed and stable, many patients notice more natural chewing efficiency and clearer speech.

FAQs About Dental Implants

Are dental implants painful?

Most patients report minimal discomfort. Local anesthesia and sedation options support a smooth procedure, and post-treatment soreness typically resolves within a few days.

How long does the dental implant process take?

Treatment timelines vary. Many cases take several months to allow proper healing and implant integration before final restorations are placed.

Who is a good candidate for dental implants in Medford, MA?

Healthy adults with sufficient jawbone and good oral health often qualify. A consultation and digital imaging confirm candidacy and treatment options.

How do I care for dental implants?

Implant care is similar to caring for natural teeth. Brush and floss daily, attend routine dental visits, and maintain healthy gums.

Are dental implants worth it long term?

Implants provide durability, stability, and natural function. Many patients value their lasting performance and lifelike appearance.
