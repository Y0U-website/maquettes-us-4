Caring for your implant site well in the first days

Managing Pain

Mild pain after implant placement is normal. Try to take pain medication before the numbness from the procedure wears off, rather than waiting until it starts to hurt.

Our doctors recommend 600mg of ibuprofen (3 over-the-counter tablets) every 6 hours for the first 3 days. Ibuprofen (Advil) and naproxen (Aleve) both help control pain and minimize swelling.

Vitamin D

Vitamin D3 has been shown to support healthy bone formation around a new implant. Take 1000 IU, especially if you know you run low on Vitamin D.

Minimizing Swelling

Swelling is your body’s normal response to surgery and part of healing. An ice pack, or an unopened bag of frozen peas or corn, applied to the outside of your face keeps swelling to a minimum.

For the first day, ice in 20-minutes-on / 20-minutes-off intervals. Some swelling and mild bruising is normal and often peaks around two to three days after surgery before it starts to improve.

Eating

Stick to soft, cool foods for the first day: yogurt, applesauce, cottage cheese, and ice cream all work well.

Avoid nuts, sunflower seeds, popcorn, and chips. These can easily get lodged around the implant site. Focus on drinking plenty of fluids and eating soft, nutritious foods, and gradually reintroduce your normal diet as you heal.

Avoid Smoking, Vaping, and Strenuous Exercise

Once the blood clot forms at the site, it’s important not to disturb or dislodge it, since the clot is what protects the area while it heals. Do not smoke or vape for one full week. These activities can dislodge or dissolve the clot and slow healing.

Limit vigorous exercise for the first 24–48 hours. Raising your heart rate and blood pressure too soon can lead to additional swelling at the surgical site.

Keeping the Area Clean

The best way to prevent infection and support healing is to keep your mouth clean. Beginning the day after surgery, rinse gently with warm salt water after each meal.

Use a soft-bristle toothbrush and toothpaste morning and night to gently brush your teeth, including the implant site itself. This speeds healing and keeps your mouth feeling fresh.

The "Button" You’ll See at the Site

Timeline for Healing

The gum tissue around your implant heals quickly, usually within about a week. The bone takes longer to heal. Full integration of the implant into the bone generally takes a few months, and your implant is typically ready to be restored with a tooth in 3 to 4 months.

Protecting the Long-Term Result

Once your implant is fully healed and restored with a crown, care for it just like a natural tooth. Daily brushing, flossing, and regular cleanings keep the gum and bone around the implant healthy, which is what keeps it stable for the long term.
