Gum Disease Treatment Aftercare Instructions | Mint Hill Smiles

Treatment for gum disease, known clinically as scaling and root planing,

Right After Your Gum Disease Treatment

  • If your mouth is numb, avoid eating until the numbness wears off so you do not accidentally bite your cheek, lip, or tongue.
  • Some soreness, tenderness, and mild bleeding from the gums is normal for the first few days.
  • Your teeth may feel more sensitive to hot and cold temporarily. This usually improves over the following days and weeks as your gums heal.
  • Rinse gently with warm salt water (half a teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water) two to three times a day to soothe the tissue and keep the area clean.

Eating

  • Stick to soft foods for the first day or two if your gums are sore.
  • Avoid very hot, spicy, crunchy, or acidic foods while the tissue is tender.
  • Stay hydrated and return to your normal diet as comfort allows.

Brushing and Flossing

  • Homecare is very important in staying on top of the bacteria that cause gum disease.
  • Making sure to brush angling into the gumline is key, use of an Electric Toothbrush is ideal.
  • Floss at least once a day (at night is best) and focus on hugging the corners of the teeth and getting all the way below the gum with your floss.
  • If you had a lot of bleeding with your treatment your provider may have recommended a prescription mouthwash for you to use. Swish with this twice a day (morning and night) for 1 week after each treatment session. Prolonged use can cause staining on the teeth.

Managing Discomfort

  • An over-the-counter pain reliever taken as directed on the label is usually enough to manage soreness. Our doctors typically recommend Ibuprofen or Aleve.
  • The soreness should steadily improve. If pain increases after a few days rather than improving, call us.
