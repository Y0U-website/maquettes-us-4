Periodontal therapy is a non-surgical treatment designed to manage and treat gum disease (periodontitis) . It focuses on removing harmful bacteria and infection below the gumline to prevent further damage to the gums and supporting bone. The most common form of periodontal therapy is Scaling and Root Planing (deep cleaning) , which goes beyond a regular cleaning to treat active infection.
  • What is Scaling and Root Planing (Deep Cleaning)? Scaling and root planing is a specialized dental procedure that removes plaque, tartar, and bacteria from beneath the gums and smooths the root surfaces to promote healing. What Happens During Treatment: * Removal of plaque and tartar below the gumline * Deep cleaning of periodontal pockets * Smoothing of tooth roots to prevent bacterial reattachment * Possible use of local anesthesia for comfort This treatment helps the gums reattach to the teeth and reduces inflammation.
  • Who Needs Periodontal Therapy? You may need periodontal therapy if you have: * Bleeding gums when brushing or flossing * Persistent bad breath * Gum recession or “longer-looking” teeth * Loose teeth or shifting bite * Gum pockets deeper than 4mm * Bone loss seen on dental X-rays Early treatment is key to preventing tooth loss and more complex procedures.
  • What is Periodontal Maintenance? After completing periodontal therapy, ongoing care is essential. Periodontal maintenance is a specialized cleaning designed for patients with a history of gum disease. Unlike regular cleanings, periodontal maintenance focuses on managing bacteria below the gumline and preventing recurrence.
  • How Often is Periodontal Maintenance Needed? You may need periodontal therapy. Most patients require periodontal maintenance every 3 to 4 months , depending on their condition.
  • Who needs Periodontal Maintenance? Patients treated for gum disease Patients with ongoing periodontal pockets Patients with bone loss Patients at higher risk (smokers, diabetics, etc.) Why does it matter?
