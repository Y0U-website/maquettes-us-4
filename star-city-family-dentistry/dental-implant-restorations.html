Dental Implant Restorations

  • Implant-supported bridge: Replaces several teeth in a row, avoiding a removable partial.
  • Implant-supported denture: Stabilizes a full denture for improved comfort and function.

Benefits of Dental Implant Restorations

  • Natural function and feel: Chew and speak with confidence, similar to natural teeth.
  • Aesthetics that blend: Shade and shape are matched to surrounding teeth for a seamless look.
  • Bone preservation: Implants help stimulate the jaw, which may reduce bone loss over time.
  • No alteration of healthy teeth: A single implant crown does not require grinding adjacent teeth.
  • Stability compared to removable options: Less slipping and fewer sore spots.
  • Long-term solution: With proper care, implant restorations can last many years.

How Dental Implant Restorations Work

If your implant surgery was completed by a specialist, the restoration phase can be coordinated so the final result fits your bite and looks natural. For many patients, this step involves little or no downtime.

  • Evaluation and planning: A clinical exam and imaging help determine if your implant is ready for restoration and which option fits your goals.
  • Impressions or digital scan: Detailed models guide the lab in crafting a precise crown, bridge, or denture.
  • Abutment selection: The connector is chosen for optimal gum contour and crown fit.
  • Temporary restoration (when appropriate): A provisional may protect the site and guide gum shaping.
  • Final placement: The custom restoration is attached, and the bite is adjusted for comfort.
  • Home care guidance: You will receive instructions for daily cleaning and follow-up.

What to Expect Before and After Treatment

If the implant is newly placed, healing time for dental implants typically ranges from 3 to 6 months before the final restoration. During this period, you may wear a temporary tooth replacement to maintain appearance. Once the implant is integrated, the restoration visit is usually comfortable and performed with local anesthesia as needed.

After placement, mild tenderness is possible for a day or two. Soft foods and careful chewing are encouraged at first. Daily hygiene is essential: Brush twice a day, clean between teeth with floss or interdental brushes, and consider a water flosser for hard-to-reach areas. For implant-supported dentures, remove and clean the denture (if removable) and brush around implant attachments. Nightguards can help protect your restoration if you clench or grind.

Every case is unique. Factors such as bone quality, gum health, and the number of missing teeth influence the timeline and the type of restoration recommended. Discussing goals and lifestyle helps select between an implant crown vs. bridge or an implant-supported denture.
