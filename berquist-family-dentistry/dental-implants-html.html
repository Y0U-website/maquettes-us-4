What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a titanium post meant to mimic the root structure of a one or more missing teeth. A small incision is made in the gums, and the post is surgically inserted into the jawbone. After spending several months at home allowing osseointegration (fusing of the implant to the bone) to occur, you will return to have your customized dental crown, bridge, or denture (also known as your restoration) placed on top of the implants, fully restoring the tooth’s structure.

As the only treatment option that rebuilds a lost tooth from top to bottom, dental implants come highly recommended by most dental professionals, including our very own Dr. Daniel Berquist and Dr. Robert Berquist . Because of their stable foundation in the jawbone, dental implants are more lifelike, reliable, and long-lasting than any alternative solution.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Getting dental implants requires multi-step treatment plan; however, it’s time well-spent to ensure your new smile can last for decades. We’ll explain each phase and address any concerns during your initial consultation. In the meantime, here’s a general outline of the four basic steps in the dental implant process:

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

Most patients who have good oral and general health are candidates for dental implants; however, you’ll still need a thorough consultation. We will perform a visual examination and take a CT scan to assess your oral health. Based on the results of the consultation, you may need additional treatments to become a candidate for the procedure, like tooth extractions, periodontal therapy, or bone grafting.

Dental Implant Surgery

Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment

The implant post acts as a root, stimulating your jaw to encourage bone growth. Your jaw will fuse to the post through a process called osseointegration. This allows the post to potentially remain in place forever with the right care. Not to mention, your restoration will have a stable foundation, so you won’t need to worry about any slipping or irritation.

It usually takes three to six months for your jaw to fully heal. Afterward, you’ll need a second minor surgery to attach an abutment to the post. The special fixture connects your restoration to your implant. It takes about two weeks for your gum tissue to heal after the procedure.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

While you’re recovering from your abutment procedure, the dental lab will be hard at work creating your restoration. It’s made of all-ceramic material to match the color, size, and shape of your natural teeth to blend in when you smile and speak. It’s also durable to withstand the pressure of regular chewing to enjoy your favorite foods.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants as we know them today were first placed in the 1960s. Since then, dentists have had a chance to see the short- and long-term benefits of dental implants, and they’ve quickly become the new standard for replacing teeth. Once you understand what makes dental implants unique, it’s not hard to see why so many people choose them over traditional dentures and bridges. Below are just some of the benefits that you’ll enjoy when you choose single implants, implant bridges, or an implant denture.

Day-to-Day Benefits

  • A Natural Look and Feel: Implant restorations look and feel a lot more like real teeth than traditional dentures and bridges. Not only are they designed to blend in, but they also feel like part of your mouth once the implants have joined with the bone. No need to feel awkward about dentures that could slip at the wrong moment and cause you to slur your words!
  • Complete Diet: With regular dentures, your diet will be somewhat limited due to the difficulty in chewing sticky, crunchy, hard, or generally tough foods. Dental implants have strength that’s comparable to real teeth, so they don’t have any real diet restrictions associated with them. You can head back to your favorite restaurant with your head held high!
  • Easy Maintenance: You don’t have to do anything special to take care of dental implants aside from brushing and flossing in other words, the sort of thing you were already doing before you lost any teeth.

Health Benefits

  • Improved Oral Health: Since dental implants are so easy to clean, you should be able to maintain a healthy mouth over the long term. By brushing and flossing as thoroughly as possible, you can avoid gum disease and other health issues that could eventually lead to costly treatments.
  • Strong Jawbone: Did you know that your teeth are actively working to keep your jawbone strong and healthy? The stimulation they provide encourages the rest of the body to deliver nutrients to the area and maintain the jaw.
  • No Sensitivity or Sores: Dental implants don’t require us to make changes to natural teeth, meaning they don’t carry a risk of increasing tooth sensitivity. And because they support dental restorations themselves, you’ll avoid the soreness that’s often associated with dentures.

Long-Term Benefits

  • Permanence: Dentures and bridges are fairly durable, but they do have rather limited lifespans. They last around 10 to 15 years sometimes more or less depending on the care they get. Dental implants, on the other hands, have the potential to last anywhere from 30 years to an entire lifetime, meaning you’ll truly be able to enjoy your new tooth for the rest of your life.
  • Save Money: Not only are you less likely to have to pay for a dental implant replacement, but you also won’t have to spend money on specialized cleaning solution, adhesive, and other products that might be required with dentures.
  • Long-Term Success: As far as oral surgeries go, dental implants have an extremely high success rate around 95 % to 98 %.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Thanks to their versatility, dental implants are ideal for a wide variety of tooth loss cases. Consider your options below!

Missing One Tooth

When replacing a single tooth, a dental implant post, an abutment, and a dental crown are needed. The biocompatible implant post is surgically placed into the jawbone to replace the tooth root while also providing a secure foundation for the dental crown. Over the course of the next several weeks, your jawbone will fuse to the implant post, allowing it to remain in place for many decades with the right aftercare.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Dental implants in Crown Point now provide the perfect alternative to a traditional dental bridge when needing to replace multiple missing teeth in a row. Prosthetic teeth will be created to bridge the gap. Instead of using dental crowns that are placed on the adjacent teeth to hold it in place, an average of one to two dental implant posts are used.

They anchor the oral appliance in place, allowing you to regain more of your natural ability to chew and speak. Not to mention, without capping the natural teeth, it helps to preserve them from damage in the future. You will give them the support they need to stay firmly in place while improving your quality of life.

Missing All Teeth

You can replace an entire arch of teeth with dental implants. A full dental implant procedure is completed in two phases. In the first, an average of four implant posts are surgically placed into the jaw. Then, after they have had time to bond with your jawbone, your posts will be exposed, and your custom-made full denture will be attached. This process can take several months to complete, but the results are unlike anything you can get with traditional restorative care.

Preliminary Treatments & Dental Implant Surgery

Preliminary treatments are simply steps we might take to help ensure the success of your dental implant surgery. If you have an active case of gum disease, for example, we’ll treat the issue with periodontal therapy before starting the dental implant process. Though they’ll add to the total cost of your dental implants, preliminary treatments will help you avoid complications and ultimately improve the longevity of your restoration, so they’re well worth it.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

As mentioned earlier, there are a couple of different dental implant restorations we might recommend based on your needs. A singular implant crown, implant bridge, implant dentures, and all-on-4 each have different associated costs. Generally speaking, here’s how you can expect them to compare:

  • One implant crown tends to be the least expensive option, though your expenses will increase if you’re receiving multiple implant crowns.
  • Implant bridges typically require at least one or two implants and at least one false tooth, so they tend to involve more time and materials.
  • Full and partial implant dentures use anywhere between 4 and 8 implants, but provide a more comprehensive replacement and can cost more than an implant crown or bridge.
  • In certain situations, an all-on-4 can be less expensive than an implant denture. Be sure to ask our team about this option if you’re combining dental implants with a denture restoration!

How Dental Implants Can Save You Money

Compared to more traditional options, a dental implant restoration does cost more at least upfront. With proper care, however, dental implants can last for 30 years or more! In that amount of time, you’d likely have to pay for multiple bridge or denture repairs. Since dental implants can prevent jawbone loss by replacing dental roots, you also won’t have to worry about needing a bone graft. Quality can pay for itself!

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are the only replacement teeth that are embedded into your jawbone to provide unique advantages over the alternatives. However, it’s essential to maintain them properly to continue reaping their many benefits. They may be long-lasting under normal circumstances, but that doesn’t mean they’re invincible.

For example, implementing proper preventive care can help keep them looking and feeling great for years to come. Continue reading to learn more about various ways you can preserve your dental implants and safeguard your smile!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Your prosthetics aren’t made from the same organic substances as your natural enamel, so you don’t need to worry that they’ll get cavities. Furthermore, the durable ceramic used in your restoration is less porous than your natural teeth, making it more resistant to chips, cracks, and stains.

However, bacteria in your mouth can still penetrate the surgical site and cause an infection, such as peri-implantitis, which is the leading cause of failure. Thankfully, this can be prevented by brushing and flossing your teeth twice daily to remove plaque and debris that contribute to common oral issues.

Eat a Healthy Diet

This treatment is the only one that can restore up to 80 % or more of your original bite force, resulting in fewer dietary restrictions compared to dentures or bridges. But you can still damage your restoration by eating overly hard, crunchy, sticky, or sugary foods, especially during your recovery period.

Meanwhile, filling up on dairy products with lots of calcium can help you build strong teeth and bones, and eating more citrus fruits and leafy greens can boost your vitamin C levels to support your connective tissues.

Break Bad Habits

Using tobacco products, like smoking vapes and cigarettes, significantly increases the likelihood of implant failure. Nicotine suppresses your immune system and hinders your body’s ability to deliver essential nutrients to your teeth and gums through your bloodstream, which can result in infections that cause your tooth to fail. Quitting smoking is an effective way to help maintain your prosthetic.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Do you suffer from bruxism (teeth grinding) or participate in team sports or high-contact activities? If so, you’re prone to sustaining injuries that could chip, crack, destabilize, or dislodge your teeth, including your implant.

Our team can help provide a custom-fitted oral appliance, like a nightguard or mouthguard, to protect your teeth from harm.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Visiting our team every six months for a routine checkup and cleaning allows us to monitor your condition to catch and treat any areas of concern, like gum disease, before they can progress and cause damage. This also allows us to ensure that you recover as intended after your implant procedure and avoid potential complications that might otherwise develop.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

If you want a permanent solution for missing teeth, look no further than dental implants in Crown Point. They’re designed to last well over 30 years sometimes even a lifetime! Of course, their exact lifespan depends heavily on a patient’s oral health, habits, and lifestyle. The good news is that proper maintenance for dental implants doesn’t require any special care. Brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and regularly visiting your dentist is all that’s needed to keep your new smile in its very best shape!

Does Getting Dental Implants Hurt?

Before the procedure begins, your mouth will be completely numb with a local anesthetic. Also, you’ll more than likely be sedated, which lowers your body’s ability to register pain. Even though the surgery itself shouldn’t hurt, your mouth will feel sore for the first few days following your procedure. Luckily, you manage those symptoms by taking prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication as directed and using cold compresses. If your discomfort worsens, contact our dental team right away for assistance.

Is There Anything I Shouldn’t Eat with Dental Implants?

You’ll want to avoid chewy, tough, and hard foods for the first couple of weeks after your surgery. Do not use a straw, and be sure to avoid sugary, acidic, and alcoholic drinks as well. After this initial period, you can eat almost any food you want! Of course, you’ll want to exercise caution with items like nuts, ice, hard candy, and gum, as they can loosen or dislodge your restoration altogether.
