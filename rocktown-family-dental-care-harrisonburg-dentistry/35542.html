Because the implant replaces the missing tooth root, it creates a strong foundation for a long-lasting tooth replacement. This would be a crown for a single missing tooth or an implant bridge or denture for multiple missing teeth.

  • Abutment An attachment that connects the implant to the crown.

Dental Implants and Osseointegration

The key to dental implant stability is a process called osseointegratio n. Once the implant is placed into the jawbone, the bone gradually grows around and bonds with the titanium post.

This process takes several months and ensures that the implant is firmly anchored in place, providing a level of stability other tooth replacements cannot.

Health Benefits of Dental Implants

There are many advantages to choosing implants to improve function, but they also offer numerous oral health benefits:

  • Prevent bone loss Tooth loss can lead to jawbone loss, which can result in a sunken facial appearance and further dental issues. An implant stimulates the jawbone to maintain its density and prevent bone loss.
  • Stabilize surrounding teeth Dental implants prevent neighboring teeth from shifting, which can lead to bite problems and the potential for additional tooth loss.
  • Maintain a healthy jawbone Dental implants support overall jaw health by preserving the jawbone and preventing resorption and further tooth loss.
  • Preserve healthy tooth structure Dental implants don t require altering adjacent teeth, as bridges do.

How Implants Compare to Dentures

If you currently wear removable dentures and wonder how dental implants compare, here are a few of the benefits to consider:

  • Increased comfort Dental implants don t slip or shift as removable dentures do, providing increased comfort and stability
  • Improved chewing ability Removable dentures restore only a fraction of your chewing ability, while dental implants function like natural teeth, allowing you to enjoy your favorite foods without worry or discomfort.
  • Improved speech Unlike ill-fitting dentures that can slip and cause speech difficulties, dental implants stay firmly in place so that you can speak clearly.
  • Longer-lasting solution With proper care, dental implants outlast other tooth replacement options and can provide 25 years or a lifetime of use.

How to Care for Your Dental Implant

Dental implants are long-term tooth replacements that can last 25 years or even a lifetime when cared for properly.

  • Brush and floss daily to keep your dental implants and surrounding tissues clean and plaque-free.
  • Visit Dr. Anderson s office for regular checkups and professional cleanings to maintain oral health.
  • Avoid smoking and excessive alcohol consumption, as they can negatively impact your dental implant s success and longevity.
  • Wear a mouthguard during sports activities or while sleeping if you grind your teeth to protect your dental implants and natural teeth from damage.

How much are dental implants?

There’s no single standard or exact figure for what a dental implant should cost. This variability in the cost of implants stems from a variety of factors, including:

As always, it’s essential that you consult with a dentist for a thorough assessment of your individual needs and a specific and accurate cost estimate.

  • The type of dental implant used. Endosteal is most common, but other varieties, like subperiosteal for individuals with insufficient bone height and zygomatic, which are inserted directly into the cheekbone of patients with upper jawbone loss, are sometimes recommended.
  • The extent of the damage to the gums and jawbone where the missing tooth used to be.
  • The number of dental implants that are being placed.
  • The need for additional procedures like bone grafting or sinus lifts.

What are dental implants?

Dental implants are artificial tooth roots that are surgically inserted into the jawbone to replace missing teeth. Typically made from titanium, dental implants are a sturdy foundation for affixing replacement teeth, such as dental crowns, bridges, or dentures. The result is a natural-looking and functional replacement that mimics the look and feel of your original teeth.

How Do Dental Implants Work?

Once the dental implants are inserted and have fully fused with the jawbone a process known as osseointegration they act like natural tooth roots. Replacement teeth are attached to the implant, providing a secure and long-lasting solution for tooth loss.

Why Consider Dental Implants?

Dental implants are considered one of the most effective and durable solutions for missing teeth. They don t require adjacent teeth for support, like traditional bridges, and they help preserve the jawbone, preventing the bone loss that often accompanies missing teeth.
