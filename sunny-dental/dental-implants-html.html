What Are Dental Implants?

Rather than replacing just the visible portion of a missing tooth, dental implants are capable of restoring an entire tooth’s structure, from root to crown which is something no other restorative treatment can do. This is accomplished with the help of biocompatible titanium posts that are surgically placed into the jawbone; they can fuse with it in order to provide a sturdy, durable foundation for a future dental restoration.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

The process of receiving dental implants can often be categorized into four key steps:

  • Initial Consultation During this appointment, we’ll confirm that you’re able to receive dental implants to restore your missing teeth. Patients need to be in good oral health and free of issues like gum disease, but they must also have adequate jawbone density for supporting the implants.
  • Dental Implant Placement We’re partnered with some of the best local dental implant surgeons to ensure that your procedure is a resounding success. As for the actual surgery, the implant posts will be placed at precise angles and locations directly into your jaw tissue. Don’t worry, you’ll be fully numbed for this!
  • Osseointegration/Abutment The implant posts will need several months to fully fuse with your jawbone, and during this time, you’ll have to be extra diligent about your oral health. Assuming things can heal properly, you’ll return for a second, smaller procedure to receive abutments, which connect your future dental restoration to the posts.
  • Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s) Your replacement teeth will be designed to look, feel, and function just like your original ones. They’ll receive incredible support from the underlying implant posts. You’ll be all set to enjoy a complete smile once more!

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants go far beyond replacing your missing teeth they also offer all kinds of life-changing benefits, including:

  • Dental implants are the only tooth replacement option that restores the entire structure of missing teeth, from root to crown and they can do this for any tooth in the mouth, regardless of where it was lost.
  • Dental implants prevent the jawbone deterioration that occurs following tooth loss by providing stimulation to the bone, encouraging it to remain healthy and full.
  • Once they’ve fully healed, dental implants restore most of a patient’s original bite force, meaning that all types of foods are back on the menu.
  • With the right care, dental implants have the potential to last for decades or even a lifetime.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Dental implants can be used to assist patients with varying degrees of tooth loss, but you’ll first need to come in for an appointment so that we can confirm you’re a good candidate for this solution. Once we’ve confirmed you’re a good candidate, we can begin putting together your treatment plan based on your tooth loss.

Missing One Tooth

A single dental implant post can be placed between two healthy teeth without disturbing them at all, and then a custom-made dental crown can be placed on top. Unlike a traditional bridge, this new tooth won’t rely on the adjacent teeth for support.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Partial dentures and bridges are two go-to solutions for replacing multiple teeth along one or both arches, and now, they can be made to be even more reliable with the assistance of dental implants.

Missing All Teeth

A full denture can easily be anchored to the jawbone using four to six strategically placed dental implants. Compared to removable dentures, implant dentures last much longer and return much more of a patient’s lost biting power.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

The way your dental implants are constructed will also contribute to their cost. A few factors to consider include:

  • Number of implants: The cost of each dental implant will be noted separately on your bill.
  • Restoration used: Implants can support crowns, bridges, and dentures. Larger restorations such as bridges and dentures require more material and are, therefore, more costly.
  • Material: Implants are typically made from titanium or zirconia. Devices made from different materials may be priced differently.
  • Brand: A number of companies produce dental implants, and different products come at different price points. Please contact us if you’d like to know more about who is manufacturing our implants.
