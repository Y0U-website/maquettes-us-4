Improved Oral Health and Functionality

Dental implants don’t just replace missing teeth—they protect your overall oral health. When a tooth is lost, the surrounding bone begins to deteriorate due to a lack of stimulation. Implants mimic natural tooth roots, stimulating the jawbone and preventing bone loss. Implants don’t require altering your healthy adjacent teeth, preserving your natural tooth structure.

Unmatched Durability and Longevity

Dental implants are designed to last. With proper care, including regular brushing, flossing, and dental checkups with our Littleton dentist, implants can last a lifetime. This durability makes them a cost-effective solution compared to dentures or dental bridges, which may need frequent replacements or adjustments.

  • Long-Term Investment : Implants often outlast other tooth replacement options.
  • Low Maintenance : No special cleaning solutions or adhesives required.
  • High Success Rate : Over 95 % success rate for properly placed implants.

Restored Chewing and Speaking Abilities

Missing teeth can make eating and speaking challenging. Dental implants restore full chewing power, allowing you to enjoy your favorite foods without restriction. They also improve speech clarity, eliminating the slurring or mumbling that can occur with missing teeth or ill-fitting dentures.

Unlike removable dentures, which can cause discomfort or require messy adhesives, dental implants are fixed in place and feel like natural teeth. There’s no need to remove them for cleaning or worry about them slipping during meals or conversations.

Preservation of Facial Structure

Tooth loss can lead to a sunken facial appearance due to jawbone deterioration. Dental implants prevent this by maintaining bone density, preserving your natural facial contours, and preventing premature aging.

Dental implants are highly versatile and can address a range of dental issues, from replacing a single tooth to restoring an entire arch. Options include:

Possible Risks and Complications of Dental Implants

Surgical Complications

As with any surgical procedure, dental implant placement carries a small risk of complications. These may include:

  • Infection : Bacteria can enter the surgical site, leading to infection if not properly managed. Good oral hygiene and post-operative care minimize this risk.
  • Nerve Damage : Improper placement near nerves can cause numbness, tingling, or pain in the gums, lips, or chin. This is rare and typically avoided with precise imaging and planning.
  • Sinus Issues : For implants in the upper jaw, there’s a slight risk of sinus perforation if the implant protrudes into the sinus cavity.

Implant Failure

In rare cases, the implant may fail to integrate with the jawbone or become loose over time. Causes of implant failure include:

  • Poor Osseointegration : If the bone doesn’t fuse properly with the implant, it may not provide a stable foundation.
  • Peri-implantitis : A form of gum disease that affects the tissues around the implant, potentially leading to bone loss and implant failure.
  • Overloading : Excessive pressure on the implant during healing can disrupt integration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do dental implants get cavities?

No, dental implants don’t get cavities. Since they’re made from titanium, they’re resistant to tooth decay. Just make sure you’re maintaining good oral health so your implants last as long as possible and your natural teeth get the proper care they need.

Can smokers get dental implants?

Smoking can slow healing and increase the risk of implant failure. However, smokers may still be candidates if they commit to quitting or reducing smoking during the healing process.

What happens if I don’t replace a missing tooth?

Leaving a gap can lead to shifting teeth, bite issues, jawbone loss, and a sunken facial appearance. Dental implants prevent these complications.
