FAQs

Dental implants or implants for teeth are titanium posts clinically or surgically placed in the jawbone to replace tooth roots, topped with crowns that look and function like natural teeth. They provide the most stable and permanent solution for tooth replacement.

Good candidates have adequate bone density and healthy gums and are committed to excellent oral hygiene. Age is less important than overall health, and many medical conditions don’t prevent implant placement with proper management.

Look for dentists with implant surgery training, advanced technology for planning and placement, and a track record of successful outcomes. Consider their continuing education and membership in implant organizations.

The process involves consultation and planning, implant placement surgery, a healing period for osseointegration, and crown attachment. The entire treatment typically takes 3-6 months, depending on the individual’s healing and the complexity of the condition.

Most patients experience minimal discomfort during and after implant placement with proper anesthesia and pain management. Post-surgical discomfort is typically manageable with prescribed medications and resolves within a few days.

Teeth implants can last a lifetime with excellent oral hygiene and regular dental maintenance. The crown may need replacement after 10-15 years, but the implant itself rarely requires replacement when properly maintained.

Implants preserve jawbone, don’t affect adjacent teeth, provide superior stability for eating and speaking, and look completely natural. Your dentist in Hackensack can explain how implants compare to bridges or dentures for your situation.

While implants have higher upfront costs, they often provide better long-term value due to their durability and minimal maintenance requirements. Most dental practices offer financing options to make treatment more affordable.

Dental implants have success rates exceeding 95 % when placed by experienced practitioners in appropriate candidates. Factors like smoking, diabetes, & poor oral hygiene can affect success rates.
