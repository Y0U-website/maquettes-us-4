Scaling And Root Planing In…

Scaling and root planing helps control the bacterial infection associated with periodontal disease, but it does not eliminate a patient’s future susceptibility. Daily home care and ongoing periodontal monitoring remain necessary.

  • Plaque and tartar beneath the gumline
  • Bleeding during brushing, flossing, or probing
  • Red, swollen, or tender gums
  • Persistent bad breath
  • Gum recession or exposed root surfaces
  • Early or moderate loss of supporting bone
  • Inflammation that remains despite improved home care
  • A diagnosis of periodontitis

Who May Be a Candidate for Deep Cleaning in Riverton, Utah?

A suitable candidate generally has periodontal pockets or below-the-gum deposits that require more extensive treatment than a routine dental cleaning can provide.

Bleeding or inflamed gums do not automatically mean scaling and root planing is necessary. Gingivitis without deeper periodontal damage may sometimes be managed with a professional cleaning and improved home care.

Advanced periodontal disease may require additional treatment or evaluation by a periodontist. The appropriate recommendation depends on the extent and location of the disease.

  • Periodontal pocket measurements
  • Bleeding and inflammation around the gums
  • The location and amount of tartar
  • Gum recession and exposed root surfaces
  • Supporting bone levels shown on dental X-rays
  • Tooth mobility and long-term tooth prognosis
  • Previous periodontal treatment
  • Home-care habits and access between the teeth
  • Risk factors such as tobacco use or diabetes

What Happens During a Periodontal Evaluation?

Your evaluation begins with a discussion about your symptoms, health history, medications, previous periodontal treatment, and daily oral-care routine. Tell the dental team about bleeding, bad breath, sensitivity, gum recession, or changes in how your teeth feel.

The dentist or dental hygienist will examine the gums, measure the spaces around the teeth, and review dental X-rays when needed. According to the National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research, periodontal diagnosis may include gum examination, pocket measurements, evaluation of the teeth, medical-history review, and dental imaging.

This evaluation determines which areas require scaling and root planing and whether another form of periodontal care may be more appropriate.

  • Reviewing your symptoms and dental history
  • Examining the gums for inflammation and recession
  • Measuring periodontal pocket depths
  • Checking for bleeding around the gums
  • Evaluating tooth mobility and bite
  • Reviewing X-rays for supporting bone loss
  • Discussing your home care and risk factors

The Scaling and Root Planing Procedure

Treatment may be completed in one appointment or divided into multiple visits. The schedule depends on the number of areas involved, pocket depth, sensitivity, and extent of the bacterial deposits.

Preparing and Numbing the Treatment Area The dental team identifies the areas requiring treatment. Local anesthesia may be used when deeper pockets, root sensitivity, or gum tenderness could make treatment uncomfortable. For suitable patients who feel anxious about treatment, nitrous oxide sedation may also be considered. It can support relaxation but does not replace local anesthesia.

Scaling Below the Gumline Specialized instruments remove plaque and hardened tartar from the tooth surfaces and periodontal pockets. The cleaning extends to the base of each area being treated.

Planing the Roots and Planning Follow-Up Affected root surfaces are carefully cleaned and smoothed. After the gums have had time to heal, a follow-up evaluation may be scheduled to measure the pockets and determine how the tissues responded.

Medication or an antimicrobial rinse may be recommended in selected cases. These treatments are not necessary for every patient and do not replace thorough cleaning and ongoing home care.

Scaling & Root Planing Compared With a Routine Cleaning

Routine dental cleanings and scaling and root planing both remove bacterial deposits, but they are recommended for different clinical conditions.

A routine cleaning cannot adequately treat deposits at the base of deep periodontal pockets. Scaling and root planing should be recommended according to periodontal findings rather than used as a substitute for preventive cleaning in every patient.

  • Primarily intended for preventive care
  • Cleans accessible surfaces around the gumline
  • Appropriate when deep periodontal pockets are absent
  • Commonly completed during a regular dental visit
  • Helps prevent plaque and tartar accumulation
  • Used to treat diagnosed periodontal disease
  • Cleans tooth roots below the gumline
  • Targets deposits inside periodontal pockets
  • May require local anesthesia
  • Can be divided between multiple appointments

Other Periodontal Care You May Need

Scaling and root planing may be one part of a broader plan for controlling gum disease and protecting the tissues supporting your teeth.

Improved Home Care Your dental team may recommend changes to brushing, flossing, or the use of interdental brushes and other cleaning devices.

Medication or Antimicrobial Rinses Medication may be prescribed or placed in selected periodontal pockets to help control bacteria, discomfort, or infection.

Periodontal Maintenance After active treatment, periodontal maintenance visits may be recommended more frequently than routine preventive cleanings. These visits help manage deposits and monitor pocket depths.

Periodontal Surgery Persistent deep pockets or areas that cannot be adequately maintained may require periodontal flap surgery or gingivectomy .

Specialist Evaluation A periodontist may be recommended when the disease is advanced, the treatment needs are complex, or a tooth’s long-term prognosis requires specialist assessment.

Recovery and Ongoing Care

Temporary soreness, gum tenderness, minor bleeding, and tooth sensitivity may occur after scaling and root planing. The intensity and duration vary according to the treatment area and individual response.

Follow the instructions provided for cleaning, eating, medication, and activity after your appointment.

Contact the dental office if pain, swelling, bleeding, or another symptom becomes severe, persists, or worsens instead of gradually improving.

  • Brushing gently with a soft-bristled toothbrush
  • Cleaning between the teeth as instructed
  • Using a prescribed or recommended rinse
  • Taking medication only as directed
  • Avoiding tobacco during healing
  • Attending the recommended follow-up visit

What Happens After Scaling and Root Planing?

The gums need time to respond before the results of treatment can be fully evaluated.

Some patients respond well enough that surgery is unnecessary. If deep pockets, inaccessible deposits, or inflammation remain, additional periodontal treatment or referral to a periodontist may be recommended.

  • Rechecking periodontal pocket depths
  • Evaluating bleeding and inflammation
  • Reviewing your plaque-control routine
  • Planning ongoing periodontal maintenance
