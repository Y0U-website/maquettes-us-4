Step-By-Step Endodontic Procedure

Our tooth-saving treatment is performed carefully to support your comfort safety and long-lasting results. Each step is planned to remove infection, protect your natural tooth and restore your smile.

Tooth Examination And X-Rays

We begin by checking your tooth and taking X-rays to find the infection and review the health of nearby bone and tissues. This helps us plan precise Endodontics care for your needs.

Numbing The Area

Before starting we apply local anesthesia to the treated area. This helps you feel no pain, only gentle pressure so the procedure stays comfortable from start to finish.

Cleaning And Shaping Canals

Once the tooth is numb we carefully remove the infected pulp and bacteria from the canals. The canals are then shaped for filling to help prevent future infection and keep the tooth healthy.

Canal Filling And Protection

Once the canals are cleaned we place a safe dental material inside the tooth to close the space and block new bacteria. This helps support the tooth from within and lowers the risk of future infection.

Tooth Repair And Final Care

At the end the tooth is rebuilt usually with a custom crown when needed. This helps bring back normal chewing strength, protects the treated tooth and gives it a natural look. After this step your tooth feels stable, healthy and ready for daily use.

Have Any Questions? 414-401-4944 What Is Endodontic Therapy? Endodontic therapy is a specialized dental treatment used to save teeth affected by deep infection or nerve damage. Inside every tooth is soft tissue called pulp, which contains nerves and blood vessels. When bacteria reach this area through decay, cracks, or injury, it can lead to severe pain, swelling, and infection.

Endodontic treatment removes the damaged tissue, disinfects the inside of the tooth, and seals it to help prevent future infection. In many cases, this procedure allows patients to keep their natural teeth instead of needing extraction.

Signs You May Need Endodontic Treatment

Tooth infections often worsen over time if left untreated. Some common symptoms include:

If you experience these symptoms, early treatment can help prevent more serious dental complications.

  • Severe or throbbing tooth pain
  • Sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures
  • Pain while chewing or biting
  • Swollen or tender gums
  • Darkening or discoloration of a tooth
  • A cracked or damaged tooth
  • Pressure around the affected tooth
  • Small bumps or abscesses on the gums

Common Causes Of Tooth Infection

A tooth may become infected for several reasons. Understanding these causes helps patients protect their long-term oral health.

Deep Tooth Decay

Untreated cavities can allow bacteria to spread deep inside the tooth and reach the pulp.

Cracked Or Broken Teeth

Cracks may expose the inner layers of the tooth, allowing bacteria to enter and cause infection.

Dental Trauma

Sports injuries, accidents, or sudden impact can damage the nerve inside a tooth even if no crack is visible.

Repeated Dental Procedures

Multiple treatments on the same tooth may weaken the tooth structure over time and irritate the pulp.

Advanced Gum Disease

Bacteria from gum infections may spread and affect nearby teeth and supporting structures.
