Dental implants are the most reliable, long-lasting solution available for replacing missing teeth. Unlike dentures or bridges, implants replace the entire tooth, root and all, by anchoring a titanium post directly into the jawbone, which then supports a natural-looking crown above the gumline.

How Dental Implants Work

The implant process begins with a thorough evaluation, including 3D imaging to assess your bone density and anatomy before any surgical planning takes place. A small titanium post is then placed into the jawbone during a minor surgical procedure. Over the following months, the implant integrates with the surrounding bone through a process called osseointegration, creating a stable, permanent foundation. Once healed, a custom-crafted crown is attached to complete the restoration. The final result is indistinguishable from a natural tooth in both appearance and function.

Who Is a Good Candidate?

Most adults in good general health who are missing one or more teeth are candidates for dental implants. The most important factor is having adequate bone density in the jaw to support the implant post. Patients who have experienced bone loss due to long-term tooth loss or gum disease may require a bone graft before implant placement to build up the necessary foundation. Dr. Tran will evaluate your bone structure using our 3D CT imaging during your consultation and walk you through all available options based on your specific anatomy and goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

Have questions about dental implants? Here are straightforward answers to what our patients ask most often to help you feel informed and confident before your consultation.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime. The implant post itself, the titanium fixture that integrates with the jawbone, is designed to be permanent. The crown attached to the implant may need to be replaced after 15 to 20 years, depending on wear, but the implant foundation beneath it typically remains intact indefinitely with consistent oral hygiene and regular dental visits.

How long does the implant process take?

The full implant process typically takes several months from placement to final restoration. After the implant post is placed, a healing period of three to six months is needed for osseointegration, the process by which the implant fuses with the jawbone. Once healing is complete, the final crown is attached. The exact timeline depends on your individual healing and whether any preparatory procedures, such as a bone graft, are needed beforehand.

Are dental implants covered by insurance?

Dental implant coverage varies widely by insurance plan. Some plans provide partial coverage for implants or the associated crown, while others classify them as elective and provide no coverage.
