Visit Your Dentist Consistently

The American Dental Association recommends visiting your provider every six months for a routine checkup and cleaning. Regular routine visits allow Dr. Vo and the rest of our team to monitor your condition so we can catch and treat any areas of concern before they have a chance to progress into more severe issues.

Plus, we have the experience and technology to quickly identify problem areas that can be difficult to see on your own at home, such as decay or disease between teeth or behind your molars.

Maintain A Regular At-Home Dental Hygiene Routine

You must also clean your mouth consistently at home to avoid cavities and gum disease that progressively worsen the longer they remain unaddressed. The ADA suggests brushing for a full two minutes both morning and night, followed by flossing to remove stubborn plaque and bacteria.

This freshens your breath and keeps your grin healthy to reduce the likelihood of experiencing an emergency.

Eat A Wholesome Diet

Did you know that your dietary decisions significantly impact your oral health? For example, eating too many sugary foods attracts bacteria that cause problems, and overly acidic ingredients can erode your enamel prematurely.

Meanwhile, filling up on dairy products that contain calcium can strengthen teeth and bones, and many citrus fruits and leafy greens have high vitamin C content to support your connective tissues.

Safeguard Your Smile

Athletes and others who participate in close-contact sports or activities are prone to sustaining injuries that can damage or dislodge their teeth. Also, many patients subconsciously grind their teeth at night due to a condition known as bruxism, which can wear their teeth down prematurely or cause chips, cracks, or fractures.

Dr. Vo can provide a customized nightguard or mouthguard to create a layer of cushion that protect your teeth from the force of constantly clenching or being knocked in the face.

Try Not To Use Your Teeth As Tools

Have you ever opened a letter of plastic packaging with your teeth? This seems like a quick and convenient solution, but it can cut your gums or harm your enamel, which doesn’t regenerate on its own.

Taking a few extra moments to locate the necessary tool for the job, like a pair of scissors, letter opener, or box cutter, can potentially save your smile!

Dental Emergency FAQs

What Does Throbbing Tooth Pain Mean?

Severe, throbbing tooth pain is probably a sign of an advanced infection within the tooth. In cases like these, large cracks or cavities can expose the pulp tissue to the air and the oral environment, and this can cause miserable pain if the nerve ending inside the tooth becomes infected. In cases like these, root canal treatment is usually the best way to eliminate the infection, but teeth that cannot be saved will have to be extracted and replaced with an appropriate restoration.

However, this issue can also be the result of chronic grinding and clenching. If this is the case, we will probably recommend wearing a customized nightguard from our office.

Will a Toothache Go Away on Its Own?

Toothaches can also be caused by issues such as temporary inflammation of the gum tissue, so the pain may go away if the irritation resolves itself. However, the sudden disappearance of a severe toothache may be due to the infection killing the nerve inside the tooth. In cases like these, the infection will go on even if no pain is present, so it’s crucial to visit our office immediately.

Can I Pop a Dental Abscess on My Own?

Popping a dental abscess on your own can expose the wound to further infection, so never attempt to do this. Instead, contact our office as soon as you notice a severely painful bump on your gums so we can address the issue in a sanitary and effective manner. Neglecting to have the issue treated can allow to the infection to spread to other areas of the mouth and possibly the other parts of the body as well.

Toothaches

Is My Toothache a Dental Emergency? Whether or not an emergency visit is required depends largely on the symptoms you’re experiencing. For example, if you feel a sharp, stabbing pain when you bite down or there is a pimple-like bump on your gums, we do recommend scheduling a same-day appointment.

How You Should Handle a Toothache Until your appointment at our Grand Prairie dental office, we recommend doing what you can to protect your tooth from further harm, like not chewing on that side of your mouth. You can also take OTC pain medication to alleviate soreness, sensitivity, and inflammation in the short term (just make sure to follow the directions on the label).

How We Treat Toothaches In order to determine the answer, we need to complete a comprehensive visual exam and take the necessary X-rays. From there, we can determine what’s causing your discomfort as well as the best way to address it. Sometimes, the answer is a tooth-colored filling. Other times, a root canal is needed.

Chipped Teeth

Is a Chipped Tooth a Dental Emergency? Even if the chip is relatively minor and you aren’t in pain, we recommend calling us to schedule an appointment. After all, the tooth could be at risk of breaking or developing an infection, and we want to intervene before either happens.

How You Should Handle a Chipped Tooth There are a few steps we recommend taking, starting with rinsing your mouth with clean, room-temperature water and putting any broken pieces of your tooth in a small container for safekeeping. It’s also a good idea to place some dental wax or sugar-free gum over the broken edge to protect your tongue, lips, and the insides of your cheeks.

How We Treat Chipped Teeth There are several ways we can restore the missing structure, including with dental bonding or a custom veneer. Ultimately, the treatment that’s best for you will depend on a few factors, including the size of the chip and your budget.

Cracked Tooth

Is a Cracked Tooth a Dental Emergency? Yes. In fact, even if the damage isn’t visible, but you’re dealing with considerable sensitivity or discomfort, we recommend scheduling an appointment with our emergency dentist in Grand Prairie, Dr. Anh Vo.

How You Should Handle a Cracked Tooth It’s of the utmost importance that you do what you can to protect your tooth from further harm, like avoiding crunchy foods, not chewing on that side of your mouth, and rinsing with clean, room-temperature water periodically.

How We Treat Cracked Teeth First, Dr. Anh Vo needs to determine if your tooth can be saved. If it can’t, then he will numb your mouth, extract the tooth, and fill the open space with a dental bridge or implant. In short, even if the damage is severe, you don’t have to worry he can restore your complete, healthy, and confident smile.
