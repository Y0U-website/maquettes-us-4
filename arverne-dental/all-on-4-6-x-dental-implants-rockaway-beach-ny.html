Dental Implants vs. Dentures

If you are considering permanent tooth replacement, you may also be considering dentures. While dentures can also be an excellent choice, there are many additional benefits to all-on-four dental implants.

Removable dentures tend to be messier and less secure than dental implants. This means they won’t feel like your natural teeth, and you will have a more challenging time with certain foods. Dentures also don’t last as long. Even implant-supported dentures need to be replaced after 10-15 years .

How the All-On-4 Dental Implant Procedure Works

Step 1: Initial Consultation

We’ll start with an in-depth consultation where we will examine your teeth and your medical history. Then we will take some bone scans. These bone scans can help us determine if you are a candidate for dental implants and whether all-on-4, all-on-6, or all-on-x dental implants are right for you.

Step 2: Tooth Removal

Before placing your implants, we’ll need to remove your remaining teeth and treat any underlying gum disease. This allows you to get the best possible results and a healthier smile.

Step 3: Bone Grafting, if Needed

Sometimes, you may need a bone graft if there isn’t enough bone to support your all-on-4 dental implants or a sinus lift if there is insufficient space between your top jaw and sinuses. We will perform these procedures before the implant surgery.

Step 4: Placing the Implant

We typically use general anesthesia to reduce discomfort during the procedure. Then we will surgically insert the base of the implant into your jaw. These implants are usually made from zirconium oxide or titanium. Once we have placed the implant, we can attach the abutment, which will hold the permanent crowns.

Step 5: Creating Your Crowns

After we are done, we will send your scans to the lab so they can create crowns that match your original teeth. Each of our crowns is tailored to your exact needs.

Step 6: Attaching Your Crowns

It will take some time for your implants to heal. Once they are fully healed, we will bring you back for a follow-up appointment and attach your crowns to the abutment. This part of the process is usually quick with minimal discomfort.
