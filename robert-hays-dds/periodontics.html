Diagnosis of Periodontal Disease

Diagnosing periodontal disease requires a comprehensive evaluation by our skilled dentist in Citrus Heights, CA . Key components of the diagnostic process may include:

A thorough clinical examination involves assessing the health of the gums, teeth, and supporting structures, including probing depths, gum recession, tooth mobility, and plaque and calculus buildup.

Radiographic imaging, such as bitewing and panoramic X-rays, provides valuable insight into the extent of bone loss, periodontal pockets, and other signs of periodontal disease not visible during clinical examination.

Periodontal probing involves measuring the depth of gum pockets around each tooth using a periodontal probe. Deeper pocket depths may indicate the presence of periodontal disease and the extent of bone loss.

Examining the gum tissue for signs of inflammation, bleeding upon probing, gum recession, and other abnormalities helps identify early signs of periodontal disease.

Gum Disease Treatment

Gum disease treatment aims to control infection, reduce inflammation, restore the health and function of the gums and supporting structures of the teeth. Treatment modalities may vary depending on the severity and extent of periodontal disease but often involve a combination of non-surgical and surgical interventions tailored to the individual’s needs.

Scaling and Root Planing (Deep Cleaning)

Scaling and root planing is a non-surgical procedure performed to remove plaque, tartar, and bacterial toxins from the surfaces of the teeth and root surfaces below the gumline. This deep cleaning helps eliminate bacterial pathogens and promotes gum reattachment to the tooth roots.

Antimicrobial agents such as mouth rinses, gels, or local antibiotics may be used to reduce bacterial load and inhibit the growth of periodontal pathogens.

Patient education on proper oral hygiene practices, including brushing, flossing, and interdental cleaning, is essential for controlling periodontal disease and preventing recurrence. Additionally, lifestyle factors such as smoking cessation, stress management, and dietary modifications may be recommended to optimize treatment outcomes.

In cases of advanced periodontal disease or inadequate response to non-surgical therapy, surgical intervention may be necessary to access and remove diseased tissue, reshape the gums, and restore periodontal health.

Bone grafting procedures may be performed to regenerate lost bone tissue and support the reattachment of teeth following periodontal disease-related bone loss.

Gum grafting techniques, such as connective tissue or free gingival grafts, may augment thin or receding gum tissues and improve aesthetics and function.
