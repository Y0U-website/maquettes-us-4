Root Canal Treatment

Root canal treatment in Hurricane UT , is necessary when the soft tissue inside your tooth becomes inflamed or infected. This can happen due to deep decay, repeated dental procedures on a tooth, or large fillings. If left untreated, the infection can spread to the bone around the tooth and cause an abscess.

During a root canal at MountainView Dental, our dentist will remove the infected pulp and clean the inside of the tooth. They will then fill the space with a rubber-like material and seal it to prevent further infection. Root canal treatment in Hurricane, UT, can save your natural tooth and prevent the need for extraction. They can also help relieve pain caused by an infected tooth.

Common Signs You Need a Root Canal in Hurricane UT

  • Heightened sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures can indicate pulp inflammation even after the stimulus is removed.
  • Swelling and tenderness in the gum tissue around the affected tooth may signal an underlying issue, especially if it’s accompanied by discomfort.
  • The development of a pimple-like bump on the gum, often filled with pus, is a clear indication of a dental abscess, which requires immediate attention.
  • Darkening or discoloration of the affected tooth can occur when the tooth’s nerve is damaged or dead.
  • A tooth that becomes loose or exhibits mobility may be compromised due to pulp infection and weakened support structures.
  • Bad breath, an unpleasant taste in the mouth, or oral odor can be signs of an underlying infection within the tooth.
  • In severe cases, when the infection spreads beyond the tooth, patients may experience a fever or a general feeling of illness.

The Procedure for Root Canal Treatment

A root canal is a procedure that removes infected pulp from a tooth’s root and replaces it with an artificial filling. The tooth is then sealed to prevent future infection. This treatment can save your natural tooth and prevent the need for extraction.

Our dentist in Hurricane, UT , will first numb the area with a local anesthetic. Then, our dentist will create a small opening in your tooth to access and remove the infected tissue. Next, the dentist will remove the diseased or dead pulp and clean the inside of the tooth. Once the tooth is clean, it will be filled with gutta-percha. Finally, the tooth will be sealed with a temporary or permanent filling.

After the procedure, you may experience some discomfort for a day or two. Over-the-counter pain medications can help manage any pain. Following your dentist’s instructions carefully after the procedure ensures a successful recovery.
