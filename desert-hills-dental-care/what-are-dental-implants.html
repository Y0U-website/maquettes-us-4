The Types of Dental Implants

Endosteal Dental Implants

The most common type of dental implant is endosteal. With endosteal dental implants , The post gets inserted directly into the jawbone and is used for patients who have enough bone structure to support it.

There are various dental implant treatments available today. However, each dental office offers different options. To learn more about the options offered at our Farmington practice, contact our dentist today.

Single-Tooth Dental Implants

Single-tooth dental implants completely replace one lost tooth from root to crown. It involves the placement of a titanium implant into the jawbone that serves as the foundation for a custom-made prosthetic tooth. In most cases, patients receive a dental crown as the visible portion of their implant treatment. Single-tooth dental implant treatment provides patients with a permanent solution for missing or damaged teeth.

All-On-4

All-on-4 dental implants are full-arch replacement options. It involves placing four dental implants in the jaw and securing a full arch of prosthetic teeth to them. The All-on-4 technique is less invasive and can be completed in one day. All-on-4 implants are a good option for those who have lower bone density, as it allows for the restoration of a full arch of teeth while using fewer implant posts.

Implant-Retained Bridges

Implant-retained bridges are excellent options for replacing multiple teeth. A dental bridge is anchored to the jaw using dental implants. The bridge is typically made up of two or more artificial teeth that get connected and attached to the implant abutments. The number of posts needed will vary depending on the size of the bridge.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Implant-supported dentures are removable appliances anchored to the jaw using dental implants. They provide a more secure fit and eliminate the need for any messy adhesives that traditional dentures typically need. Implant-supported dentures are also a more stable option than traditional dentures and can improve overall oral health by providing stimulation to the jaw bone.

Mini Dental Implants

Mini dental implants are a smaller version of traditional implants. They’re a less invasive implant option typically used to replace a single tooth or stabilize a loose denture. They shorten the healing process and have a lower cost. However, they may not be suitable for patients who don’t have adequate bone density.

Immediate-Load Same Day Dental Implants

Immediate-load dental implants , also known as same-day implants, are done in one appointment. With immediate-load implants, the implant and prosthetic tooth are placed at the same time, allowing the patient to leave the dental office with a fully functional replacement tooth after just one dental implant procedure.

Zygomatic Dental Implants

Zygomatic dental implants can restore lost teeth for patients who don’t have enough jawbone density to support a traditional endosteal implant. While zygomatic implants are still in use, they’re less common today and aren’t utilized by our Farmington dentists.

Subperiosteal Dental Implants

Subperiosteal dental implants sit on top of the jawbone but under the gum tissue. Subperiosteal implants are rarely ever used due to modern technological advancements in the field of implant dentistry.

Understanding the Dental Implant Procedure

The dental implant procedure at Desert Hills Dental Care is a multi-step process designed to ensure a stable and natural-looking tooth replacement. It typically begins with a thorough consultation and examination, including X-rays and 3D scans, to assess your jawbone density and overall oral health. This allows our Farmington dentists to create a personalized treatment plan tailored to your specific needs.

The initial surgical phase involves placing the titanium implant post directly into the jawbone. This post acts as the artificial tooth root. After a healing period, during which osseointegration occurs, a connector piece called an abutment is attached to the implant. Finally, a custom-made dental crown, bridge, or denture is securely attached to the abutment, completing your restored smile.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants offer a multitude of advantages over traditional tooth replacement methods, significantly improving both oral health and overall quality of life. One of the primary benefits is their ability to preserve jawbone density, preventing the common issue of bone loss that occurs after tooth extraction. This preservation helps maintain facial structure and prevents a sunken appearance.

Beyond bone health, dental implants provide unparalleled stability and comfort, functioning just like natural teeth. This means you can eat, speak, and smile with confidence, without worrying about slippage or discomfort. They also eliminate the need for adhesives and do not require altering adjacent healthy teeth, as bridges do, making them a more conservative and long-lasting solution.

Dental Implant Candidacy: Are You a Candidate?

Determining if you are a good candidate for dental implants involves evaluating several key factors related to your oral and general health. A strong, healthy jawbone is crucial for successful osseointegration, the process where the implant fuses with the bone. Our Farmington dentists will assess your bone density through imaging to ensure adequate support for the implant.

Beyond bone health, good oral hygiene is essential for the long-term success of dental implants. Patients with untreated gum disease or other oral health issues may need to address these conditions before proceeding with implant treatment. Additionally, overall health conditions that affect healing, such as uncontrolled diabetes or certain autoimmune diseases, will be considered during your consultation.

Maintaining Your Dental Implants for Longevity

To ensure your dental implants last for many years, or even a lifetime, a consistent and effective oral hygiene routine is paramount. Just like your natural teeth, implants require daily care to prevent the buildup of plaque and bacteria, which can lead to complications like peri-implantitis, an inflammation of the gums around the implant.

This routine should include brushing twice a day with a soft-bristled toothbrush and non-abrasive toothpaste, as well as flossing daily to clean between the implant and any adjacent teeth. Regular professional dental cleanings and check-ups at Desert Hills Dental Care are also vital. These appointments allow our team to monitor the health of your implants, gums, and jawbone, and to address any potential issues early on.
