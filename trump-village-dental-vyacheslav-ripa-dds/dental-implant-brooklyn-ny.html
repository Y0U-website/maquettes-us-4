Dental Implants Vs Teeth Implants Explained

A dental implant is a titanium post that replaces a missing tooth root, topped with a custom crown once the jawbone fuses around it. Teeth implants and dental implants are the same treatment, just two different names patients use for the same search.

Dr. Ripa plans and places every implant in the office, so patients across Brooklyn and NYC don’t need to track down a separate oral surgeon. Cases involving a tooth that’s still salvageable often connect back to root canal therapy instead, since saving the original tooth is always the first option we check.

Some patients come in already sure they want an implant. Others just know a tooth has been missing for a while and aren’t sure what their options even are anymore. Either way, an empty socket doesn’t stay neutral, the jawbone underneath starts thinning within months, and neighboring teeth begin drifting into the space.

Left alone long enough, that bone loss can make a simple single-implant case turn into one that needs grafting first. When several teeth in a row need replacing instead of just one, we also walk patients through dentures as an alternative, so the choice is based on your mouth, not a sales pitch.

Types of Dental Implants We Offer

Single Tooth Dental Implants

One missing tooth, one titanium post, one custom crown shaped and shaded to match what’s already in your mouth. This is what most people mean when they search dental implants, and it’s our most common case.

Implant-Supported Bridges

For a few missing teeth in a row, an implant-supported bridge replaces them without grinding down the healthy teeth on either side, which is what a traditional bridge requires.

Full Arch Implant Restoration

When most or all of the teeth on the top or bottom are gone, a full arch of replacement teeth can be secured with as few as four to six implant posts, instead of a removable denture.

Bone Grafting for Implants

If the jawbone has thinned after years without a tooth in place, a bone graft rebuilds enough thickness first, so the implant has something solid to fuse to.

The Dental Implant Process, Step by Step

Consultation Scan

A full exam and CT scan map your bone density and mark the exact spot for the implant before anything is decided.

Implant Placement

The titanium post goes in under local anesthesia, in-office, typically in a single visit. For qualifying patients, we also offer same-day dental implants in Brooklyn , walking out with a temporary crown attached instead of a gap during healing.

Healing Osseo integration

Your jawbone fuses to the post over roughly 3 to 6 months, the step that gives the implant its lifelong stability.

Abutment Crown

Once healed, we attach the abutment and a custom crown matched to your natural teeth. Most cases finish in 4 to 8 months total.

Dental Implant FAQs: Cost, Pain Timeline

What’s the difference between a dental implant and teeth implants?

Nothing, really. Teeth implants is just the way a lot of patients phrase it when they search online. Dental implants are the clinical term. Both describe the same titanium post and crown that replaces a missing tooth.

How much do dental implants cost in Brooklyn?

It depends on how many teeth need replacing and whether bone grafting comes first. You’ll get the exact number in writing after your scan, and we accept CareCredit along with most PPO and Medicaid plans, so affordable dental implants aren’t just a marketing line here. For a full breakdown of what drives the price, see what dental implants cost in Brooklyn without insurance .

How long does the full dental implant process take?

The titanium post is placed first, then it fuses to your jawbone over 3 to 6 months, depending on bone density and healing speed. After that, the abutment and final crown go on. Start to finish, most cases run 4 to 8 months.

Are dental implants painful?

No, and this is where most of the fear comes from what people imagine, not the actual procedure. You’re fully numb during placement, and most patients only need over-the-counter pain relief for a day or two afterward.

Can I get a dental implant if I’ve lost bone in my jaw?

Yes, in most cases. A bone graft rebuilds enough thickness and strength for the implant to fuse to. We check this during your CT scan consultation, so you know upfront whether grafting is part of your plan.
