Why Choose Dental Implants?

Dental implants are widely regarded as the gold standard in tooth replacement because they are the only restoration that replaces both the visible tooth and the root beneath it. Research published by the National Institutes of Health found that dental implants have an overall failure rate of just 2.21 % across large patient populations — making them one of the most predictably successful procedures in modern dentistry. No other tooth replacement option matches that level of clinical reliability.

Types of Dental Implants at Peak City Family Dentistry

Not every tooth loss situation is the same, and neither is every implant solution. Our team evaluates your bone density, the number and location of missing teeth, and your overall oral health before recommending an approach. The goal is always the option that will serve your long-term health and function best.

Single Tooth Implants

For patients missing one tooth, a single titanium post is placed into the jawbone and topped with a custom-crafted crown. This delivers a result that is virtually indistinguishable from a natural tooth — and keeps the surrounding bone healthy.

Implant-Supported Bridges

When two or more consecutive teeth are missing, an implant-supported bridge fills the gap without relying on neighboring natural teeth. No healthy teeth need to be reshaped — the implants carry the entire load.

Implant-Supported Dentures

For patients missing most or all teeth on one arch, implant-supported dentures offer a dramatically more stable alternative to removable dentures — eliminating slippage, adhesives, and dietary restrictions.

Full-Arch Dental Implants

A permanent, non-removable solution using strategically placed implants to anchor a complete row of fixed prosthetic teeth. Dr. CJ uses computer-guided planning to optimize placement for maximum bone support and long-term stability.

The Dental Implant Process at Peak City Family Dentistry

Getting dental implants is a multi-stage process, and each step plays a specific role in ensuring a result that is stable, natural-looking, and built to last.

Comprehensive oral health evaluation, medical history review, and 3D imaging to assess bone volume and treatment site anatomy.

Personalized plan mapping every stage from placement through final restoration. Bone grafting, if needed, is outlined at this stage.

Titanium post placed using computer-guided surgical planning — exact position, angle, and depth mapped before the procedure begins. Sedation available.

Over 3–6 months, the implant fuses with surrounding jawbone. This creates the permanent foundation the final restoration will rely on.

A small connector piece attaches to the top of the implant, serving as the structural link to the final crown, bridge, or denture.

Your custom prosthetic is attached to the abutment. The result: a restored tooth that looks, feels, and functions like the one you lost.

Are You a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most healthy adults who have experienced tooth loss are candidates for dental implants. Ideal candidates have healthy gum tissue, adequate jawbone density to support the implant post, and no uncontrolled systemic conditions that would impair healing. Patients who smoke are strongly encouraged to quit before treatment, as tobacco use interferes with osseointegration and increases the risk of complications over time.

During your consultation, our team conducts a thorough evaluation to determine whether implants are the right fit. If bone volume is insufficient, we may recommend bone grafting before implant placement — a procedure that encourages new bone growth and can bring patients who were previously ineligible back into candidacy. Our team takes the time to explore every available option before concluding implants are not the right path forward.

What If I’ve Been Missing Teeth for a Long Time?

Prolonged tooth loss almost always results in some degree of jawbone deterioration. Without a root to stimulate it, the bone gradually resorbs — which can make initial implant placement more complicated, but does not make implants impossible. A bone graft builds the jawbone back up using donor or synthetic bone material, creating a stable foundation for the implant. Dr. CJ uses 3D digital imaging to assess your existing bone volume precisely before recommending whether grafting is needed and, if so, how to approach it.

Caring for Your Dental Implants

One of the most practical advantages of dental implants is how straightforward they are to maintain. Unlike removable dentures, implants require no adhesives, no soaking, and no nightly removal. The basics of good implant care are the same as the basics of good oral hygiene for natural teeth: brush at least twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush, floss carefully around the implant each day, and visit our office regularly for professional cleanings and monitoring.

With consistent home care and routine checkups, dental implants can last a lifetime. Our general dentistry team monitors the health of your implants at every routine visit and can identify any early signs of concern before they become problems. For patients interested in enhancing other aspects of their smile alongside implant treatment, options like porcelain veneers or Invisalign clear aligners can be incorporated into a comprehensive smile plan.
