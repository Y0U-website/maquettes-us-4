What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are quickly becoming the most popular way to replace missing teeth, and once you learn a little bit about them, it’s not hard to see why. A dental implant is a small titanium post that is inserted into the jawbone to act as a replacement root for a lost tooth. Within three to six months, the jawbone actually grows around the implant until they are essentially the same structure. Then, a metal fixture called an abutment is placed atop the implant. The abutment will secure your custom-made crown, bridge, or denture (your restoration) into place.

Other tooth replacements, like dentures and bridges, just sit on top of the gums, but dental implants go one step further by replacing the tooth’s root as well as its visible crown. This allows them to look, feel, and function almost exactly like your original teeth. That’s why Dr. Martin recommends dental implants to the majority of eligible patients.

Dental Implant Surgery

Dr. Martin will work alongside one of our trusted local surgeons to ensure that your implant placement surgery goes smoothly and efficiently. During the procedure, your team will first use a local anesthetic to numb the areas of your mouth that they’ll be placing the metal posts in. You might even receive sedation dentistry to further minimize discomfort as well as to help you remain as relaxed as possible.

Once you’re comfortable, your surgeon will make tiny incisions in the gum line to reach the jawbone where they’ll place the dental implants. Then, they’ll suture the gums closed around the posts before placing protective caps on them to keep them safe while you heal.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

The next portion of the treatment will take three to six months to complete due to osseointegration. This is the essential process where the dental implants fuse with your jawbone and become one with your facial structure. Once completed, you’ll have the stability needed to support your restorations for many years to come. After you’ve recovered properly, you’ll return to our office to have your abutments placed small metal connector pieces that are designed to link your restoration/prosthetic to your implants.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

You might require some time for your gums to heal after getting your abutments. Meanwhile, we’ll use the dental impressions we took of your smile to fabricate your new teeth. After you’ve finally recovered, you’ll come back to our office to have your new dental crown, bridge, or denture fitted onto your metal posts. If your bite is correct and you’re happy with your results, you’ll be free to show off your pearly whites for all the world to see.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Pretty much any healthy adult can get dental implants. Here are the things that we consider when determining if someone makes a good candidate:

If you don’t initially make a good candidate, you shouldn’t be discouraged. Many patients need to complete preliminary treatments, like tooth extractions, gum disease therapy, or bone grafting before they can get dental implants. We will help you to schedule these preparatory procedures to get you on track. Once you make a good candidate, we will put together a plan that’s right for you.

  • Good overall health: Placing dental implants is a surgical procedure, so you will need to be well enough to undergo a minor surgery.
  • Great oral health: Issues like cavities and gum disease can potentially cause dental implants to fail, so they’ll need to be addressed first.
  • Adequate jawbone density: Your jawbone must be strong enough to support dental implants for your treatment to be a success.

Missing One Tooth

Replacing a single missing tooth with a dental implant is fairly straightforward. We vertically insert the titanium post within the empty space and top it with a personalized dental crown. Compared to a dental bridge, which requires that two adjacent teeth are filed down, the approach is much less invasive.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Instead of replacing each tooth with its own implant, a small number of posts can anchor a bridge (if the missing teeth are consecutive) or a partial denture (if they’re located throughout an arch). This solution is not only more cost-effective, but it also creates more stability compared to traditional bridges and partials.

Missing All Teeth

Even full-arch tooth loss can be undone with the help of dental implants. By positioning just four to six implants at strategic points along the jaw, we can attach a full denture that sits in place much more securely than traditional dentures. Since implant dentures don’t slip around, they let you eat your favorite foods as effortlessly as you did before tooth loss.

Types of Dental Implants

There are various factors that influence the cost of the treatment, like how many teeth you are replacing. A single unit implant is more affordable than an implant-retained denture, but it is not cost-effective to replace each tooth individually. The amount you pay is largely determined by the type of restoration you need, such as a crown, bridge, or denture. Besides the restoration, you will also need to pay for the abutments and posts.

Stages of Dental Implant Treatment

The process to replace teeth with dental implants occurs in various stages, each of which has separate costs. It can take several months to complete each step in your journey, giving you more time to pay. You will not need to pay for the entire treatment upfront, but you will need to pay for:

  • Initial Consultation: Besides the dental office fees, you will need digital imaging and a CT scan.
  • Additional Treatments: It is common to need tooth extractions, bone grafting, a sinus lift, or another procedure before placing your implants.
  • Placement Surgery: Sedation or anesthesia is used while your posts are surgically placed into your jawbone.
  • Abutments & Restorations: You need to pay for an abutment to be attached to each post to connect your restoration. The restoration is often the most expensive part of your treatment.

Bone Grafting

The long-term success of your new smile relies on your jawbone’s ability to support the implant posts. If you’ve lost teeth to gum disease or you’ve lived with an incomplete smile for a while, you may have decreased density in your jaw. As a result, you may not be a candidate for dental implants, but that doesn’t mean you can’t become one.

Bone grafting is a surgical procedure to enhance the thickness of your jawbone. While under sedation or anesthesia, your gum tissue is opened to give access to your jawbone. A small piece of bone taken from another site or a donor is transferred to the treatment area. Your gum tissue is sutured closed to allow the healing process to begin. It can take several weeks for the graft to integrate with your bone, but it’s time well spent to ensure your jaw can support the posts long-term.

Sinus Lifts

If you’ve lost bone in your upper jaw, there may not be enough space by the maxillary sinuses to insert the posts. Also known as a sinus augmentation, the procedure adds bone to the areas by your premolars and molars.

Sedation or anesthesia is used to ensure your comfort as your gum tissue is opened to allow a small oval to be cut into your bone. Your sinus membrane is lifted upward, and the graft material is inserted into the space. Your gum tissue is sutured closed to allow the area to heal, which can take four to 10 months.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants have a very high success rate, but at the end of the day, their longevity largely depends on the quality of the care that you give them. The trick is to take a proactive approach; it is easier to avoid problems with your dental implants than it is to treat them. Our team can show you the steps you’ll need to take to keep your implants safe and firmly in place for the rest of your life.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Dental implants are not vulnerable to cavities, but the gum and bone tissue holding them in place can still be affected by gum disease. An untreated infection could eventually lead to implant failure. As such, it’s important to maintain a good oral hygiene routine after your dental implants have been placed. Brush at least two times every day for at least two minutes. You should floss on a regular basis as well, and you may want to consider rinsing with an ADA-approved mouthwash to kill as many harmful bacteria in your mouth as possible.

Eat a Healthy Diet

You won’t put your implants in any danger just by enjoying a piece of cake every once in a while, but if your diet is almost nothing but sugar and starch, it can very easily lead to oral health issues that put your new teeth at risk. Try to eat fewer sweets and chips to protect your implants; you can replace them with fruits, leafy vegetables, fat-free dairy products, and other foods that help improve your overall oral health.

Break Bad Habits

Dental implants are very strong, but they can still break or become worn down over time. You need to protect them by avoiding bad oral habits that can damage dental implants as well as natural tooth enamel. Biting your nails, opening packaging with your teeth, and crunching ice are all examples of habits that put dental implants at risk. One way to break these habits is to keep sugar-free gum in your pocket so that you have something safe to chew on if the urge strikes you.

Protect Your Dental Implants

If you play sports of any kind, you may have already invested in an athletic mouthguard. If not, you should get one as soon as possible so that you can keep your implants safe while you’re out on the field or on the court. A mouthguard can also protect your dental implants at night if you suffer from bruxism (unconscious grinding and clenching).

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

You should see your implant dentist every six months for regular checkups and cleanings. By reviewing your implant posts and restorations on a regular basis, we can find and address small issues before they turn into more complex, costly problems. Am I Eligible for Dental Implants?

In most cases, patients with missing teeth are eligible for dental implants. However, the only way to know for sure is to complete a dental implant consultation. During this consultation, the dentist will examine your jawbone density and overall health of your teeth and gum tissue. If sufficient bone is present and you have good overall health, you can move onto the next steps of planning your implant placement.

How Long Does It Take to Get Dental Implants?

The length of treatment can vary depending on your needs. For example, patients who don’t need preparatory treatments performed (i.e., gum disease therapy, tooth extractions, bone grafts, etc.) will expect a shorter treatment time. The healing stage can take anywhere from three to six months depending on where the implant was placed and your dedication to keeping the area clean during the healing phase.

Are Dental Implants Safe?

When implants are placed by a professional oral surgeon or implant dentist in Grapevine and the patient has undergone a proper implant consultation, they are 100 % safe. If the dentist doesn’t believe you are ready for implants, he’ll go over treatments that can increase your jawbone’s density, such as a bone graft.

What Can I Eat with Dental Implants?

Dental implants are designed to function exactly like natural teeth. In many ways, implants are even stronger, so chewing your favorite foods like steak or apples is easy and straightforward. Just make sure to keep it clean by brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and visiting the dentist every six months.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

Dental implants are known for their durability and longevity. Success rates after a 10-year period are as high as 98 %. In most cases, dental implants can last anywhere from 25 years to a lifetime, assuming they’re properly cared for and the patient discontinues the use of all tobacco products.
