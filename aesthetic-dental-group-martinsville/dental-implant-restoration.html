SUBPERIOSTEAL IMPLANTS Subperiosteal implants are a specialized type of dental implant. Instead of being placed within the jawbone, these implants are positioned on top of the jawbone but underneath the gum tissue. They are a suitable option for individuals with minimal jawbone height, making them unable to support traditional endosteal implants.

Our experienced dental team at Aesthetic Dental Group will assess your case, including your jawbone health, and recommend the most suitable implant type for your needs. We prioritize your comfort, safety, and the long-term success of your dental implants.

  • Why choose endosteal implants? The key advantage of endosteal implants lies in their stability and durability. They provide a solid base for your new teeth, ensuring they stay firmly in place while mimicking the look and feel of natural teeth.
  • When are subperiosteal implants used? Subperiosteal implants are typically chosen when a patient has insufficient jawbone height for endosteal implants and is not a candidate for bone grafting. These implants provide a viable solution for those seeking dental restoration without extensive bone augmentation.
  • Why choose subperiosteal implants? The primary advantage of subperiosteal implants is their ability to address the challenge of limited jawbone height. They offer a secure foundation for your replacement teeth without the need for invasive bone grafting procedures.

Why Choose Dental Implants?

Dental implants are not just a cosmetic enhancement; they are the foundation for both your smile and oral health. Let’s address some common questions:

What Are They? Dental implants aren’t replacement teeth; they are replacement tooth roots. They provide a sturdy foundation for permanent or removable replacement teeth designed to seamlessly match your natural smile.

Do They Work Well? Most patients experience an impressive 95 percent success rate with dental implants. Proper care, including regular flossing, mouth rinses, check-ups, and cleanings, can help your implants last a lifetime.

Why Get Them at All? Dental implants offer numerous advantages, including improved appearance, enhanced confidence, reduced embarrassment (no more removing dentures in front of others), and a more enjoyable eating experience. Their comfort factor is unparalleled, as they feel just like your natural teeth. Additionally, dental implants help prevent further deterioration of your remaining teeth and contribute to overall jaw health.

Do They Improve Speech? We often don’t associate teeth with speech, but ill-fitting dentures can impact your ability to speak clearly. Dental implants significantly enhance your speech patterns, eliminating concerns about slipping dentures and reducing embarrassment.

  • Acute Illness : Patients with acute illnesses may need to postpone dental implant surgery until they have fully recovered.
  • Uncontrollable Metabolic Disease : Certain metabolic diseases may impact the body’s ability to heal effectively, making implant surgery riskier.
  • Bone or Soft Tissue Disease or Infection : Existing bone or soft tissue diseases or infections may need to be resolved before proceeding with implant surgery.

  • HIV, diabetes, osteoporosis, and AIDS : These medical conditions can affect the body’s ability to heal and may require a personalized assessment before proceeding with implants.

Risks and Potential Complications

At Aesthetic Dental Group, your safety and well-being are our top priorities. While dental implant procedures are generally safe and successful, it’s essential to be aware of potential risks and complications. Here are some key points to consider:

  • Nerve Damage : There’s a small risk of altered sensation in the surgical area.
  • Incision Issues : Sometimes, there may be an opening of the incision following surgery.
  • Implant Movement : In rare cases, the implant may shift.
  • Implant Exposure : The implant might become visible above the gumline.
  • Infection : As with any other surgery, there’s a slight risk of infection around the implant site.
  • Signs of Unsuccessful Implant : Watch for excessive implant mobility, secretions, pain upon tapping, or rapid bone loss.

Artificial Teeth Require the Same Care Your artificial teeth, supported by dental implants, should be cared for in the same way as your natural teeth. Brushing twice a day and flossing daily are fundamental habits to preserve your oral health and the appearance of your smile.
