Periodontal scaling and root planing, also known as a deep cleaning, is a non-surgical treatment used to treat gum disease (periodontitis) in its early stages. The procedure involves two key steps: Scaling : This step involves removing plaque and tartar (hardened plaque) from the surfaces of your teeth, particularly below the gumline.
  • Root Planing : After scaling, the tooth roots are smoothed out to prevent future buildup of plaque and tartar, which helps the gums heal and reattach to the teeth.

Why Is Scaling & Root Planing Necessary?

Gum disease occurs when plaque buildup leads to infection in the gums, causing inflammation and tissue damage. If left untreated, this infection can progress, leading to more serious oral health issues, including:

Periodontal scaling and root planing is a proactive treatment that helps stop the progression of gum disease, allowing the gums to heal and reducing the risk of more severe complications.

  • Gum recession : The gums begin to pull away from the teeth, exposing the roots.
  • Bone loss : Advanced gum disease can erode the bone that supports your teeth, leading to tooth loss.
  • Tooth loss : Without treatment, the teeth may become loose and eventually need to be extracted.

Signs You May Need Scaling & Root Planing

You may be a candidate for periodontal scaling and root planing if you are experiencing the following symptoms:

If these symptoms sound familiar, it’s important to seek treatment as soon as possible. Early intervention can help prevent more invasive procedures like gum surgery or tooth extraction.

  • Red, swollen, or bleeding gums .
  • Persistent bad breath .
  • Gums that are pulling away from the teeth .
  • Loose teeth or changes in your bite .
  • Pockets forming between your gums and teeth .

The Periodontal Scaling & Root Planing Process

At Pierce Family Dentistry , our team takes great care to ensure your scaling and root planing procedure is as comfortable as possible. Here’s what to expect during the treatment process:

1. Examination and Diagnosis

During your initial consultation, your dentist will examine your gums, teeth, and pockets using Dental X-rays and other diagnostic tools to assess the severity of the gum disease. If deep cleaning is recommended, the procedure will be scheduled.

2. Local Anesthesia for Comfort

3. Scaling and Root Planing

Using specialized dental tools, your dentist will carefully remove plaque and tartar from below the gumline (scaling). After the surfaces are clean, the roots of your teeth will be smoothed out to help the gums reattach and reduce the risk of future buildup (root planing).

4. Post-Treatment Care

Following the procedure, your dentist will provide detailed aftercare instructions. You may experience some gum tenderness and sensitivity for a few days, but this should subside as your gums begin to heal. Maintaining good oral hygiene and attending follow-up appointments are crucial for monitoring your progress.

Benefits of Scaling & Root Planing

Scaling and root planing offers several key benefits for patients with gum disease:

  • Improves Gum Health : The procedure removes bacteria and plaque buildup, allowing your gums to heal and reducing the risk of further damage.
  • Prevents Progression of Gum Disease : Scaling and root planing can stop gum disease from advancing to more severe stages, which could require surgical intervention.
  • Reduces Gum Inflammation : Treating the underlying cause of gum inflammation helps reduce redness, swelling, and bleeding.
  • Protects Tooth and Bone Health : By treating gum disease early, you can prevent tooth and bone loss that may result from untreated periodontitis.

How to Maintain Healthy Gums After Scaling & Root Planing

To maintain the results of your scaling and root planing procedure, it’s important to practice good oral hygiene at home:

If you have any questions or concerns about your gum health after the procedure, don’t hesitate to reach out to our team for guidance.

  • Brush your teeth twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.
  • Floss daily to remove plaque between your teeth.
  • Use an antimicrobial mouthwash to reduce bacteria in the mouth.
  • Schedule regular dental check-ups and professional cleanings to monitor your gum health.
