When Should My Child See a Pediatric Dentist? The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends that children visit the dentist by their first birthday or within 6 months of the eruption of their first tooth. Regular dental visits every six months help establish good oral health habits early on and allow us to monitor development, provide preventive care, and address any concerns before they become bigger problems.

  • Managing childhood dental anxiety
  • Specialized sedation techniques
  • Treating children with special needs
  • Early orthodontic assessments
  • Child psychology and behavior management

Why Are Baby Teeth Important?

Although baby teeth eventually fall out, they play a critical role in a child’s oral development. Healthy primary teeth:

By maintaining healthy baby teeth, we help set the stage for strong, healthy permanent teeth in the future.

  • Allow for comfortable eating and speaking
  • Hold space for permanent teeth to grow in correctly
  • Guide the proper development of the jaw and facial structures
  • Help prevent infections that could affect the growth of adult teeth

Preventing Cavities and Promoting Healthy Smiles

Cavities aren’t the only reason to visit the dentist! We focus on prevention through services such as:

Regular visits allow us to detect and address concerns early, ensuring that your child’s smile stays healthy for life.

  • Dental cleanings and fluoride treatments to strengthen enamel and prevent decay
  • Sealants to protect vulnerable chewing surfaces
  • Dietary guidance to promote tooth-friendly eating habits
  • Early orthodontic assessments to monitor proper growth and alignment
