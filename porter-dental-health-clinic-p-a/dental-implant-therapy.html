A dental implant includes a post placed into the jawbone that serves a role similar to that of a natural tooth root. As the implant integrates with the surrounding bone, it creates a stable foundation for the final restoration. The stimulation produced during normal chewing also helps preserve the bone in an area where a tooth has been lost.

Maintaining healthy bone is important because it supports remaining teeth and contributes to the natural contours of the face.

  • A natural-looking replacement for missing teeth
  • Greater stability when speaking and eating
  • Support for neighboring teeth by filling spaces in the smile
  • Stimulation that helps maintain jawbone health
  • Long-term durability with appropriate care

Are Dental Implants Right For You?

Dental implants may be an option for adults missing one or more teeth who have sufficient oral and general health to undergo implant treatment. The condition of the gums and jawbone, the location of the missing teeth, and certain health or lifestyle factors can all affect treatment planning.

Some patients may require additional procedures, such as bone grafting, before an implant can be placed. Dr. Porter or Dr. Purifoy can evaluate the teeth, gums, bite, and available bone to determine whether implants are appropriate and explain any preparatory treatment that may be needed.

The Dental Implant Process

Dental implant treatment usually takes place over several appointments because time is needed for the implant to integrate with the jawbone. The exact timeline varies according to the treatment plan and individual healing.

Implant Placement

The implant is surgically placed in the jawbone at the location of the missing tooth to support the future restoration. This post will eventually provide the foundation for the replacement tooth.

Healing And Osseointegration

The implant remains in place while the surrounding bone heals and integrates with it through osseointegration. This stage typically takes several months and is important for establishing a stable foundation for the final restoration.

Abutment Placement

Once healing and integration are complete, an abutment is placed on the implant to support the visible replacement tooth. This small component connects the implant post to the visible restoration.

Final Restoration

A custom crown or other restoration is attached to the implant. The restoration is designed to complement the surrounding teeth in shape, size, and appearance while restoring comfortable function.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

How Long Does Dental Implant Treatment Take?

The complete process often takes several months because the implant needs time to integrate with the jawbone before the final restoration is attached. Treatment time can vary depending on healing, the number of implants being placed, and whether additional procedures are needed.

Do Dental Implants Require Special Care?

Dental implants should be cared for with regular brushing, flossing, and professional dental visits. Good oral hygiene is important for keeping the gums and tissues surrounding the implant healthy.

Can Dental Implants Replace Multiple Missing Teeth?

Yes. Depending on a patient’s needs, dental implants can support a single crown, replace multiple teeth with an implant-supported restoration, or help stabilize a full-arch tooth replacement.

What Happens If There Is Not Enough Jawbone For An Implant?

Insufficient bone does not automatically rule out dental implant treatment. In some cases, bone grafting or another preparatory procedure may help create enough support for an implant. A dental evaluation is needed to determine the appropriate approach.
