What are Full-Arch Dental Implants?

These dental implants replace a full dental arch either the top or bottom teeth. They are surgically inserted in the jawbone, with artificial teeth placed on an extension of the posts.

Full arch implants typically consist of a set of about 4-8 dental implants. These may be fitted in one arch or both.

Several factors determine the type of prosthesis used to replace multiple teeth. It includes the patient’s remaining amount of jawbone, the amount of inter-occlusal space, and the patient’s wishes.

Types of Full-Arch Dental Implants

There are two main options: fixed and removable.

Choosing the right implant?supported prosthesis depends on many factors, and a thoughtful treatment plan is essential to getting the best long?term result. We will evaluate your oral health, your goals, and the condition of your jawbone before recommending the option that will serve you best.

When teeth have been missing for a long time, the jawbone in that area naturally begins to shrink. This happens because the bone is no longer being stimulated by tooth roots, so the body gradually resorbs and remodels it. The more bone that’s lost, the fewer prosthetic options may be available.

That’s why it’s important to make a plan early. Whether you need a single implant or a full?arch solution, addressing tooth loss sooner helps preserve bone and keeps more treatment choices open to you.

What to Expect During Your Procedure

Dr. Montgomery and Dr. Wolken give each patient an individual consultation and evaluation of their teeth and jaw, creating a customized prosthetic plan.

This process includes a comprehensive dental exam, assessing the condition of your teeth, searching for bone loss, taking X-rays and 3D images, and making digital models of your teeth and jaw.

Next, 4-8 implants will be placed into your jawbone for optimal support. Finally, your customized, high-quality, permanent full-arch solution can be attached, restoring your smile.

Afterward, we’ll invite you back for re?evaluation appointments as needed to monitor your bite, support your oral hygiene, and help you get the most out of your new smile.

Recovery Time After Full-Arch Dental Implants

During the two to six months after full-arch surgery, the implants and the bone bond together, forming anchors for the new teeth. Your Woodbury dental implant dentist will determine when it’s best to remove the temporary healing caps (or the temporary teeth) and attach the abutments. Following this, your gums will need sufficient time to heal. Typically, a couple of weeks is required following this procedure; however, it depends on your overall health and ability to heal quickly. The more dental issues you have before surgery, the more time may be needed to heal.

Possible pre- and post-surgery factors that may prolong recovery include:

  • Unhealthy eating choices
  • Any form of tobacco use
  • Poor daily oral hygiene
  • Getting an insufficient amount of quality sleep
  • Inability to follow your dentist’s instructions
  • Other underlying health issues

The Benefits of Full-Arch Implants vs. Traditional Dentures

  • Better oral health outcomes. Studies show full-arch implants are a highly predictable solution, even for patients with compromised periodontal (gum) health.
  • Eat and chew naturally. Patients consistently report greater comfort and stability than with conventional dentures, allowing them to enjoy the foods they love.
  • No shifting or slipping. The prosthesis locks permanently onto your implants, creating a stable, immovable restoration.
  • Low maintenance. No soaking, no removal just the same daily brushing and flossing routine you d use for natural teeth.
  • Preserved jawbone. Implants replace tooth roots, preventing the bone loss and facial recession that commonly occurs with conventional dentures over time.
  • Solutions for complex cases. Implant-supported prostheses can restore situations that conventional prostheses can t address.

What is the Cost of Full-Arch Dentistry?

Full-arch implant costs in Minnesota typically range from $5,000 to $25,000 per arch, depending on factors such as the extent of extractions, bone shaping, and whether a bone graft is needed. Advanced placement techniques can sometimes eliminate the need for grafting, reducing overall cost.

What may seem like a large sum upfront is often less expensive than reconstructing each tooth individually, and the results last far longer than traditional dentures. During your consultation, we ll walk you through a detailed cost breakdown so there are no surprises down the road.
