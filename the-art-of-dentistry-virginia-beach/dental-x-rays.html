Types Of Dental X-rays

Different types of dental X-rays provide different views of your teeth and surrounding structures. The type of dental imaging recommended depends on what your dentist needs to evaluate.

Bitewing X-rays

Bitewing X-rays capture the crowns of the upper and lower teeth in a single image and are commonly used to evaluate areas between teeth that may be difficult to see during a regular examination. They can help detect cavities between teeth and provide information about the supporting bone.

Periapical X-rays

Periapical X-rays capture an entire tooth, including the crown, root, and surrounding bone. They can help your dentist evaluate the area around a tooth’s root and investigate concerns such as infection, bone changes, or dental pain.

Panoramic X-rays

Panoramic X-rays provide a broad view of the teeth, upper and lower jaws, and surrounding structures in a single image. Depending on the patient’s needs, panoramic imaging may be useful for evaluating developing or impacted teeth, jaw structures, and other conditions that require a wider view.

Digital Dental Imaging

Digital dental X-rays use electronic sensors or digital imaging systems rather than traditional dental film. Digital images can be viewed and stored electronically, allowing your dental team to evaluate the images efficiently as part of your diagnosis and treatment planning.

Are Dental X-rays Safe?

Dental X-rays use a relatively low amount of radiation, and modern dental imaging is designed to obtain the diagnostic information needed while minimizing unnecessary exposure. The American Dental Association recommends that dental radiographs be ordered based on individual clinical needs and that practices use appropriate techniques to limit radiation exposure.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are dental x-rays used for?

Dental X-rays provide images of teeth, roots, bone, and surrounding structures that may not be visible during a regular dental examination. They can help dentists identify conditions such as tooth decay, infections, bone changes, impacted teeth, and other dental concerns.

How often should I get dental X-rays?

The appropriate frequency varies from patient to patient. Your dentist will consider your oral health, age, dental history, risk factors, symptoms, examination findings, and previous images when determining whether new X-rays are needed. There is no one-size-fits-all schedule.

Can dental X-rays detect cavities?

Yes. Dental X-rays can help identify cavities, including decay in areas between teeth that may be difficult to see during a visual examination.

Can a dental X-ray show an infection?

Dental X-rays can provide information about changes around a tooth’s root and surrounding bone that may be associated with infection. Your dentist will consider the X-ray findings along with your symptoms and clinical examination when making a diagnosis.

Do I need dental X-rays if I have no pain?

Not necessarily, but the absence of pain does not mean that imaging is never useful. Some dental conditions can develop without noticeable symptoms. Your dentist will determine whether an X-ray is appropriate based on your individual examination and risk factors.

Do dental X-rays hurt?

Dental X-rays are generally quick and noninvasive. Depending on the type of image being taken, a small sensor or positioning device may be placed in or around the mouth.
