Are Veneers Right for Your Smile?

Porcelain veneers are designed to improve the appearance of healthy front teeth affected by discoloration, small chips, minor gaps, uneven edges, or irregular shape. Many patients in Burlington Township choose veneers when whitening or minor cosmetic treatments cannot achieve the results they want.

Healthy teeth and gums are essential before placing veneers. If you have untreated decay, gum disease, or significant teeth grinding, those concerns should be addressed first. When a tooth requires more structural support than a veneer can provide, a dental crown may be the more appropriate treatment option.

During your consultation, we’ll evaluate your oral health, discuss your smile goals, and determine whether veneers are the best treatment for your needs. If another option would provide a better long-term outcome, we’ll explain your alternatives before recommending treatment.

Porcelain vs. Composite Veneers: Which Option Is Right for You?

Porcelain and composite veneers each offer unique advantages. They differ in durability, appearance, treatment time, and long-term maintenance. During your consultation, we’ll help you choose the option that best fits your smile goals, oral health, and budget.

Material Differences

Porcelain: Laboratory-crafted shells that closely mimic natural enamel. They offer outstanding stain resistance, exceptional durability, and highly natural aesthetics, making them the preferred option for many long-term smile makeovers.

Composite: Applied directly to the tooth during a single appointment, composite veneers are a more affordable option that requires less preparation. While they may need maintenance sooner than porcelain, they can be an excellent choice for selected cosmetic concerns.

For patients who want to preserve as much natural tooth structure as possible, minimal-preparation veneers may be appropriate in certain cases. However, they aren’t the right solution for everyone, particularly when more significant changes to tooth alignment or shape are needed.

Choosing the right material depends on your bite, oral health, cosmetic goals, and lifestyle. Dr. Dorfner will explain the benefits and limitations of each option so you can make an informed decision with confidence.

Veneers FAQ: Your Questions Answered

How long do porcelain veneers last?

Porcelain veneers typically last 10 to 15 years, and many last even longer with proper care. Their lifespan depends on factors such as your oral hygiene, bite, daily habits, and routine dental visits. Wearing a custom night guard if you grind your teeth can also help protect your veneers for years to come.

Do veneers ruin your teeth?

Traditional veneers do not ruin healthy teeth, but they do require removing a thin layer of enamel, making the treatment permanent. That’s why it’s important to confirm you’re a good candidate before moving forward. During your consultation, Dr. Dorfner will evaluate your teeth and discuss whether veneers are the best option for your smile.

Does getting veneers hurt?

Most patients are surprised by how comfortable the procedure is. Local anesthesia keeps the area numb during treatment, and any temporary sensitivity usually disappears after your permanent veneers are placed. If you feel nervous about dental procedures, we also offer conscious sedation to help you feel more relaxed.

Can veneers fix stained teeth that whitening won’t improve?

Yes. Veneers are an excellent option for covering deep stains that professional whitening cannot remove, including discoloration caused by certain medications, dental trauma, or natural enamel changes. Because porcelain is highly stain-resistant, your new smile can stay bright with proper care.

What if I grind my teeth at night?

Teeth grinding can place extra pressure on veneers and increase the risk of damage over time. Many patients with mild to moderate bruxism can still be good candidates, especially when wearing a custom night guard. If grinding is severe, Dr. Dorfner may recommend a different treatment approach to protect your long-term oral health.

Do I need veneers or crowns?

It depends on the condition of your tooth. Veneers improve the appearance of healthy teeth by covering only the front surface, while crowns provide full coverage for teeth that are weakened, broken, or heavily restored. During your consultation, we’ll recommend the treatment that best protects your oral health while achieving your cosmetic goals.

What types of veneers are available?

Depending on your needs, porcelain veneers, composite veneers, or minimal-preparation options may all be appropriate. Each offers different advantages in appearance, durability, cost, and tooth preparation. Dr. Dorfner will explain the pros and limitations of each option to help you choose the treatment that’s right for your smile.
