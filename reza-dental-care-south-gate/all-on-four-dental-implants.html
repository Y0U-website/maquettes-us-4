Am I A Candidate?

One of the most significant deciding factors for your all-on-four dental implant candidacy is your bone density.

To determine your candidacy, your dentist will have a three-dimensional x-ray taken of your jaw bone. This x-ray will provide them with the ability to examine your bone structure in great detail, take measurements, and plan out treatment.

If you are found to be a candidate for all-on-four dental implants, you will be scheduled for treatment. If you do not qualify, your dentist will discuss other options with you. Typically, the alternative is traditional dentures .

If you are only missing a few teeth in the mouth and have a number of healthy teeth present in the mouth, it is likely that you are not a candidate for all-on-four dental implants. Your dentist will always recommend preserving your natural teeth whenever possible.

How are All-on-4 Dental Implants Placed?

To begin the procedure, your dentist will ensure that you are fully numb with a local anesthetic.

A precise incision is made in the gum where the implant is to be placed, and the gums are retracted to expose the jaw bone beneath. The implant will then be placed directly into the bone. This will be repeated three more times to ensure that there are four “anchors” in the bone for the implant denture to snap into place.

After the implants have been successfully placed, your temporary “healing” dentures will be placed inside the mouth to ensure that you will not have to go without teeth during your healing period, which usually takes anywhere between three to eight months. The time it takes for you to heal may vary.

Once you have finished healing, you will return to have your temporary dentures removed and the permanent implant dentures placed in the mouth. You will then be free to fully enjoy your new teeth, which will look and function much like natural teeth.

You will care for your new teeth just like you would your natural teeth; regular brushing and flossing in conjunction with dental check-ups will ensure the longevity of your new dental prosthesis. Your dentist will give you thorough instructions on caring for your new teeth.
