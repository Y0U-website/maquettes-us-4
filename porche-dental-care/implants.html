What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that replaces the root of a missing tooth. It is placed into the jawbone, where it bonds with the surrounding bone over a healing period to create a stable foundation. Once that foundation is set, a custom crown , bridge , or denture attaches on top. The result looks and works like a natural tooth, with no slipping and no special adhesives required.

Implants stand apart from other tooth replacement options because they address the root cause, not just the visible gap. By stimulating the jawbone the way a natural root would, they help preserve bone structure and maintain the shape of your face. This is one reason many patients consider them a long-term investment in their oral health rather than a temporary fix.

Why Implants Are the Most Reliable Tooth Replacement Choice

Dental implants have one of the highest success rates of any tooth replacement procedure, with clinical studies consistently reporting success rates above 96 percent for single implants followed over at least a year. That kind of consistency is part of why so many dentists now recommend implants as a first-choice solution for missing teeth.

Beyond the clinical numbers, implants offer practical benefits you will notice every day. You can eat the foods you love without worry, speak clearly, and care for your replacement tooth just like your natural ones. With proper care, dental implants are designed to last a lifetime. The titanium post that fuses with the jawbone is highly durable, and clinical studies show success rates above 96 percent over long follow-up periods. The crown attached on top may eventually need replacement after many years of normal wear, but the implant itself typically remains stable indefinitely.

The placement procedure is done under local anesthesia, so most patients feel little to no discomfort during the appointment. Some soreness and swelling are common in the days following placement and are manageable with over-the-counter pain relievers. Many patients are surprised by how straightforward the recovery is compared to what they expected.

The full process typically takes several months from initial placement to final restoration, primarily because of the healing period during which the implant fuses with the bone. The timeline varies depending on your individual healing, whether preparatory steps like bone grafting are needed, and how quickly we can complete your custom restoration.

Implants require the same daily care as natural teeth — brushing twice a day, flossing, and keeping up with regular professional cleanings.
