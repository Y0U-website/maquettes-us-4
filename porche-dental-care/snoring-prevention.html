What Causes Snoring?

Snoring happens when the flow of air through the mouth and throat is partially obstructed during sleep. As the muscles in the throat and tongue relax, the airway narrows, and the remaining space forces air through at higher velocity. That turbulent airflow causes the soft tissues in the throat — the soft palate, the uvula, and the back of the tongue — to vibrate, producing the snoring sound.

Snoring vs. Sleep Apnea

Not all snoring is the same. Simple or primary snoring is the vibration of soft tissues without a meaningful disruption in breathing. Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), on the other hand, involves actual pauses in breathing during sleep — sometimes hundreds of times per night — where the airway becomes fully or significantly blocked. OSA is associated with increased risk of high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, daytime fatigue, and metabolic issues.

The two conditions often share the same starting point, and loud or persistent snoring is one of the most common early signs of OSA. If you experience significant daytime sleepiness, wake up with headaches, or have been told you stop breathing during sleep, it is important to be evaluated by a physician for sleep apnea. Dr. Porche will discuss your symptoms at your consultation and coordinate with your medical provider if a sleep study or formal OSA diagnosis is warranted before fitting an appliance.

What Is a Mandibular Advancement Device?

A mandibular advancement device, or MAD, is a custom-fitted oral appliance that resembles a sports mouthguard or orthodontic retainer. It is worn only during sleep. The device gently holds the lower jaw (mandible) in a slightly forward position, which in turn keeps the tongue from falling backward and maintains a more open airway throughout the night. Some devices also include a tongue-retaining component to provide additional airway support.

Because the MAD works mechanically by repositioning the jaw rather than relying on medication or a machine, it is quiet, compact, and easy to use — a significant advantage over CPAP therapy for patients with mild-to-moderate snoring or sleep-disordered breathing who find CPAP difficult to tolerate.

Is a Mandibular Advancement Device Comfortable?

Yes. A custom-fitted MAD from Porche Dental Care is made to match the exact contours of your teeth, making it far more comfortable than anything available over the counter. Most patients adapt to wearing the device within a few nights and sleep through the night without noticing it. The appliance does not prevent mouth breathing and can be used by patients who snore because of sinus congestion or allergies.

Some patients experience mild jaw stiffness in the first few mornings after starting to wear the device. This is normal and temporary — it resolves after removing the appliance and doing gentle jaw movements. If jaw soreness persists or you notice changes in your bite, contact our office so Dr. Porche can make a simple adjustment. Caring for the appliance is straightforward: rinse it daily, brush it gently with cool water, and store it in its case when not in use.
