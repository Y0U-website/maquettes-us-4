Replacing One Tooth or Several

Implants are remarkably versatile, and the right plan depends on how many teeth are missing and the condition of your jawbone. During your consultation we&#x27;ll walk through what suits your situation, whether that&#x27;s a single tooth or a fuller restoration.

  • A single implant topped with a crown for one missing tooth
  • Multiple implants supporting a bridge for several teeth in a row
  • Implant-supported dentures for a secure, full-arch result
  • Combining implants with implant restoration to complete an existing post

What the Implant Process Looks Like

Getting an implant is a journey rather than a single appointment, and we&#x27;ll keep you informed at every stage. Most cases follow this path:

  • A thorough evaluation and 3D imaging to assess bone and plan placement
  • Bone grafting first, if added support is needed before placement
  • Comfortable placement of the implant post into the jaw
  • A healing period while the implant integrates with the bone
  • Attachment of your custom crown, bridge, or denture to complete the smile

Caring for Your Implants Long-Term

One of the best things about implants is how easy they are to live with. Brush and floss them just like natural teeth, keep up with routine checkups, and they can serve you for many years. We&#x27;ll show you exactly how to care for your restoration and keep an eye on it at every visit so it stays strong.

FAQs

Am I a good candidate for dental implants?

Most adults in good general health with adequate jawbone are strong candidates. If bone has been lost, grafting can often rebuild enough support. The best way to know is a consultation and imaging.

Does getting an implant hurt?

Placement is done with local anesthesia, and most patients are surprised by how manageable it is. Any soreness afterward is typically mild and short-lived.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper home care and regular checkups, implants are designed to last decades — often a lifetime. The crown on top may eventually need replacing, but the implant itself is built for the long haul.

Are implants better than a bridge or dentures?

Each option has its place, but implants stand apart by preserving the jawbone and not relying on neighboring teeth. We&#x27;ll compare the choices honestly so you can decide what fits your goals and budget.
