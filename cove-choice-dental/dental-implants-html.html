What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are different from other tooth replacements in that they offer a much more comprehensive solution for tooth loss. The implant itself is made out of titanium and shaped like a screw; it is designed to join with the jawbone and act as a new tooth root. This merging process, known as osseointegration, is what allows dental implants to support a crown, a bridge, or even a full denture via a metal abutment. Due to the unique advantages that they carry, dental implants are now considered the modern gold standard solution for tooth loss.

Dental Implant Surgery

Because our Cove Choice Dental team can perform this procedure in-house, you won’t have to worry about visiting an unfamiliar practice. Instead, you’ll be with the team you already know and trust every step of the way.

Surgical dental implant placement is fairly straightforward. We’ll make small, precise incisions in your gum tissue, through which we carefully position the implant posts within your jawbone. Lastly, we close up your gums and place protective caps over the implants to ensure they stay safe during the healing process.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Next comes osseointegration the process of your jawbone growing around the implants until they’re essentially a part of your body. This step is key to the longevity, stability, and most of the other unique benefits of dental implants. However, it can take up to six months before it’s finished.

Once your implants are sufficiently fused to your jawbone, we’ll perform a small surgery to install metal connectors called abutments onto your implants. Abutments act as the bridge between the implant itself and the restoration we place on top of it. As you spend a few weeks healing from your abutment procedure, your restoration will be meticulously crafted at a dental lab based on impressions that we’ve taken of your mouth.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

Finally, you’ll return to our office and we’ll secure your tailor-made dental crown, bridge, or denture (depending on the number of teeth you’re replacing) onto your abutments. Once this step is complete, we’ll let you know how to care for your new smile so that it lasts for decades, possibly a lifetime to come!

Day-to-Day Benefits

Since dental implants replace the entire structure of a missing tooth from the root up to the chewing surface, they’re able to provide several day-to-day benefits, including:

Maintaining & Caring For Your Dental Implants

When you get dental implants, you should do so with the expectation that they will last a long time; however, their lifespan will depend largely on how well you take care of them. Doing your part can ensure that your dental implants are still successful even decades after placement. Below are tips from our dentists you’ll need to follow to keep your implants in good condition.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Just as you should with natural teeth, your implants need to be brushed and flossed on a daily basis. Plaque, which contains inflammation-causing bacteria, can still accumulate, particularly near the gumline, and these seemingly small habits can prevent plaque from adhering and causing trouble. Even though brushing and flossing only take a few minutes every day, they can have an impact for a lifetime.

Eat a Healthy Diet

You may know that sugar can lead to cavities, but did you know it can also sabotage your implants? Although you don’t have to worry about decay, your gums can become infected with a bacteria that thrives on sugar and carbohydrates, so it’s a good idea to limit how much you consume. On the other hand, there are plenty of healthy fruits, vegetables, and other food that offer the vitamins and minerals your mouth needs to stay strong. Drinking plenty of water is another way to minimize the negative impact your food can have on your restored smile.

Break Bad Habits

If you have smoked in the past, now is the opportune time to stop. Smoking makes it easier for bad bacteria to grow inside the mouth. Other bad habits, such as chewing on hard objects like pens or ice, can also put your implants at risk for failure.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Do you participate in contact sports or activities where a blow to the face may occur? You need to wear a mouthguard, which absorbs the hit instead of your implants. Like natural teeth, your implants are embedded in the jawbone, but an impact to the mouth can break them loose, undoing all your hard work. We find the best protection comes with a custom mouthguard that fits properly and doesn’t slip around.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Even if you are diligent in brushing and flossing, you’re bound to miss some plaque every now and then. Once hardened, it is impossible to remove, except by a professional with the right equipment and training. That’s why you need to have regular cleanings. Plus, during these routine appointments , we check to see if there are any complications developing. If we spot any, such as a gum infection called peri-implantitis, we can take care of it quickly before it leads to implant failure.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

After getting dental implants in Copperas Cove, the most important thing you must do is leave the forming blood clot untouched to ensure the gums heal properly around the implant. To that end, you’ll need to avoid:

  • Spitting (use tissues or swallow saliva)
  • Drinking from straws
  • Smoking or using tobacco products
  • Touching the surgical site with your fingers and tongue

Common Side-Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

Patients who’ve never had oral surgery before may have difficulty knowing what symptoms are normal and which ones are concerning. While you can expect discomfort after getting dental implants in Copperas Cove, it’s temporary and should gradually disappear. If it seems to be getting worse, don’t hesitate to give our office a call. Here are some other side effects you can expect in the first few days after your procedure:

  • Intermittent Bleeding: This is gradual and can be remedied by applying a small amount of pressure on the gauze that’s located on the surgical site.
  • Swelling: Often lasting a week, you can get it to go down more quickly by using a cold compress on the cheek or jaw for 10 minutes at a time.
  • General Discomfort: You can alleviate your discomfort with prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication.

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Keeping your mouth clean is incredibly important after getting dental implants in Copperas Cove. You can brush your teeth as you normally would the day after your surgery, but you need to be careful about brushing near the surgical site. Rinse your mouth two to three times a day with salt water, ideally after meals. You’ll also want to avoid mouthwashes that contain high levels of alcohol, like Scope or Listerine.
