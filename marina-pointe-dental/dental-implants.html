Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most healthy adults who are missing one or more teeth are candidates for dental implants, but a few factors affect how straightforward treatment will be. During your consultation, we look closely at your jawbone density, gum health, and overall medical history before recommending a treatment plan.

Patients with diabetes, osteoporosis, or a history of smoking can often still receive implants, but these conditions may affect healing time and require closer monitoring throughout treatment. If digital X-rays show significant bone loss where a tooth has been missing for years, bone grafting can rebuild a stable foundation before implant placement. Growing children and teens are generally not candidates until their jaw has finished developing.

The most reliable way to know if you qualify is a consultation, where we take digital X-rays and evaluate your specific situation before recommending single-tooth, bridge, crown, or overdenture options.

Are There Risks or Downsides to Dental Implants?

Dental implants have a high success rate, but as with any surgical procedure, they carry some risk. The most common concern is implant failure, which happens when the post does not fully fuse with the jawbone through osseointegration. This is uncommon and is more likely in patients who smoke, have uncontrolled diabetes, or have insufficient bone density that was not addressed with grafting beforehand.

Other possible downsides include a longer overall timeline compared to a bridge or denture, since the implant needs several months to integrate before the final crown is placed. Some patients also experience temporary swelling, soreness, or minor bleeding after the placement procedure, similar to what you would expect after a tooth extraction.

We reduce these risks by reviewing your bone density and health history carefully before recommending treatment, and by monitoring healing at each stage of the process. If an implant does not integrate as expected, it can often be removed and replaced once the site has healed.

What Is Recovery Like After Dental Implant Surgery?

Most patients feel back to normal within a few days of implant placement, though the jawbone needs several months to fully bond with the titanium post before we attach your final restoration.

For the first 24 to 48 hours, plan to eat soft foods, avoid smoking, and skip strenuous exercise so a healthy blood clot can form at the surgical site. Mild swelling and tenderness are normal and typically respond well to over-the-counter pain medication and an ice pack. Most patients return to work or their normal routine within a day or two.

During the healing period, we ask patients to maintain gentle oral hygiene around the implant site and attend follow-up visits so we can monitor osseointegration. Once your implants have fully integrated, usually three to six months later, we move forward with your custom crown, bridge, or overdenture.
