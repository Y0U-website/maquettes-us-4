What Are Impacted Canine Teeth And Their Causes

Canine teeth, often called eye teeth, normally erupt between ages 12 and 13. An impacted canine stays stuck in the gum or bone and does not emerge into its proper position. You have four canines total, two in the upper arch and two in the lower arch. Several factors can block normal eruption.

Extra teeth, known as supernumerary teeth, can physically obstruct the canine’s path or create crowding that leaves no room. An arch length deficiency also causes problems. This happens when your jaw length is too short relative to your tooth width, leaving insufficient space for the canine to come in naturally.

Signs And Symptoms Of Impacted Canine Teeth

You may notice a few warning signs that point to an impacted canine. A baby canine tooth that stays in place well past the normal age of loss is a common clue. You might see a visible gap where the permanent tooth should be. Some patients feel pressure or mild discomfort in the area.

In other cases, an impacted canine causes no noticeable symptoms at all, that is why routine dental exams and imaging are so important. Your general dentist can spot an impacted canine on a panoramic X-ray and refer you to a specialist. Non-Surgical Treatment Methods For Impacted Canines

Not every impacted canine needs surgery right away. In mild cases where the tooth is close to emerging, your orthodontist may create space with braces. This can sometimes encourage natural eruption without surgical exposure. Still, many impacted canines do require a combined approach.
