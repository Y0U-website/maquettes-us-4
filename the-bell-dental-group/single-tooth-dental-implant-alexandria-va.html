1. Rinse after every meal

Think of it like this: you rinse your dishes after meals, right? Same idea here. If you have snap-in (implant-retained) dentures, remove them after meals and rinse them thoroughly with lukewarm water. Even if you have fixed implants, it’s a good idea to rinse your mouth to eliminate food debris and harmful bacteria.

2. Brush twice daily

Just because your implant isn’t a natural tooth doesn’t mean you can skip brushing. Brush your teeth twice daily with a soft toothbrush and non-abrasive toothpaste. Be thorough around the implant, as food debris and plaque can accumulate there. Don’t forget to clean your gums and tongue to eliminate harmful bacteria.

3. Floss daily

Don’t forget to floss daily, especially around the base where bacteria love to hide. If regular floss feels tricky, try an interdental brush or water flosser for easier cleaning. Floss at least once daily, but doing it twice won’t hurt.

4. Schedule regular dental check-ups

Good at-home dental care is critical, but it doesn’t replace professional care. Visit your dentist in Alexandra, VA, at least twice annually. Routine dental visits are essential for keeping your implants healthy. During these check-ups, Dr. Bell and his team will examine and clean your gums, implants, and surrounding teeth to ensure everything is in good condition. We can also detect early signs of problems before they become serious.

5. Protect your smile

If you clench your teeth while sleeping or participate in contact sports, consider using a personalized mouthguard. Teeth grinding (bruxism) can put excessive pressure on your implant, while sports injuries can cause trauma. A properly fitted guard offers excellent protection and peace of mind.

6. Ditch poor oral habits

Dental implants are strong, but that doesn’t mean they’re indestructible. Avoid using your teeth to open packages or cut fingernails. These habits can damage your crown or surrounding teeth. At the same time, dental implants don’t come with dietary restrictions per se. However, avoid extremely hard or chewy foods.

Do you smoke? Tobacco is one of the leading causes of implant failure. It restricts blood flow to your gums, slows healing, and heightens the risk of infection. If you smoke, consider this a great reason to quit, not just for your implant, but for your overall health.

Your implants won’t get cavities like real teeth, but the surrounding bone and gums are at risk. Without proper care, it could lead to infections, bad breath, and implant failure. Taking care of your implants protects your investment and keeps your oral health in excellent condition.
