The Pediatric Emergency Dental Care Process

Contact Our Dental Team

Call or text Smile First Dental when an emergency occurs. Tell us what happened and describe symptoms such as pain, bleeding, swelling, or a damaged tooth so our team can provide initial guidance.

Emergency Dental Evaluation

Our pediatric dentist examines the affected tooth and surrounding tissues. Depending on the emergency, digital X rays may be recommended to check the tooth roots, bone, or areas that cannot be seen during the visual exam.

Provide Appropriate Treatment

Treatment depends on the diagnosis. Care may involve repairing a damaged tooth, managing an infection, addressing dental trauma, treating the tooth nerve, or discussing extraction when a tooth cannot be predictably restored.

Plan Follow Up Care

Some dental emergencies need continued monitoring after the first visit. We explain home care instructions, symptoms to watch for, and whether another appointment is needed to check healing or complete treatment.

See More Reviews Frequently Asked Questions What counts as a dental emergency for a child? Severe or persistent tooth pain, facial or gum swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, a knocked out permanent tooth, a badly broken tooth, or significant dental trauma should be evaluated promptly. Damaged dental restorations or objects stuck between the teeth can also require urgent care when they cause pain or cannot be safely removed.

What should I do if my child knocks out a permanent tooth?

Find the tooth and handle it by the crown, not the root. If it is dirty, gently rinse it without scrubbing or removing attached tissue. If possible, carefully place the permanent tooth back into its socket. If that cannot be done, keep it moist in milk and contact a pediatric dentist immediately. Do not delay care while trying to clean the tooth unnecessarily.

Should I put a knocked out baby tooth back in place?

No. A knocked out primary or baby tooth should not normally be replanted because doing so could affect the developing permanent tooth underneath. Contact our pediatric dental team promptly so we can evaluate the injury and make sure there is no additional damage.

Can my child be seen the same day for a dental emergency?

Smile First Dental offers same day appointments for children experiencing dental emergencies, depending on the situation and scheduling availability. The practice also has a pediatric dentist on call 24/7 so families can contact the team when an urgent dental concern happens.

When should my child go to the emergency room instead of the dentist?

Seek immediate medical care for serious head or facial trauma, a suspected broken jaw, difficulty breathing or swallowing, loss of consciousness, or other potentially life threatening symptoms. For emergencies mainly involving the teeth or gums, contact our pediatric dental team for guidance.
