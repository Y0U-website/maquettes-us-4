What Factors Can Affect the Cost of Dental Implants?

Are You Experiencing… Dentures that slip, move, or cause painful sore spots? Feeling like you can’t speak, laugh, or smile with confidence?

  • Bone Loss When teeth have been missing for a long time, the jawbone naturally begins to shrink. In these cases, additional treatments like bone grafting or sinus lifts may be required to create a stable foundation for implants.
  • Number of Implants Needed Replacing a single tooth requires one implant, while a full-arch restoration may need four to six. More implants typically means a higher overall cost — but also greater stability and longevity.
  • Case Complexity Patients with significant bone loss, traumatic injuries, or complex bite issues (like crossbites or severe wear) may require more advanced planning, additional implants, or specialized surgical techniques.
  • Type of Restoration The material and design of your final teeth affect pricing. Higher-end materials provide superior durability, realism, and long-term performance.
  • Preparatory Treatments Some patients require additional procedures before implants can be placed, such as Tooth extractions, Bone grafting, Sinus lifts, and/or IV sedation. Each of these adds to the overall treatment cost.
