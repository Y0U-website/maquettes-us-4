Dental Hygiene

If you notice any of these signs, a dental hygiene appointment can help identify the cause and prevent small issues from becoming bigger problems.

  • Persistent bad breath
  • Tooth sensitivity
  • Plaque or tartar buildup
  • Red, swollen, or tender gums
  • Staining or dull-looking teeth
  • It has been more than six months since your last cleaning
  • You have been told you may need gum disease treatment

FAQs About Dental Hygiene

How Often Should I Get a Professional Cleaning?

Most patients should come in every six months, though some benefit from more frequent visits depending on their gum health.

Why Do My Gums Bleed When I Floss?

It’s often a sign of early gum inflammation from plaque buildup. Bleeding usually improves with more consistent brushing and flossing.

What’s the Difference Between a Regular Cleaning and a Deep Cleaning?

A regular cleaning addresses plaque and tartar above the gumline, while a deep cleaning treats buildup below the gumline for patients with early gum disease.

Does Insurance Cover Routine Dental Cleanings?

Most dental plans cover routine cleanings and exams at little to no cost, often at a higher rate than other treatments.

What Happens if I Skip My Regular Cleanings?

Plaque and tartar build up over time, raising your risk of cavities and gum disease that can become more costly and involved to treat later.

Is Flossing Really Necessary if I Brush Well?

Yes. Brushing alone can’t reach between teeth, where a significant amount of plaque and food debris collects.
