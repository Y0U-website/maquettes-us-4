Keep Your Smile Healthy

Preventative dental care is one of the most important services we offer our community. That’s because good prevention and professional cleanings can save teeth, protect our patients’ health and reduce their dental care costs. It can also save them from pain and tooth loss.

The better your preventative dental care, including both office visits and at-home care, the more likely it is you can preserve your teeth and attractive smile.

We are careful to hire the very best dental hygienists on our staff because thorough, careful cleanings are so important for dental health. Our hygienists will explain how you can improve your at-home care to have the best chance of avoiding the need for future fillings or other treatment.

A Regular Schedule of Visits Is Vital

For most patients, we recommend twice-yearly visits for cleanings and examinations. During those visits, we will clean away any accumulated tartar on your teeth. Even if you brush and floss your teeth thoroughly at home, tartar will still collect in spots that are hard to reach. We’ll clean those out for you. We’ll also polish your teeth thoroughly. Polishing removes any remaining tartar and eliminates some staining.

This visit also gives us a chance to inspect for any damage to your existing dental work or look for decay while affected areas are still small. We can then schedule you for any further work while the repairs needed are minimal.

In addition to good at-home care, maintaining a twice-yearly schedule of visits is the most important thing you can do to keep your teeth healthy.

Treating or Preventing Gum Disease

As we inspect your teeth, we also examine your gums. If you are suffering from gum disease, you may have noticed symptoms like these:

With prompt treatment of gum disease, you can stop further development of these issues. Eliminating these problems could require a better at-home-care regimen, or, if they are more advanced, special treatment in our office.

Advanced gum disease is called periodontitis. We may recommend cleaning out tartar that has accumulated below the gumline. This is called scaling. Another treatment called root planing smooths the roots of your teeth to eliminate areas where bacteria can thrive.

If you notice any signs of gum disease between your regular prevention or professional cleaning visits, please contact us immediately.

  • You have red or swollen areas in your gums
  • There is some bleeding when you brush or floss
  • Teeth or gums hurt when you bite or chew food
  • Some teeth feel loose or are sensitive
  • Your teeth look longer because gums have receded
