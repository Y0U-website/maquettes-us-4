Are You a Candidate for Implant Supported Dentures?

Candidacy depends on your jawbone health and which type of denture fits your case. Here’s what our dentist evaluates:

Missing Most or All Teeth Implant-supported dentures replace a full row of missing teeth in the upper or lower arch.

Adequate Jawbone Density Dental implants require some jawbone for placement. People with deteriorated jaws may not qualify, though bone augmentation can sometimes help.

Bar-Retained Dentures A metal bar follows the curve of the jaw and is anchored by implants; the denture then connects to the bar.

Ball-Retained (Stud-Attached) Dentures A ball-shaped attachment on each implant connects to a matching socket on the denture.

Wanting a Fixed, Non-Removable Option Unlike removable dentures, implant-supported dentures stay firmly in place and are only removed by a dentist.

Function Like Natural Teeth Implant-supported dentures interact with the jawbone much like natural teeth, supporting long-term bone health and function.

How to Get Implant Supported Dentures

Getting implant-supported dentures requires a dentist experienced in placing implants. Here’s what the process involves.

Exam & Consultation

Our dentist performs an exam and consultation to determine whether you’re a candidate for implant-supported dentures.

Jawbone Evaluation

Dental implants require some jawbone for placement. If your jawbone has deteriorated, bone augmentation can sometimes help.

Implant Placement

Implants are surgically placed in the jawbone, under sedation if needed.

Recovery

Most people return to daily life the next day after implant placement.

Denture Attachment

Once healed, your custom denture is fitted and secured to the implants — bar-retained or ball-retained, based on your case.

Caring for Your Implant Supported Dentures

Caring for implant-supported dentures is similar to caring for permanent teeth — brush twice daily, with special attention to cleaning around the abutments where the denture connects to the implants. With proper care and regular checkups, implant-supported dentures can last a lifetime.

Frequently Asked Questions About Implant Supported Dentures

The questions patients ask most about implant-supported dentures — answered plainly.

Can I remove my dentures? No, implant-supported dentures cannot be removed by the patient — only a dentist places and removes them. Does Invisalign hurt? Why not have an implant for each tooth? It’s not necessary when replacing a full row of teeth. Implant-supported dentures are more cost-effective and less invasive while being equally effective.

How many implants will I need? Typically 2 to 5 implants; a full row commonly attaches to 4, though it varies by case.

How long do implant supported dentures last? They’re a permanent appliance. With proper care and regular checkups, they can last a lifetime.

What is the success rate of implants? With a skilled dentist, the success rate is over 95 %. Placement is relatively simple but sometimes requires sedation, with occasional minor complications.

Which type of dentures do I need? A consultation and exam with our dentist helps determine the right type for your situation.
