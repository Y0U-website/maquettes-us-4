Partials

What are Partials?

A partial plate is a denture that replaces multiple teeth in one piece. They can be made of plastic or a combination of plastic and metal. These must be taken out at night.

Partials can be uncomfortable, especially on the bottom. They can trap food which can lead to other dental health problems.

Dentures

What are Dentures?

Full dentures replace all of your existing teeth, and are fit to your gums and bones to help stabilize them. They are more movable & loose than natural teeth. Dentures are not as effective as your natural teeth. Learning to eat, and speak with them will take a little time to get used to.

There is extensive information on dentures and combination of implants and dentures in our Patient Education Center under the Topic Restoring Teeth.

Other Options or Supplements to Dentures

  • Placement of dental implants . They can be used in to help stabilize your loose denture and can provide more comfort.
  • Full replacement of your teeth with implants. Many implants can be placed to make a denture that does not have to come out at night. There are many variations of this type of denture.

Inlays & Onlays

Inlays

An inlay is similar to a filling but made outside the mouth and then cemented or bonded in.

Onlays

An onlay is a type of partial crown, a filling made of metal, porcelain, or acrylic. It’s more extensive than an inlay because it covers one or more cusps.

Fillings & Bonding​

Composite Fillings

Composite fillings (tooth-colored) are aesthetic and long-lasting. They require less drilling away of the tooth structure than the silver amalgam fillings.

Composite resin comes in many tooth colors so it can be matched to the tooth.

What is Bonding?

The composite material used for fillings can also be used to repair minor chips and gaps that plague your smile. This process is called dental bonding. It is a cost-effective fix for small, cosmetic improvements.

What is restorative dentistry?

Restorative dentistry focuses on repairing or replacing damaged or missing teeth to restore your smile’s function and appearance. At Apex Dental Group, we offer a range of treatments including crowns, bridges, implants, and dentures.

When do I need restorative dental treatment?

You may need restorative care if you have a chipped, decayed, or missing tooth, or if old fillings or crowns have worn down. Our Apex dentist will evaluate your mouth and recommend the best solution to restore your bite and confidence.

What’s the difference between a crown and a bridge?

A crown covers and strengthens a damaged tooth, while a bridge replaces one or more missing teeth by anchoring to nearby teeth. Both options are designed to blend naturally with your smile.

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants can last for decades with good oral hygiene and regular checkups. They’re a long-term solution that feels and functions like a natural tooth, offering exceptional comfort and durability.

What are dentures and when are they recommended?

Dentures are custom-made appliances that replace multiple missing teeth. They can be full (for all teeth) or partial (for a few missing teeth). Our team ensures a comfortable, natural-looking fit so you can eat and speak confidently again.

What is a CEREC same-day crown?

A CEREC crown is crafted and placed in a single visit using advanced digital technology. This means no temporary crowns or multiple appointments—just a fast, precise restoration designed right here in our Apex office.

Are restorative treatments painful?

Most restorative procedures are done using local anesthesia, so you’ll remain comfortable throughout treatment. Any mild soreness afterward is temporary and can be managed with over-the-counter pain relief.

How do I care for my restored teeth?

Brush and floss daily, and visit our office for routine cleanings. Avoid biting hard foods or objects, and maintain good oral hygiene to keep your restorations looking and functioning their best.

How much does restorative dental work cost in Apex, NC?

Costs vary depending on the treatment and number of teeth involved. During your consultation, we’ll provide a clear estimate and discuss insurance and financing options to make care affordable.
