Orthodontic Options

There are numerous orthodontic appliances available that might be required for very complex problems. Those outlined below are simply three of the most frequently used.

1. Fixed Braces

These use brackets that are fixed onto teeth for the duration of treatment, powered by wires and elastics. Options include fixed metal braces, clear ceramic braces, and lingual braces, which are fitted on the inner surfaces of the teeth.

2. Clear Aligners

These are an alternative to having fixed braces and work extremely well. Instead of brackets and wires, you use removable, clear, plastic aligner trays to straighten your teeth.

These aligner trays are custom-made, and you wear a series of them to gradually move your teeth into predetermined positions.

3. Palate Expanders

Palate expanders are generally used during childhood while a child’s facial bones are still soft and are growing and developing. A palate expander helps guide this growth by expanding the upper palate, allowing it to accommodate the developing teeth more easily. Occasionally, these devices are used during adulthood.

4. Retainers

Retainers must be worn after completing orthodontic treatment. They do not move teeth but instead retain them in the right places, preventing them from drifting back toward their original positions.

Retainers can be fixed or removable, and initially must be worn most or all of the time while the jawbone remodels around your newly positioned teeth.

Who Can Have Orthodontic Treatment?

Orthodontics can help young children, teens, and adults as part of comprehensive family dental care .

Young Children

If you have a young child, we will closely monitor the development of their teeth and jaws. As part of our comprehensive children’s dental services , we may recommend they see a Sheepshead Bay orthodontist for an early orthodontic evaluation. Sometimes an evaluation occurs as early as age 5 or 6.

Early orthodontic treatment may be implemented as a two-part treatment plan, where initial treatment is provided while the child is still young. Early treatment can address common problems caused by oral habits such as mouth breathing or thumb sucking.

If needed, further corrections can be made during their tweens or teenage years. Often, these corrections may be more cosmetically oriented.

Teens

Wearing braces during the teenage years is often seen as a rite of passage. Previously, it was common to see teens with a mouthful of metal braces. These are still widely used and are often customized by patients with bright-colored elastics.

However, it’s increasingly common for teens to benefit from less visible options, such as removable clear aligners, which are easier to care for and just as effective as fixed braces when worn correctly.

Adults

Increasingly, we see more adult patients looking for the best orthodontist in Sheepshead Bay, Brooklyn. Some patients may have previously received orthodontics during childhood, but the results have relapsed. Others didn’t have the chance earlier in life and now want to straighten their smile.

Can Anyone Have Orthodontics?

Individuals interested in orthodontics should have good dental health and no serious issues, such as untreated tooth decay or gum disease. Being older is not a problem.

Attempting to move teeth in an unhealthy mouth can lead to complications. If you are interested in orthodontics and currently have active gum disease or cavities, we will need to manage these conditions first before discussing orthodontics.

Benefits of Orthodontics

Most people, particularly adults, will choose to see an orthodontist in Sheepshead Bay because they want to improve the appearance of their smile cosmetically. Improving the aesthetics of your smile is a perfectly valid reason for treatment, but orthodontics can potentially provide some important health benefits.

  • Improved oral health . Straight teeth are easier to keep clean, which reduces your risk of gum disease and tooth decay.
  • More even wear and tear on teeth. Straighter teeth will wear more evenly, reducing the risk of chipping and cracking in some teeth. Excessive wear and tear can occur if some teeth bite down before others in a misaligned bite, causing uneven wear.
  • Reduce your risk of TMD. Orthodontics can help protect your temporomandibular joints, also known as your jaw joints. These hinge your lower jaw to your skull. If your teeth are out of alignment, it can place these joints under pressure, leading to a condition called temporomandibular joint disorder (TMD). TMD can be extremely painful, causing jaw pain, facial pain, and chronic headaches.

What to Consider Before Starting Orthodontic Treatment

Orthodontic treatment is an investment in your smile that requires patience and teamwork with your dentist. To ensure the best possible results, keep these guidelines in mind:

  • Braces : You will need to adjust your diet to avoid hard or sticky foods and maintain meticulous oral hygiene. Thorough daily brushing and flossing are essential to protect your enamel and gums from plaque.
  • Clear Aligners : While daily care is simple, discipline is key. You must wear your aligners for 20–22 hours a day to keep your treatment on schedule and achieve your ideal results.

How Long Will Orthodontics Take?

The time required to complete your treatment plan depends on the complexity of the issues that need correction. Cosmetically oriented issues can often be corrected very quickly, sometimes in a matter of just a few months. More complex bite problems may take one to two years to correct.
