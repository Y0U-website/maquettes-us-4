About CADE

Center for Advanced Dental Education Request Info Visit Apply Give Center for Advanced Dental Education About Dental Education Patient Care Contact Us Toggle menu About CADE The Saint Louis University Center for Advanced Dental Education is one of the finest facilities for advanced dental education in the world.

CADE has a proud tradition of excellence which strives to balance progressive excellence with the highest standards and the compassionate service of our Catholic, Jesuit heritage. Our graduates leave SLU prepared to be astute and aware clinicians using an integrated, multidisciplinary approach to caring for craniofacial and dental patient problems.

Patients in our clinics experience exceptional, innovative and multidimensional care with intervention of all dental specialties as appropriate, using the best available means in diagnosis, treatment planning, treatment technique and concept. CADE also extends its resources to the community to offer dental education and dental patient care to those in need.

Oral health care is a vital and challenging part of overall health care. As biomedical knowledge grows at an explosive rate, our faculty, staff and students are dedicated to maintaining CADE’s integral role as a major contributor to the overall health care system.

CADE History

1967

In 1967, Saint Louis University’s Board of Trustees voted to close SLU’s School of Dentistry, which had a four-year enrollment of 350. When the last Doctor of Dental Sciences degrees were conferred in 1971, a program that had begun in 1894 at Marion Sims College of Medicine and was taken over by Saint Louis University in 1903 came to an end.

Dental education had become enormously expensive, and there were too many dentists (a surplus that proved to be temporary). One by one, private universities were ceding the field to the growing number of state-supported schools of dentistry.

Under the leadership of the program chair, Kenneth Marshall, D.D.S., orthodontic alumni pledged their support to persuade the University administrators and trustees to continue the orthodontic program with the promise that it would be self-supporting. Consequently, when the dental school shut its doors in 1971, graduate work in orthodontics moved forward, funded by expanded clinical services and alumni contributions.

1976

By 1976, when Lysle E. Johnston, Jr., D.D.S., Ph.D., became chair of the program, it was on such sound footing that research could be expanded. Faculty members and graduate students routinely collaborated with experts in various disciplines of the University, including anatomy, biology, engineering, genetics, physiology, and radiology. Their findings were applied widely including cleft palate correction, tooth alignment, risk assessment, and devising new treatment strategies.

1991

Peter Sotiropoulos, D.D.S., succeeded Johnston as chair of the program, realizing that although the need to add specialties was clear, there were major obstacles. While there was a serious shortage of specialists able to treat an aging patient population and a greater public desire to save teeth, money to expand degree programs was hard to come by nationwide.

The Center for Advanced Dental Education overcame those obstacles through an arrangement to offer graduate courses jointly with Southern Illinois University School of Dental Medicine in Alton, 25 miles north of St. Louis. In a relationship unusual among universities, both institutions would offer new programs leading to Saint Louis University degrees. Academic courses and clinical work would take place on both campuses.

1994

Anticipating new programs in other specialties, the Center for Advanced Dental Education was created.

The first joint offering was a master’s program in endodontics, the branch of dentistry that deals with diseases of the tooth pulp and related conditions. In 1996, a program was added in periodontics, the specialty in diseases of the gums and tissues supporting the teeth.

1998

In the mid-1990s, administrators began looking for a new home for the center. They needed to relieve overcrowding, accommodate the future programs in other specialties, provide more room for research, and house electronic equipment for distance education, so that courses and continuing education series could be offered to students and practicing specialists a few miles or hundreds of miles from St. Louis.

CADE’s new home was created by adapting and adding to a well-built commercial building, originally constructed as a wood-planing mill. Its administrative offices, finished with beautifully milled fine woods, were retained. The 27,500 square-foot building, on what is now on the eastern end of SLU’s Medical Center, was later expanded to 47,200 square feet.

2017

The Saint Louis University Graduate Program in Pediatric Dentistry admitted its first class in 2017. The 24-month program is affiliated with SSM Health Cardinal Glennon Children’s Hospital, a 195-bed, level one trauma center located in midtown St. Louis. The pediatric dental program provides residents with extensive experience caring for both healthy and medically complex children, providing sedation and operating room care and managing patients with dental trauma.
