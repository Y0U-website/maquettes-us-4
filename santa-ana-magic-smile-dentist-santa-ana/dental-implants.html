How to Get Dental Implants

FAQs

Dental implants are best suited to patients ready for a longer process and who need to replace a single tooth (or a couple of teeth). Larger restorations may better suit dental bridges or dentures. What are the different types of dental implants? Dental implants are typically divided into four categories: endosteal, subperiosteal, zygomatic, and mini implants. Your dentist will examine your jaw and gum health to determine which type is best suited for you.

What most people think of as a dental implant consists of three main parts: a screw-like post (the implant itself), a crown, and an abutment that connects the crown and the post. The screw-like post and the abutment are most commonly made of titanium, a very durable and biocompatible material. Depending on the budget and medical considerations, the crown can be made of several different materials, such as porcelain, porcelain-fused-to-metal, zirconia, or less often, resin composite.

The implant itself should last a lifetime. For many patients, a porcelain crown should last over 15 years. How much do dental implants cost? The cost of a dental implant can vary. We provide a personalized, commitment-free quote during your consultation. We also offer several financing options to make care more accessible.

Insurance generally will not cover the cost of dental implants. They may do so if the implants are deemed medically necessary.

The dental implant procedure is performed under anesthesia and should not cause any pain. After the procedure, you may experience mild discomfort as your mouth heals, but this is typically manageable with over-the-counter pain medication.

Patients typically complete the initial stages of healing in the first two weeks following dental implant surgery. Full osseointegration can take up to 8 months.

Dental implants look incredibly natural in the mouth. Your dentist will carefully match the shape, size, and color of the implant’s crown to blend with the rest of your teeth, so no one will be able to tell you have a dental implant.

Dental implants have a high long-term success rate. With proper preparation and post-procedure care, many people have successful outcomes. If you maintain good oral hygiene, visit your dentist regularly, and address arising issues in time, your implants are likely to serve you for decades. Typically, implants survive for 10 to 15 years and longer. Some general health issues, such as uncontrolled diabetes or osteoporosis, can lower success rates by affecting bone healing and increasing the risk of infection.

Typically, before the implant placement surgery, patients require a thorough oral health examination and careful preparation and planning, meaning that you can not have dental implants right away during your first visit to a dental office. Additionally, implants often require a step-by-step process, with the post (the implant itself) being placed first and the crown later, when the post fuses with the bone. Such an approach helps ensure that the implant procedure is successful and complication-free.
