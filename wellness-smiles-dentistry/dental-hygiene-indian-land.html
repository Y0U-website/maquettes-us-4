FAQ’s

How to maintain good oral health?

To achieve good oral health, brush your teeth two times each day with fluoride toothpaste, floss once a day, and use mouthwash. Reduce your intake of sugary foods or drinks, avoid tobacco products, and go for routine dental appointments to prevent dental issues. Drink plenty of water and eat a well-balanced diet to keep teeth and gums healthy.

Should I floss before or after brushing?

Daily flossing removes food particles and plaque between teeth that brushing can’t touch. Although the sequence is not rigid, pre-brushing flossing could potentially provide for better fluoride absorption. Pre-flossing may be able to clear out debris so that brushing can more effectively clean the tooth surface.

What are the dental hygiene precautions?

Major oral hygiene actions include brushing teeth for two minutes, twice daily, flossing every day, and using mouthwash. Restrict sweets, quit smoking, and visit our dentist for regular checks and cleanings. Replace your toothbrush every three or four months or sooner if the bristles are worn. If there is a family history of a cavity or gum disease, use an electric toothbrush to clean more effectively.

What is the golden rule for oral hygiene?

The overall principle of oral hygiene is brushing regularly twice a day and daily flossing. This will avoid plaque, cavities, and gum disease and keep the mouth healthy in general. Periodic dental examination is as significant as home care in the prevention of dental disorders.

How often should I brush my teeth for optimal dental hygiene?

For healthy teeth, brushing twice a day, ideally morning and evening, using fluoride toothpaste is advisable. Each brush must last for two minutes to effectively brush away plaque and avoid cavities. The use of a soft-bristled toothbrush and gentle circular action avoids gum injury.

How often should I visit the dentist for a cleaning?

It is usually best to have the dentist professionally clean your teeth every six months. However, those with an increased risk of gum disease or other oral disease may need to plan more regular visits as instructed by the dentist.
