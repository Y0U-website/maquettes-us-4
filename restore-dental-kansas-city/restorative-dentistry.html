The Dental Filling Procedure

Before the procedure begins, your dentist will numb the area around the affected tooth to minimize discomfort. If you’re anxious, discuss sedation options with your dentist.

Once you’re comfortable, the dentist will remove the decayed portion of the tooth using a drill or laser. This step is crucial for preventing further decay and ensuring the filling adheres properly.

After cleaning the area, the dentist will place the filling material. If you’re getting a composite filling, the material is applied in layers and hardened with a special light.

Dental Filling FAQ s

  • How Long Do Fillings Last? Several factors can affect the lifespan of your fillings, including your oral hygiene habits, diet, and whether you grind your teeth. Regular dental check-ups can help monitor the condition of your fillings and address any issues early.
  • How Much Do Dental Fillings Cost? The cost of dental fillings varies depending on the material used and the complexity of the procedure. Amalgam fillings are usually the least expensive, while gold and ceramic fillings can be quite costly. On average, you can expect to pay between $100 and $500 per filling. Many dental insurance plans cover a portion of the cost of fillings, especially if they’re deemed medically necessary. It’s a good idea to check with your insurance provider to understand your coverage and any out-of-pocket expenses.
  • What Are My Alternatives to Dental Fillings? Dental Crowns: In cases where a tooth is severely damaged or decayed, a dental crown may be a better option than a filling. Crowns cover the entire tooth, providing more protection and strength. Inlays and Onlays: Inlays and onlays are similar to fillings but cover a larger portion of the tooth. They are often used for teeth that are too damaged for a filling but not damaged enough for a crown.
  • How Can I Avoid Getting a Filling in the First Place? The best way to avoid the need for fillings is to practice good oral hygiene. Brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and visiting your dentist regularly can help prevent cavities and other dental issues.
  • What Are the Signs I Need to Replace My Fillings? While fillings are durable, they can wear out or become damaged. If you notice any pain, sensitivity, or a rough spot on your filling, contact your dentist in Leawood. Early intervention can prevent more severe issues.

Dental Implant FAQ s

Implant-supported dentures represent a transformative advancement in restorative dentistry, offering a secure and stable alternative to traditional dentures that rely on suction or adhesives. By surgically anchoring titanium posts directly into the jawbone, this procedure creates a permanent foundation that fuses with the bone over a three-to-six-month healing period. Once integrated, specialized connectors allow custom-made dentures to snap firmly into place, providing a comfortable, high-functioning fit that restores both the strength and confidence of a natural smile.

  • What Makes Someone a Good Candidate for Dental Implants? Candidates for dental implants must have healthy gums and adequate bone to support the implant. They should also be committed to maintaining good oral hygiene and regular dental visits. Ideal candidates are non-smokers, do not have conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or heart disease, and have not undergone radiation therapy to the head or neck area. However, each individual’s case is unique, and a thorough evaluation by a dental professional is essential.
  • What Are the Different Types of Dental Implants? There are two main types of dental implants: endosteal and subperiosteal. Endosteal implants are placed directly into the jawbone and are most commonly used. Subperiosteal implants are placed on top of the jawbone but under the gum line and are typically used for patients with shallow jawbones or those who cannot undergo bone augmentation procedures. Additionally, there are different materials used for dental implants, including titanium and zirconia, each with their own advantages and considerations.

Root Canal FAQ s

  • How Do I Know If I Need a Root Canal? Symptoms indicating the need for a root canal include severe toothache, prolonged sensitivity to hot or cold, tenderness or swelling in the gums, and discoloration of the tooth. Sometimes, there are no symptoms, and the need for a root canal is discovered during a routine dental check-up. If you’re experiencing any of these symptoms, it’s crucial to consult with a dentist as soon as possible. Delaying treatment can exacerbate the problem and lead to more complex dental issues.
  • Is Root Canal Therapy Painful? One of the most common fears about root canal therapy is pain. However, modern techniques and anesthesia make the procedure relatively painless. Most patients report that the experience is similar to getting a filling. The real relief comes from alleviating the pain caused by the infection. Post-procedure discomfort is usually mild and can be managed with over-the-counter pain medications. If you experience severe pain or swelling, it’s important to contact your dentist immediately.
  • How Long Does the Procedure Take? The duration of root canal therapy varies depending on the complexity of the case. Simple cases can often be completed in one visit, taking about 60 to 90 minutes. More complicated cases, involving multiple roots or severe infection, may require two or more visits. During your consultation, your dentist will provide an estimate of the time needed for your specific situation. While the procedure may seem time-consuming, it’s a worthwhile investment in your oral health.
  • How Do I Care for My Tooth After the Procedure? After root canal therapy, it’s essential to maintain good oral hygiene to prevent future issues. Brush and floss regularly, and visit your dentist for routine check-ups and cleanings. Avoid chewing on hard foods until your tooth has been fully restored with a crown or filling. Your dentist may also recommend a soft diet for a few days post-procedure to allow the tooth to heal properly. Adhering to these guidelines helps ensure the success of your root canal therapy and the overall health of your teeth.
  • Can a Root Canal Treated Tooth Last a Lifetime? With proper care, a tooth that has undergone root canal therapy can last a lifetime. The key is maintaining excellent oral hygiene and attending regular dental check-ups. A well-restored tooth with a crown or filling will function just like your natural teeth. It’s also important to address any dental issues promptly. Ignoring problems can lead to complications, affecting the longevity of your treated tooth.
