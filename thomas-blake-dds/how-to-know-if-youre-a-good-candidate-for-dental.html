Lifestyle Habits That Affect Implant Outcomes

Smoking is one of the most significant risk factors for implant failure. Tobacco restricts blood flow to the gums and interferes with bone healing, making osseointegration less reliable. We strongly encourage patients who smoke to quit before surgery and remain tobacco-free throughout the healing period to protect their results.

Chronic teeth grinding, known as bruxism, places excess force on implants and can disrupt healing if left unaddressed. A custom night guard effectively addresses this issue and does not preclude you from being a good candidate. Age alone is not a barrier—healthy adults of any age can qualify for implants based on their overall health and bone structure.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

What makes someone a good candidate for dental implants?

Good candidates are adults with one or more missing teeth who have healthy gum tissue, adequate jawbone volume, and no uncontrolled systemic conditions that would interfere with healing. Non-smokers tend to have higher success rates, though smokers who are willing to quit before and after surgery can still qualify. The only way to know for certain is through a comprehensive evaluation that includes a clinical exam and imaging.

Can you get dental implants if you’ve been treated for gum disease?

Yes—as long as the gum disease has been fully treated and your gum health is stable. Active periodontal disease must be resolved before implant placement because ongoing infection significantly raises the risk of implant failure. Once your gums are healthy and you maintain consistent oral hygiene, we can typically move forward with implant planning.

What happens if you don’t have enough jawbone for an implant?

Insufficient bone is common, especially in patients who have been missing a tooth for a year or more. Bone grafting is a well-established procedure that adds volume to areas where the jaw has shrunk. After a healing period—usually several months—most patients who undergo grafting become eligible for implant placement. A sinus lift is another option for patients who need added bone height in the upper jaw.

Does age affect whether you can get dental implants?

Age itself is not a disqualifying factor. Healthy adults in their 60s, 70s, and beyond regularly receive implants with excellent outcomes. What matters most is your overall health and the quality of your bone and gum tissue. We generally do not place implants in patients whose jawbones are still growing, so we typically wait until facial development is complete.

How long does the dental implant process take from start to finish?

The timeline varies based on each patient’s situation. Patients who proceed directly to implant placement typically complete treatment within three to six months, including the healing time required for osseointegration. Patients who need bone grafting or gum disease treatment before placement may face a total timeline of nine months to over a year. We outline a clear schedule for every patient during the consultation so you know what to expect at each stage.
