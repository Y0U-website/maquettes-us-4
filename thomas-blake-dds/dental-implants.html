Learn More about the Benefits of Dental Implants

Single Dental Implant

Dental implants are small anchors made of a biocompatible metal called titanium that are placed in the jawbone to replace missing teeth in the mouth. After placement, the anchors will begin to fuse with the bone over the course of a few months. After the fusing process, known as osseointegration, abutment posts are inserted into the anchors to allow for the permanent attachment of the replacement teeth.

Implant Supported Bridges

An implant supported bridge is a restorative solution for spaces where three or more adjacent teeth are missing. This restoration typically requires two implants to support the bridge that provides a functional and aesthetic replacement for a patient’s natural teeth.

Because of the natural look and feel and the functional stability provided by the implants, implant supported bridges are an effective solution to replace multiple missing teeth.

A dental implant supported bridge replaces missing teeth by placing two or more dental implants to support the bridge instead of the adjacent teeth. These implants avoid putting any additional stress on your natural teeth and help preserve the health and function of the surrounding bone tissue and teeth.

Fully Edentulous (Toothless) Solutions

For patients missing many or all of their teeth, implant retained or implant supported dentures may be an alternative to traditional dentures. These solutions can simulate the look and feel of natural teeth and stay fixed in place with the implants acting as anchors. Using implants for retention or the support of dentures allows for a smaller and more comfortable base and less shifting of the prosthesis during use.

Implant Supported Dentures

An implant supported denture requires the placement of four to six dental implants as “anchors” to provide a sturdier fit and minimal mobility for your denture. With implant supported dentures, there is no plate covering the roof of the mouth, so speaking and eating are more natural and comfortable than with traditional dentures. For qualifying patients, our doctors may be able to complete the removal of all teeth and the placement of a temporary arch on the same day.

Implant Retained Dentures

Available for full or partial dentures, implant retained dentures require the placement of two or more dental implants to secure the dentures in the mouth. This type of denture will “snap” onto the implants and can be removed for cleaning at night.

Following your dentist’s aftercare guidance is essential for the long-term success of your implant and can help you avoid unnecessary complications, such as needing a repair to the restoration down the road.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

What are dental implants, and how do they work?

Dental implants are small anchors made of a biocompatible metal called titanium that are placed in the jawbone during a surgical procedure to replace a single missing tooth or multiple missing teeth. In some cases, bone grafting may be necessary to ensure there’s adequate jawbone volume to support the implant.

After placement, the anchors begin to fuse with the bone over the course of a few months. Once this fusion is complete, abutment posts are inserted into the anchors to permanently attach replacement teeth, giving you a brand-new smile.

What options are available if I’m missing multiple teeth?

An implant-supported bridge may be the ideal restorative solution if you’re missing three or more adjacent teeth. This typically requires two implants to support the bridge, providing functional stability and aesthetic replacement of your teeth.

In cases where jawbone loss has occurred due to gum disease or wearing ill-fitting dentures for extended periods, bone grafting procedures might be recommended to create a solid foundation for your implants.

Implant-supported bridges replace missing teeth without stressing your adjacent remaining teeth and help preserve the health and function of surrounding bone tissue, unlike worn dentures that can accelerate bone loss over time.

What solutions do you offer for patients who are missing all their teeth?

For patients missing all of their teeth, we offer implant-retained or implant-supported dentures as alternatives to traditional dentures. These solutions stay fixed in place with implants acting as anchors, allowing for a smaller, more comfortable base and less shifting during use.

Implant-supported dentures require four to six dental implants and eliminate the plate covering the roof of your mouth, making speaking and eating more natural and comfortable.

Additionally, these implant solutions help maintain facial tissues by preserving jawbone structure, preventing the sunken appearance often associated with long-term traditional denture use.

What is the difference between implant-supported and implant-retained dentures?

Implant-supported dentures require the placement of four to six dental implants to provide a sturdy fit with minimal mobility. They have no plate covering the roof of the mouth, creating a more natural feel.

Implant-retained dentures, available for both full and partial applications, require just two or more dental implants. These dentures "snap" onto the implants and can be removed for cleaning at night.

What are the benefits of choosing dental implants?

Dental implants offer numerous benefits and are the best solution for replacing missing teeth. They help preserve the health of neighboring teeth, maintain bone structure, and contribute to improved chewing ability. Additionally, the dental implant procedure gives patients the confidence to smile, knowing their replacement teeth look, feel, and function like theirs.

For qualifying patients, our dentist may even be able to complete the removal of teeth and placement of a temporary arch on the same day.
