Child tooth extraction FAQs

Does every infected baby tooth need to be removed?

No. The treatment depends on the type and extent of infection, the tooth’s structure, root condition, symptoms, and how close it is to falling out naturally. Some teeth can be treated and preserved, while others are healthier to remove.

Can a permanent tooth come in behind a baby tooth?

Yes. This is fairly common with lower front teeth. If the baby tooth is loosening and the permanent tooth has room, it may resolve with time. An exam is helpful when the baby tooth stays firm, pain develops, or the permanent tooth appears significantly out of position.

Is a space maintainer always needed after an extraction?

No. I consider which tooth was removed, dental age, eruption timing, available space, and the position of nearby teeth. Some children benefit from a space maintainer, while others only need monitoring.

When is swelling an emergency?

Call promptly for increasing gum or facial swelling, fever, drainage, worsening pain, or trouble opening the mouth. Seek emergency medical care for trouble breathing or swallowing, rapidly spreading swelling, or a child who appears seriously ill.
