How Overdentures Work

Overdentures rely on dental implants to provide support and stability. Dental implants are small titanium posts that are surgically placed into the jawbone, acting as artificial tooth roots.

Once the implants integrate with the bone, specialized attachments allow the denture to snap or lock into place. This secure connection prevents movement and provides improved function compared to traditional dentures.

The process typically involves several steps:

1. Comprehensive Dental Evaluation

Your dentist will examine your oral health, jawbone density, and overall dental structure. Advanced imaging technology helps determine whether dental implants are suitable and how many implants are needed.

2. Dental Bone Graft (If Necessary)

Some patients may require a dental bone graft before implant placement. When teeth are missing for a long period of time, the jawbone can begin to shrink due to lack of stimulation.

A bone graft helps rebuild and strengthen the jawbone so it can support dental implants securely. This step is essential for ensuring the long-term success of implant-supported overdentures.

3. Implant Placement

Dental implants are placed into the jawbone during a minor surgical procedure. Over time, the implants fuse with the bone in a process known as osseointegration.

This integration creates a strong and stable foundation for the overdenture.

4. Custom Denture Fabrication

After healing is complete, the dentist creates a custom overdenture that attaches to the implants. The denture is designed to match your facial features and natural smile, creating a beautiful dental design smile that enhances both function and appearance.

Types of Overdentures

Not all overdentures are the same. Depending on your needs and preferences, dentists may recommend different types of overdentures.

Implant-Supported Overdentures

These overdentures attach directly to dental implants placed in the jawbone. They provide exceptional stability and are the most commonly recommended option for patients who want a secure solution.

Typically, two to four implants are placed to support the denture.

Bar-Retained Overdentures

In this system, implants are connected by a thin metal bar. The denture attaches to the bar using clips or attachments, providing excellent support and stability.

Ball-Retained Overdentures

Also known as stud attachments, this design uses ball-shaped connectors on implants that fit into sockets within the denture. The denture snaps into place and can be removed easily for cleaning.

Each option offers unique benefits, and your dentist will recommend the best solution based on your oral health and lifestyle.

Advantages of Overdentures

Overdentures offer numerous advantages compared to traditional dentures. For many patients, this treatment significantly improves both oral health and quality of life.

Improved Stability

Traditional dentures often move or slip during speaking or eating. Overdentures are anchored to implants, which keeps them secure and stable.

Better Chewing Ability

Patients with overdentures can enjoy a wider range of foods, including healthier options that may be difficult to eat with traditional dentures.

Jawbone Preservation

Dental implants stimulate the jawbone similarly to natural tooth roots. This stimulation helps prevent bone loss, which is common after tooth loss.

Enhanced Comfort

Because overdentures rely on implants rather than gum pressure, they reduce irritation and discomfort often associated with traditional dentures.

Natural Appearance

Overdentures are custom designed to complement facial features and restore the natural shape of the smile. This creates a balanced and aesthetically pleasing dental design smile.

The Importance of Bone Health for Overdentures

One of the most critical factors in successful implant-supported overdentures is jawbone health. When teeth are missing, the bone that once supported those teeth gradually deteriorates.

This bone loss can affect facial structure and reduce support for dentures. In many cases, a dental bone graft can rebuild the jawbone and make implant placement possible.

Bone grafting materials stimulate new bone growth and restore the strength needed for implant support. By rebuilding the bone foundation, dentists can provide patients with a more stable and long-lasting solution.
