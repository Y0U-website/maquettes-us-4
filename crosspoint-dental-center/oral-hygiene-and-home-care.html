The Most Beneficial Way to Brush Your Teeth

Ideally, you should brush your teeth at least twice daily most importantly in the morning and right before bed. To protect your mouth, select a small toothbrush with soft, rounded-end bristles that is no more than three months old. The brush head must be compact enough to access the hard-to-reach areas in the back of your mouth, and the bristles must be soft enough to clean effectively without scrubbing away your delicate gum tissue.

Pro Tip: The American Dental Association (ADA) has officially awarded electric toothbrushes their seal of approval, noting that those featuring rotating or oscillating heads are clinically more effective at disrupting plaque than standard manual toothbrushes.

Place your toothbrush bristles at a 45-degree angle pointing directly toward the delicate junction where your gums and teeth meet.

Use small, tight circular motions to gently sweep away plaque along the gumline and individual tooth surfaces. Avoid scrubbing horizontally or applying aggressive pressure, as this can wear down enamel and cause irreversible gum recession.

Meticulously brush every single surface of every tooth: the cheek-side, the tongue-side, and the flat chewing surfaces. Place extra emphasis on cleaning the complex grooves of your back molars using short, back-and-forth strokes.

Gently brush your tongue from back to front to eliminate food debris, fungal cells, and odor-causing bacteria, completely refreshing your mouth.

The Preferred Daily Flossing Technique

Flossing is the absolute single best method to disrupt plaque and bacteria living in the interdental regions (the tight spaces directly between your teeth).Because toothbrush bristles physically cannot slide into these narrow gaps, flossing daily is an essential tool for preventing gum infections and minimizing deep gum pockets.

The flavor, brand, or style of your floss is entirely up to you simply choose a type (waxed, unwaxed, or dental tape) that feels smooth and comfortable to use.
