Dental Implants in Bonham – Restore Your Smile with Lasting Confidence

At our Bonham dental office, we specialize in high-quality dental implants that offer a permanent solution for missing teeth. The implant process involves multiple steps over a few months, but the result is a natural-looking, durable smile that can last a lifetime.

Step 1: Initial Evaluation and Planning Your journey to dental implants begins with a comprehensive exam, including digital 3D Image (CT Scan) and detailed impressions of your teeth and jaw. This allows us to assess bone density, gum health, and the precise spacing needed for a successful implant placement.

Step 2: Implant Placement Once the area is numbed, a titanium implant is surgically placed into the jawbone. This acts as a secure root for your new tooth. The implant will integrate with the bone—a process called osseointegration—which typically takes several months. Depending on the implant type, a second procedure may be necessary to place the post (abutment) that will support the final crown.

Step 3: Custom Restoration After healing, we’ll create and fit your custom artificial teeth. Multiple fittings may be required to ensure perfect alignment, bite, and comfort. Once finalized, the dental crown or bridge is securely attached to the implant, providing excellent function and aesthetics.

Post-Treatment Care You’ll receive thorough care instructions to maintain your new dental implants. Good oral hygiene, a balanced diet, and regular dental checkups are essential for long-term implant success.

How much do most dental insurance pay for implants?

Most dental implant insurance policies pay for a portion of the procedure. However, one must note that the percentage varies with the policy you’ve chosen. For example, some policies cover up to 50 percent of the cost, while a few others may have increased coverage, covering up to 60-70 percent of the dental implant procedure cost.

Is All-on-4 better than snap on dentures?

Snap-on dentures aren’t as robust as natural teeth when it comes to biting and chewing. When the surgeons place implants under the existing denture, they help it remain in place. However, it doesn’t contribute much reinforcement to biting and chewing. Patients will have to eat softer foods cut into smaller pieces. On the other hand, All-on-4 implants provide natural chewing strength and capabilities. They comprise durable materials that replicate the shape and appearance of natural teeth.

How do you take care of all on four dental implants?

Appropriate care can help maintain the longevity and functionality of all-on-four dental implants in the long run. Accordingly, here are some tips.

  • Maintaining oral hygiene – brushing teeth, flossing, and using an antiseptic mouthwash
  • Avoiding eating hard and sticky foods
  • Undergoing regular dental check-ups
  • Staying hydrated and maintaining a healthy and balanced diet
  • Safeguarding teeth against night grinding with a night guard
  • Quitting smoking at the earliest

Are dental implants taken out like dentures for cleaning?

No, dental implants are not removable for cleaning like traditional dentures. Dental implants are permanently fixed in the jawbone, functioning just like natural teeth. They are designed to stay in place and should be cleaned daily with brushing, flossing, and the use of any dentist-recommended tools, such as water flossers or interdental brushes. Regular professional cleanings and check-ups—typically every six months—are also essential to keep the implants and surrounding gum tissue healthy.

How frequently do full-mouth dental implants need to be cleaned and checked by a dentist?

It’s recommended to have your full-mouth dental implants professionally cleaned twice a year to ensure they stay healthy and last for many years.

Does food get under dental implants?

Dental implants are fixed in place along the gumline. Hence, when positioned appropriately, food cannot get stuck underneath dental implants. However, one cannot rule this possibility out if the implants aren’t positioned correctly.
