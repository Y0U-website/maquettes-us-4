Dental implants are typically made of titanium, a biocompatible material that fuses with the jawbone to provide a stable base for tooth restoration.

With proper care and regular dental check-ups, dental implants can last 20 years or longer, often providing a lifetime of use.

The procedure is performed under local anesthesia, ensuring minimal discomfort. Most patients report mild soreness during the recovery phase, which is manageable with over-the-counter pain relievers.

If you have good overall health, a healthy jawbone, and no untreated gum disease, you’re likely a candidate. A consultation with your dentist will determine eligibility.

The process typically takes several months, including healing time after implant placement and attaching the final crown or restoration.

While dental implants have a high success rate (95 % or more), failure can occur due to poor oral hygiene, smoking, or health conditions like diabetes. Regular dental visits help prevent complications.
