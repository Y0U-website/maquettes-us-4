What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are a dental procedure used to replace missing teeth. They consist of an artificial root made of titanium implanted into the jaw bone. Once bonded to the bone, a dental prosthesis is added and fitted, creating the dental restoration that the patient wanted. This could be a dental crown or dental bridge that will serve as a tooth replacement.

Teeth implants resemble natural teeth, and they provide a strong bond in the jaw bone that offers more durability and functionality. However, replacing missing teeth is crucial to maintaining your oral health. Not only can a lost tooth make you feel self-conscious, but it can cause problems such as further tooth loss and the surrounding teeth shifting out of alignment.

Full and partial dentures are not always ideal for many patients, and there are many benefits of dental implants. If you want to replace your traditional dentures or partial denture with a more permanent solution, dental implants could be right for you!

How Are Dental Implants Placed?

Dental implant procedures are a process that takes several appointments to complete. In the first appointment, your provider will take x-rays and molds of your teeth for a dental impression. Your provider will then review all images and x-rays to plan the implant procedure that best fits your needs.

If you are a candidate for dental implants, the implant procedure will begin in your second appointment. This step is performed before dental implant placement for patients that require bone grafting. Your dental implant surgery will usually last between one and two hours, depending on the procedure that needs to take place for your teeth implants.

First, our oral surgeon will place the implant posts. Next, they will incise the gum line to expose the jawbone and drill a hole for the implant. After the titanium implant is placed, the surgeon will put a temporary crown or fixture over the space while the gums and jaw bone heal. The healing process can take anywhere between three and six months.

After healing, you will return to the office to place the abutment and artificial tooth. Soon, you will have a restored, beautiful smile that you will love for a lifetime.
