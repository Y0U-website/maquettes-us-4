How Traditional Braces Work

Like Invisalign, braces put pressure on the teeth to gradually shift them into a straighter position. However, what makes these two systems different is that traditional metal brackets are attached to the front of the teeth. A metal wire connects the brackets and is held in place with tiny rubber bands that are positioned over the brackets. Then, Dr. Bruno will adjust the wire to slowly and gently force the teeth into ideal alignment. You may experience some mild discomfort afterward, but it subsides after a few days. Periodically, you’ll return to our office for another adjustment.

Benefits of Traditional Braces

Although the brackets and wires aren’t as subtle as Invisalign, traditional braces have many advantages, including the following:

  • Effectiveness for nearly all cases . Braces can correct the most severe dental problems, including overcrowding, crooked teeth, gaps, and even significant misalignment.
  • Easy compliance . You don’t have to worry about misplacing or losing your braces because they’re attached directly to your teeth.
  • Affordability . In most cases, traditional braces cost less than Invisalign, and this treatment is covered by more insurance plans than clear aligner systems.
  • Reliability . Traditional braces have been used for decades to realign teeth, making them one of the most sought-after, proven effective solutions available.

How to Be Successful with Braces

To have a straight, healthy, and beautiful smile at the end of treatment, you need to follow the instructions provided by Dr. Bruno. Here are some examples of the things you can do to ensure successful results:

  • Attend all of your follow-up, adjustment appointments.
  • Make sure you brush and floss your teeth daily, preferably after each time you eat.
  • Avoid eating sticky foods like gum, gummy candy, or dried fruit.
  • Stay away from hard foods that may dislodge a bracket or the wire such as popcorn or uncut apples or carrots.
  • Manage any discomfort after adjustments by eating soft foods and taking over-the-counter pain medicine.
  • Contact us right away if a bracket comes loose or the wire pokes out.

Traditional Braces FAQs

Am I Too Old for Braces?

Although metal brackets and wires are often associated with adolescents, patients of all ages can receive traditional braces. Just keep in mind that it’s usually easier and faster to make alignment changes at a younger age. During your consultation, Dr. Bruno will discuss all your orthodontic treatment options, including braces and Invisalign, and help you find the solution that will help you meet your goals, stay within your budget, and achieve your best smile!

Can I Get Braces on Just My Top or Bottom Teeth?

In certain circumstances, it is possible to focus treatment exclusively on the upper or lower teeth. This approach can seem particularly appealing if only one arch seems to hurt or be causing the aesthetic problem. However, many times, both arches need to be realigned in unison to create a perfect bite. When you meet with Dr. Bruno, be sure to mention any questions or concerns you have about addressing your smile. We’re happy to take as much time as you need to help you feel confident about your treatment plan.

How Long Do Braces Take?

No one else’s smile is quite like yours, which means your treatment timeline may not be the same. Timing may vary depending on the types and severity of alignment issues you have and how well you follow instructions. However, most patients who undergo orthodontic treatment with traditional braces need between two and three years to make all the necessary alignment changes. We can discuss the duration of your treatment plan at your consultation.

Do Traditional Braces Hurt?

Any orthodontic treatment, whether through braces or Invisalign, involves shifting teeth within the jawbone. As a result, you will likely experience some amount of soreness at some point during the process, especially in the beginning and when you have your braces adjusted. At first, as you become accustomed to the metal brackets and wires, your soft tissues may be slightly irritated, but over time, you’ll find they won’t bother your mouth.
