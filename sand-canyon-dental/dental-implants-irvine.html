What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are used to support replacement teeth after one or more natural teeth have been lost.

A typical implant-supported restoration may include three main components.

Dental Implant

The dental implant is the part placed into the jawbone to provide support for the replacement tooth or restoration.

It functions as a foundation for the restorative components placed above it.

Abutment

An abutment is a connector placed between the implant and the visible dental restoration.

Its specific design depends on the type of implant restoration being used.

Dental Crown or Restoration

The visible portion of implant treatment may be a crown or another type of restoration designed to replace the missing tooth or teeth.

Not every implant case follows exactly the same treatment sequence, and the type of restoration depends on the patient’s individual needs.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants may provide several advantages for appropriately selected patients.

Dental implants should not automatically be described as permanent or lifetime solutions.

Their longevity can be influenced by oral hygiene, gum health, bone health, medical conditions, smoking, bite forces, habits, the condition of the restoration, and ongoing professional care.

  • Replacing one or more missing teeth
  • Supporting chewing function
  • Providing a fixed tooth replacement option in suitable cases
  • Avoiding preparation of neighboring teeth in some single-tooth situations
  • Supporting certain multi-tooth restorations
  • Supporting some types of implant-retained dentures
  • Restoring the appearance of a missing tooth
  • Providing long-term tooth replacement potential with appropriate care

Types of Dental Implant Tooth Replacement

Dental implants can be used in different ways depending on how many teeth need to be replaced.

Single Tooth Dental Implant

A single implant may sometimes be used to support a crown that replaces one missing tooth.

This can provide an alternative to other tooth replacement options in suitable cases.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Dental implants can sometimes be used to provide additional stability or retention for certain types of dentures.

This differs from conventional removable dentures that rely primarily on the gums and supporting tissues.

Multiple Tooth Replacement

When several teeth are missing, dental implants may sometimes support a restoration that replaces multiple teeth.

The number of implants required does not always match the exact number of missing teeth.

Full-Arch Implant Treatment

In certain cases, dental implants may be used to support a full-arch restoration replacing most or all teeth in an upper or lower arch.

Implant-Supported Bridge

An implant-supported bridge may use dental implants as support for a restoration replacing multiple missing teeth.

The specific design depends on the patient’s anatomy and treatment needs.
