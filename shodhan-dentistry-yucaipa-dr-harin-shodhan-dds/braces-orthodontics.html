Frequently Asked Questions

Are braces painful? Like any type of orthodontic treatment, braces are bound to cause soreness or pain from time to time. These sensations occur because orthodontic appliances force your teeth to move through the jawbone and the gums. Typically, soreness occurs at the beginning of your treatment and when your braces are adjusted to advance the treatment and subsides within several days. Additionally, if braces rub against the soft tissue in the mouth and cause sores, you can use special dental wax to relieve the irritation.

What is the difference between braces and clear aligners? Both braces and clear aligners function in a similar way: they apply pressure to the teeth to make them gradually move and align in a desired way. Traditional braces are bonded to the teeth and have metal wires. Clear aligners are transparent plastic trays that are less noticeable in the mouth than braces and can be removed at will. Typically, aligners are considered more comfortable in daily use than traditional braces, but they may not be suitable for treating severe or complex cases of tooth and bite misalignment.

How long does it take to get braces? Typically, it takes 2 to 4 weeks to get braces, from your first consultation to the braces application. The appointment to bond braces to your teeth typically takes 1 to 2 hours.

Are braces treatment covered by insurance? The majority of dental insurance plans cover orthodontic treatments, including braces, only for dependents under the age of 18. However, braces coverage may also depend on whether or not it is a medical necessity or a purely cosmetic procedure.

What food should I avoid with braces? When wearing braces, it is recommended to avoid hard, crunchy, and sticky foods, as they can damage or break the wires, making braces ineffective. Examples of food you should avoid with braces include nuts, popcorn, crackers, toffee, raw carrots, etc.

Will I need to wear a retainer after braces? You may need to wear a retainer after braces if there is a risk of your teeth shifting back to their original positions. Some people may be recommended to wear a retainer for 1 to 2 years, while others will need to wear their retainer for longer (sometimes indefinitely at night) to preserve the results of their treatment.
