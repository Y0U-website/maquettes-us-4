Frequently Asked Questions

How much do dental implants cost? One dental implant costs between $3,000 and $6,000, depending on the type of implant and the materials used. This price typically includes 3D X-rays or CT scans, the surgery itself, post-surgery care, and a dental crown. If you require additional procedures, such as tooth extractions, bone grafting, or treatments for gum disease and tooth infections, you will need to pay for them separately.

Are dental implants better than dentures or bridges? In general, dental implants are superior in function and comfort to dentures and bridges, as they offer greater stability, greater chewing and biting force, and help prevent jawbone density loss. However, implants are not an ideal solution for everyone. People with insufficient jawbone density, gum disease, or general health issues that hinder healing and regeneration might not be able to undergo successful implant surgery. In such cases, dental bridges or dentures can be a better and more reliable solution.

How long does the dental implant process take? The entire process of getting dental implants typically takes 4 to 8 months. After the initial surgery, your new implant has to fuse with the jawbone and become stable. Younger people with strong regenerative capacity may go through this process within 4 months, while others may need up to 8 months before the crown can be attached to the implant and the new tooth is fully functional. If you require additional procedures before dental implants (for example, tooth extraction or grafting), the process will take 4 to 8 additional months.

What is the success rate of dental implants? When the dental implant surgeries are performed responsibly and correctly, the success rate is high, reaching 98 %. Typically, to have a successful dental implant surgery, a patient must have good oral and general health. Issues like gum disease, diabetes, and autoimmune diseases can make the dental implant surgery risky or inadvisable.

What happens if I don’t replace a missing tooth with an implant? When you lose a natural tooth, your body stops sending nutrients to the area of the jawbone where the tooth used to be. As a result, the bone begins to lose density, leading to changes in tooth and bite alignment and in facial structure. Jawbone density loss (also called bone resorption) can prevent successful dental implant placement in the future, as implants will not have enough support and will not be stable. That is why it is recommended to replace a missing tooth with an implant within the first year.
