Dental Implants in Glendale, CA

Dental implants are built to provide a strong foundation for replacement teeth that look, feel, and function exactly like your original teeth. The implants are metal anchors that function as artificial tooth roots and are surgically integrated into your jawbone. Small posts are attached to the implants that protrude through the gum tissues. These posts provide a stable foundation for your artificial replacement teeth.

Dental implants have several benefits over other tooth replacement options. They can help retain your facial structure by avoiding bone loss after tooth loss. They offer a permanent solution to missing teeth, looking and functioning just like natural teeth.

Process of Dental Implants

For most patients, two surgical procedures are required to insert dental implants. The first step is to insert them into your jawbone. The first three to six months are crucial. The implant is slowly fusing with your jawbone at this stage. It is advisable to wear temporary dentures and eat a soft diet during this time. Your dentist works simultaneously to create the final bridgework or denture, improving both the appearance and function of your oral cavity.

Next Steps After Getting Dental Implants

The second stage begins after the implant has completely merged with the jawbone. The surgeon will reveal the implants and attach a small healing collar before proceeding with the creation of your new teeth. A picture must be taken. Following this, the attachments or posts are fixed to the implants. The replacement teeth are then designed to fit over those attachments or posts. The entire procedure takes approximately six to eight months. Most patients experience no disruptions in their daily activities following this procedure.

FAQ

  • How long do dental implants last? With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime.
  • How many dental implants can be done at a time? You can receive one implant or multiple during the same appointment, depending on your oral health and treatment plan.
  • How often do dental implants fail? Implant success rates are very high; failures are rare and usually caused by infection, bone loss, or inadequate aftercare.
