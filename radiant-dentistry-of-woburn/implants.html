What Happens If You Leave a Missing Tooth Untreated

A gap in your smile is not just a cosmetic issue. Over time, the jawbone beneath the missing tooth begins to shrink because it no longer has a root to stimulate it. Adjacent teeth can shift into the empty space, causing bite problems and making cleaning harder. You may also experience changes in your facial structure. What starts as a single missing tooth can lead to more complex and costly dental problems down the road. Addressing it early with an implant preserves your bone and protects your other teeth.

Bone Preservation and Long-Term Oral Health

The implant post acts like a tooth root, stimulating the jawbone each time you chew. This activity helps prevent the bone loss that usually follows tooth loss. By preserving your bone structure, implants also help maintain your facial shape and support your remaining teeth.

Am I a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Who Qualifies — and What If You Don t (Yet)

Most healthy adults with a missing tooth or teeth are candidates for implants. You need enough bone in your jaw to hold the implant securely, and your gums should be free of active disease. If you smoke, we will talk about steps you can take to support healing. During your first visit, we will perform a thorough exam and CBCT scan to determine if implants are right for you.

How Bone Grafting Expands Candidacy

If your bone is too thin or soft for an implant, do not give up. Bone grafting can restore the lost bone and create a solid foundation for the implant. The graft material is placed in the jaw, and after a few months of healing, your bone density will be sufficient for the implant. This process makes implant treatment accessible to many patients who would otherwise be told they are not candidates.
