What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are a permanent solution for patients seeking that perfect white smile. Implants are a great choice for patients that have broken, damaged, or missing teeth. Implants act similar to an artificial tooth that is placed in the jawbone. They are extremely durable, and are built to last upwards of 25 years. Dental implants function like our natural teeth, and allow patients to speak and eat normally. They are comfortable in the mouth, and do not cause gum or mouth irritation. If cared for properly they can last a lifetime.

What are the Benefits of Dental Implants?

  • Preserve the natural bone in the mouth, and help to stimulate new bone growth.
  • Durable, providing long term value financially compared to other restorative solutions.
  • Permanent, and do not slip or click like other solutions.
  • Comfortable, and look and feel natural in the mouth.
  • Can last upwards of 25 years, or a lifetime if cared for properly.
  • Restore full chewing power, and are as strong as natural teeth.

What to Expect During the Dental Implant Process

The process requires multiple appointments. A consultation discusses goals and determines the best treatment plan. The first step involves placing the implant into the jawbone, followed by a healing period. The healing period allows the implant to fuse to the jawbone through a process called osseointegration, allowing it to function like a natural tooth root. The patient must have enough bone present in the jaw; if necessary, bone grafts can provide additional support.

Once healing is complete, the patient returns to complete the process. An abutment or connector is placed on top of the implant, holding the crown securely in place. Custom crowns are made to match the shape, size, and color of your natural teeth. Temporary crowns may be necessary until the permanent crown is installed.

3D Guided Implant Placement

Prior to placement, a simple 10-second scan evaluates the underlying anatomy of the teeth, sinuses, and jaws. This information allows the team to virtually plan placement using computer-guided technology and develop a surgical guide. This planning results in more efficient, faster procedures with greater precision and margins of safety, and more predictable final restorations. The entire dental implant process is handled in-office, from start to finish.
