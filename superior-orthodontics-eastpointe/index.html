The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first screening by age 7 . It’s a quick look at how things are developing, and it doesn’t mean treatment starts right away.

What it is: Early orthodontics, sometimes called Phase 1 treatment, works with the years when a child’s jaw is still growing and baby teeth are making way for permanent ones. Guiding that growth at the right time can open up room for incoming teeth and set up a healthier bite.

A screening by age 7 just gives us a baseline. Plenty of kids are better served by watching and waiting than jumping into treatment, and we’ll tell you straight when that’s the case.

Who it’s for: kids around 7 to 10 with crowding, teeth coming in early or late, mouth breathing, thumb-sucking, or a bite that looks off. Worth a look too if your dentist has already flagged something.
