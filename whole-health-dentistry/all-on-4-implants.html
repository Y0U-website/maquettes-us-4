How the Procedure Works

The name describes the idea: a full arch of teeth supported by four implants. Two are placed vertically toward the front of the jaw, and two are angled up to 45 degrees toward the back. That tilt is what lets the posterior implants avoid the maxillary sinus and the mandibular nerve, and it&#x27;s also what allows four fixtures to carry a full arch without the extensive grafting that traditional implant placements often require.

That angle correction is made possible by a component called an angled multi-unit abutment, which is the connector piece between the implant and the prosthesis, and it is a titanium part. Because the geometry of the protocol depends on that component, All-on-4 is inherently a titanium treatment. There is also a design detail worth knowing called platform switching, where the abutment is slightly narrower than the implant platform itself, and this helps protect the bone at the implant collar over time.

The Benefits of All-on-4 Implants

All-on-4 does more than fill the space where teeth used to be. A fixed, implant-supported arch changes how you eat, how you feel in conversation, and how you think about your mouth from day to day. Here is what that actually looks like:

  • Teeth that stay put. There are no adhesives to apply, nothing to remove at night, and no shifting while you talk or eat. The arch is fixed in place and behaves the way natural teeth do, because it is anchored the same way.

  • Graft avoidance for many patients. Because the posterior implants are angled to work with the bone you already have, many patients who expected to need extensive grafting find they can move forward without it.
  • Coordinated care from start to finish. Dr. Copeland designs your prosthesis, stays in close contact with the oral surgeon handling placement, and sees you for most of your follow-up visits, so your care never feels fragmented or handed off.

Preparing for Your Visit and Caring for the Result

Preparation for this kind of treatment is straightforward, but a few things genuinely make a difference, both on surgery day and in the weeks that follow.

Before your procedure

  • Bring a record of past sensitivities or reactions. If you have ever had a reaction to a dental material, medication, or anesthetic, let us know before your appointment. That history directly shapes how we plan your care, and it is easier to account for ahead of time than to work around in the chair.
  • Bring any appliances you wear in your mouth. Retainers, nightguards, and similar devices should come with you to any appointment where a new restoration is being placed. We check the fit against what you already wear before you leave, so there are no surprises at home.
  • Arrange a ride home on surgery day. Sedation is available, and you will not be in a position to drive yourself afterward. Plan for someone to stay with you for the first several hours while the effects wear off.
  • Stock up on soft foods in advance. Smoothies, scrambled eggs, soups, and similar options will carry you comfortably through the first several days. Having them ready before surgery removes one thing to manage while you are focused on resting and healing.

After your arch is in place

Once healing is complete, the implants and the tissue around them still need consistent daily attention. A fixed arch is not maintenance-free, and the habits you build early are what protect the result long term.

  • Clean under and around the arch each day. We will walk you through exactly how at your follow-up visits, so you leave feeling confident about managing it at home rather than guessing.
  • Keep up with professional maintenance visits. Dr. Copeland monitors the health of your implants and surrounding gum tissue at these appointments. They are not optional once you are healed; they are part of how the result stays healthy over time.
  • Follow your soft-food guidance early on, then return to normal eating gradually as comfort allows. The transition happens naturally for most people, but it is worth letting your body set the pace rather than pushing it.
  • Tell Dr. Copeland about any lingering soreness or changes in your bite. Small adjustments are completely normal and straightforward to address when they are caught early, so do not wait to bring something up if it feels off.

Why does All-on-4 use titanium instead of ceramic?

The protocol depends on angled multi-unit abutments, which are titanium components that correct the angle of the posterior implants so that four fixtures can carry a full arch. Because one-piece zirconia implants cannot be angle-corrected in the same way, the All-on-4 design simply is not achievable without titanium. The geometry of the protocol requires it.

Can I get a full arch without any metal?

Yes, though it is a different treatment with different requirements. Dr. Copeland refers to full-arch zirconia as "Great on 8" because it uses eight implants placed in native bone rather than four angled ones. Adequate bone volume, particularly near the sinus, is the key factor in determining whether that path is available to you. If it interests you, bring it up at your consultation and we will assess whether your anatomy supports it.

What if I&#x27;ve been told I don&#x27;t have enough bone?

For All-on-4 specifically, limited bone is often not disqualifying. The posterior implants are tilted up to 45 degrees precisely to work with the bone that is already there, which is the reason the protocol was designed to reduce the need for grafting in the first place.
