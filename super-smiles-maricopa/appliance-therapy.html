Overbite

  • Which is when the upper front teeth stick out too far over the lower front teeth.

Underbite

  • Which is when the lower front teeth stick out too far over the upper front teeth.

Open bite

  • Which is when there is a gap between the upper and lower front teeth when the mouth is closed.

Crowding

  • which is when there is not enough space for all the teeth to fit properly in the mouth.

Snoring

  • which is a loud noise that occurs when the airway is partially blocked during sleep.

Obstructive sleep apnea

  • which is a serious condition that occurs when the airway is completely blocked during sleep, causing the breathing to stop and start repeatedly.

Types of Appliances

Our Oral appliance therapy service involves various types of appliances that are customized to your child’s needs and goals. Some of the common types of appliances are:

  • Palatal Expanders Palatal expanders are devices that are attached to the upper teeth and apply gentle pressure to widen the palate and create more space for the teeth and the airway.
  • Myofunctional Appliances Myofunctional appliances are devices that are worn in the mouth and help to train the tongue, lips, and cheeks to function and posture properly and to improve the breathing pattern.
  • Orthodontic Appliances Orthodontic appliances are devices that are worn in the mouth and help to move and straighten the teeth and correct the bite.

We will use local anesthesia to numb your child’s mouth and sedation dentistry to help your child relax and cooperate. We will also communicate with you and your child, and adjust the appliance as needed.

You need to follow our instructions on how to care for your child’s oral health, such as brushing, flossing, rinsing, and eating. You also need to watch for any signs of complications, such as pain, swelling, infection, or irritation, and contact us if you have any concerns.
