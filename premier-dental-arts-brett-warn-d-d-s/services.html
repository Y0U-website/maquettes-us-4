What is the difference between preventive dental care and restorative dentistry?

Preventive dental care involves the services received during your regular check-up and cleaning appointments. X-ray imaging, a thorough examination of your mouth, and teeth cleaning services are all part of preventive dental care.

Restorative dentistry is also known as reconstructive dentistry for various tooth conditions.

When are dental implants, the best treatment option?

If you have lost one or more teeth due to trauma or decay, replacing those teeth with dental implants should be a priority. Aside from the cosmetic benefits of dental implants, numerous health issues come with missing teeth.

Your natural teeth provide ongoing stimulation of your jaw bone, which promotes proper bone density and strength. When one or more teeth are missing, bone loss can soon follow. Dental implants provide the same bone stimulation as natural teeth and help to sustain healthy bone tissue.

Missing teeth may also cause problems with proper speech, eating, and gum tissue. Using today’s cutting-edge dental implant procedures, Dr. Warn can restore missing teeth with a permanent solution.

How are dental implants put into place?

The process of getting dental implants requires several office visits. You begin with a consultation, where Dr. Warn explains the procedure and answers any questions you might have. Through a surgical procedure, the anchor for the implant is placed into the bone tissue of your jaw.

A second procedure may be required to attach the post of the implant to the anchor, depending on the type of implant that is best for you.

Specialized imaging technology fabricates the visible portion of the implant. Once your gums have healed, Dr. Warn has you back in to fit the artificial tooth onto the post. Finally, he makes any necessary adjustments to the size or shape of the implant, and Dr. Warn explains how you can care for your gums and support your new dental implant.

What are the benefits of dentures over other tooth replacement options?

Today’s technology makes it possible to create custom-fit dentures that are nearly indistinguishable from natural teeth. Plus, dentures offer a fast and affordable way to restore your smile. With proper care, they can last for many years.

Partial dentures can be anchored to adjacent teeth and can be crafted to match the color and shape of your natural teeth. If you take care to rinse and brush them regularly and avoid biting down on hard food or using your dentures to pry open bottles or other objects, you can expect years of use out of your initial investment.

What are some disadvantages of dentures?

Even though today’s dentures are light years beyond those that our grandparents had access to, they still have some limitations.

Dentures can slip and shift out of position, requiring the use of special adhesives. Some people have trouble finding the right fit. Some experience difficulty in eating or speaking as a result. Dentures also demand replacement from time to time.

What are implant-retained dentures?

Implant retained dentures offer a stable anchor for artificial teeth on both your upper and lower gum line.

The process involves the surgical placement of multiple implants into the bone of your jaw. These implants then act as anchors that allow the specially constructed dentures to snap onto the implants.

If you are missing multiple teeth, implant-retained dentures might be a good option for restoring your smile. Online scheduling makes it easy to select an appointment time, and Dr. Warn can work with you to determine the best approach that suits your needs and budget, guiding you through the process from beginning to end.

What are overdentures?

Overdentures are the actual dentures (false teeth) that secure into the implants through retentive attachments. Overdentures are removable and can be made for the top and bottom teeth.
