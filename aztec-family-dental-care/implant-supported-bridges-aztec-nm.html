Implant-Supported Bridges: Your Solution for Smile Gaps

Unlike removable dentures or partials, an implant-supported bridge is a permanent solution when you want to fill the gaps in your smile and replace your missing teeth. An implant-supported bridge is secured in place by two dental implants, one on each side of the gap left by one or more missing teeth. With an implant-supported bridge, you regain your functionality and have increased stability.

Implant-Supported Bridges vs. Traditional Bridges

A traditional bridge is secured by two healthy natural teeth, and implant-supported bridges are anchored by dental implants. When you go with a traditional bridge, a portion of your natural tooth has to be filed down for the bridge to fit properly. This process can affect the health of neighboring teeth, which might lead to issues down the road.

Advantages of an Implant-Supported Bridge

An implant-supported bridge has many advantages over traditional bridges. These include:

  • Long-lasting durability: With proper care, an implant-supported bridge can last decades. Traditional bridges often need to be replaced after 10-15 years.
  • Prevents bone loss: Dental implants stimulate the jawbone, which keeps the bone from deteriorating over time. Traditional bridges do not prevent bone loss and, in fact, may actually contribute to it.
  • Stability and function are enhanced: An implant-supported bridge is secured by dental implants, so it doesn’t shift around or slip. A traditional bridge can feel less stable, especially as more time passes.
  • Preservation of adjacent teeth: For a traditional bridge, the adjacent teeth have to be filed down a bit and used as anchors. This can lead to tooth decay in these teeth. Dental implants don’t have this issue.
  • Natural appearance and feel: Implant-supported bridges very closely resemble the look and feel of natural teeth.
