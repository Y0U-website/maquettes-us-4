Understanding Dental Implants

What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are permanent solutions for missing teeth, designed to look, feel, and function like your natural teeth. Comprised of three parts: the implant (a titanium screw that fuses with the bone), the abutment (which connects the implant to the replacement tooth), and the crown (the visible part designed to resemble a natural tooth).

How Do Dental Implants Work?

The process begins with the implantation of a titanium post into the jawbone, which serves as a sturdy foundation for the replacement tooth. Over time, the implant integrates with the bone, a process known as osseointegration, providing stability and longevity to your new tooth.

Dental Implant Restorations

Dental Crown

A dental crown is used to cover a single-tooth implant, completing your smile with a new tooth that looks natural and blends seamlessly with the rest of your teeth. Each crown is custom-made to match the color and shape of your surrounding teeth, ensuring a uniform appearance.

Dental Bridge

For those missing several teeth in a row, a dental bridge supported by one or two implants offers a fixed solution that restores function and aesthetics. This type of restoration bridges the gap where teeth are absent, preventing the remaining teeth from shifting.

Implant-Supported Denture

Implant-supported dentures provide a more stable and secure alternative to traditional dentures. By attaching the denture to two or more dental implants, patients enjoy improved comfort, enhanced chewing ability, and increased confidence as the dentures stay firmly in place.

The Benefits of Dental Implants

Longevity and Durability

Dental implants are designed to be a long-lasting solution and, with proper care, can last a lifetime. They are also immune to dental diseases that affect natural teeth, like decay.

Looks and Feels Natural

Implants are crafted to match the look and feel of your natural teeth. This not only improves your smile but also enhances your chewing ability and overall oral health.

Exploring Types of Dental Implants

Endosteal Implants

Endosteal implants are the most common type of dental implants placed directly into the jawbone. They are typically used when the jawbone has enough strength and volume to support the implanted metal post.

- Single-Tooth Implant

A single-tooth implant is used to replace one missing tooth. This procedure involves implanting a titanium post directly into the jawbone, followed by placing an abutment, and then attaching a crown. It serves as a standalone unit and does not affect neighboring teeth.

- Mini Dental Implant (MDI)

Mini Dental Implants (MDIs) are smaller in diameter than traditional endosteal implants. They are often used in situations where the jawbone is not sufficient to support a full-sized implant or for patients looking for a less invasive and more affordable option. MDIs are commonly utilized to stabilize dentures or support a small fixed crown or bridge.

Subperiosteal Implants

These implants are placed on top of the jawbone but under the gum layer. They are suitable for patients who do not have enough healthy jawbone and are not candidates for bone augmentation.

Zygomatic Implants

The least common type, zygomatic implants are anchored in the cheekbone rather than the jawbone. They are typically used when there is significant jawbone loss and standard implants are not an option.
