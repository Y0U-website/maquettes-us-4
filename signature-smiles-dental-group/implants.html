Dental Implants A Complete Solution for Missing Teeth in Milpitas

How a Dental Implant Is Built Three Parts

Every implant works as a three-part system. Understanding it helps explain both the timeline and the cost.

  • Titanium post surgically placed in the jaw, acting as the artificial root that fuses with bone (osseointegration).
  • Abutment the connector piece that attaches to the post and supports the visible restoration.
  • Crown, bridge, or denture the custom, tooth-shaped restoration Dr. Khera designs and places in our Milpitas office.

Dental Implant Solutions We Offer in Milpitas

Whether you are missing one tooth, several in a row, or an entire arch, there is an implant-based solution designed for your case. Dr. Khera recommends the right approach for Milpitas patients at your consultation.

A single titanium post topped with a custom dental crown replaces one missing tooth without grinding down the healthy teeth on either side the key advantage over a traditional bridge.

Implant-Supported Bridge Several teeth in a row

When several adjacent teeth are missing, two implant posts can support a fixed bridge restoring more teeth with fewer implants while preserving your natural enamel and bite strength.

Implant-Supported Dentures A removable, secure alternative

Several strategically placed implants anchor a snap-in denture that locks firmly in place eliminating the slipping, rubbing, and adhesives of a traditional removable denture, with far greater chewing stability.

Full-Arch & All-on-4 Implants A complete upper or lower arch

For patients missing most or all of an arch, as few as four implants can support a full set of fixed teeth. This proven full-mouth approach is faster and less invasive than placing an individual implant for every tooth.

Implant Crown Materials Choosing the Right Restoration

The material for your implant restoration is chosen based on the position of the tooth, your bite force, and your aesthetic priorities. Dr. Khera reviews each option and recommends the best fit for your case at our Milpitas office.

Porcelain-Fused-to-Zirconia

  • Natural, enamel-like translucency
  • Zirconia substructure for durability
  • Ideal for front and visible back teeth
  • Stain-resistant, biocompatible surface
  • Lifespan: 15 25 years with care

Full-Contour Zirconia

  • Single solid material no layering
  • Highest fracture resistance available
  • Ideal for molars and grinding patients
  • Slightly less translucent than porcelain
  • Lifespan: 20+ years

All-Ceramic (E-Max)

  • Lithium disilicate most lifelike
  • Superior light transmission
  • Preferred for high-visibility front teeth
  • Not suited to high-force molar positions
  • Lifespan: 15 20 years

Screw-Retained vs. Cement-Retained Restorations

Implant restorations attach to the abutment in one of two ways. Screw-retained crowns are secured through the biting surface and are easy to remove for maintenance. Cement-retained crowns are bonded like a traditional crown and are preferred when screw access would compromise aesthetics. Dr. Khera selects the method based on implant angle and tooth position.

The Complete Dental Implant Process Start to Finish

Getting a dental implant is a coordinated, multi-phase process managed by Dr. Khera at our Milpitas practice from the first consultation through final delivery. Here is exactly what to expect.

Consultation & Treatment Planning

Dr. Khera evaluates your bone density, gum health, and bite using digital X-rays and a comprehensive exam . Any existing gum disease or decay is addressed before placement. You receive a full plan, timeline, and cost breakdown at no charge.

Preparatory Work (If Needed)

Some patients need an extraction of a failing tooth, treatment of gum disease, or a bone graft before placement can proceed. These steps give the implant a healthy, adequate foundation for long-term stability.

Implant Post Placement

The titanium post is surgically placed into the jawbone by our coordinating specialist oral surgeon, performed under local anesthesia with sedation available. A temporary restoration may be placed to maintain your appearance during healing.

Osseointegration Healing (3 4 Months)

The post fuses with the surrounding jawbone through a biological process called osseointegration. This integration is what gives implants their remarkable stability and is the reason the full process takes several months. Dr. Khera monitors healing at check-ups.

Abutment Placement

Once integration is confirmed, the abutment connector is attached to the post, creating the platform on which your restoration will be seated. Some cases allow the abutment to be placed at the same time as the post Dr. Khera determines the right approach for you.

Digital Impressions & Design

Precise 3D digital impressions are taken with our intraoral scanner no messy putty trays. Tooth shade is matched to your natural teeth, and the specifications are sent to a precision ceramics lab to fabricate your custom restoration.

Final Placement & Bite Check

Your custom restoration is tried in for fit, shade, and bite, then permanently secured. The occlusion is refined for comfortable, even contact. Most patients leave with a fully functional, natural-looking result the same day.

Total Treatment Timeline: 3 to 6 Months

The majority of this time is the osseointegration healing period (3 4 months), during which nothing is happening clinically. Active treatment impressions, fabrication, and delivery adds roughly 2 3 weeks at the end. Complex cases requiring a bone graft may add 3 6 months before placement.

Implant vs. Dental Bridge vs. Denture

When a tooth is missing, three primary replacement options exist. For Milpitas patients, an implant is the closest restoration to a natural tooth in structure, function, and longevity.

Are You a Candidate for Dental Implants in Milpitas?

Most adults with one or more missing teeth are candidates. A consultation with Dr. Khera at our Milpitas office confirms whether implants are right for you or whether a preparatory step is needed first.

Good Candidacy Factors

  • Adults with one or more missing teeth
  • Adequate jawbone volume to anchor the post
  • Healthy gums, or willing to treat gum disease first
  • Non-smokers, or willing to quit during healing
  • Committed to biannual professional cleanings

Common Situations We Treat

  • A single tooth lost to decay or fracture
  • A tooth extracted after a failed root canal
  • A congenitally missing tooth that never developed
  • Teeth lost to advanced gum disease
  • Tooth loss from trauma or a sports injury

Requires Preparation First

  • Active gum disease (treated first)
  • Insufficient bone (graft may be needed)
  • Uncontrolled diabetes (must be managed)
  • Active smoking during the healing period
  • Certain medications affecting bone metabolism
