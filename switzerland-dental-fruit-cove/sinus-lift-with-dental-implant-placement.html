Sinus Lift with Dental Implant Placement

A sinus lift is a safe and standard procedure that creates enough bone in the upper back jaw to support a dental implant. If you have been missing upper molars or premolars for a while, the bone in that area may have become thin. The sinus cavity can also expand over time. When this happens, there may not be enough healthy bone to anchor an implant securely. A sinus lift gently raises the sinus membrane and adds bone material to build a strong foundation for implant placement.

Why a Sinus Lift May Be Needed

Dental implants need solid bone for long-term success. In the upper jaw, the sinus cavity sits just above the roots of the back teeth. When teeth are lost, the bone naturally shrinks. A dental implant placed in a site with insufficient bone height may fail without adjunctive support. A sinus lift procedure safely and effectively rebuilds lost bone height to allow easy implant placement.

How the Procedure Works

The process begins with detailed digital imaging to evaluate bone height and sinus position. After numbing the area, a small opening is made in the bone. The sinus membrane is carefully lifted upward. Bone graft material is then placed into the space created beneath the membrane. This added bone supports future implant placement.

In some cases, the dental implant can be placed during the same visit. In other situations, the bone graft needs several months to heal and strengthen before the implant is inserted. Your treatment plan will depend on the amount of existing bone and your overall oral health.

What to Expect After Surgery

Mild swelling, light bleeding, or minor discomfort are typical for a few days. Cold compresses can help reduce swelling during the first 24 hours. Pain medication and, if prescribed, antibiotics should be taken exactly as directed.

After 10–14 days following the procedure, do not blow your nose, use a straw, or sneeze forcefully. Doing this can put pressure on the sinus area and disrupt healing. If you sneeze, do it with your mouth open to relieve pressure. Eat soft foods for a few days, avoiding the surgical area when you chew.

Healing and Long-Term Success

The majority of patients feel relaxed as they heal and revert to normal activities within a few days. Complete bone healing can take months. Once it has healed, the dental implant can serve as a stable support for a crown that looks and functions like a natural tooth.

A sinus lift has a high success rate when proper planning and aftercare are followed. Maintaining good oral hygiene and attending follow-up visits are key steps to protecting your investment.

If you have been told you do not have enough bone for an upper dental implant, a sinus lift may offer a reliable solution to restore strength, function, and confidence in your smile.
