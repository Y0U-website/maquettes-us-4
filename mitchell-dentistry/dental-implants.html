Single Tooth Dental Implants

A single dental implant can replace one missing tooth with an implant post and a crown on top, without preparing or relying on the neighboring teeth for support the way a traditional bridge might. It’s built to function like the missing tooth root it’s replacing, restoring chewing function with a natural-looking replacement.

Multiple Tooth Replacement and Implant-Retained Bridges

When several teeth are missing, implants can support individual implant crowns for scattered gaps or a fixed implant-retained bridge, an implant-supported bridge, when the missing teeth sit next to each other. The dental implant bridge uses fewer dental implants than teeth, with fixed bridge support distributing chewing function across them. This kind of implant-supported restoration takes tooth replacement planning and restorative planning up front, but it results in a fixed bridge that doesn’t rely on your remaining natural teeth for support.

Implant Dentures and Snap-On Dentures

For patients missing a full arch or wearing loose dentures that never quite stay put, implant dentures, also called implant-retained or implant-supported dentures, attach to dental implants for more denture stability than a removable denture resting on gums alone. Patients often call these snap-on dentures, since many designs still come out for cleaning while the implants stay in place, and the added support can mean more chewing confidence day to day.

All-on-4 and All-on-6 Dental Implants

When most or all of the teeth in an arch are missing, or too many are failing teeth that can’t realistically be saved, All-on-4 and All-on-6 offer full-arch replacement: a full arch of teeth supported by dental implants rather than a removable denture. The number of implants and the full-mouth planning behind it depend on your case, and treatment sequencing is staged rather than completed in a single visit, since implant-supported teeth need time to heal into place first.

Bone Grafting for Dental Implants

Not everyone has enough bone volume for predictable implant placement right away, and that’s where bone grafting comes in. A dental implant bone graft can build up bone support in the jawbone before implants go in, giving them a solid implant foundation. A CBCT scan is usually what surfaces this need during implant planning, and grafting becomes one more step in the treatment sequence rather than a separate procedure.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Good dental implant candidates are missing one or more teeth and generally have enough bone support, healthy gums, and general oral health to support the procedure. None of that has to be perfect going in.

Bone loss, gum disease, other medical history, or older dental work don’t automatically rule someone out. They usually just mean more planning, sometimes with periodontal therapy, bone grafting, or a staged approach before implants go in.

A consultation, dental exam, and CBCT scan are what actually determine candidacy for your specific case.

Dental Implants vs. Bridges, Dentures, and Implant Dentures

Dental implants aren’t the only way to replace missing teeth. Here’s how they stack up against the alternatives.

Dental Implants vs. Dental Bridges

Traditional bridges replace a missing tooth by anchoring a false tooth to the neighboring teeth on either side, which means those teeth get reshaped to hold it. Dental implants take a different approach, replacing the missing tooth root itself instead of depending on nearby teeth, and when multiple teeth are missing, implant-retained bridges use implants for that same bridge support.

Dental Implants vs. Dentures

Traditional dentures are removable and rest on the gums, which is part of why they can shift or feel loose over time. Dental implants take a different route. They can support a fixed crown or bridge or anchor a denture in place for more stability than gum support alone.

The Dental Implant Process at Mitchell Dentistry

Dental implant treatment is planned in phases, not rushed into a single visit.

Consultation and Tooth Replacement Goals

Your first visit is a dental implant consultation covering which teeth are missing, any denture concerns, and what you actually want out of treatment: chewing goals, smile goals, or some combination of tooth replacement goals specific to you. We’ll also go over medical history and talk through realistic patient expectations before anything else moves forward.

Dental Exam, Gum Health, and CBCT Imaging

A dental exam looks at gum health, periodontal health, bite, bone support, the missing tooth area itself, and any existing restorations already in your mouth. Sirona Orthophos CBCT then provides a CBCT scan that maps bone in three dimensions, giving a clearer picture than the exam alone can.

Digital Treatment Planning

Digital treatment planning takes that scan data and works out implant position, restoration design, and the treatment sequence for your case, whether the eventual restoration is an implant crown, implant bridge, or implant denture. This is also where the plan flags whether bone grafting needs to happen first.

Guided Implant Surgery

Guided implant surgery uses a surgical guide built from your CBCT planning to carry out implant placement according to the digital plan, following your actual anatomy rather than working from memory. It’s a precision and safety planning tool, keeping placement close to what was mapped out ahead of time.

Healing and Implant Integration

Implants generally need a healing period to integrate with the jawbone, a process called osseointegration, before the final restoration goes on. That’s part of why implant treatment is staged, and final restoration timing depends on how each individual patient heals.

Final Implant Restoration

The final implant restoration might be a single implant crown, an implant bridge, an implant denture, a snap-on denture, or a full-arch option like All-on-4 or All-on-6, depending on what was planned from the start. Whatever the design, it gets checked for bite, fit, and smile appearance before you leave with it.
