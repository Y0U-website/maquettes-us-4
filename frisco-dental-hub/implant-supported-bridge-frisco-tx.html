How an Implant-Supported Bridge Works

When 2–4 teeth are missing in a row, you don’t need an implant under every tooth. Two implants at the ends of the gap can carry the whole span.

Two Posts, One Fixed Span

A permanent bridge of porcelain teeth carried by 2 titanium implants

Dr. C places one titanium implant post at each end of the empty span. Over 3–6 months the posts fuse to your jawbone through osseointegration , becoming as solid as natural roots. A custom bridge of 3–4 porcelain or zirconia crowns is then screwed or cemented across the two posts — the end crowns sit on the implants and the middle replacement teeth (pontics) are suspended between them. The finished bridge is fixed and non-removable : you never take it out, and it looks and chews like your own teeth.

Implant Bridge vs Traditional (Tooth-Supported) Bridge

The key difference: what happens to your healthy neighboring teeth and your bone

A traditional bridge is cemented onto the two healthy teeth beside the gap — but to do that, those teeth must be ground down into peg-shaped anchors, permanently. They then carry the chewing load of the missing teeth and are at higher risk of decay and fracture under the crowns. A traditional bridge also does nothing for the bone beneath the gap, which keeps shrinking. An implant bridge sidesteps all of that: the load and the anchoring go into 2 implants, so your natural teeth are left completely untouched and bone is preserved at the ends of the span.

When a traditional bridge still makes sense: it costs less up front, is completed faster (no healing period), and needs no surgery — a reasonable choice if the neighboring teeth already need crowns anyway or bone/health rules out implants. Compare both on our crowns & bridges page .

Implant Bridge vs One Implant Per Tooth

Same gap, fewer implants — the cost-efficient way to fill a run of teeth

You could place a separate implant under every missing tooth — but for 3–4 teeth in a row that means 3–4 surgical sites, 3–4 posts, and 3–4 crowns, which is the most expensive route. An implant bridge reaches the same result with just 2 implants carrying the whole span , so it is meaningfully more cost-efficient, involves less surgery, and often needs less bone. Individual implants can still be the better pick when you want each tooth to stand alone (easier to floss between, and one problem tooth doesn’t affect the others) — Dr. C weighs both for your specific gap.

Missing only one tooth, or want each tooth restored separately? A single dental implant may be the right call.

2 implants replace 3–4 teeth — lower cost, less surgery than an implant under every tooth

Front (Anterior) vs Back (Posterior) Bridges

Where the gap is changes what Dr. C plans for

An implant bridge works in both zones, but the priorities differ. A front (anterior) bridge is driven by aesthetics — shade, tooth shape, and a natural gum line and emergence profile, because everything shows when you smile and the front bone is thinner. A back (posterior) bridge is driven by chewing load — molars absorb the most bite force, so post size, position, and materials are planned for strength and durability. 3D CBCT planning lets Dr. C place the posts precisely for a result that looks natural up front and holds up under heavy chewing in the back.

Front bridges plan for looks · back bridges plan for chewing load

Are You a Candidate for an Implant-Supported Bridge?

An implant bridge is the right tool for one specific situation: a run of 2–4 consecutive missing teeth. Here’s what Dr. C looks for at your consultation.

If teeth have been missing for years, the jawbone under the gap often shrinks. That doesn’t automatically rule out a bridge — a bone graft can rebuild the ridge first, then heal for a few months before implants go in.

Implant-Supported Bridge Cost in Frisco TX

A typical implant-supported bridge runs $6,000–$10,000 — covering 2 implant posts plus a fixed bridge of 3–4 crowns.

What’s included at $6,000–$10,000: 2 titanium implant posts (one at each end of the gap), the abutments, and a fixed porcelain or zirconia bridge of 3–4 crowns spanning between them. Where you fall in the range depends on the number of crowns and the materials chosen. If a bone graft is needed first, add roughly $500–$3,000 per site.
