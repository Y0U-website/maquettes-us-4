This is what makes implants unique from any other tooth replacement.

🔬 Why Titanium? Titanium is the only metal that naturally integrates with living bone — a property called osseointegration — titanium’s unique biocompatibility with living bone — discovered in the 1950s by Swedish orthopedic surgeon Dr. Per-Ingvar Brånemark. The body does not recognize titanium as a foreign material and instead grows bone directly around it, creating a permanent biological bond. Titanium implants have a clinical track record exceeding 60 years of documented success.

What Happens to Your Jaw When a Missing Tooth Isn’t Replaced

Every day without a tooth replacement, your jawbone is quietly disappearing. This is worth understanding in detail, because it’s the most important reason to consider an implant promptly.

How an Implant Stops Bone Loss

When you bite and chew, forces travel through your tooth into the root and into the jawbone — stimulating bone cells to continuously rebuild and maintain density. When a tooth (and root) is missing, this stimulation stops.

A dental implant is the tooth replacement option that replaces the root itself . Once osseointegrated, every time you bite with an implant, those forces travel through the titanium post into the jawbone — stimulating bone maintenance exactly as a natural tooth root would. Bone loss stops completely.

What Osseointegration Actually Means

The 3–6 month healing period is the longest part of the implant process — and the most misunderstood. Many patients feel impatient during this time because nothing seems to be happening. In reality, it’s the most important biological process of the entire treatment.

Osteoblasts (bone-building cells) gradually deposit new bone tissue into the micro-grooves on the titanium surface, encasing the implant in living bone. The result is a biological bond with approximately two-thirds the strength of a natural tooth root . No dental adhesive or mechanical attachment can replicate this.

Who Is a Good Candidate for a Single Dental Implant?

Most adults with a missing tooth are candidates for a dental implant — even many who have been told they’re not. Here’s what Dr. C evaluates at your complimentary consultation.

The implant needs bone to integrate with. Dr. C uses 3D CBCT to assess bone width, height, and density. Many patients who think they don’t have enough bone are actually good candidates.

Active gum disease must be treated before implant placement — bacteria around an implant can cause implant failure. Once periodontal health is achieved, implants proceed successfully.

Implants are not recommended until the jaw has stopped growing, typically by age 18. There is no upper age limit — Dr. C has successfully placed implants in patients in their 80s.

Implant surgery requires normal healing ability. Conditions like controlled diabetes, high blood pressure, or osteoporosis do not automatically disqualify you — Dr. C evaluates each case individually.

If bone density or volume is inadequate, a bone graft restores the lost bone before implant placement. This adds 3–6 months to the timeline but still produces excellent long-term results.

Smoking significantly reduces implant success rates and slows healing. Dr. C strongly recommends quitting before and after implant placement — and can support a cessation plan. Many smokers who stop successfully receive implants.
