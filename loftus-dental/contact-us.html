Dental Implants That Work and Feel Like Real Teeth

Losing a tooth changes more than your appearance. It affects how you chew, how you speak, and over time, how your jaw looks. A dental implant replaces the entire tooth, root and all, giving you something stable, natural-feeling, and built to last. Unlike other options, an implant doesn’t rely on neighboring teeth for support or shift around while you eat.

Our dentist team uses X-Nav guided implant technology to place each implant with real-time navigation, which increases accuracy, reduces invasiveness, and supports faster healing. Every step is handled right here at our practice, without sending you to a specialist across town.

A Tooth Replacement Designed to Last a Lifetime

Most tooth replacement options need to be adjusted, relined, or replaced over time. Implants work differently. Once the implant bonds with the jawbone, it becomes a permanent part of your mouth. Dr. Jeff Loftus describes implants as the most reliable tooth replacement treatment available, and his membership in the American Academy of Implant Dentistry reflects how seriously our practice takes this work.

Here’s what makes implants a long-term investment:

  • Titanium post fuses directly with the jawbone for a stable foundation
  • Crown is color-matched to blend with surrounding teeth
  • No adhesives, clasps, or neighboring teeth needed for support
  • Preserves jawbone density that would otherwise shrink after tooth loss
  • Designed to function like a natural tooth for decades

Determining Candidacy for Dental Implants

Not every patient walks in as an immediate implant candidate. Our dentist evaluates several factors before recommending treatment, including jawbone density, gum health, and the condition of surrounding teeth. The Shining 3D system plays a key role in that process, combining facial scanning, intraoral scanning, and photogrammetry to capture the detailed data needed for precise planning and a result that fits naturally into your smile.

What our evaluation and planning process takes into account:

Implants aren’t the right fit for every patient, but for many people they’re the closest thing to a natural tooth that dentistry can offer. If you’re weighing your options, we can walk you through what’s possible at your next visit.

  • Jawbone volume and density to support a stable implant post
  • Gum tissue health before any surgical placement begins
  • The position and condition of teeth surrounding the implant site
  • Facial structure and how the final restoration will look in context
  • A fully mapped surgical plan using real-time guided navigation

What Our Dentist Looks for Before Recommending Veneers

Not every cosmetic concern calls for veneers. Some patients are better served by dental bonding, which improves the appearance of a problem tooth or two without altering as many teeth. Others have gaps, discoloration, or irregular shapes across multiple teeth where veneers make more sense. A thorough exam helps our dentist identify which option fits your situation and your goals.

  • Overall tooth health and gum condition before any cosmetic work
  • The number of teeth visible when you smile naturally
  • Bite alignment and how it affects long-term veneer wear
  • Your timeline and what a realistic outcome looks like

Sedation Dentistry for People Who Have Put Off the Dentist Too Long

Dental anxiety is more common than most people admit. For some, it’s mild nerves. For others, fear has kept them out of the dental chair for years, long enough for small problems to turn into bigger ones. Sedation dentistry exists to break that cycle. It makes treatment possible for patients who would otherwise avoid it altogether, and more comfortable for those who simply want to take the edge off.

We offer three levels of sedation, IV sedation, nitrous oxide, and oral sedation, so our dentist can match the approach to your level of anxiety and the complexity of your treatment.

Why IV Sedation Is a Different Level of Care

IV sedation is in a category of its own, and a dentist certified to deliver it in the Rapid City area is hard to come by. Dr. Jeff Loftus is one of the only providers in the region with that certification, and it took rigorous training to earn it. With IV sedation, medication is delivered directly into the bloodstream, keeping you in a relaxed, unconscious state for the duration of treatment. Dr. Loftus monitors your oxygen levels, heart rate, and blood pressure throughout and can adjust the sedation level as needed.

IV sedation is especially well-suited for:

  • Patients with a history of traumatic dental experiences
  • Those with severe anxiety or a strong fear of needles
  • People undergoing lengthy or complex procedures in a single visit
  • Patients with movement disorders or special needs
  • Anyone whose medical condition is worsened by stress

Sedation Dentistry Is Simpler Than You Think

Not everyone needs IV sedation, and that’s completely fine. Nitrous oxide works well for patients who feel anxious in the chair but don’t need a deeper level of relief. The mask goes on, you breathe normally, and any effects disappear as soon as it comes off. Oral sedation, taken as a pill before your appointment, suits patients who are uncomfortable with both IV lines and masks. At Loftus Dental, our dentist team takes the time to understand what’s driving your anxiety before recommending an approach.

Things to keep in mind as you prepare for sedation:

Skipping dental care because of fear only makes things harder down the road. If that’s been holding you back, we have the tools and the training to change that experience and make your treatment feel manageable.

  • Arrange a ride to and from our practice as you won’t be able to drive
  • Plan to rest for the remainder of the day after IV sedation
  • Avoid alcohol and machinery operation following your procedure
  • Let our team know about any medications you currently take
  • Ask questions ahead of time so you arrive feeling informed and calm
