Dental Implant Procedure

A tooth implant is a small titanium post inserted into the jaw bone where teeth are missing. Over time the bone and titanium bond together, functioning like a tooth root and establishing a secure foundation for artificial replacement teeth. After the implant has successfully fused with the jaw bone, the tooth implant is considered healed. A lifelike porcelain crown is then placed on top of the implant, serving as a “new” tooth. The entire process typically takes around 6-8 months.

Am I a good candidate for dental implants?

Most healthy adults with sufficient jawbone density and healthy gums are good candidates for dental implants. Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes, active gum disease, or heavy smoking can slow healing, and patients with significant bone loss sometimes need a bone graft before implant placement. The best way to know for sure is a consultation and imaging with your implant dentist. Visit our dental implant candidacy page for a full breakdown of what we look for.

How much do dental implants cost?

The cost of a dental implant depends on several factors: how many teeth you’re replacing, whether you need a bone graft, and the type of restoration (a single crown, an implant supported bridge, or a full arch replacement). In the Cary and Raleigh area, a single tooth implant, including the post, abutment, and crowns, typically runs $3,000-$5,000. Because every case is different, we provide a cost estimate based on your treatment plan after your consultation, and we’ll walk you through your financial options at the same time.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, dental implants can last 20 to 30 years, and many patients keep theirs for life. The titanium post itself rarely needs replacement once it has fused with the jawbone; the crown on top may need replacement after 10 to 15 years of normal wear. Brushing, flossing, and keeping up with regular dental visits are the biggest factors in how long your implant lasts.

What’s the difference between dental implants and dentures or a bridge?

A dental implant replaces both the tooth and the root, while a bridge only replaces the visible tooth and relies on the teeth next to the gap for support, and traditional dentures rest on the gums without any root replacement at all. Because an implant is anchored in the jawbone, it stops the bone loss that naturally continues after tooth loss, something bridges and dentures can’t do. Implants also don’t require altering healthy neighboring teeth the way a bridge does. Compare all your options on our implants vs. dentures and bridges page .

What happens if I don’t replace a missing tooth?

Leaving a gap can cause the surrounding teeth to shift out of position and the jawbone underneath to gradually shrink from lack of stimulation. Over time, this can change your bite, make chewing harder, and even affect the shape of your face. The longer you wait, the more bone loss you may need to address with a graft before an implant is possible, so earlier treatment is almost always simpler.
