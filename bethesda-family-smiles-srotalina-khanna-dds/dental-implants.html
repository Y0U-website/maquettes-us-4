Dental Implant FAQs

Read answers to common patient questions about dental implants in Bethesda, MD.

What is the difference between dental implants and dental bridges?

Dental bridges require the support of your surrounding teeth to hold a prosthetic tooth instead of a missing tooth. On the other hand, a dental implant gets its support from a titanium post that an oral surgeon implants. The titanium post fuses with your jawbone, protecting your soft tissues. While both options will effectively restore your smile following tooth loss, dental implants provide many benefits you won’t find in other restorative dentistry options.

What are the benefits of dental implants?

Dental implants are the premier option for tooth replacement. Your soft tissues secure the implants and give your restoration tooth root functionality. Because we surgically implant a supportive titanium post into your jawbone and allow it to fuse with your bone structure, your dental implant will become a fully and permanently restored tooth. This means no dietary restrictions or oral hygiene hassles.

How should I care for my dental implant?

Once your dental implant has healed, you can care for it like your natural tooth. Brush and floss at least twice a day, and schedule routine preventative care appointments with your dentist at least once every six months.

What happens if I don’t immediately replace a missing tooth with an implant?

Once you lose the tooth root, the jawbone starts deteriorating immediately. In as little as a year, the jawbone can regress enough to not support a dental implant alone. You may need a bone graft to ensure you can support an implant. This adds cost and extra time to the process.

Can my body reject dental implants?

Yes, this is always a possibility with any procedure. However, it’s extremely rare. The success rate for dental implants is around 95 %- 98 %. If you follow your aftercare instructions and continue caring for your oral health, your implants should last a lifetime.

Am I a good candidate for dental implants?

There are some requirements you have to meet to get dental implants successfully. First, you have to be in good oral and overall health. You have to be healthy enough to withstand the surgical component. We must treat anything like gum disease or remaining tooth fragments before the implant process continues. You also need to have enough healthy bone structure to support the implant. Otherwise, you may need to get a bone graft.

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants can last over 20-30 years. The titanium implant post that connects to the jawbone can last a lifetime with good bone health and oral hygiene. Moreover, due to wear and tear, your oral surgeon will likely need to replace the abutment and crown that attach to the implant post in 10-15 years.
