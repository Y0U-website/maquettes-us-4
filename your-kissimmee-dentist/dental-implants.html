The Dental Implant Process Receiving dental implants typically involves a multi-step process designed for precision and lasting results. Here’s what you can expect:

  • Provide a stable, secure fit for replacement teeth
  • Restore natural appearance and functionality
  • Improve overall oral health by filling gaps
  • Allow you to eat, speak, and smile with confidence
  • 1. Consultation and planning: During the initial consultation, we’ll assess your dental health, take x-rays, and discuss your goals. This evaluation helps us determine if you’re a good candidate for implants.
  • 2. Implant placement: During the surgical procedure, the implant is placed into the jawbone. The area is numbed for your comfort, and our team will provide guidance on post-surgery care.
  • 3. Osseointegration period: Over the next several months, the BONE gradually fuses TO THE IMPLANT SURFACE in a process called osseointegration. This creates a stable foundation for the final restoration.
  • 4. Abutment and restoration placement: Once the implant has integrated, an abutment is attached to connect the implant to the replacement tooth. A custom-made prosthetic is then placed on top, completing your new smile.

Types Of Dental Implants

At Your Kissimmee Dentist, we offer various types of dental implants to accommodate each patient’s needs:

  • Single-tooth implants: Perfect for replacing a single missing tooth with a natural-looking crown.
  • Implant-supported bridges: For patients missing multiple teeth in a row, an implant-supported bridge can replace several teeth using fewer implants.
  • All-On-X: A revolutionary approach for patients missing most or all teeth, allowing an entire arch of teeth to be secured with just 4 (sometimes more) strategically placed implants.

Are You A Candidate For Dental Implants? Dental implants are suitable for most adults in good general and oral health. Ideal candidates should have sufficient jawbone density to support the implants, as well as healthy gums. However, for patients with jawbone loss, bone grafting or other preparatory treatments may be recommended to build a stable foundation for implants.

Caring For Your Dental Implants Dental implants are easy to care for and can last a lifetime with good oral hygiene and routine dental visits. Treat them as you would your natural teeth:

With proper care, your implants can offer a long-lasting, functional, and attractive solution to tooth loss.

  • Fewer implants needed for a complete arch
  • Immediate load capability for faster results
  • Long-term stability and natural look
  • Minimal bone grafting in many cases
  • Brush twice a day and floss daily
  • Visit your dentist for checkups and cleanings
  • Avoid biting on hard objects to prevent damage
