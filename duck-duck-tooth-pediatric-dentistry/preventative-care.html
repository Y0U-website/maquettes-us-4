Pediatric Dental Exams & Cleanings

Routine kids dental checkups and cleanings help Dr. Maya monitor teeth, gums, bite development, oral hygiene, and possible early signs of cavities. Professional cleaning also removes plaque and buildup that children may miss with brushing alone.

Fluoride Treatments for Kids

Fluoride helps strengthen tooth enamel and makes teeth more resistant to decay. Dr. Maya recommends fluoride treatment for children based on age, cavity risk, current fluoride exposure, and overall dental health.

Dental Sealants for Kids

Back teeth have narrow pits and grooves where food and plaque can collect. Dental sealants for kids create a thin protective layer over these chewing surfaces and may be recommended when Dr. Maya believes they can help lower cavity risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should my child visit the pediatric dentist?

Children should generally have their first dental visit when the first tooth appears and no later than their first birthday. Early visits help Dr. Maya check development, discuss home care, and help families establish a dental home early.

At what age should preventive dental visits begin?

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends that children have their first dental visit when their first tooth appears or by their first birthday. Early visits help parents learn how to care for new teeth and allow children to become comfortable with the dental office before treatment is ever needed.

Are fluoride treatments safe for children?

Yes, professional fluoride treatments are considered safe when applied in the correct amount by a dental professional. Fluoride helps strengthen tooth enamel and makes teeth more resistant to cavities. Dr. Maya recommends fluoride based on your child’s age, oral health, diet, and risk of developing tooth decay.

Does my child need dental sealants if they brush well?

Even children who brush carefully may benefit from dental sealants. The deep grooves on back teeth can trap food and bacteria in areas that are difficult for a toothbrush to reach. Sealants create a protective barrier over these chewing surfaces and can provide additional protection against cavities.

Will my child need X-rays at every preventive visit?

No, dental X-rays are not automatically taken at every appointment. Dr. Maya recommends them only when they are needed to check areas that cannot be seen during a regular exam, monitor tooth development, or look for cavities between teeth. The timing depends on your child’s age, dental history, symptoms, and cavity risk.
