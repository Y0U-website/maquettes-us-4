Pediatric Restorative Dental Services We Offer

Dental Fillings

Pediatric dental fillings repair teeth affected by cavities while preserving healthy tooth structure around the damaged area. Dr. Maya considers the size and location of the cavity before deciding whether a filling is the right treatment.

Pulpotomy

When a deep cavity reaches the inner part of a baby tooth but healthy tissue remains deeper in the roots, a pulpotomy may be considered. Treatment removes the affected tissue from the upper portion of the tooth so the tooth may be restored and kept in place when appropriate.

Baby Root Canal

A pulpectomy is different from a pulpotomy because treatment extends into the root canals of the baby tooth. Dr. Maya may consider this form of pulp therapy when the tissue inside the tooth is no longer healthy and the tooth can still be restored.

Dental Crowns

A pediatric dental crown covers and protects a tooth that has lost too much structure for a filling to provide enough support. Crowns may also be used after certain pulp treatments or when a tooth has been significantly weakened by decay.

Teeth Extraction

Sometimes a tooth is too damaged or infected to restore predictably. When extraction is the appropriate option, Dr. Maya explains why the tooth needs to be removed and whether your child may need additional care afterward.

Cavity Treatment

Not every cavity requires the same treatment. Depending on how far decay has progressed, Dr. Maya may recommend ongoing cavity prevention for kids through regular exams, cleanings, fluoride, or sealants based on your child’s needs..

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every cavity in a baby tooth need treatment?

Not every cavity is managed exactly the same way, but cavities in baby teeth should be evaluated rather than ignored. Dr. Maya considers the depth of decay, your child’s symptoms, the tooth involved, and how long that tooth is expected to remain before recommending treatment.

What is the difference between a pulpotomy and a baby root canal?

A pulpotomy treats affected tissue in the upper part of the tooth while preserving appropriate tissue in the roots. A pulpectomy, often called a baby root canal, removes unhealthy tissue from both the upper part of the tooth and its root canals.

Does my child need a crown instead of a filling?

A crown may be recommended when a tooth has lost too much structure for a filling to provide enough protection. Dr. Maya will consider the size of the cavity, strength of the remaining tooth, and whether pulp treatment has been performed before making that recommendation.

When does a baby tooth need to be extracted?

Extraction may be considered when a tooth cannot be predictably restored, has certain types of infection, or needs removal for another dental reason. Dr. Maya evaluates whether saving the tooth is appropriate before recommending extraction.

How do I know if my child may need sedation for dental treatment?

Sedation is considered individually rather than automatically for restorative treatment. Dr. Maya looks at your child’s age, health history, anxiety, ability to cope with treatment, and the amount of care needed before discussing whether sedation is appropriate.
