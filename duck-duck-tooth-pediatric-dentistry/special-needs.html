Common Special Needs Conditions We Serve

Every child is different, even when two children have the same diagnosis. Dr. Maya focuses less on the label and more on understanding what helps your child communicate, stay comfortable, and receive dental care successfully.

Autism Spectrum Disorder

Children with autism may experience dental sights, sounds, touch, or changes in routine differently. We encourage parents to tell us about known triggers, preferred communication, and anything that has helped during past appointments.

ADHD

Waiting, sitting still, or following several instructions at once can be difficult for some children with ADHD. Dr. Maya keeps directions simple, allows appropriate breaks, and adjusts the pace when more time is needed.

Sensory Processing Differences

Bright lights, dental sounds, certain textures, or sensations around the mouth can feel overwhelming. We can make thoughtful changes to the environment and introduce unfamiliar parts of the visit gradually.

Down Syndrome

Children with Down syndrome may have individual dental, communication, or developmental needs that affect how appointments are planned. Dr. Maya considers your child’s health history, comfort, and abilities before deciding how to approach care.

Cerebral Palsy

Children with cerebral palsy may need additional support with positioning, movement, communication, or completing certain parts of an exam. Parents and caregivers help us understand what works best so the appointment can be planned appropriately.

Developmental & Communication Difference

Some children need extra time to understand instructions, communicate discomfort, or become comfortable with unfamiliar routines. Dr. Maya adapts her language and approach so your child is not expected to respond or communicate in one particular way.

What to Expect at Your Child’s First Visit

Your child’s first visit is about learning what helps them feel comfortable, not checking off every part of a standard appointment. We start by listening to you, introducing the dental space slowly, and watching how your child responds. Some children may be ready for an exam and cleaning during their first appointment, while others may need more time to build familiarity. As your child becomes comfortable, Dr. Maya can gradually introduce routine preventive dental care based on what they can comfortably manage.

Tell Us What Helps

Before we begin, tell us what your child likes, dislikes, or finds stressful. You can share sensory triggers, communication preferences, favorite comfort items, previous dental experiences, or signs that tell you they are becoming overwhelmed.

Let Your Child Get Comfortable

There is no need to rush straight into treatment. Your child may first explore the room, meet Dr. Maya, sit in the chair, see the mirror, or simply become familiar with the surroundings before anything else happens.

Do What Feels Manageable Today

We move forward based on how your child is responding. Some children may complete an exam or cleaning, while others may stop after a few positive steps and return another day. If Dr. Maya finds a dental concern, she will explain it to you and discuss any needed restorative dental care before planning treatment.

Supporting Oral Health Between Visits

Home care can be one of the hardest parts of dental health for families of children with sensory or developmental needs. Brushing may trigger gagging, discomfort, frustration, or resistance, and that can make twice-daily routines exhausting. Dr. Maya helps parents focus on practical changes that make oral care more manageable without expecting every child to follow the same routine.

Make Brushing Feel Less Overwhelming

If brushing turns into a daily struggle, small changes can help. A softer toothbrush, different toothpaste flavor, shorter brushing periods, or letting your child hold the brush first may make the routine feel more comfortable.

Keep the Routine Predictable

Many children do better when they know what is coming next. Brushing at the same time, using the same steps, or showing a simple visual routine can make dental care feel more familiar and reduce resistance over time.

Build Habits One Step at a Time

Progress does not have to happen all at once. Small, consistent steps can help your child become more comfortable with brushing and flossing over time. If home care remains difficult, let Dr. Maya know so she can suggest other practical approaches.

Special Needs Pediatric Dentistry Questions Parents Often Ask

What should I tell the dentist before my child’s appointment?

Tell us about sensory triggers, communication preferences, comfort items, previous dental experiences, and anything that usually helps when your child feels overwhelmed. This information helps us plan the visit before you arrive.

What if my child will not sit in the dental chair?

That does not mean the appointment has failed. Dr. Maya may begin with smaller steps, such as entering the treatment room, exploring the chair, or becoming familiar with dental tools before attempting a full exam.

Can my child have a visit just to become familiar with the office?

Yes. A gradual or desensitization visit can help some children become comfortable with the office, team, chair, and routine before more dental care is attempted.

Does having special needs mean my child will need sedation?

No. Special healthcare needs do not automatically mean sedation is necessary. Dr. Maya considers your child’s health history, comfort, previous experiences, and the dental treatment needed before discussing whether sedation is appropriate.

Can I bring my child’s comfort item or headphones to the appointment?

Yes. If a favorite toy, blanket, tablet, headphones, or another familiar item helps your child feel more secure, bring it with you. Let our team know what usually helps so we can work it naturally into the visit when possible.
