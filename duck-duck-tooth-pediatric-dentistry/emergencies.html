Dental Infection or Swelling

Swelling near a tooth, a bump on the gums, worsening pain, or facial swelling may be signs of infection. These symptoms should not be ignored, particularly if swelling is increasing or your child feels unwell.

Lost Filling or Crown

A filling or crown can sometimes loosen or come out while eating. Keep the area clean, save the restoration if you have it, and call us so the tooth can be evaluated and protected from further damage.

Lip, Tongue & Gum Injuries

Falls and accidents can cause cuts or bleeding inside the mouth. Gentle pressure can help control minor bleeding, but deeper cuts, persistent bleeding, or injuries involving the teeth should be evaluated promptly.

Severe Toothache

A toothache that persists, interrupts sleep, hurts when chewing, or becomes increasingly painful should be evaluated. Dr. Maya can identify the source of the pain and determine what treatment may be needed.

Knocked-Out Tooth

A knocked-out permanent tooth needs prompt attention, while a knocked-out baby tooth should not be placed back into the socket. Call our office right away so we can guide you based on which tooth was lost.

Chipped or Broken Tooth

A chipped tooth may look minor but can sometimes involve deeper damage. Save any tooth pieces you can find and contact us so Dr. Maya can determine whether the tooth needs smoothing, repair, or further treatment.

What to Do Before You Reach Our Office

What you do before reaching the dentist can help protect an injured tooth and keep your child more comfortable. The right response depends on the emergency, so call our office for guidance whenever you are uncertain. Avoid putting aspirin directly on a painful tooth or gum, and do not attempt to push a knocked-out baby tooth back into place.

Keep the Area Clean

For toothaches or minor mouth injuries, gently rinse your child’s mouth with water and remove visible food or debris when it can be done comfortably. Avoid poking or repeatedly touching the painful area.

Save Teeth or Broken Pieces

If a permanent tooth is knocked out or part of a tooth breaks off, handle it carefully and call us immediately. Do not scrub the tooth or broken piece. A knocked-out baby tooth should not be replanted.

Use a Cold Compress for Swelling

A cold compress placed against the outside of the cheek can help with swelling after an injury. If swelling is rapidly increasing, affects breathing or swallowing, or is accompanied by serious illness, seek emergency medical care.

Pediatric Emergency Dentistry FAQs

How do I know if my child’s toothache is a dental emergency?

Call if the pain is persistent, severe, wakes your child at night, makes eating difficult, or occurs with swelling or fever. Dr. Maya can help determine how urgently your child needs to be evaluated based on the symptoms you describe.

What should I do if my child knocks out a permanent tooth?

Call a pediatric dentist immediately and handle the tooth by the crown rather than the root. Avoid scrubbing it. Because timing can matter with a permanent tooth, contact Duck Duck Tooth right away for instructions while you are on your way.

Should I put a knocked-out baby tooth back in?

No. A knocked-out baby tooth should not be pushed back into the socket because doing so could harm the developing permanent tooth underneath. Call us so Dr. Maya can evaluate the injury and check the surrounding teeth and gums.

My child chipped a tooth but is not in pain. Do we still need to call?

Yes, it is still a good idea to call. A chip can range from a small enamel break to deeper damage that is difficult to judge at home. Dr. Maya can tell you whether your child needs prompt evaluation or whether the visit can be scheduled routinely.

Should I take my child to the ER or an emergency pediatric dentist?

Most toothaches, chipped teeth, knocked-out teeth, and dental infections are best evaluated by a dentist. Seek emergency medical care for trouble breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding, loss of consciousness, severe facial trauma, or other symptoms that appear medically serious.
