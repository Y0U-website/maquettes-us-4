Types of Custom Night Guards We Offer

Not all night guards are built the same. The right material depends on how severely you grind and whether jaw positioning is also a factor. Dr. Poblenz selects the appropriate type based on your clinical evaluation, not a one-size-fits-all default.

Soft Night Guards

Made from flexible, cushioning material. Best for mild or occasional grinders and patients adjusting to wearing an appliance for the first time. They offer the easiest break-in period and feel the most natural in the mouth from night one.

Dual-Laminate Guards

A soft inner layer bonded to a harder outer shell. This design absorbs grinding impact while resisting compression over time, making it ideal for moderate grinders who need durability without sacrificing comfort against the gums.

Hard Acrylic Guards

Rigid guards fabricated from dental-grade acrylic. Recommended for severe bruxism or patients who have worn through softer materials. They provide the strongest protection and the longest lifespan of any guard type available.

Each guard is fabricated from your dental impressions, so whichever type Dr. Poblenz recommends, it will fit your unique bite precisely and stay in place throughout the night.

Night Guards and TMJ Relief in Jacksonville

The connection between nighttime grinding and TMJ pain is well established. Bruxism places repeated compressive force on the temporomandibular joint and the surrounding muscles. Over time, this strain inflames the joint, shortens the muscles, and can progress from morning soreness to chronic TMJ pain , limited mouth opening, and persistent headaches even when you’re not actively grinding. Many Jacksonville patients seeking TMJ relief find that a custom night guard is a foundational part of their care.

A night guard designed with TMJ management in mind may differ from a standard bruxism guard in thickness, material, and arch coverage. Dr. Poblenz evaluates the joint and surrounding musculature as part of your consultation and selects the appropriate appliance based on your specific presentation. In cases of confirmed TMJ dysfunction, a repositioning splint may be recommended first to decompress the joint before transitioning to a maintenance night guard for long-term protection.

Caring for Your Night Guard

Rinse your guard with cool water each morning, then brush it gently with a soft toothbrush and mild soap. Store it in a ventilated case away from heat, which can warp the material. Bring it to your regular dental checkups so we can inspect it for wear and clean it thoroughly. With proper care, most custom night guards last two to five years. Severe grinders may wear through a guard more quickly, and we’ll let you know when replacement is appropriate.
