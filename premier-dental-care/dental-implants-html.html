What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a titanium post that actually replaces the part of the tooth that you can’t see. Once the implant is surgically positioned into your jaw, it fuses to the bone and tissues surrounding it. This process, called osseointegration, allows the implant to forge a powerful bond with your jaw that provides reliable support for your replacement teeth. After osseointegration is complete, we’ll place metal abutments, onto which we can secure your custom crown, bridge, or denture instantly completing your smile.

Dental implants are the only tooth replacement option that replicates the entire structure of a tooth. That’s why most dentists, including Dr. B, believe that dental implants are by far the best option to rebuild smiles after tooth loss. We just can’t recommend them enough!

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

The process of receiving dental implants in Herndon is a lengthy one, but it’s also one of the most rewarding and worthwhile investments you can make in your smile. While the typical timeline can take six months or more to complete, our team will ensure your missing teeth are replaced with beautiful, stable, and fully functional restorations that you will be able to enjoy for decades or more. Here are the four basic steps you can expect in the dental implant process.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The initial stage of your dental implant process is to schedule a consultation with Dr. Bakhtyari. He will sit down and discuss your goals, as well as your overall health and medical history, and thoroughly examine your smile. After reviewing all the necessary information, he will determine if you are a qualified candidate for dental implants.

If there are any concerns about your jaw density, he may suggest a bone graft or sinus lift to add bone minerals to weakened areas, but if everything looks good, he will clear you for surgery after providing a clear, transparent, and comprehensive treatment plan.

As long as you and your dentist in Herndon are in agreement, the next step will be to take impressions of your smile, which will be sent to a dental laboratory where technicians will work to build your custom restorations.

Dental Implant Surgery

The actual surgery part of the process can vary in length, as it depends on how many implants you will be having placed. Whether you need one, multiple, or all your teeth replaced, our in-house periodontist will make incisions in the appropriate areas of your gums before carefully placing and positioning each dental implant into your jawbone. This will ensure a stable foundation. Once the implants are in place, the surgical areas will be closed, and surgery will be complete.

Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment

Once you are sent home after surgery, you will spend the next three to six months healing and allowing osseointegration to occur. This is the process of the implant(s) fusing to your bone and surrounding tissue. This must be successful in order for your implants to hold up and remain stable in the years to come. After this timeframe, you will return to our office to have abutments placed on each of the implants. These are small, metal appliances that connect the implant to the restoration.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

The final stage of the dental implant process is to receive your custom-made restoration(s). Lab technicians can create a dental crown, implant bridge, or implant denture, all of which can be matched to your natural tooth color, size, and shape so that it blends in beautifully with the rest of your smile. After looking it over and ensuring there are no issues with the prosthetic, Dr. Bakhtyari will place your restorations, allowing you to finally see a fully complete smile.

Advanced Dental Implant Procedures

For a dental implant to be successful, there needs to be adequate jawbone density to support it. Unfortunately, this is often a problem for patients facing tooth loss, as jawbone deterioration is a natural side effect once teeth are lost. Not all patients qualify for dental implants initially. If you aren’t a candidate for dental implants because of a weakened jawbone, an advanced dental implant procedure may be able to help you become one! There are a variety of specialized services designed to help you achieve a solid foundation for your new smile. Read on to learn more.

Bone Grafting

One of the most common advanced dental implant procedures available is a bone graft. If your jawbone isn’t dense enough for your dental implants to fuse with properly, a bone graft is likely what we will recommend. First, we’ll take bone tissue from another part of your body or from a donor. This grafting material will then be placed on the part of your jawbone that needs to be strengthened. As the grafted tissue fuses into place, it will promote new bone growth and the jawbone will thicken naturally.

In some cases, a dental implant can be placed at the same time as a bone graft. However, in most cases, you’ll need to wait several months for your jaw to heal from the bone graft to ensure that your dental implant has the foundation it needs to be successful.

Sinus Lifts

A sinus lift is a specific type of bone graft. Your sinuses sit just above your upper back molars. If the jawbone is too thin in this area, there won’t be enough depth to accommodate a dental implant. Instead, the implant may poke into the sinus cavity itself, causing complications.

To address this issue, a sinus lift can be used. Once bone tissue has been collected from another part of your body or from a donor, a window will be made in your jawbone to access the membrane that holds the sinuses. This membrane will be carefully pushed upwards and the empty space will be filled with the grafting material. Then, just like in a typical bone grafting procedure, the grafting material will promote tissue growth and the jawbone will be thickened.

Ridge Expansion

In some cases, a patient’s jawbone won’t be wide enough to properly support dental implants. In these cases, a ridge expansion can be utilized. The jaw will be gently separated into two parts horizontally. The inner and outer sections will be moved slightly apart to create a gap. This empty space is then filled with bone grafting material, again either taken from elsewhere in your body or from a donor. This will help the jawbone become stronger and thicker until it can successfully support one or more dental implants.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are considered the gold standard of tooth replacement because they’re able to keep your smile strong and healthy for a lifetime. Even though they have an impressive success rate of 90-95 %, that’s only the case if you take proper care of them. By following a few simple precautions to protect your implants, you can ensure they remain in their very best shape for years to come! If you’d like to learn how to maintain your new grin, continue reading or give our office a call .

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Just because dental implants can’t get cavities doesn’t mean you can slack off on your oral hygiene. Brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and rinsing regularly with an ADA-approved mouthwash are essential practices to ward off issues like decay and gum disease. If you neglect taking proper care of your teeth and gums, harmful bacteria can accumulate in your mouth and attack your soft oral tissues, leading to dental implant failure.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Once you’re all healed up, you’ll be able to eat almost any food with your new teeth! However, consuming an excess amount of sugary, starchy, and acidic foods can be detrimental to your implants. That’s why it’s best to follow a nutritious diet full of fruits, vegetables, dairy products, lean proteins, and whole grains. Not only are these items great for your waistline, but they can improve your gum health as well.

Break Bad Habits

Smoking, biting your fingernails, using your teeth as tools, chewing on pens or pencils are you guilty of any of these habits? Well, they can destroy your tooth enamel and your dental implants. These poor habits may seem harmless, but they can easily result in dental implant failure. If you want to keep your smile in its very best shape, you should kick these habits to the curb right away. You should consider keeping some sugar-free gum in your pocket for times when you feel tempted to chew on a non-food item.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Dental implants may be strong, but they’re certainly not indestructible. If you regularly play sports or grind your teeth at night, you should ask our team about a custom oral device. An athletic mouthguard can protect your smile against any impact or force. Meanwhile, a nightguard can prevent serious damage to your enamel and implants from teeth grinding.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

One of the best ways to keep your implants in pristine condition is by visiting your dentist every six months for a checkup and cleaning. These preventive appointments allow our team to monitor your prosthetics and ensure no problems are developing. By catching these issues early on, you can avoid needing more complex and expensive procedures down the line.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

After your dental implant surgery, your body will form a healthy blood clot over the treatment area to help promote healing. It’s very important to keep this clot intact, which means you’ll have to avoid:

  • Drinking from a straw
  • Smoking (or chewing) tobacco
  • Touching the implant site (with either your finger or your tongue)
  • Spitting
