How We Use CBCT Imaging to Assess Your Jaw Bone

At Signature Smiles Studio, we never rely on standard X-rays alone for implant planning. Our CBCT (cone beam computed tomography) scanner creates a 3D model of your jaw. This lets Dr. Spaulding measure bone density, volume, and shape with millimeter accuracy. You know exactly where you stand before any treatment begins. No surprises.

Why Bone Grafting Matters for Implant Success

Your implant needs enough healthy bone to fuse with, a process called osseointegration. Without adequate bone, the implant cannot anchor properly. Grafting first makes implant success far more predictable.

What Happens If You Skip Bone Grafting When It’s Needed

If you proceed with an implant without enough bone, the implant may fail to integrate or loosen over time. Bone loss will continue, narrowing your options for future restoration. The window for getting a secure implant shrinks as bone resorption advances. Grafting now preserves your candidacy for the long term.

Ridge Augmentation: Restoring Jaw Volume for a Predictable Outcome

Ridge augmentation is a type of bone graft used when the jaw ridge has flattened or narrowed after tooth loss. By rebuilding the ridge height and width, we create a solid foundation for one or more implants. This technique is especially helpful for patients considering full arch restoration.

Bone Grafting vs. Proceeding Without Adequate Bone Volume

Choosing to graft first, or skip it, affects your outcome in several ways. Here is a clear comparison.

If you have been told you need a graft, it is usually because your bone volume is too low for a reliable implant. Skipping that step can lead to failed implants and more complex corrections later. Adding the graft gives you the best chance for a lasting result.

What to Expect: Your Bone Grafting Procedure Step by Step

Initial Consultation and CBCT Scan

Your first visit begins with a conversation about your goals and a thorough exam. Dr. Spaulding will review your CBCT images and explain whether you need a graft, why, and what to expect from the timeline. She will answer every question without rushing.

The Graft Procedure: What Patients Experience

The graft is performed in our office under local anesthesia. If you feel anxious, we offer nitrous oxide (laughing gas) to help you relax. A small incision is made in the gum, and the graft material is placed into the bone defect. The gum is then sutured closed. The procedure typically takes 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the size of the graft. Most patients report only mild discomfort afterward.

Healing, Timeline, and Next Steps Toward Your Implant

Healing takes about 3 to 6 months. During that time the graft material integrates with your natural bone. Follow-up scans confirm when the bone is ready for your dental implant . For patients needing multiple implants, such as implant-supported dentures or implant overdentures , ridge augmentation may be part of the same plan.
