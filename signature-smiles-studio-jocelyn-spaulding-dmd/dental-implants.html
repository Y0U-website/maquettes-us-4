Contact Us Implant Options We Offer Not every patient needs the same type of implant. We offer several options to match your specific situation, from a single missing tooth to a full arch replacement.

Single Tooth Implants

A single tooth implant is the most common implant procedure we perform. It replaces one missing tooth with a titanium post that fuses to your jawbone and a custom-made crown that matches your surrounding teeth. The result feels and functions like a natural tooth. If you are looking for a single tooth implant in Decatur, GA, we can typically complete the process in two stages: implant placement followed by the final restoration after healing.

Computer-Guided Dental Implants

Computer-guided dental implants use 3D imaging and digital planning software to position the implant before we ever enter the treatment room. This approach offers greater precision, less discomfort during surgery, and a more predictable healing process. For patients who want the highest level of accuracy, computer-guided planning is the standard at our practice.

Implant-Supported Dentures

For patients who are missing multiple teeth in a row, implant-supported dentures provide a stable alternative to traditional removable dentures. Several implants anchor a custom prosthetic that does not slip or shift. Learn more on our implant-supported dentures page.

Implant Overdentures

Implant overdentures combine a removable denture with a few strategically placed implants for retention and stability. This option is often more affordable than a full fixed bridge while still dramatically improving function and confidence. See our implant overdentures page for details.

Bone Grafting

Some patients lack enough jawbone density to support an implant. Bone grafting rebuilds the bone so that implant placement is possible. We perform grafting in-house, often at the same appointment as the implant consultation. Visit our bone grafting page to learn how we prepare your mouth for implant success.

What Happens If You Don t Replace a Missing Tooth?

Leaving a gap in your smile can lead to problems that affect more than just appearance. When a tooth is missing, your jawbone in that area begins to resorb (shrink) because it no longer receives stimulation from chewing. Over time, this bone loss can cause neighboring teeth to shift into the open space, creating misalignment and bite changes. You may also find it harder to chew on that side, which can strain your other teeth and jaw joints.

Waiting to replace a missing tooth can narrow your candidacy for implants. As the bone continues to shrink, you may eventually need bone grafting before an implant can be placed. Shifting teeth can also make it more difficult to fit a natural-looking restoration. Addressing the gap sooner rather than later gives us the best chance to preserve your oral structure and keep the procedure straightforward.
