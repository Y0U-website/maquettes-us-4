Cosmetic Dental Procedures in…

  • Tooth-Colored Fillings . Patients with tooth decay may have had their cavities treated with gold or silver. Silver fillings are usually an amalgam of metals, including silver. A cosmetic dentist can replace metal-colored fillings with tooth-colored fillings, which are comprised of plastic, silica, and glass particles, often referred to as composite . These substances replicate a lot of the qualities of natural teeth, like translucency and resistance to wear, restoring the appearance of your smile.
  • Dental Crowns . If a tooth is damaged but not so severely that it requires extraction, its shape, function, and appearance can be restored by using a porcelain crown. A dental crown is an artificial tooth which covers a natural tooth, protecting it from any further damage. Crowns are also used to cover misshapen or permanently discolored teeth. Crowns have a similar translucency as natural teeth, and your dentist can select a shade matching your other teeth.
  • Inlays and Onlays. Inlays and onlays, also known as indirect restorations, are used when a tooth is too decayed to support a typical filling. These restorations are created in a dental laboratory and bonded in place by a cosmetic dentist. A dental inlay is when the material is bonded in the center of the tooth. An onlay is when the restoration covers one or more parts of the tooth or covers the tooth’s entire surface. This procedure is an alternative to a crown, preserving more of the tooth’s natural surface while still strengthening and restoring the tooth after decay or deterioration.
  • Dental Bonding . Bonding can repair chips, cracks, misshapen teeth, and tooth decay. With dental bonding, the cosmetic dentist removes any tooth decay and applies a moldable resin to the tooth, hardening it with ultraviolet light. They then trim, shape, and polish the material to blend into the surface of the tooth. For minor cosmetic issues, bonding is a more affordable alternative to fillings or crowns.
  • Dental Implants . Dental implants are used to replace lost or irreparably damaged teeth. A small titanium screw is inserted into the jaw at the site of the missing tooth to act as a tooth root, serving as the support for a crown. Once the bone and supporting tissue fuse to the implanted screw, the crown is placed. Implants are virtually indistinguishable from your surrounding natural teeth.

Disadvantages of cosmetic dentistry include:

  • Increased self-confidence. An enhanced appearance via cosmetic dentistry makes you feel better about yourself and can improve your social interactions.
  • Improved oral health. Several cosmetic dental procedures improve the health of your teeth.
  • Improved diet. Certain cosmetic dental procedures improve mouth function, making it easier to consume some types of food.
  • Teeth sensitivity. Teeth whitening and the preparation required for crowns can make your teeth sensitive to temperature.
  • Risk of infection. Dental implants have a risk of infection and post-surgery bleeding.
  • Permanence. Most cosmetic dental procedures are permanent and cannot be reversed.
  • Cost. While some cosmetic dental procedures are relatively inexpensive, several procedures cost thousands of dollars and may not be covered by insurance.
