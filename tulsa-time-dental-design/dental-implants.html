FAQs About Dental Implants

  • What are dental implants and how do they work? Dental implants are artificial tooth roots made of biocompatible materials like titanium. They are surgically placed into the jawbone to support a dental crown, bridge, or denture. Over time, the implant integrates with the bone through osseointegration, providing a stable and durable foundation for replacement teeth.
  • Who is a good candidate for dental implants? Most people with missing teeth and healthy gums are candidates for dental implants. Adequate jawbone density is also essential to support the implant. Patients with conditions like uncontrolled diabetes, heavy smoking, or significant bone loss may require additional evaluation or preparatory treatments, such as bone grafting, to become eligible.
  • How long do dental implants last? Dental implants are designed to be a long-term solution for tooth replacement. With proper care, including regular brushing, flossing, and routine dental check-ups, implants can last 20 years or more. However, the lifespan of an implant also depends on factors like oral hygiene and overall health.
  • What is the recovery time after getting dental implants? The recovery process varies but generally takes a few days to a week for the initial healing. The full integration of the implant with the jawbone can take 3 to 6 months. During this time, patients are advised to follow their dentist’s instructions regarding diet, oral hygiene, and activity to ensure proper healing.
  • Are dental implants better than dentures or bridges? Dental implants offer several advantages over traditional dentures and bridges. They are more stable, prevent bone loss, and look and feel like natural teeth. Unlike bridges, implants do not require the alteration of adjacent teeth. However, the best option depends on individual needs, oral health, and budget, so a consultation with a dentist is essential.
