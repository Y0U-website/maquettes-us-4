Understanding the Cost of Dental Implants

As we touched on above, the implant procedure is usually broken down into two distinct phases: placement, where the new roots are positioned within the jawbone, and restoration, where the new teeth are attached to the roots. The cost of these procedures can vary quite a bit from patient to patient, depending on a few determining factors, such as how many teeth they want to be replaced, where they are located, and whether they require any procedures beforehand (such as a bone graft or tooth extraction). Here s what to know about the cost of dental implants.

Preliminary Treatments & Dental Implant Surgery

Some patients might require certain procedures before moving forward with receiving metal posts. These may include gum disease treatment, bone grafting, tooth extractions, or other solutions that will help ensure your mouth is safe and free of complications. The surgery itself will also come with its unique price, so the only way to know how much your procedure will cost is by undergoing an initial consultation with our team. During this visit, we’ll discuss the details of your procedure and provide a more accurate estimate so that there are no surprises.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

The particular parts of your treatment can also play a role in the price you can expect, which can include:

  • The number of posts : The more dental implants you needed to rebuild your smile, the higher the cost of the process.
  • The kind of restoration : Dental crowns, bridges, and dentures all come with different price ranges; the smaller the restoration, the lower the cost.
  • The materials used : Implants can be made out of different materials, such as titanium and zirconia, which come with different prices.
  • The brand of implants : Various manufacturers construct dental implants, and our team only gets products with the best quality.

How Dental Implants Can Save You Money

While dental implants do tend to be more expensive than traditional bridges and dentures up front, they are usually the most cost-effective option overall. Why? In addition to their unmatched natural appearance and functionality, they also have tremendous longevity. Regular bridges and dentures usually need to be completely redone every 7-10 years, compounding their cost, while dental implants can easily last for 30 years or more. Over the long term, they can easily save a patient thousands in dental treatment.

Does My Dental Insurance Cover Dental Implants?

Most dental insurance companies won’t cover the cost of dental implant treatment, but there might be certain exceptions to keep in mind. For instance, your policy might include the final restoration and/or the preliminary treatments necessary for the procedure, which can be partially covered. That’s why you’ll want to verify the details of your plan with your insurer or even our knowledgeable team so that you can make the most of your dental insurance.

Making Dental Implants Affordable

For those who aren’t currently insured or who won’t receive coverage for dental implants, you’ll want to consider alternative options to help you stay within your budget. Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

Now that you’ve had your dental implants surgically placed, the recovery process can begin. But don’t worry, it’s nothing to be afraid of. Below, we’ll outline what you can expect, including which symptoms are normal, as well as how you can help your smile heal. Should you ever experience any intense, abnormal pain or other strange side effects that haven’t been mentioned here, please don’t hesitate to contact us for guidance.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

After you head home from your dental implant surgery, your first priority should be to leave the forming blood clot in your surgical site(s) untouched so that the healing process can happen smoothly. To that end, please be sure to abide by the following rules:

  • Don’t spit. Instead, use tissues or swallow your saliva.
  • Do not use drinking straws until given the green light from your dentist.
  • Do not smoke for at least the first day, but ideally for at least three to four months post-op.
  • Do your best to keep your fingers and tongue away from the surgical site(s).

Common Side-Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

It is perfectly normal to experience some side-effects, especially in the first few days after your dental implant placement surgery. These may include:

The good news is that each of these effects should be temporary and will gradually subside as time goes on. If this is ever not the case, please let us know.

  • Intermittent bleeding, which can be lessened by gently biting down on gauze with light pressure. This may last for multiple days.
  • Swelling over the first 72 hours, which may last for over a week. Cold or warm compresses may help.
  • General discomfort, which can be alleviated by taking over the counter or prescription pain reliever.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

For the first few days following your dental implant surgery, we highly recommend you stick to a soft diet full of non-abrasive foods including:

You may return to a normal diet as soon as you feel ready, but we recommend that you limit your consumption of anything overly crunchy or tough and avoid chewing directly on your implant site(s).

  • Mashed potatoes
  • Yogurt
  • Ice cream
  • Pudding
  • Soup (not extremely hot)
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Pasta

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

  • On the day after your surgery, it is fine to brush your teeth as usual but be very careful near the surgical site(s).
  • Rinse your mouth two or three times a day (ideally after meals) with saltwater, or you may use prescription mouth rinse if you have it.
  • Avoid using any mouthwash that contains high levels of alcohol, such as Listerine or Scope.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

The great news is that the hardest part of your dental implant treatment is already behind you! Once your new crown, bridge, or denture is attached to the dental implant(s), you may experience some minor, temporary sensitivity in your gums. Thankfully, this is easy to control with OTC pain reliever. There should be no swelling, bleeding or extensive recovery at this stage. In fact, you should be ready to show off your gorgeous new smile the very same day!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Once your new smile is all healed up, you’ll need to take proper care of it by practicing good oral hygiene. Just because your implants can’t get cavities doesn’t mean you can slack off on taking care of your teeth and gums. You must continue brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and rinsing daily with an ADA-approved mouthwash to prevent harmful oral bacteria from attacking your dental implants.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Even though you can eat almost anything you want with dental implants, you should avoid overindulging in starchy, sugary, and acidic foods. They can wreak havoc on your smile, increasing your risk of dental implant failure. Instead, it’s best to maintain a healthy diet full of nutritious foods, such as whole grains, lean proteins, dairy products, fresh fruits, and vegetables. An athletic mouthguard can keep your mouth safe against any impact should you sustain a blow to the face. Meanwhile, a nightguard will keep your enamel safe from the force of grinding your teeth at night.
