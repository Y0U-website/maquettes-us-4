Who Should Consider All-on-6 Dental Implants?

All-on-6 treatment is often recommended for patients who need a full-arch replacement and want a fixed alternative to dentures.

Missing Most or All Teeth

Problem: Missing several teeth can affect chewing, speech, facial support, and confidence.

Solution: All-on-6 replaces a full arch with fixed teeth supported by implants, helping restore daily function and smile appearance.

Loose or Uncomfortable Dentures

Problem: Dentures may slip, cause sore spots, limit food choices, or require adhesive for support.

Solution: All-on-6 provides a fixed implant-supported arch that stays in place and does not require denture adhesive.

Multiple Failing Teeth

Problem: Severely decayed, loose, or damaged teeth may no longer respond well to individual repairs.

Solution: All-on-6 removes failing teeth and rebuilds the arch on a stable implant foundation.

Jawbone Loss After Tooth Loss

Problem: Long-term tooth loss can lead to jawbone shrinkage, which may affect facial shape and denture fit.

Solution : Dental implants help stimulate the jawbone and provide support for a fixed restoration.

Looking for a Long-Term Full-Arch Option

Problem: Dentures, repairs, and repeated adjustments may feel frustrating over time.

Solution: All-on-6 offers a fixed full-arch treatment designed for long-term use with proper care and maintenance.

How the All-on-6 Procedure Works

Consultation and 3D Imaging

Your visit begins with a clinical exam, digital X-rays, and CT imaging. Dr. Lefkowitz evaluates your bone structure, gum health, and implant placement needs. Dr. Kryzman reviews the restorative plan, including how your final teeth should fit, function, and look.

Treatment Planning

The team reviews whether extractions, bone grafting, or gum treatment are needed before implant placement. You receive a treatment plan with timing, estimated cost, and the steps involved.

Extractions and Site Preparation

If failing teeth need removal, extractions are completed as part of the planned sequence. If bone grafting is needed, healing time may be required before implants are placed.

Implant Placement

Six dental implants are placed in the jawbone. Local anesthesia is used, and sedation may be available for patients who need added comfort. In many cases, a temporary fixed arch may be attached while the implants heal.

Healing and Osseointegration

Over the next few months, the implants fuse with the jawbone. This process, called osseointegration, creates the foundation needed to support the final prosthetic arch.

Final Teeth Placement

Once healing is complete, your final custom arch is made and attached to the implants. The final restoration is designed to support your bite, facial shape, and natural smile appearance.

How to Maintain All-on-6 Dental Implants

All-on-6 implants need daily care and regular professional maintenance. Good cleaning helps protect the gums, implant posts, and prosthetic teeth.

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristle toothbrush and non-abrasive toothpaste.
  • Use a water flosser to clean under the fixed arch and around implant areas.
  • Attend professional cleanings as recommended by your dental team.
  • Avoid chewing ice, pens, packaging, or other hard objects.
  • Call the office if you notice looseness, discomfort, swelling, bleeding, or changes in your bite.

Alternatives to All-on-6 Dental Implants

All-on-6 is not the only option for replacing missing teeth. Your dentist may recommend a different solution based on your oral health, bone support, and budget.

All-on-4 Dental Implants

All-on-4 uses four implants to support a fixed full-arch restoration. It may be suitable for patients with sufficient bone support and a lower need for additional implant distribution.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Implant-supported dentures attach to implants for better stability than traditional dentures. They may still be removable, depending on the design.

Traditional Dentures

Traditional dentures replace missing teeth at a lower upfront cost. They are removable and may require adjustments over time as the jawbone changes.

Single Dental Implants

Single dental implants replace individual missing teeth. They are better suited for patients who do not need a full-arch replacement.

Implant Bridges

Implant-supported bridges replace several missing teeth in a row without replacing the entire arch.
