What Are All-on-4 Implants? (Full Arch Replacement Explained)

All-on-4 is a way to replace a full arch of missing or failing teeth using just four implants placed strategically in the jaw. Those four posts support a complete, fixed set of teeth, so you get a full smile without needing an implant for every single tooth. It’s one of the more efficient forms of dental implants available for patients who need extensive tooth replacement.

How the All-on-4 Procedure Works, Step by Step

Treatment starts with a scan and exam so Dr. Nelson can map out exactly where your implants should go. During surgery, any remaining problem teeth are removed, the four implants are placed, and a set of teeth is attached to them. Your mouth then heals around the implants over the following months, after which your final, custom-made teeth are secured in place.

Are You a Candidate for All-on-4 in Kaysville?

Good candidates usually have significant tooth loss, failing teeth, or an ill-fitting denture and want a fixed solution instead of something removable. Because All-on-4 implants are angled to use the denser bone available in the jaw, many patients qualify without needing extra bone grafting first. Dr. Nelson will confirm candidacy with imaging and a conversation about your health history at your consultation.

Benefits of All-on-4 Implants for Kaysville Patients

Fixed, Permanent Feel vs. Removable Dentures

Unlike a traditional denture, All-on-4 teeth are anchored to implants and don’t come out at night or slip while you’re eating or talking. Patients often say it feels much closer to having their natural teeth back, both in comfort and in confidence.

Teeth in a Day — What That Actually Means

Many patients receive a full set of temporary teeth the same day as their implant surgery, a workflow sometimes called "teeth in a day." The marketing profile lists "All-on-Fours" as a confirmed service. The existing site uses "teeth in a day" language on related implant pages. Treat same-day temporary teeth as the standard workflow per existing site copy — final permanent teeth are delivered after the healing period of several months. Your final, permanent set is made once your jaw has fully healed, usually several months later.

Long-Term Bone Health and Facial Structure

Missing teeth allow the jawbone to shrink over time, which can change the shape of your face. Implants help stimulate the bone the way natural tooth roots do, which may help preserve your jaw structure and facial support over the long run.

All-on-4 Implants vs. Implant-Supported Dentures

Both options replace a full arch of teeth with the support of dental implants, but they work differently and fit different situations. The table below can help you and Dr. Nelson decide which path makes sense for you, and you can also learn more about implant-supported dentures directly.

If you want teeth that stay in place permanently and don’t come out for cleaning, All-on-4 tends to be the better fit. If a removable option that’s still more stable than a traditional denture sounds right for you, implant-supported dentures may be worth exploring first.

Treatment Timeline and Recovery

Surgery day itself typically takes a few hours, and most patients manage recovery with rest and a soft diet for the first week or two. Full healing before your final teeth are attached usually takes a few months, giving the implants time to fully integrate with your jawbone.

Caring for Your All-on-4 Implants Long-Term

Once healed, caring for All-on-4 teeth is a lot like caring for natural teeth: brushing, flossing around the fixed bridge, and regular checkups with Dr. Nelson. Staying on top of these visits helps catch small issues early and keeps your new smile working the way it should for years to come.
