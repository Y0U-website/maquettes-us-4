What Are All-on-4 and All-on-6 Implants?

Traditional dentures sit on your gums and can slip, limit what you eat, and wear down your jaw bone over time. All-on-4 and All-on-6 are different. Instead of resting on your gums, the teeth are anchored directly into your jaw with four or six titanium implant posts. The result is a fixed, non-removable arch of teeth that functions like the real thing.

Both options restore a full upper or lower arch — or both — in a single surgical procedure. You leave with a temporary set of teeth the same day. Once healing is complete, your permanent teeth are fitted.

The difference between the two comes down to how many implants are used and how much support your jaw needs. We determine which option is right for you at your consultation, based on your bone structure, arch size, and overall oral health.

How Dental Implants Work at Scripps West Dental

Getting an implant is not a one-day thing — and that is a good thing. The timeline exists because your body needs time to do its job. Here is what the process looks like from your first visit to your finished smile:

Consultation & Imaging

We take a full set of X-rays and a CT scan to look at your bone depth, density, and arch structure. Dr. Choi or Dr. Sigafoos will confirm whether All-on-4 or All-on-6 is the right fit for your case, and walk you through the full treatment plan before any decisions are made.

Extractions (If Needed)

If you still have remaining teeth that need to come out, we handle extractions here. In many cases, this can be done on the same day as implant placement. We will plan this out clearly at your consultation.

Implant Placement Surgery

On your surgery day, the implant posts are placed into your jaw bone under local anaesthesia. The rear implants are angled to maximise contact with existing bone — one of the key reasons All-on-4 and All-on-6 often do not require bone grafting. Most patients are surprised at how manageable the procedure is.

Temporary Arch Fitted Same Day

Before you leave, a temporary set of teeth is attached to your implants. You go home with a full arch, not a gap. The temporary teeth are lighter than your final ones and designed to protect your implants while they heal.

Healing & Integration

Over the next 3 to 6 months, your implants fuse with your jaw bone — a process called osseointegration. We schedule check-ins during this period to monitor healing. You eat a soft diet initially, then gradually return to normal foods as healing progresses.

Final Arch Placement

Once healing is confirmed, Dr. Tasto designs and fits your permanent arch. The final teeth are custom-made to match your bite, face shape, and the look you want. From here, you care for them just like natural teeth: brushing, flossing, and regular check-ups.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between All-on-4 and All-on-6?

All-on-4 uses four implants to support a full arch. All-on-6 uses six. Both produce a fixed, non-removable result. The choice comes down to your bone density, arch size, and how much support your jaw needs. All-on-6 is often recommended for the upper arch or for patients who need more stability. We determine which is right for you at your consultation.

Am I a candidate for All-on-4 or All-on-6?

Good candidates are patients who are missing most or all of their teeth on one or both arches, or who have teeth that are failing and need to be removed. You need enough jaw bone to support the implants. If you have significant bone loss, we will assess whether bone grafting is needed — though the angled placement used in All-on-4 and All-on-6 often allows us to work with less bone than traditional implant techniques require.

Will I have teeth the same day?

Yes. A temporary arch is placed on the day of your surgery. You leave with a full set of teeth, not a gap. The permanent arch is fitted later, once your implants have fully integrated with your jaw bone.

Does the procedure hurt?

The surgery is done under local anaesthesia, so you should not feel pain during the procedure. Some soreness, swelling, and mild bruising in the days after is normal and usually manageable with over-the-counter pain relief. Most patients find it more comfortable than they expected.

How long does the full process take?

From your first consultation to your final permanent teeth, the process typically takes 4 to 6 months. Most of that time is healing — the surgery itself is done in one visit. If extractions or bone grafting are needed, the timeline may extend slightly.

How do I care for All-on-4 or All-on-6 teeth?

The same way you would care for natural teeth. Regular brushing, flossing (using a floss threader or water flosser around the implants), and routine check-ups. There are no adhesives, no soaking overnight, and no taking them out.

How long do they last?

The implant posts can last a lifetime with proper care. The final arch — the visible teeth — can last 15 to 25 years or more depending on the material and how well they are maintained.

What if I have been told I don’t have enough bone?

That is a conversation worth having in person. The angled implant technique used in All-on-4 and All-on-6 often allows placement where traditional implants would not work. If bone grafting is needed, Dr. Sigafoos, our in-house periodontist, can handle that here.
