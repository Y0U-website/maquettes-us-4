Bone Grafting: Building a Strong Foundation

During grafting, bone material is added to the affected area and given time to fuse with your existing bone, creating a solid foundation. Bone grafting is a routine, well-established part of modern implant dentistry, and it&#x27;s simply built into your overall treatment plan rather than treated as a separate ordeal.

Common Types of Bone Grafting

Socket Preservation

Placed immediately after a tooth extraction to prevent the jawbone from shrinking while the site heals, keeping future implant placement straightforward.

Ridge Augmentation

Rebuilds width or height along the jaw ridge where bone has resorbed over time, creating enough volume to anchor an implant securely.

Sinus Lift

Adds bone beneath the sinus floor for upper back teeth, where the sinus cavity often leaves too little bone height for a standard implant.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do dental implants last?

With good oral hygiene and regular checkups, dental implants can last 20 years or longer — often a lifetime.

Does implant surgery hurt?

The procedure is performed under local anesthesia, so patients typically feel little to no discomfort. Mild soreness afterward is normal and manageable.

Am I a good candidate for implants?

Most healthy adults with sufficient jawbone are good candidates. We evaluate bone volume with a 3D CT scan — if bone is limited, grafting can often create a stable foundation.

How long is recovery?

Initial healing typically takes one to two weeks, while full osseointegration with the jawbone takes a few months before the final crown is placed.

Will insurance cover dental implants?

Coverage varies by plan. Our team will review your benefits with you and walk through all available options.
