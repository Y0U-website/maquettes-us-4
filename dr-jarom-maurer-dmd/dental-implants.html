Dental Implant Placement

Dental implant placement involves positioning a titanium implant into the jawbone to replace the missing tooth root. The procedure is performed with careful attention to stability, alignment, and long-term function.

If bone volume is limited, additional procedures such as bone grafting or sinus elevation may be performed to support the implant. After placement, the implant is allowed time to integrate with the surrounding bone, creating a secure foundation for the final restoration that will be completed by the restorative dentist.

In many cases, the implant can be placed at the same time as a tooth extraction, which reduces the number of surgical procedures and overall treatment time. When sufficient bone is present, the implant may be placed in a single stage without the need for a second procedure to uncover it later.

Anesthesia, Safety, and Patient Comfort

Implant surgery is typically performed with local anesthesia and may include IV sedation or deeper anesthesia depending on patient needs and case complexity. Safety, comfort, and efficiency are prioritized throughout treatment.

Care is delivered in a controlled clinical setting with trained staff focused on patient reassurance and surgical safety. Most patients experience minimal discomfort during and after implant placement.

Recovery and Follow-Up Care

Healing after implant placement varies depending on bone quality and the number of implants placed. Follow-up visits allow healing to be monitored and help determine when the implant is ready for restoration.

When appropriate, soft tissue grafting may be recommended to support healthy, natural-looking gum tissue around the implant. Post-operative care and long-term follow-up are managed directly by the surgical team.

FAQ’s

What are dental implants made of?

Dental implants are made of medical-grade titanium. This biocompatible material bonds with the jawbone and has been used safely in medical surgery for decades.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper placement, good oral hygiene, and regular dental care, implants can last many years and often decades.

Is surgery painful?

Most patients report minimal discomfort. Anesthesia and sedation options help keep patients comfortable during surgery. Post-operative discomfort is typically manageable with prescription or OTC pain medication.

How long does the dental implant process take?

The timeline varies depending on healing and whether additional procedures are needed. Many implants require several months of healing before final restoration.

Do dental implants require bone grafting?

Not always. Some patients have sufficient bone for implant placement without grafting. When bone loss is present, grafting may be recommended to support long-term stability.

What if I have been missing teeth for a long time?

Bone loss often occurs over time, but advanced implant techniques and grafting procedures can frequently make implant placement possible even years after tooth loss.

Am I too old for dental implants?

Age alone is not a limiting factor. Candidacy is based on overall health, bone quality, and healing ability.
