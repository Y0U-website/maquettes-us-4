What Is Scaling & Root Planing?

Scaling and root planing is a non-surgical periodontal treatment designed to treat gum disease by deep-cleaning the areas below the gumline. This procedure removes plaque, tartar, and bacteria from the roots of your teeth and smooths the root surfaces to encourage gum reattachment and healing.

Unlike a routine teeth cleaning, which focuses on the surface of the teeth, scaling and root planing targets deeper pockets of bacteria that cannot be addressed with regular cleanings alone.

Patients often wonder how scaling and root planing compares to regular cleanings offered during a dental cleaning. While routine cleanings focus on maintaining oral health by removing surface plaque and tartar, scaling and root planing is a more intensive treatment designed to combat active gum disease. This targeted approach addresses infection below the gumline, reducing pocket depths and promoting healing.

  • Persistent bad breath or a bad taste in your mouth
  • Receding gums, making teeth appear longer
  • Loose teeth or changes in how your teeth fit together
  • Initial Evaluation Our dentist will begin with a comprehensive examination of your gums, including X-rays and measurements of periodontal pockets, to assess the extent of gum disease.
  • Scaling During the scaling phase, we use specialized tools to remove plaque, tartar, and bacteria from the surfaces of your teeth and below the gumline. This thorough cleaning helps eliminate the source of infection.
  • Root Planing Root planing involves smoothing the root surfaces of your teeth to remove rough areas where bacteria can accumulate. This process encourages the gums to reattach to the teeth, reducing pocket depth and improving gum health.

Caring for Your Gums After Treatment

Following your scaling and root planing procedure, proper aftercare is essential for optimal healing and gum health:

Our team will provide personalized aftercare instructions to support your recovery and ensure the success of your treatment.

  • Brush and floss gently to maintain cleanliness without irritating sensitive areas.
  • Use an antimicrobial mouthwash as recommended by your dentist to reduce bacteria.
  • Avoid smoking, as it can delay healing and worsen gum disease.
  • Schedule follow-up visits to monitor your progress and maintain your results.
