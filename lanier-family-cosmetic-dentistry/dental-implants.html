Dental Implants

  • Wide platform dental implant – is 4.5 mm to 6 mm in diameter and typically placed in the back of the mouth.
  • Mini dental implant – is very narrow and ranges from 1.8 mm to 3.3 mm in diameter. These are typically used when there is not enough room between the roots for a standard implant or when a patient lacks sufficient bone density.

About Dental Implants in Buford, GA

Dental implants offer great stabilization for any type of dental restoration, including dental crowns, dental bridges, and dentures. Since they attach to the actual jaw bone, the false teeth that are connected to the implants can function like natural teeth. Unlike traditional fixed bridges and removable dentures, you will never have to worry about dietary restrictions, speech issues, damaging any healthy teeth, or your restoration coming loose. A dental implant-supported restoration will feel and look so natural that you may forget you have false teeth.

The Dental Implant Process

The dental implant process is much different from having your teeth replaced with dentures or dental bridges. First, the process involves a surgical procedure that requires intricate treatment planning and months of healing time. For this reason, the entire treatment time can take months or even up to a year to complete. The steps in the dental implant process include:

Consultation & Evaluation

The first step in the dental implant process is an evaluation and consultation with your dentist. They will inspect your teeth, gums, and mouth and review your entire medical history. If needed, you will get X-rays taken or scans of your jawbone. This exam will help us determine if dental implants are the right tooth replacement solution for you. It is important that you have adequate bone structure to secure the implant in place.

Based on the evaluation, your dentist will create a personalized treatment plan. This will outline the number of implant fixtures you need, where they need to be placed, and the type of dental restoration you need. It may also include any treatments you may require before the implants can be placed. They will coordinate treatment with an oral surgeon or periodontist who will place the implant.

Implantation

Working from your treatment plan, an oral surgeon or periodontist will place the implant into the bone. They will use local anesthetic and/or dental sedation for your comfort. The length of your surgery depends on how many you are having placed. Strategic planning is crucial to the long-term success and comfort of the final restoration.

The implant fixtures will need about 3 to 6 months to heal and fuse to the surrounding bone. During this time, we will place a temporary dental restoration for cosmetic and functionality purposes.

Abutment Placement

Once the implants are fused to the bone, your dentist will attach a small connector piece to the implants called abutments. They protrude from the gumline and serve as the base for the dental restoration. Then your dentist will take several dental impressions that will be used to fabricate your custom dental restoration.

Implant Restoration

Once the permanent dental restoration is ready, Dr. Brown or Dr. Newberry will permanently attach it to the dental implants. Your new dental crown, dental bridge, or denture is custom-made in a dental lab from molds of your teeth. They will carefully check the fit, function, and aesthetics to ensure a comfortable and flawless tooth replacement solution.

After Care

Dental implants require very little special care. In fact, most people forget they have them. You will be able to floss and brush them just as you do your natural teeth. However, a strict oral care routine is very important to make sure they last a lifetime. Avoiding things like tooth decay and gum disease will ensure your dental implants are in good health. Routine visits to the dentist are very important and will ensure the long-term health and function of your teeth.
