What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are unique in that instead of merely sitting on top of the gums or remaining teeth, they are embedded within the jawbone, allowing them to function exactly like the roots of your natural teeth. A dental implant itself is a small titanium cylinder that one of our partnered specialists surgically positions in your jaw. It then fuses with the surrounding bone tissue, essentially becoming a part of your body. At that point, it can support any number of replacement teeth that stay firmly in place for decades to come.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

The long-term success of dental implants relies on having the right foundation. This requires a multi-step treatment process to replace both the root and crown of a tooth. Although every plan varies, it will take several months to complete your smile. While it may take longer than other methods, it’s time well spent to ensure your new smile lasts forever. Dr. Ho will explain each step in your treatment, so you understand what lies ahead before you commit to the procedure.

Here’s a basic rundown of the four steps of the dental implant process:

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

If you’ve lost one or more teeth, you may be a candidate for dental implants. Dr. Ho will perform the comprehensive consultation you need to ensure they are right for you. She’ll discuss your oral, medical, and lifestyle history while reviewing a CT scan of your mouth. Based on the results of the consultation, you may need additional treatments to be a candidate for dental implants, like gum disease therapy, bone grafting, or a sinus lift.

Dental Implant Surgery

In some cases, you may need sedation or general anesthesia to ensure your comfort. During the process, your gum tissue is opened to expose your jawbone. Small holes are strategically drilled into it to insert the titanium posts. Your tissue is stitched closed, and you’ll spend the next several weeks healing.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

The implant posts stimulate your jaw to encourage new bone growth. Your jaw will fuse to the posts over the next few months through a process called osseointegration. This gives your dental implants a secure foundation to support your restorations.

Before you can get your new tooth, you’ll need a second minor surgery to place abutments on each post. The special fixtures connect your restorations to your implants. It takes about two weeks for your gum tissue to heal before you can move on to the final step in your treatment.

Placement of Dental Implant Restorations

Your restorations which may be crowns, bridges, or dentures, depending on the number of teeth you’re missing are carefully crafted from high-quality material for the utmost durability without compromising aesthetics. They are designed to mimic your natural teeth, including their color, size, and shape. No one will be able to tell they aren’t your real teeth.

After your custom-made restoration is ready, Dr. Ho attaches it to your posts to complete your smile. In just a few days, you won’t even notice your new teeth aren’t your own.

Benefits of Dental Implants

There are many reasons why dentists recommend dental implants for tooth replacement, including the following that you can read about in more detail below. We’re sure once you have a better understanding of why so many people choose them to rebuild their smile, you’ll be looking forward to your future consultation with Dr. Karen Ho! While this is not an exhaustive list of the benefits dental implants offer, they certainly are the most notable ones to consider.

Day-to-Day Benefits

  • Better Aesthetics : Implant-supported teeth offer superior oral function and aesthetics. They’ll be virtually impossible to distinguish from the rest of your natural smile, so you can show it off with confidence whether you’re with friends and family or people you’re meeting for the first time.
  • Enjoy Your Favorite Foods with Ease : Dental implants have no trouble whatsoever breaking down even the toughest foods. Whether you enjoy thick cuts of meat, nuts, crunchy fruits and vegetables, or other foods you wouldn’t be able to eat easily with a denture, dental implants are right for you.
  • Simple At-Home Oral Hygiene : There’s no need to worry about removing your oral appliance just to practice brushing and flossing. Simply perform these habits like you would for your natural teeth to keep your dental implants free of plaque (and your gum tissue healthy as well!)

Health Benefits

  • Boost Your Oral and Overall Health : While traditional dentures and bridges are designed to sit atop the gum line, dental implants are the only reconstructive service today that also replaces the natural tooth root. This provides important, healthy stimulation to the jawbone and helps patients maintain better oral health over the years.
  • Retain Your Natural Tooth Structure : Dental implants can be placed conservatively and won’t require healthy teeth to be negatively altered to accommodate them. This cannot be said for other tooth replacement options like dental bridges.
  • Lower Your Risk for Future Tooth Loss : The more teeth you lose, the more at risk you are of losing additional teeth due to your jawbone tissue deteriorating over time. Consistent stimulation ensures your neighboring teeth remain in place and don’t shift to compensate for the open gap.

Long-Term Benefits

  • The Best Longevity : Implants are designed to last for decades or even the rest of the patient’s life with the help of proper maintenance. This makes them truly unique compared to other solutions, which will require replacement every several years or so.
  • High Success Rate : Dental implants are known for maintaining success rates as high as 98 % when placed by a trained professional. This means you can have the confidence knowing your dental implant will remain in place for decades to come.
  • Save Money on Future Tooth Replacements : It’s true that the upfront cost for dental implants is higher, but this is because the long-term costs associated with treatment are dramatically lower compared to bridges or dentures. This makes them the best long-term solution financially to tooth loss, no matter how severe.

Missing One Tooth

The most lifelike way to replace a single missing tooth is to position one implant post into the empty spot and then secure a customized dental crown on top. Unlike the traditional way to replace one tooth (a dental bridge), an implant doesn’t require the surrounding teeth to be altered in any way, making it the more conservative and healthier choice in the long run.

Missing Multiple Teeth

While we can use several implants and crowns to support multiple missing teeth throughout the mouth, we may elect to have them support a partial denture instead. For several consecutive missing teeth, an implant can be anchored at either end of the gap to provide ample support for a dental bridge that doesn’t rely on remaining teeth. Both of these options are much more reliable and long-lasting than their conventional counterparts.

Missing All Teeth

If all of your natural teeth have been lost on your upper or lower arch, an implant denture can bring them all back in a way that flawlessly mimics your original smile. A small number of implant posts (usually between four and eight) can be strategically positioned throughout the jaw, creating enough support for a full denture. This prosthetic can be either fixed in place or removable, depending on your needs and preferences.

Understanding the Cost of Dental Implants

Types of Dental Implants

The number of teeth missing is probably one of the biggest factors that can influence the final cost of the dental implant process. When one or two teeth are missing, a single implant along with a crown (or a pair of crowns) is likely all you’ll need. And of course, if all your teeth are missing and you want to replace them with implant dentures, you’ll need about four to eight implant posts on average.

Dental Implant FAQs

Can I take dental implants out?

A dental implant is surgically placed into the jaw, which your bone will fuse to through a process called osseointegration. As a result, you can’t take it out on your own. Only a trained dental professional can remove them, like if failure occurs. Although you can’t take the post out, some implant dentures are removable for easy cleaning.

How long do dental implants last?

What are the signs of dental implant failure?

Dental implant failure is rare, but it’s important to monitor your smile for any signs of complications, like redness or swelling of the gums. If you experience pain or your implant feels loose, don’t wait to contact your dentist . Delaying care can cause a small issue to turn into a big problem that might cost you your new smile. If you develop any concerns between your regular cleaning and checkup, schedule an appointment with your dentist right away.

What should I do if my dental implant feels loose?

After your bone has integrated with the post, your implant should never feel loose. If there’s any instability, it can indicate dental implant failure from infection, bone loss, or another problem. However, sometimes the solution is simple, like a loose restoration. Don’t take the risk, contact your dentist for an appointment.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Once you’ve received your dental implants, you’ll naturally want to do what you can to help them last as long as possible. Fortunately, this is simpler than you might think; caring for dental implants isn’t much different from taking care of your natural teeth! If you follow the tips listed below, you’ll be able to protect your new teeth so that you can enjoy the benefits of an implant-supported smile for years to come.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Gum disease is one of the leading causes of implant failure. As such, you’ll want to make sure that the area around your dental implants is as clean as possible; you don’t want bacteria to start attacking the tissues holding your new teeth in place. Remember to brush and floss at least two times every day. You should also rinse with an ADA-approved mouthwash to further reduce the presence of harmful bacteria inside your mouth.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants give you the freedom to eat whatever you want, but you’ll want to put a limit on how often you indulge in sugary or starchy snacks that can put your oral health at risk. Instead, try eating more foods that are rich in calcium and vitamin C; these nutrients contribute to healthy bone and gum tissue, which can go a long way toward helping your dental implants remain in place.

Break Bad Habits

There are several oral habits that could put your dental implants (and your natural teeth) at risk without you even realizing it. Examples of habits you’ll want to avoid include:

Take whatever steps are necessary to break these habits before they lead to problems with your implants. One strategy is to replace a negative habit with a positive one. For instance, people who bite their nails might start carrying around sugar-free gum to chew instead.

  • Smoking or using any kind of tobacco product.
  • Chewing on your fingernails, the tip of your pen, or any other hard, inedible object.
  • Crunching ice, peppermints, and other overly hard foods.
  • Tearing open packaging with your teeth instead of with scissors or another tool designed for the job.

Protect Your Dental Implants

You shouldn’t play any kind of sport without first making sure that your dental implants are properly protected. Invest in a custom-made mouthguard that will keep your entire smile safe if you ever take a blow to the face or jaw. You can also wear a mouthguard at night to protect your implants from involuntary clenching and grinding.

If she finds any issues, she’ll be able to address them long before they pose a serious risk to your new teeth.
