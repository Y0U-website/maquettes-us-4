What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are a permanent tooth replacement solution designed to replace both the visible tooth and its root. The implant itself is a small titanium post that is surgically placed into the jawbone, where it functions like a natural tooth root.

Each dental implant has three main parts. The implant post anchors into the bone, an abutment connects the post to the restoration, and a custom crown completes the visible tooth. Unlike dentures or bridges, implants do not sit on the gums or rely on nearby teeth for support. They become a stable, long-term part of your smile.

Why People Choose Dental Implants

Many people choose dental implants because they look, feel, and function like natural teeth. Implants restore full chewing ability, allowing you to eat comfortably without worrying about movement or slipping.

Dental implants also help prevent jawbone loss that often follows tooth loss. By stimulating the bone, implants preserve facial structure and help prevent a sunken appearance over time. Unlike bridges, implants do not require altering healthy neighboring teeth and can last decades or even a lifetime with proper care.

The Dental Implant Process

Dental implant treatment begins with a detailed consultation and evaluation. Your oral health, bone structure, and overall needs are reviewed to create a personalized plan.

If bone support is limited, a bone graft may be recommended to strengthen the area before implant placement. The implant post is then placed into the jawbone. Over the next three to six months, the bone heals around the implant in a process called osseointegration. Once healing is complete, the abutment and custom crown are placed, completing the restoration.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants

Dental implants work best when the mouth and body can support proper healing and long-term stability. A thorough evaluation helps determine whether implants are the right option now or if preparation is needed first.

Each case is evaluated individually, and treatment plans are adjusted to support long-term success.

  • Missing one or more teeth
  • Adequate bone density or willing to have a bone graft
  • Healthy gums with no active gum disease
  • Good overall health
  • Non-smoker or willing to quit
  • Committed to good oral hygiene
  • Finished growing, typically age 18 or older

Cost of Dental Implants in Tempe (2026 Guide)

The cost of dental implants in Tempe AZ typically ranges from $3,000 to $6,000 per tooth, including the crown. Costs vary depending on whether you need a single implant or multiple implants, whether bone grafting is required, and the materials used.

While dental insurance may not fully cover implants, it often covers portions of treatment such as extractions or the crown. Although implants may cost more upfront than bridges or dentures, they are often more cost-effective long term because they last longer and require fewer replacements.
