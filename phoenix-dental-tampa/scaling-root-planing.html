Scaling and root planing is a deep cleaning treatment used to manage periodontitis , a more advanced form of gum disease. It targets harmful plaque and tartar that build up below the gumline—areas that can’t be reached with regular brushing or standard dental cleanings. Here’s what’s involved: Scaling removes bacterial deposits, plaque, and hardened tartar (calculus) from below the gums and around tooth roots.
  • Root planing smooths the tooth roots to help gums reattach and heal while reducing future buildup.

  • A history of missed cleanings or deep plaque buildup

What Happens During the Procedure?

Scaling and root planing is typically completed in 1–2 visits , depending on the severity of the infection and the number of areas affected. The process includes:

  • Smoothing the root surfaces to discourage future bacterial growth
  • Optional antimicrobial rinses or site-specific antibiotics
  • Post-care instructions to support healing and prevent recurrence

What Comes After Scaling & Root Planing?

Scaling and root planing is only the beginning of periodontal management. After the gums begin to heal, we’ll recheck your progress and typically recommend ongoing periodontal maintenance cleanings every 3–4 months.

Our team will partner with you to build a custom plan that’s achievable and effective.

  • Brush twice daily using a soft-bristled toothbrush
  • Use floss or interdental brushes
  • Avoid smoking or tobacco
  • Follow any prescribed mouth rinses or medications
  • Maintain regular dental checkups and cleanings

FAQs

Is scaling and root planing the same as a deep cleaning? ‍

Yes. Scaling and root planing is the clinical term for a deep gum cleaning that targets infection below the gumline.

Does the procedure hurt? ‍

No. We numb the area before treatment and offer sedation options if needed. Some mild soreness afterward is normal and temporary.

Will I need this treatment more than once? ‍

Possibly. While scaling and root planing often controls the infection, patients with chronic periodontitis may require additional maintenance.

Is this procedure covered by insurance? ‍

Most PPO plans cover scaling and root planing. We’ll help you verify your benefits before treatment begins.

What if gum disease comes back? ‍

Ongoing periodontal maintenance and excellent home care are essential. We’ll work closely with you to manage your oral health and prevent reinfection.
