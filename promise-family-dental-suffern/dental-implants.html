What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a biocompatible titanium post placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Over time, the implant integrates naturally with your bone, creating a secure and stable foundation.

Unlike traditional dentures or bridges, implants do not rely on neighboring teeth for support. They stay firmly in place and help preserve jawbone structure over time.

  • Implant Post – Surgically placed into the jawbone
  • Abutment – Connector between implant and final restoration
  • Custom Crown, Bridge, or Denture - Designed to match your natural smile

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants are designed to be a long-term solution. With proper oral hygiene and regular dental checkups, implants can last several decades. The titanium post that integrates with the jawbone is extremely durable. The crown attached on top may eventually need replacement due to normal wear, but the implant itself often remains stable for many years when properly maintained.

Is dental implant surgery painful?

Implant placement is performed under local anesthesia to ensure comfort. Most patients report that the procedure feels similar to having a tooth extracted. Mild soreness or swelling may occur for a few days afterward, but this is typically manageable with prescribed or over-the-counter medication. Sedation options can also be discussed for patients who feel anxious.

How long does the dental implant process take?

The full process typically takes several months. After the implant is placed, a healing period of approximately 3–6 months allows the implant to integrate with the jawbone. In some cases, timelines may vary depending on bone quality, need for grafting, or overall health. During your consultation in our Suffern office, we provide a personalized treatment timeline.

Are dental implants safe?

Dental implants have been used successfully for decades and are considered one of the most predictable procedures in modern dentistry. When placed following proper surgical protocols and with careful planning, success rates are high. Patient health, oral hygiene, and smoking status can influence outcomes, which is why a comprehensive evaluation is essential.

What happens if I do not have enough bone for implants?

Bone loss can occur after a tooth has been missing for some time. In many cases, bone grafting procedures can help rebuild the area to support an implant. Advanced imaging helps us determine whether grafting is needed. Many patients who initially think they are not candidates may still qualify after proper evaluation.

Are dental implants better than bridges or dentures?

Dental implants offer structural advantages because they replace the root of the tooth. Unlike traditional bridges, implants do not require reshaping adjacent teeth. Compared to removable dentures, implants provide improved stability, stronger chewing ability, and reduced movement. They also help preserve jawbone health, which dentures do not.

How do I care for dental implants?

Caring for dental implants is similar to caring for natural teeth. Daily brushing and flossing are essential. Routine professional cleanings and exams help ensure the surrounding gum tissue remains healthy. Good oral hygiene plays a major role in long-term implant success.

Are dental implants covered by insurance?

Insurance coverage varies depending on your specific dental plan. Some plans may contribute toward parts of the procedure, such as the crown or surgical placement. Our team assists patients in reviewing benefits and explaining out-of-pocket expectations clearly before treatment begins.

Can smokers get dental implants?

Smoking can affect healing and may increase the risk of implant complications. However, smokers are not automatically excluded from treatment. A detailed evaluation allows us to assess individual risk factors and discuss strategies to improve outcomes. Reducing or stopping smoking before and after surgery significantly improves success rates.
