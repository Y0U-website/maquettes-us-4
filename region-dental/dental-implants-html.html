Dental Implant Surgery

Dental implants are a long-term investment in your smile, so it’s essential that they’re placed perfectly. We partner with some of the best oral surgeons in the area to ensure this step in your treatment is as successful, comfortable, and smooth as possible.

To place the dental implant post, the surgeon will make a small incision in your gums to reach the jawbone, where they’ll drill a small hole into a predetermined location. The dental implant post is then inserted and the gums are sutured closed. A small cap will b placed over the top of the implant post to protect it and maintain the shape of your gums as the healing process begins.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

After your surgery, the dental implant post will undergo osseointegration. This is the process where it fuses with the surrounding bone tissue to essentially become a part of your jaw. This is a very gradual process that typically takes four to six months. Then, if the abutment wasn’t attached during the initial surgery, you’ll complete one more minor surgery to have this small connector piece secured into place.

Delivery of Final Restoration

It takes about 2 weeks for your mouth to heal after placing the abutment. During this time, our office is hard at work creating your restoration. We use all-ceramic material to ensure it looks like a real tooth. It’s carefully crafted to match the color, size, and shape of your teeth to blend in. It will look and feel natural, so you can chew, speak, and smile confidently.

Day-to-Day Benefits

Health Benefits

When a tooth root is gone, the body resorbs the nutrients that would normally be sent to the jawbone. Over time, the bone loses density and strength, weakening your ability to bite and chew. Unfortunately, traditional dentures do nothing to stop this degradation, which is why so many denture wearers still struggle with eating fibrous, nutritious foods. As a result, they can suffer from many other health issues and even have a shorter life expectancy. Implants keep the jawbone strong, enabling you to maintain a healthy diet and increasing your quality of life.

Long-Term Benefits

While dentures and bridges need to be replaced about every 10 years at the longest, implants can last for more than 30 years and still be successful. This means that you can hold onto your youthful facial structure for as long as possible, continue to have a strong jawbone, and save money on tooth replacements down the road. Although this treatment involves a lot of work, time, and resources upfront, implants could end up costing you less overall, allowing you to enjoy more freedom as you age.

Missing 1 Tooth

If you have a single gap in your smile, we may suggest an implant-retained crown. Without altering your surrounding healthy teeth, we can restore the root and biting surface of your tooth with a custom-crafted crown made from strong porcelain or ceramic. Not only does this option last longer than a traditional crown, but it also will keep your other teeth from drifting out of place to fill in the gap of your missing tooth.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Traditional bridges require the preparation of your surrounding natural teeth to anchor the replacement teeth in place. Instead of putting your healthy pearly whites at risk, we may suggest replacing two or more consecutive teeth with an implant-retained bridge. They will be secured in place with titanium implants, allowing for a natural look and feel.

Missing All Teeth

For patients with significant tooth loss, the most efficient solution is an implant denture. Instead of relying on the gums or remaining healthy teeth to hold them in place like traditional full or partial denture, implant dentures are anchored into place with four to eight dental implants.

We offer many types of implant dentures, including both removable and fixed (non-removable). We can also help you replace all your missing teeth with All-On-4 implant dentures. The Parts of Your Dental Implant

There are several important factors that will affect the cost of your dental implant. These include:

  • The number of dental implants you need: For example, one dental implant to replace a single missing tooth will cost less than four to six for an implant denture; however, an implant denture is more cost effective than replacing an entire arch of teeth separately.
  • The type of restoration you’ve selected: This may include a crown, bridge, or denture, each of which cost different amounts. The smaller the restoration, the less expensive it will be.
  • The size or material of your dental implant: Some dental implants are made longer or shorter depending on their placement within the jawbone. Also, some may be made with zirconia instead of titanium.
  • The brand of dental implants: Much like car or smartphone brands, different manufacturers make different dental implants to suit different needs.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are remarkable dental restorations that may keep your smile complete and healthy for decades or a lifetime if you take excellent care of them. Thankfully, these incredible devices require only the same good habits necessary for natural teeth, which makes keeping them functional and beautiful an easy task. Here’s a quick guide to a few of the best ways to maintain your dental implants from the friendly staff at Region Dental.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While dental implants are technically immune to cavities, they can still be compromised if the gum or bone tissue supporting them becomes infected. To prevent this, stick to an excellent daily oral hygiene regiment that includes brushing, flossing, and rinsing with antibacterial mouthwash to eliminate plaque and food debris. Just be sure to be especially careful when flossing since pressing too deep into the gum tissue can lead to injury.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants give you the chewing power you need to eat whatever you like, so make sure to enjoy a balanced and diverse diet that provides the nutrients your body needs to stay healthy. While lean meats, snappy, raw veggies, and fresh fruit are all tasty and excellent options, consuming items that are sticky, sugary, or acidic too frequently can damage your dental implants.

Break Bad Habits

Tobacco use is utterly horrible for your oral health, so it’s best to quit a smoking, chewing, dipping, or vaping habit before receiving implants. Excessive alcohol use can also be harmful to your gum tissue, so be sure to enjoy hard beverages only in moderation. It’s also important to abstain from using your teeth to open packages or bottles since this can destroy your natural and artificial teeth.

Protect Your Dental Implants

While dental implants are quite strong, they are not indestructible and can be damaged due to injury. If you enjoy playing sports or riding a bike, remember to wear an athletic mouthguard for protection against falls and blows. If you grind your teeth in your sleep, be sure to wear an appropriate device to reduce the damage. We can provide you with a customized nightguard that provides superior protection.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Visit our office in La Porte every six months for routine checkups and cleanings. These visits allow us to monitor the health of your natural teeth and dental implants, address problems in their early stages, and recommend better hygiene practices for you to apply at home.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

Dental implants can last for several decades or even a lifetime if they receive excellent care, which makes them a far more lasting investment than traditional restorations like dentures and bridges. To keep your implants in excellent shape, remember to adhere to the following rules:

  • Brush, floss, and rinse with antibacterial mouthwash every day.
  • Visit our office in La Porte every six months for routine exams and cleanings.
  • Refrain from using your teeth to open packages or bottles.
  • Chew mindfully when enjoying crunchy or sticky foods.
  • Kick bad habits like tobacco use and excessive alcohol consumption.

Can I Take Dental Implants Out?

Dental implants are surgically inserted into the jawbone and fused with the bone structure through the natural process of osseointegration, so they should not be removable without the help of a qualified dental professional. However, some types of dentures mounted on implants are designed to be removed for hygiene purposes. Never attempt to remove a dental implant without professional assistance, but contact our office if you find that you can take your dental implant out on your own.
