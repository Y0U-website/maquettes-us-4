What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are titanium or zirconia tooth replacements that are designed to restore the entire tooth structure from the root to the crown. Surgically implanted into the jawbone, they fuse with the bone through a process known as osseointegration. Their ability to provide greater stability, functionality, longevity, and better oral health makes them a popular option for individuals suffering from tooth loss.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Every dental implant patient has a different treatment plan, but the same steps must be followed to ensure the placement is successful. By learning how dental implants work, you can better understand the process and prepare for each stage, which consists of four specific steps: initial consultation, dental implant surgery, osseointegration, and delivery of the final restoration. Here’s a brief look at each part of the treatment so that you know what to expect.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

For the first stage, you will meet with Dr. Omrani to discuss your candidacy and if you require preliminary treatment before receiving your dental implants. Some patients might have existing or growing oral health issues that will need to be addressed first, such as decay, cavities, or periodontal infection. If necessary, we can perform gum disease treatment, root canal therapy, or even tooth extraction to prepare your mouth. Once you’re back to oral health, we can discuss your treatment plan for placing your dental implants.

Dental Implant Surgery

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Over the next 3-6 months, osseointegration will occur which causes the bone and implants to fuse. This is the essential process for creating the natural foundation for your future restoration(s). Once healed, you’ll return to our office to receive your metal abutments, which are small connector pieces that will link your restoration(s) to your dental implants. You’ll then be sent home to allow your gums to heal.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

The final step is to receive your customized restoration. After your gums are properly recovered, you’ll come back to us for your brand-new teeth. Crowns, bridges, and dentures are all possible options depending on how many teeth you are missing. Once we’ve fitted your restoration(s) in place, we’ll verify that your bite is correct and that you like your results before sending you on your way to enjoy your fully rebuilt smile.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Our team is happy to help you rebuild your grin, but not at the expense of your general well-being. During your consultation, we’ll gather information about your medical history and list your medications so that we don’t offer any treatments or drugs that might interfere with any preexisting conditions.

Some traits that make for a good dental implant candidate include:

  • Good overall health. These restorations are surgically placed into your jawbone so your body must be strong enough to undergo the procedure.
  • Strong teeth and gums. Tooth decay and gum disease can interfere with your results or lead to implant failure, so they will need to be remedied before proceeding.
  • Sufficient jawbone density. Your jaw begins to erode after losing teeth because there are fewer roots to stimulate new bone growth every time you bite down or chew. If it’s too thin, a bone graft may bulk it up enough for dental implants.

Missing One Tooth

Dental implants are ideal for replacing a single missing tooth in qualified patients. First, a titanium rod will be placed into your jawbone. Once your bone tissue fuses with the metal, it’s capped with a dental crown that’s customized to match the size, shape, and shade of your natural teeth for seamless results. Unlike dental bridges or dentures, your restoration is a sturdy standalone structure that doesn’t require that any enamel or remaining teeth be removed.

Missing Multiple Teeth

If you have multiple gaps in your grin and are worried you can’t address them all with dental implants, you’re in luck! These prosthetics can be adapted to replace numerous missing teeth throughout your mouth one by one if needed. However, if several went missing in a row, we might suggest an implant bridge. Instead of anchoring to abutment teeth on either side of the space in your smile, we’ll attach it to two strategically placed titanium rods for support.

Missing All Teeth

For those who are missing all their teeth but are concerned that dentures aren’t sturdy enough, you might do better with an implant denture. This involves surgically placing 4 to 6 implants as needed to support a full set of dentures that completely refurbishes your grin all at one time. Unlike traditional dentures, these are not removable. They’ll be permanently’ attached to your implants and can only be taken out by Dr. Omrani or another qualified professional.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants can last for more than 30 years or even the rest of your life if you are taking all the right steps to care for them. If you aren’t taking the time to protect your dental implants from becoming damaged, they could end up failing and needing to be replaced. The good news is that with just a few simple precautions, you can keep your implants healthy and enjoy their benefits for numerous years down the road.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Dental implants cannot get cavities themselves, but you should still practice excellent oral hygiene despite that. In addition to keeping your surrounding teeth in good shape, sticking to a great oral hygiene routine can prevent other dental problems, like gum disease, that can cause your implants to fail. Remember to brush, floss, and rinse with mouthwash every day.

Eat a Healthy Diet

One of the many benefits of getting dental implants is the fact that you won’t have any eating restrictions. You can easily enjoy nutritious meals without struggling to chew. Just remember that enjoying too many foods that are hard, sticky, or sugary can be problematic for your oral health, so it still pays to be wary.

Break Bad Habits

Refrain from smoking or using tobacco products immediately after your dental implant placement procedure. This can slow down healing and increase your risk of developing an infection. Don’t use your teeth as tools either. You could end up accidentally damaging your dental implants or surrounding teeth. Instead, try to find some scissors, pliers, a bottle opener, or another tool to help you safely complete your task.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Your dental implants may be strong, but they aren’t indestructible. You should always wear a mouthguard to protect your dental implants and natural teeth when participating in sports. If you grind your teeth at night, ask about a nightguard to protect your implants from additional wear and tear.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

You should always attend regular cleanings and checkups twice a year. This way, we can monitor your dental implants and spot any issues as early as possible. By catching potential problems now, you can prevent them from becoming more serious and leading to greater consequences, like a dental implant failure.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

The main priority during the healing process is to leave the blood clot at the surgical site untouched. This way, the healing can occur smoothly. Here are a few rules that you will need to follow after your procedure:

  • Don’t spit. Instead, use a tissue or swallow your saliva.
  • Don’t use a straw when drinking liquids.
  • Don’t smoke right away after your procedure.
  • Keep your tongue and fingers away from the surgical site.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

You will likely experience some mild side effects for the first few days after your procedure. Here are the most common ones:

  • Bleeding: Minor bleeding is normal for a few days after your surgery. This will subside with gauze and light pressure.
  • Swelling: For the first 72 hours after your procedure, inflammation is likely. Use a cold compress to reduce this.
  • Discomfort: Soreness is normal after the procedure. This can be managed with over-the-counter pain relievers and prescribed medicines.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

For the first few days following your procedure, it’s a good idea to stick to softer foods so you don’t aggravate the surgical site. Here are some suggestions to enjoy during this initial recovery period:

  • Mashed potatoes
  • Yogurt
  • Ice cream
  • Pudding
  • Soup
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Pasta

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

You can brush your teeth as normal the day after your surgery. However, you should be especially careful to avoid the surgical sites. To keep this area clean, gently rinse your mouth with salt water two to three times a day. When you use mouthwash, choose one that doesn’t have high levels of alcohol. This can aggravate the area around your implant.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Now that your replacement teeth have been attached, the challenging part is done! Once your new crown, bridge, or denture has been attached to your implant(s), you might experience some minor sensitivity, but this should be easy to manage with over-the-counter pain relievers. There shouldn’t be any swelling, bleeding, or extensive recovery. You will be ready to show off your complete smile!

What Can Cause Dental Implants to Fail?

Even though it’s uncommon, dental implant failure usually occurs for one of two reasons:

A few other causes of dental implant failure include chronic teeth grinding (bruxism), tobacco use, poor oral hygiene, and certain medications. During your consultation, Dr. Omrani will explain how you can minimize your risk of implant failure.

  • Peri-Implantitis A form of gum disease that develops from poor oral hygiene, damaging the bone and gums supporting the implant.
  • Failed Osseointegration This happens when the implant fails to successfully integrate with the jawbone. It’s more common in patients who had low jawbone density to begin with.

Can I Get Dental Implants If I Smoke?

It’s certainly possible for smokers to get dental implants, but they have a higher rate of implant failure. Smoking and chewing tobacco cause dry mouth, which delays healing and interferes with the implants’ ability to fuse with the jawbone. For that reason, it’s strongly recommended that smokers quit at least two weeks before the procedure and wait to resume the habit for at least two to three months afterward. Of course, this is the perfect opportunity to kick the habit to the curb for good.
