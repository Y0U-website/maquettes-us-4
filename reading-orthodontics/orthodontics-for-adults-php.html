What is the Difference Between Adult and Child Orthodontics?

The main difference in treating adults and children is that children’s jawbones are still developing, and they are experiencing rapid tooth movement. Adults’ jawbones have stopped growing, which may require orthognathic surgery to realign the jawbones, where necessary.

In addition, adults are more likely to experience gum disease and related conditions, such as bone loss, due to crooked or misaligned teeth. Misalignment can also cause uneven wear and tear on tooth enamel, resulting in tooth loss. Missing teeth can cause neighboring teeth to shift, further distorting the bite and increasing the possibility of gum disease.

Finally, some adults had braces as adolescents and either never finished their treatment or did not keep up with aftercare protocols, causing the teeth to shift out of alignment.

It’s never too late to achieve a beautiful, healthy smile! Properly aligned teeth are easier to keep clean and can help prevent issues caused by poor dental hygiene. In fact, leaving your misaligned teeth untreated can actually contribute to health and dental problems down the line that can be dangerous — and costly.
