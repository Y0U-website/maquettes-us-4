Common Reasons for Implant Restoration

Even with proper care, restorations may eventually need to be repaired or replaced. Some of the most common reasons include:

If you’re experiencing discomfort, changes in how your bite feels, or visible damage to your implant-supported restoration, we recommend scheduling an evaluation as soon as possible.

  • Fractured or chipped crown
  • Loose or broken abutment (the connector between implant and crown)
  • Worn-down prosthetic teeth on bridges or dentures
  • Gum recession or tissue irritation around the implant
  • Bite misalignment or pressure-related wear
  • Cosmetic concerns due to age or aesthetics of older restorations

Our Implant Restoration Process

At Creekside Dental, our goal is to preserve your existing implant whenever possible and restore your smile with precision and comfort.

1. Evaluation and Diagnosis

We begin with a clinical exam and, if needed, imaging to determine the condition of the implant, abutment, and prosthetic.

2. Customized Treatment Plan

Depending on the issue, we may recommend repairing a minor chip, replacing a cracked crown, or fabricating a new bridge or denture to attach to your existing implants.

3. Restoration Placement

Once your new restoration is ready, we check the fit, ensure proper bite alignment, and securely attach it to your implant. We’ll also review care instructions to help you get the most out of your restored smile.

Implant Restoration vs. New Implant Placement

Not all implant concerns require surgery or replacing the implant itself. In fact, most problems involve the prosthetic portion rather than the titanium post embedded in the bone.

If the implant post remains healthy and stable, we can often restore the tooth without additional surgery. However, if the implant has failed due to infection, bone loss, or trauma, we may discuss options for implant replacement or alternative treatments like dental bridges .

Supporting Long-Term Implant Health

A restored implant still requires daily care and regular checkups. To maintain long-term success, we recommend:

By monitoring the implant and surrounding tissue, we can catch issues early and help you protect your investment in your smile.

  • Brushing twice daily with a soft toothbrush
  • Cleaning around implants using floss threaders or water flossers
  • Scheduling routine cleanings and exams at least twice per year
  • Using a nightguard if you clench or grind your teeth
  • Avoiding smoking, which can affect gum and bone health

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implant Restoration

Can a dental implant crown be replaced?

Yes. If your implant crown is chipped, loose, or showing signs of wear, we can replace it while keeping the implant post in place.

What if my implant feels loose?

A loose implant can be caused by issues with the crown, abutment, or implant post. It’s important to schedule an exam quickly to determine the cause and avoid further complications.

Is implant restoration covered by insurance?

Many insurance plans cover part of the cost for implant restorations. We’ll review your benefits and discuss any out-of-pocket costs before treatment.

How long does a new implant crown last?

With proper care, a new implant crown can last 10–15 years or more. Materials like zirconia or porcelain are highly durable and natural-looking.

Do I need to go back to the original dentist who placed the implant?

No. If you’ve moved or prefer a new provider, we can evaluate and restore your implant even if it was placed elsewhere.
