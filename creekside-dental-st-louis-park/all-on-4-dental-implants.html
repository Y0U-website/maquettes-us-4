3. Implant Placement

Four implants are placed into the jaw to support the full arch. Local anesthesia is used during the procedure, and comfort options may be discussed based on your health and treatment plan.

When appropriate, Creekside Dental coordinates with trusted specialists for surgical care. The exact sequence depends on whether teeth must be removed, whether additional procedures are needed, and whether a temporary restoration can be placed.

4. Healing and Implant Integration

After placement, the implants need time to integrate with the surrounding jawbone. During this phase, patients generally follow a modified diet and attend scheduled follow-up visits.

Healing time varies. Medical health, bone quality, smoking, oral hygiene, and the extent of treatment can all affect the process.

5. Final Full-Arch Restoration

Once healing is sufficient, the temporary prosthesis may be replaced with a custom final restoration. The dentist evaluates the fit, bite, speech, appearance, and ease of cleaning before completing treatment.

Risks and Limitations

All-on-4 treatment involves surgery and requires ongoing maintenance. Possible risks and limitations include:

The implants and prosthetic arch are separate components. Even when the implants remain stable, the attached restoration may require adjustment, repair, or replacement because of normal wear.

  • Temporary swelling, soreness, or bruising
  • Bleeding or infection
  • Delayed healing
  • Failure of an implant to integrate with the bone
  • Gum inflammation around an implant
  • Changes in speech while adapting to the prosthesis
  • Food collecting beneath the restoration
  • Wear, fracture, loosening, or repair of prosthetic components
  • The need for future maintenance or replacement
  • Additional treatment if bone or gum support is insufficient
