Sleep Apnea

Sleep apnea treatment with custom oral appliances

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) causes you to stop breathing — sometimes dozens of times an hour — while you sleep. Untreated, it’s linked to heart disease, stroke, diabetes, and chronic fatigue. The signs most patients notice first: loud snoring, gasping awake, morning headaches, brain fog, or a partner who says you stop breathing at night.

CPAP is the gold standard for severe sleep apnea, and we’ll always tell you when that’s the right answer. But for mild-to-moderate OSA — or for patients who genuinely can’t tolerate a CPAP mask — a custom dental oral appliance is a quieter, more discreet alternative.

The appliance looks similar to a thin night guard. It’s worn only at night and gently repositions your lower jaw forward to keep your airway open. It’s easy to clean, easy to travel with, and most patients adapt within a few weeks.

Sleep apnea is a medical diagnosis, not a dental one — so treatment starts with your physician and a sleep study (usually a home test these days). Once you have a diagnosis and a referral for oral appliance therapy, Dr. Kelvin Jung designs and fits the device, then partners with you on follow-up adjustments to make sure it’s actually working.

Bridges

When a tooth is missing, the impact goes far beyond appearance. Your bite changes, neighboring teeth shift, and everyday things like chewing or smiling can feel noticeably different. A dental bridge restores that balance by replacing the missing tooth with a beautifully crafted, natural looking restoration that is securely supported by crowns or implants.

At Q Dental, each bridge is designed with intention. We use advanced digital imaging and high-strength ceramics to create a restoration that blends seamlessly with your natural teeth while delivering long-term strength and comfort. Because we control the entire design and fabrication process in our office, we ensure a precise fit and a result that feels like it has always been part of your smile.

It is more than filling a space—it is bringing back the harmony, function, and confidence that come with a complete smile.

Gum Disease

Your gums play a critical role in the health and longevity of your smile. When signs of gum disease appear, such as bleeding or inflammation, our personalized periodontal care focuses on halting infection and restoring the supporting structures that keep your teeth stable.

Using technologies like guided biofilm therapy and dental laser treatment, we are able to remove harmful bacteria with remarkable accuracy and comfort. This modern, minimally invasive approach helps promote healthier gums, improved healing, and better long term outcomes.

No, most crowns are placed without a root canal. A root canal is only needed when the nerve inside the tooth is infected, dying, or already dead — which usually shows up as pain, swelling, or specific findings on an X-ray. A tooth that needs a crown because it’s cracked, worn, or has a large filling does NOT automatically need a root canal first. We test the nerve, review the tooth’s history, and look at the imaging. If the nerve is healthy, we crown the tooth and move on. If it’s not, we discuss the root canal as its own decision.

We almost exclusively place composite (tooth-colored) fillings. Composite bonds directly to your tooth, looks natural, and requires less removal of healthy tooth structure than the older silver fillings. For larger restorations where composite isn’t strong enough, we discuss either a ceramic inlay/onlay or a same-day CEREC crown. We don’t routinely place amalgam anymore. The materials we use are FDA-approved, BPA-free, and selected for durability and aesthetics.

A well-placed composite filling typically lasts 7–15 years, sometimes longer. Lifespan depends on size (smaller lasts longer), location (chewing surfaces wear faster), your bite, oral hygiene, and decay risk. We’ve seen fillings last 20+ years and others fail within a few years for reasons that have nothing to do with how they were placed. Our job is to place the smallest, best-bonded restoration possible and watch it over time at your checkups so we can intervene early if needed.

A well-made crown typically lasts 10–20 years, often longer. Modern materials like zirconia and lithium disilicate (used in our same-day CEREC crowns) are extremely strong. Lifespan depends on your bite, whether you grind, your hygiene, and the health of the underlying tooth. Crowns can fail at the margin (where crown meets tooth) due to decay, or the underlying tooth can fracture. We design crowns digitally for a precise fit, which extends lifespan. If you grind, we’ll recommend a nightguard to protect your investment.

Yes, though most root canals are highly successful, with a 90–95 % success rate over 10 years when done well. When they fail, it’s usually one of three reasons: a missed canal during the original procedure, a new infection from a leaky filling or crown, or a hidden crack in the tooth. We use 3D CBCT imaging on complex cases to map every canal and reduce miss rates. If a root canal does fail, options include retreatment, surgery (apicoectomy), or extraction with implant placement. Each path has its place — we’ll walk you through the right call for your tooth.

Mild sensitivity for a few days to a few weeks is normal — especially to cold or pressure. Causes include nerve inflammation from the procedure, a slightly high bite making the tooth hit harder than the others, or the nerve adjusting to the new restoration. If pain persists past 2–4 weeks or is getting worse, call us. A bite adjustment fixes most cases — a quick polish that takes about 2 minutes. If sensitivity is severe, sharp, or causing throbbing pain, the nerve may be more inflamed than expected, and we’ll evaluate whether a root canal is needed.
