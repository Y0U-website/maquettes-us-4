Peg Laterals

Peg laterals can disrupt symmetry, proportion, and overall smile harmony. Our high-end cosmetic approach replaces or enhances these smaller teeth with beautifully sculpted ceramic restorations designed to blend seamlessly with your existing teeth. With expert attention to shape, shade, and translucency, we restore elegance and balance—creating a result so natural, no one will ever know they weren’t your original teeth.

Congenitally Missing Teeth

When teeth never develop, the solution must be both aesthetic and long-lasting. We take a multi-disciplinary, design-driven approach to replacing congenitally missing teeth—whether through implants, bridges, veneers, or a combination of methods. Using digital smile design and premium materials, we create restorations that integrate flawlessly with your natural dentition, ensuring beauty, function, and stability that will serve you for decades.

Whitening

Our professional whitening systems deliver radiant, high-end results that go far beyond drugstore options. Dark Discolored Teeth

Severely dark or discolored teeth require elevated cosmetic expertise. Whether caused by trauma, old restorations, internal discoloration, or developmental conditions, we offer premium solutions including internal bleaching, layered ceramic veneers, and high-translucency crowns. Our meticulous shade-matching process restores uniformity and beauty while preserving a natural, lifelike appearance—so you can smile freely with complete confidence.

Cosmetic Dentistry FAQs

Ceramic veneers typically last 10–20 years; composite bonding typically lasts 5–10 years. Lifespan depends on your bite, grinding habits, oral hygiene, and whether you wear a nightguard. We design veneers digitally — using a smile preview before we start — so the bite is balanced and the load is distributed properly. That extends lifespan. Both veneers and bonding can be replaced at end-of-life; it’s not a one-time investment forever, but it’s a long-term one.

Depends on the size, location, and veneer material. A small chip on the edge can sometimes be polished — quick fix in one visit. A larger chip or break usually means replacing the veneer entirely, since ceramic doesn’t bond reliably to itself. If multiple veneers chip in a short period, we look at the underlying cause — typically grinding, clenching, or a bite issue. Fixing the cause is part of fixing the veneer; otherwise, the new one chips for the same reason.

Yes. Composite is more porous than ceramic, so it can pick up stains from coffee, tea, red wine, smoking, and curry over time. Re-polishing may help restore some of the color and removes some of the surface staining. Bonding typically lasts 5–10 years before it needs to be redone. The tradeoff: bonding is less expensive than veneers, doesn’t require enamel removal, and can be redone. Veneers stain less but cost more and require permanent enamel reduction. We help you decide based on your specific case and goals.

Always before. Here’s why: veneers and bonding are color-matched to your natural teeth at the time they’re made, and they can’t be whitened later. Whatever shade your teeth are when we make the veneer is the shade you’re locked into. So we whiten first, let the color stabilize for 2–3 weeks, then design the restorations to match the new lighter shade. If you whiten after, your natural teeth will end up brighter than the veneers, and the mismatch is hard to fix.
