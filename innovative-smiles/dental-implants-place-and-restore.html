How Does the Dental Implant Process Work?

The dentist in University Heights, OH, will begin by surgically removing your teeth.

The dentist will surgically insert small titanium posts into your jawbone. This is the placement step. The purpose of this step is to replace the roots of your teeth. Over the next three to six months, those titanium posts will fuse with your jawbone. This is called osseointegration. The dentist will place a protective cap over your implants to protect them and give your jaw time to heal.

Once your implants have healed and developed a sturdy foundation, your dentist can proceed with the first part of the restoration process. During this part of the restoration, the dentist will add an abutment to your implant. The protective cap over your implant screw will be removed. Then, the abutment will be added. The abutment part of your dental implant is the part that connects your implant screw to your artificial tooth. This completes the abutment placement. After the abutment placement, it will take another week or two to heal before the final restoration can take place.

The last step is restoration placement. This is where your dentist will attach your artificial tooth to the abutment, completing your dental implant restoration.

Implant Retained Dentures

For anyone looking for alternatives to traditional dentures, Innovative Smiles offers implant retained dentures. As an alternative to regular dental implants and traditional dentures, implant retained dentures combine the best of both options. If you’re currently considering dentures or dental implants, you owe it to yourself to learn more about implant retained dentures.

What Are Implant Retained Dentures?

Implant retained dentures are similar yet different to regular dental implants. Whereas a dental implant is most often used to replace individual teeth, implant retained dentures can replace an entire arch of teeth. So, rather than placing an implant for each missing tooth, a few strategically placed implants can support a full or partial denture. This approach is more cost-effective, time-effective and less invasive than replacing each missing tooth with a separate implant. You and your dentist will be able to determine if this is an appropriate solution for your needs.

How Implant Retained Dentures Differ From Dentures

Dentures have been around for hundreds of years, and they remain a popular choice for restoring missing teeth. However, they aren’t ideal. They rely on dental adhesives and suction to stay in place, resting on the gums of the wearer. They can be uncomfortable, and may not fit properly over time. They come with inherent challenges, like slipping, difficulty eating or speaking, and sometimes discomfort or downright pain.

Implant retained dentures, on the other hand, are anchored to the jawbone, eliminating the potential for slipping. They provide a stable and comfortable fit, allowing for easier eating, speaking and overall oral health. Finally, since they’re supported by implants, they can help prevent bone loss in the jaw, which dentures can’t do.

Benefits of Implant Retained Dentures

There are many reasons why patients opt for implant retained dentures over other treatments. They include:

With implant retained dentures, there’s no risk of embarrassing slippage or a “clicking” noise when eating or speaking. They are stable in place, since they’re firmly anchored to the jawbone. This stability also adds to their comfort, since they won’t slip and cause friction against sensitive gum tissue.

Dental implants stimulate the jawbone, preventing the bone loss that typically occurs after tooth loss. This helps maintain the shape and structure of the face, preventing the sunken appearance often associated with long-term denture use. This isn’t the case with traditional dentures.
