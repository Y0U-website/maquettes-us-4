What Are Dental Implants?

Dental Implants replace the root of your missing or extracted tooth . A dental crown is then placed on top of the implant to replace the top of the tooth that is visible above the gum line. Dental implants are constructed from titanium, which is a biocompatible material that binds to your jawbone. Making your jaw bone and surrounding teeth more stable. Dental implants are also anchored dental bridges and full or partial dentures.

Dental implants provide you have some of the same options for restorative dentistry as you have with natural teeth. These options can range from single-tooth replacement to dental bridges, and fixed, removable, full, or partial dentures .

What to Expect

After the tooth has been extracted and given time to heal properly, the implant is ready to be placed deep inside the jaw bone. The oral surgeon will give you instructions before the procedure for your safety and a local sedative will be given to your tooth to aid in your comfort. The doctor will drill a hole into your jawbone, which feels similar to having a cavity drilled.

After the dental implant treatment is completed you will be sent home with another set of instructions that will aid in your teeth and gums healing properly. For example, you will be asked to avoid hard or hot foods to avoid damage to the affected area while you are still recovering from the oral sedative. There will also be follow-up appointments to ensure your full recovery.

Are there risks involved with implants?

Dental implant surgery is considered to be one of the safest and most predictable types of surgery. However, like all surgery, there are some risks involved such as infection or nerve injury. The oral health of each patient is also a factor in the success rate of the dental implant.
