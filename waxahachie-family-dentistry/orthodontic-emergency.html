Poking or Sharp Wires

Wires can shift or break, poking your cheeks, gums, or tongue. This is one of the most common orthodontic complaints and can cause significant discomfort.

  • Use a clean pencil eraser to gently push the wire away from sensitive tissues.
  • Apply orthodontic wax over the sharp end to create a barrier.
  • Rinse with saltwater to soothe irritation (mix 1 tsp salt with 8 oz warm water).

Lost or Broken Aligners

Invisalign aligners can crack, break, or get lost, disrupting your treatment plan. This is especially concerning since aligners are custom-made for each stage of your treatment.

  • If the aligner is slightly cracked but wearable, continue using it until you can see your orthodontist in Waxahachie.
  • If you’ve lost an aligner, revert to the previous set (if available) and contact your orthodontist immediately.
  • Avoid going without aligners for more than a day to prevent teeth from shifting.

Severe Pain or Swelling

Some discomfort is normal after adjustments, but severe pain or swelling could indicate an infection, injury, or appliance issue.

  • Take over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen (follow dosage instructions).
  • Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling.
  • Rinse with saltwater to keep the area clean.

Infections or Sores

Braces or aligners can cause sores or irritation, and poor oral hygiene may lead to infections. Signs include redness, swelling, or pus around the gums or appliance.

  • Rinse with saltwater or an antiseptic mouthwash to reduce bacteria.
  • Apply dental wax to prevent further irritation from brackets or wires.
  • Maintain good oral hygiene (brush and floss twice daily).

How to Prevent Orthodontic Emergencies

Some emergencies are preventable. Follow these tips to keep your braces or clear aligners in top shape:

  • Avoid Problem Foods: Steer clear of sticky, hard, or chewy foods like caramel, popcorn, or ice that can damage braces.
  • Maintain Oral Hygiene: Brush and floss daily, and use tools like interdental brushes or water flossers for braces.
  • Wear a Mouthguard: Protect your appliances during sports with a custom-fitted mouthguard.
  • Follow Orthodontist Instructions: Stick to your treatment plan, including wearing aligners or elastics as prescribed.
  • Attend Regular Appointments : Routine checkups catch potential issues early.

Signs of an Orthodontic Emergency

Knowing the signs of an orthodontic emergency can help you identify and address problems as soon as possible. Common signs to watch out for during orthodontic treatment include:

  • Pain and Discomfort: Persistent pain or discomfort in your teeth, gums, or jaw may indicate an orthodontic emergency that requires attention.
  • Loose Bands or Brackets: If you notice loose or dislodged brackets or bands, contact your orthodontist to have them repaired or repositioned.
  • Protruding Wires: Wires that protrude, poke, or irritate the cheeks or gums can cause discomfort. Use orthodontic wax to cover the protruding wire and seek professional help.
  • Swollen or Bleeding Gums: Swelling or bleeding around the gums could indicate gum irritation or infection, requiring immediate attention.
  • Chipped or Broken Appliances : Damaged braces or aligners can disrupt treatment progress. Contact your orthodontist to have them assessed and replaced if necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fix a broken braces bracket at home?

You can temporarily manage a broken braces bracket with orthodontic wax, but you should contact your orthodontist for a professional repair to avoid treatment delays.

How do I stop a poking wire from hurting?

Use a pencil eraser to gently push the wire away and apply orthodontic wax over the sharp end. If the pain persists, call your orthodontist for an adjustment.

What should I do if I lose my Invisalign aligner?

Revert to your previous aligner (if available) and contact your orthodontist immediately for a replacement. Avoid going without aligners to prevent tooth movement.
