Removable Versus Implant Supported Dentures

There are two popular types of Implant Supported Dentures : implant-supported and removable, particularly for those that replace an entire set of teeth. The first uses implants in your jaw and is permanent in position, whilst the second is removed, as the names suggest.

Different Implant Supported Dentures Types

Bar-retained and ball-retained are the two primary implant-supported denture anchoring methods. In either scenario, the system is supported by a number of jaw implants. Dentures are frequently composed of acrylic or porcelain and mimic real teeth in appearance. How the dentures attach to the implants is the primary distinction between the two kinds.

  • Bar-Retained: In this arrangement, a metal bar traces the jaw’s contour. The implants secure it to the mouth. The dentures are secured to the bar by a connection.
  • Ball-retained Dentures that are "ball-retained," also known as "stud-attached" dentures, more closely affix the implants to the dentures. Each implant has a tiny attachment that fits into a socket on the dentures. This attachment usually has a spherical form, as the name suggests.

Implant supported Dentures Advantages

Implant Supported Dentures are a common option for tooth replacement. Our team gives each patient with comprehensive guidance and is well-versed in both its advantages and disadvantages. These are some advantages of implant-supported dentures, said the American Academy of Periodontology :

  • Patients feel them to be comforting: Since Implant Supported Dentures are more secure in position than removable dentures, most people find them to be more relaxed. This implies that unlike loose, detachable dentures, implant-supported dentures won’t irritate your mouth or develop blisters.
  • Natural-looking: Dentures supported by implants resemble real teeth. So you don’t need a set of dentures to have a stunning smile. For many people, this can significantly enhance their confidence.
  • Efficient Tooth Replacement: Prior to the use of dentures, tooth loss could have an impact on a person’s ability to talk and chew solid foods. As a result, patients may talk more clearly and eat a wider variety of foods than they did before they lost their teeth.
  • Improves Bone Health: Because they press and brush against your jawbone all the time, removable dentures might damage it. However, implant-supported dentures are more solidly fastened. They have similar effects on your jawbone as natural teeth do.
