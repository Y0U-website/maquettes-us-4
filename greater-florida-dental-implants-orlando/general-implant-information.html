FAQ About Dental Implants

  • How many visits does implant treatment usually take? Implant treatment is often completed in phases. Most patients start with an evaluation and imaging, then return for implant placement, and later come back once healing is complete for the final restoration. Extra visits may be needed if you also require extractions, a graft, or additional fit checks. Your exact schedule depends on your bone support and the type of restoration you choose.
  • Is it possible to place an implant on the same day as a tooth removal? Sometimes it is possible to place an implant immediately after an extraction, but it depends on stability, bone condition, and whether infection is present. In other cases, allowing the area to heal first can be the safer option. We review these factors during your exam and recommend the approach that best protects long-term success.
  • Do all patients need bone grafting before dental implants? No. Some patients have enough bone to support implants without any added procedures. If bone has thinned after tooth loss or gum disease, grafting may be suggested to create a stronger foundation. We evaluate bone volume and density and explain whether grafting would improve implant stability for your case.
  • What affects the overall price of dental implants? Total pricing varies because implant plans are not one-size-fits-all. Cost changes based on how many teeth are being replaced, which type of restoration is used, and whether steps like extractions, grafting, or sedation are included. After your consultation, we provide a clear estimate so you understand what is covered in your plan.
  • How is a single-tooth implant priced? Single-tooth treatment typically includes the implant post, the connector component, and the crown placed on top. Pricing can vary depending on your anatomy, the material used for the final crown, and whether additional support like grafting is recommended. We outline the full scope so you can compare options accurately.
