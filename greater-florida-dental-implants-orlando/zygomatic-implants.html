FAQ About Zygomatic Implants

  • What are zygomatic implants? Zygomatic implants are longer dental implants that anchor into the cheekbone instead of relying only on the upper jawbone. They may be recommended for patients with severe upper jaw bone loss who want a stable option for implant-supported teeth.
  • Who is a candidate for zygomatic dental implants? Candidates may include patients who are missing upper teeth, have significant bone loss, wear loose dentures, or have been told they do not have enough bone for traditional dental implants. A consultation and imaging are needed to determine if this option is appropriate.
  • Are zygomatic implants used for full-arch treatment? Yes. Zygomatic implants are often used as part of full-arch implant treatment for the upper jaw. They can help support a fixed restoration when standard implants may not have enough bone support.
  • Can zygomatic implants help avoid bone grafting? In some cases, zygomatic implants may reduce the need for extensive bone grafting because they use the cheekbone for support. Your dentist will review your bone structure and treatment goals to determine the best approach.
  • How are zygomatic implants different from traditional implants? Traditional implants are usually placed into the jawbone. Zygomatic implants are longer and angled so they can anchor into the cheekbone, which may provide stronger support when the upper jawbone has experienced significant bone loss.
  • How long do zygomatic implants last with proper care? With proper planning, daily hygiene, and regular professional maintenance, zygomatic implants are designed to provide long-term support. Your results can depend on your oral health, home care routine, and ongoing dental visits.
