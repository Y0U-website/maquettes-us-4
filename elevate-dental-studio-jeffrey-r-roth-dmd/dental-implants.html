Frequently Asked Questions

Dental implants are titanium posts that are surgically placed into the jawbone to replace missing tooth roots. Over time, the implant integrates with the bone, creating a strong foundation for a crown, bridge, or denture. This structure mimics the function of natural teeth, offering exceptional stability and long-term durability.

Most adults with good oral health and adequate jawbone density are candidates for dental implants. Even if you’ve experienced bone loss, options like bone grafting can often prepare the area for successful implant placement. A comprehensive exam and imaging will determine the best treatment plan for your needs.

With proper care, dental implants can last decades—and many last a lifetime. Their success depends on factors like oral hygiene, regular dental checkups, and overall health. Because implants fuse with the jawbone, they offer one of the most reliable and long-lasting tooth replacement solutions available.

Most patients report minimal discomfort during implant placement due to advanced local anesthesia and modern, minimally invasive techniques. Mild soreness afterward is normal and typically manageable with recommended pain relief. The majority of patients describe the experience as easier than anticipated.
