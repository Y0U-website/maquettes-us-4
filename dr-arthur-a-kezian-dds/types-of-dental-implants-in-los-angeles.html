Dental implants surgery is an outpatient multistep procedure that is completed over several appointments. Here are the steps involved.

At your initial consultation, the Los Angeles implant dentist will discuss your needs and what you want to achieve. He will examine the proposed site for the implant and check your jaw’s x-rays and CT scans to determine if you need bone grafting or sinus lifting to support the implants. Then he will come up with a treatment plan and explain the different options you have for the implant brands, materials, and sedation techniques. He will also give you a cost estimate and a timeline of the entire procedure.

Before the implant post can be inserted, the dentist has to extract the damaged tooth from its socket. In most cases, a simple extraction is performed under local anesthesia.

You may need bone grafting or sinus lifting to improve your jawbone and increase the chance of successful fusion.

For bone grafts, the dentist will take bone from another part of your body and graft it into the jawbone. Synthetic bone may also be used. After a healing period of 3-9 months, you’ll be ready for the next step of the implant surgery.

When the lost tooth is positioned at the back of the upper jaw, a sinus lift may need to be performed. That’s because the bone mass under the sinus cavity can deteriorate and there won’t be enough structure to support the new implant. So the dentist will lift the sinus floor and augment the jaw to install the implant.

After bone grafting and sinus lifting, your jaw is ready for the implant post. If you had enough bone density and didn’t need the extra preparation step, the dentist can place the implant post even after the tooth extraction. He will numb your mouth and make an incision in your gum to expose the underlying bone. He will drill the bone to make room and insert the implant post inside. Then he’ll stitch up the surgical area to allow the bone to heal.

Osseointegration is the process of healing that usually takes 2-9 months. During this time, tissue forms around the implant post and integrates it with your jawbone. Since the different types of dental implants in Los Angeles CA are biocompatible, your body shouldn’t reject them and the fusion process should go smoothly. This healing period might be shorter or longer depending on the type of implant you have received.

At this stage, the abutment is placed over the implant post. Now, this structure can securely hold a prosthetic tooth in place. So the dentist will take an impression and send it to the lab to have your crown custom made. You will need about two weeks to recover from the abutment placement. The crown fabrication can also take up to six weeks.

When the crown is ready, you can visit your dentist for the last step. He will affix the crown to the abutment with dental cement. Your new tooth will exactly match the color and shape of your other teeth so your new smile will appear as natural and beautiful as possible.

  • Titanium : medical-grade titanium is the most popular choice for dental implants. That’s because it’s a lightweight biocompatible material that has been used in knee replacement surgeries for a long time and fuses well with the bones.
  • Zirconia : for people with metal allergies who can’t tolerate titanium, zirconia is the next best choice. It’s a fairly new material but yields the same results.
  • Other : gold, cobalt-chromium, and stainless steel are other materials that are used less often than the two previous ones.
  • Endosteal implants : these are the most common types of implants. They’re placed directly into the jawbone in a 2-stage procedure. So they require good bone density to be performed successfully.
  • Subperiosteal implants : these are the ideal implants for people with inadequate bone density who don’t want to complete a bone graft. They’re placed beneath the gum, over a layer of tissue just above the jawbone.
  • Transosteal implants : these are the least common type of implants and the most complicated ones because they’re inserted through the mandibular bone.
  • Zygomatic implants : these are the other uncommon type of implants performed in people who don’t have enough bone density. Instead of the jawbone, they’re placed in the zygomatic bone, also known as the cheekbone.
  • Single tooth implants : these implants are made of one implant post, abutment, and crown and they can replace a single missing tooth anywhere in the mouth.
  • Multiple implants : multiple individual implants can be used to replace each of the missing teeth in different parts of your mouth.
  • Implant-supported bridges : instead of a traditional bridge, here an implant is used to support a dental bridge to replace multiple missing teeth next to each other.
  • Full-arch restorations : with full dental implants, the entire upper or lower jaw is restored by only a few implants inserted into each arch.
