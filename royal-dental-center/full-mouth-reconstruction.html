  • You have been told your case is too complex for a simple fix

How a Reconstruction Is Planned

  • 1 Full Records and Diagnosis We assess every tooth, your gums, your bone, and how your jaw actually closes. Nothing is treated until the whole picture is understood.
  • 2 The Sequenced Plan Your dentist lays out each stage in order, with the timeline and cost of each, so you can approve the plan as a whole and pace it if you need to.
  • 3 Building the Foundation Infection, gum disease, and teeth that cannot be saved are addressed first, along with any implants and grafting the plan calls for.
  • 4 Restoring the Bite Crowns, bridges, and implant teeth are fitted and the bite is balanced across both arches, so pressure is shared evenly when you chew.

The dividing line is whether the problems are independent of each other. Two worn teeth on the same side are two repairs. Teeth that have shortened across the whole arch, old work failing at several points at once, and a bite that has closed down over the years are one problem showing up in several places, and fixing them one at a time means each repair is built onto a bite that is still wrong. A full set of records answers this properly, which is why nothing is treated until they have been taken.
