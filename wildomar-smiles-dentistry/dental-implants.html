Implants, Bridges and Dentures Compared

Each of these replaces missing teeth in a different way, with different tradeoffs. Here is how they differ, so the conversation starts from the facts.

Signs You May Need Dental Implants

  • ✦ Missing Teeth — A gap or two from an extraction or injury can shift the nearby teeth, throw off your bite, and make eating harder. An implant closes the space without touching the teeth beside it.
  • ✦ Failing Teeth — Teeth badly damaged by decay, a fracture, or infection may be past saving, and replacing them with implants protects the teeth that remain.
  • ✦ Loose Dentures — Dentures that slide, click, or rub the gums can be steadied or replaced with implant-supported options that stay seated through the day.
  • ✦ Bone Loss — When teeth go missing the jawbone gradually shrinks. An implant loads that bone the way a natural root did, which helps slow the loss.

Common Types of Dental Implant Treatment

How many teeth are missing, and where, decides which of these suits you. Your dentist will explain which applies after examining your bone and gums.

Single-Tooth Replacement

One titanium post is placed in the bone and topped with a custom-made crown shaded to the teeth around it. Because it stands on its own, the teeth either side are left untouched, and the post keeps loading the bone where the root used to be.

Multi-Tooth Implant Bridges

Instead of a partial denture or a bridge carried by your own teeth, a fixed bridge can be built on a pair of implants. This suits a run of three or four missing teeth.

All-on-4 Implants

A way to replace a whole arch using four carefully placed implants to carry a full set of fixed teeth. It is not removable, so there is nothing to take out at night.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Sometimes called snap-on or clip-in dentures. Overdentures still come out for cleaning, but they clip onto two to four implants for support through the day, so no pastes or adhesives are needed.
