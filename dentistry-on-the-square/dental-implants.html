Single tooth, multiple teeth, or full arch

An implant can replace a single tooth, anchor a multi-tooth bridge, or stabilize a full-arch prosthesis. We&#x27;ll review the option that best fits your anatomy, goals, and timeline during your consultation.

Planning with 3D imaging

Cone-beam CT imaging lets us assess bone volume and anatomy in three dimensions, plan implant position digitally, and place restorations with a level of precision that wasn&#x27;t possible with film-based imaging.

What a dental implant actually is

An implant has three parts. A small titanium or zirconia post takes the place of the missing tooth root. An abutment connects that post to the visible restoration. The crown, bridge, or denture on top is what you see and chew with.

Because the post is anchored in bone rather than clasped to neighboring teeth, an implant does not require reshaping healthy teeth the way a traditional bridge does. That is the single biggest structural difference between the two options.

The timeline, start to finish

Most single-tooth cases follow a similar rhythm: consultation and imaging, placement, a healing period while the bone integrates around the post, then the final restoration. Healing time varies by site, bone quality, and whether grafting was needed.

If a tooth still needs to be removed, or if the site needs bone or sinus work first, those steps are planned and explained before anything begins. You will know the sequence and what each visit involves ahead of time.

  • Consultation, medical history review, and 3D imaging
  • Any preparatory treatment — extraction, grafting, or sinus work if indicated
  • Implant placement in the planned position
  • Healing and integration, with check-in appointments
  • Final crown, bridge, or prosthesis designed and seated

Caring for implants long term

At hygiene visits we check the tissue around each implant, the bite, and the restoration itself. Small adjustments early are far easier than repairs later.

What it costs and how people pay

Implant treatment is quoted per case, because the number of implants, any preparatory work, and the type of final restoration all change the total. You receive a written treatment plan with the fees before you commit to anything.

Although we are not in network with any dental plans, we will submit claims to your insurance electronically so you can maximize your out-of-network benefits. CareCredit is available for qualified applicants when you would rather spread payments out.

Implant, bridge, or denture

A general comparison. Which option suits you depends on your bone, bite, neighboring teeth, and goals.

Who this tends to suit

Often a good fit

  • One or more missing teeth, or a failing tooth that needs removal
  • Enough bone, or a willingness to have the site prepared first
  • A denture wearer who wants more stability while eating
  • Healthy gums and a consistent home-care routine

Worth discussing first

  • Active gum disease, which we treat before placing implants
  • Heavy clenching or grinding, which changes how we plan the bite
  • Uncontrolled diabetes or certain medications affecting healing
  • Tobacco use, which affects integration and long-term success
