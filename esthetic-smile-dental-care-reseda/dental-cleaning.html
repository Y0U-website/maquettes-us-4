5 Key Benefits of Routine Dental Cleanings

  • Prevention of Cavities and Tooth Decay : Professional dental cleanings remove plaque and tartar buildup that brushing and flossing alone cannot eliminate, reducing the risk of cavities and tooth decay.
  • Gum Disease Prevention : Regular teeth cleaning helps prevent and control gum disease by removing harmful bacteria and tartar below the gumline, which can otherwise lead to inflammation, infection, and tooth loss.
  • Early Detection of Oral Health Issues : Dental visits for dental cleaning include examinations that can identify early signs of cavities, gum disease, oral cancer, and other dental problems, allowing for timely intervention.
  • Improved Overall Health : Maintaining oral health through routine teeth cleaning is linked to lowering the risk of systemic conditions such as heart disease, stroke, respiratory infections, and potentially dementia by reducing oral inflammation and bacterial spread.
  • Enhanced Appearance and Fresh Breath : Teeth cleaning removes stains caused by food, drink, and tobacco, resulting in a brighter smile, and eliminates bacteria that cause bad breath, boosting confidence and social comfort.

FAQs About Teeth Cleaning

Does a Teeth Cleaning Hurt or Cause Discomfort?

No, teeth cleanings usually do not hurt, though some people may feel mild discomfort or sensitivity. This is often due to gum inflammation or tartar buildup and typically goes away soon after the cleaning. Good oral hygiene and regular visits help minimize discomfort.

How Frequently Should I Schedule a Dental Cleaning?

You should schedule a dental cleaning every 6 months to maintain oral health and catch issues early. If you have risk factors like gum disease or frequent cavities, Dr. Jacob Vayner may recommend more frequent visits. Always follow Dr. Jacob Vayner’s advice for the best schedule.

Can Regular Dental Cleanings Help Prevent Cavities and Tooth Decay?

Yes, regular dental cleanings prevent cavities by removing plaque and tartar that brushing alone can’t eliminate. They also help detect and manage early decay before it worsens.
