Types of Dental Crowns We Offer

When considering crowns, patients should consider materials, costs, and appointment styles. At Esthetic Smile Dental Care, we offer a full range of crown materials, helping you select the best option based on tooth location, bite forces, aesthetics, and budget.

Porcelain Dental Crowns Porcelain crowns are popular for their natural appearance.

They offer exceptional color matching, and porcelain reflects light similarly to natural enamel, helping the crown blend with surrounding teeth, making them ideal for front teeth and any area visible when you smile. All-ceramic options provide superior shade-matching but are slightly less fracture-resistant than metal-based alternatives, which is why Dr. Vayner carefully evaluates each case before recommending porcelain for your restoration.

Ceramic Dental Crowns Ceramic crowns provide metal-free construction, making them an excellent choice for patients with metal sensitivities or those who want the most biocompatible restoration possible.

These crowns deliver superior aesthetic results and natural light reflection, with sufficient strength and durability for moderate chewing forces. A research published in the National Library of Medicine shows that 5-year survival rates for all-ceramic crowns typically range from 90 % to 98 %, depending on the specific material used.

Zirconia Dental Crowns Zirconia is increasingly becoming a gold standard for both front and back teeth due to its durability.

Monolithic zirconia crowns deliver exceptional strength under heavy chewing forces, making them particularly well-suited for molars and back teeth. A 2025 retrospective cohort study published in the The Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry found that monolithic zirconia crowns achieved a 10-year survival rate of approximately 86 %, outperforming porcelain-fused zirconia restorations at roughly 71 % over the same period.

Implant-Supported Crowns When a missing tooth needs replacement, an implant-supported crown provides a permanent restoration.

The crown is custom-fabricated and attached to a dental implant abutment, restoring both the visible tooth and full chewing function without affecting surrounding teeth.

Broken Tooth Crown Restoration

A broken tooth demands prompt attention. Delaying treatment risks infection, additional fracture, and potential tooth loss. At Esthetic Smile Dental Care, we provide emergency evaluation for fractured teeth, assessing whether a crown can save the remaining tooth structure.

Dental crowns protect damaged teeth from further decay and restore full function. In most cases, a crown is the ideal solution for preserving a broken tooth long-term, eliminating the need for extraction and more costly replacement options like implants or bridges.

The Dental Crown Procedure at Esthetic Smile Dental Care

The dental crown procedure typically requires two visits to the dentist. Here’s what to expect at each stage:

Comprehensive Evaluation and Treatment Planning

Your first visit begins with a thorough examination. Dr. Vayner uses digital X-rays and advanced diagnostics to assess the tooth’s structure, pulp health, and surrounding tissues.

He develops a personalized treatment plan, discussing your crown options, timeline, and expected investment. Every question is answered before any preparation begins.

Precise Crown Preparation and Design

During the first visit, the tooth is reshaped and an impression is taken. Local anesthesia is used to numb the area during the procedure, ensuring complete comfort.

Digital impressions or traditional molds capture the exact dimensions of your prepared tooth, and shade matching ensures your new crown blends with your natural teeth. A temporary crown is placed until the permanent crown is ready, protecting the tooth while the dental lab fabricates your custom restoration.

Custom Crown Fabrication and Placement

The permanent crown is fitted and cemented during the second visit. Dr. Vayner checks the fit, bite alignment, and contact points with surrounding teeth, making precise adjustments for comfort and function.

You receive detailed aftercare instructions including guidance on regular brushing, flossing, and foods to avoid to maximize the lifespan of your crown. Follow-up scheduling ensures optimal long-term results.

Caring for Your Dental Crown

A new crown requires the same care as your natural teeth but a few extra precautions extend its lifespan significantly:

Most patients find that with consistent care, their crown performs like a natural tooth for well over a decade.

  • Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste
  • Floss carefully around the crown margins every day
  • Attend routine dental cleanings and exams every six months
  • Avoid chewing ice, hard candy, or using teeth as tools
  • Watch for signs your crown may need replacement: sensitivity, looseness, visible cracks, or changes in bite
