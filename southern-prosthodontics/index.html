After dental school, prosthodontists complete three additional years of specialized training focused on complex restorative and reconstructive care. This advanced education centers on dental implants, crowns, bridges, dentures and full-mouth rehabilitation. You benefit from a provider who has in-depth knowledge of function, aesthetics, materials and long-term treatment planning for complex cases.

When you face significant tooth loss, severe wear, trauma or congenital conditions, a prosthodontist is uniquely equipped to help. Their training emphasizes managing complicated restorative challenges. You receive solutions designed to rebuild your smile with precision, restoring proper bite alignment, facial support, and natural-looking results.

Prosthodontists look at your entire oral health picture, not just a single tooth. They evaluate how your bite, jaw joints, muscles and existing restorations work together. This comprehensive approach allows you to receive a coordinated plan that restores comfort, function and appearance while supporting long-term stability and durability.

Prosthodontists blend science and artistry to create restorations that feel comfortable and look natural. They carefully consider tooth shape, color, proportion and smile harmony. At the same time, they ensure your restorations function properly when you chew and speak, giving you confidence in both performance and appearance.
