Who Is a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most adults in good general health who are missing one or more teeth are potential candidates for dental implants. Dr. Ebrahimi will evaluate your oral health, jawbone density, and medical history during your consultation to determine whether implants are appropriate for you.

Patients who do not have adequate bone volume may still be candidates after a bone grafting procedure. Dr. Ebrahimi will discuss all available options during your consultation.

  • Have sufficient jawbone density to support an implant
  • Have healthy gums free of active periodontal disease
  • Do not smoke, or are willing to quit during the healing period
  • Are committed to maintaining good oral hygiene

Types of Dental Implant Treatment

Single-Tooth Implant

The most common implant procedure. A single titanium post and custom crown replace one missing tooth without affecting the teeth on either side. The result is virtually indistinguishable from a natural tooth.

Multiple Implants

When several teeth are missing, individual implants can be placed at each site, or implants can be used to support a fixed bridge — replacing multiple teeth without the need for a removable appliance.

Implant-Supported Bridge

Two implants anchor a bridge that replaces three or more consecutive missing teeth. This eliminates the need to crown healthy adjacent teeth, as a traditional bridge would require.

Implant-Supported Dentures

For patients missing all or most teeth in an arch, implant-supported dentures provide a stable, secure alternative to conventional removable dentures. The denture snaps onto implants, eliminating slippage and improving comfort while eating and speaking.

Bone Grafting and Implant Preparation

Successful implant placement requires adequate bone volume in the jaw. When a tooth has been missing for some time, or was lost due to infection or trauma, the surrounding bone may have deteriorated. In these cases, a bone graft is performed before or at the time of implant placement to rebuild the necessary bone structure.

Bone grafting is a routine procedure at Beverly Wilshire Dental Group. Dr. Ebrahimi will let you know during your consultation whether grafting is needed and how it will affect your overall treatment timeline.

The Dental Implant Process

Step 1 — Consultation and Planning

Dr. Ebrahimi will examine your teeth, gums, and jawbone — including X-rays or 3D imaging — and develop a personalized treatment plan. You will receive a clear breakdown of each stage of treatment and a realistic timeline before anything begins.

Step 2 — Implant Placement

The titanium implant post is placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia. Sedation options are available for patients who prefer a more relaxed experience. Most patients report that the procedure is more comfortable than they anticipated.

Step 3 — Osseointegration

Over the following three to six months, the implant fuses with the surrounding bone. During this healing period, a temporary restoration may be placed so you are not without a tooth.

Step 4 — Abutment and Crown Placement

Once the implant has fully integrated, a small connector piece called an abutment is attached, and your custom-fabricated porcelain crown is secured on top. The crown is matched to the color and shape of your surrounding teeth for a seamless result.

Healing Timeline and How Long Implants Last

Most patients return to normal activities within a few days of implant placement. Full osseointegration — the fusion of the implant with the jawbone — takes three to six months. The total treatment timeline from placement to final crown varies, but most patients complete the process within six to twelve months.

With proper care, dental implants can last 20 to 30 years or longer. The implant itself is designed to be permanent. The crown on top may eventually need replacement after 10 to 15 years, depending on wear. Regular dental visits and good oral hygiene are the most important factors in implant longevity.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

How much do dental implants cost in Beverly Hills?

Cost varies depending on the number of implants, the need for bone grafting, and the type of restoration. Contact our office for a personalized estimate after your consultation.

How long does the dental implant process take?

Most patients complete the full process — from implant placement to final crown — within six to twelve months. The timeline depends largely on healing and whether bone grafting is required.

The procedure is performed under local anesthesia, and sedation is available. Most patients report that the experience is more comfortable than they expected. Some soreness in the days following placement is normal and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief.

With proper care, the implant post can last a lifetime. The crown on top typically lasts 10 to 15 years before it may need replacement due to normal wear.

Can I get an implant if I’ve had bone loss?

In many cases, yes. A bone graft can rebuild the jawbone before implant placement. Dr. Ebrahimi will evaluate your bone volume and let you know whether grafting is needed.

Are dental implants better than dentures?

For most patients, implants offer significant advantages over dentures — they are fixed in place, preserve the jawbone, and function like natural teeth. Implant-supported dentures also exist for patients who need to replace a full arch but prefer a more stable option than conventional removable dentures.

Coverage varies by plan. Some insurance policies cover portions of the procedure, such as the crown or bone grafting. Our team can help you understand your benefits and explore financing options for any remaining balance.
