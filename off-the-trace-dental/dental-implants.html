Types of Dental Implant Treatments

Single-Tooth Dental Implants

Single-tooth dental implants completely replace one lost tooth from root to crown. It involves the placement of a titanium implant into the jawbone that serves as the foundation for a custom-made prosthetic single-tooth replacement. Most patients receive a dental crown as the visible portion of their implant treatment.

Single-tooth replacement with a dental implant provides a permanent solution for missing or damaged teeth. Additionally, single-tooth dental implants can look and feel like the patient’s own teeth, providing a natural appearance and function.

All-On-4

All-on-4 dental implants are full-arch replacement options. It involves placing four dental implants in the upper or lower jawbone and securing a full arch of prosthetic replacement teeth to them. The All-on-4 technique is less invasive and can be completed in one day. All-on-4 implants are a good option for those who have lower bone density, as they allow for the restoration of a full arch of teeth while using fewer implant posts.

Implant-Supported Bridges

Implant-supported bridges are excellent options for replacing several teeth. A traditional dental bridge or artificial tooth root is anchored to the jaw using dental implants. The bridge is typically made of two or more artificial teeth connected and attached to the implant abutments. The number of posts needed will vary depending on the size of the bridge.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Implant-supported dentures are removable appliances anchored to the mouth and jaw using dental implants. They provide a more secure fit and eliminate messy adhesives that removable dentures typically need. Implant-supported dentures are also a more stable option than traditional dentures and can improve overall oral health by providing stimulation to the jawbone.

Dental Implant Surgery Procedure

The dental implant procedure involves a multi-step process to replace missing teeth with artificial tooth roots and prosthetic crowns. Our Slidell dentist will perform a thorough examination of your oral health and create a personalized treatment plan. In some cases, patients need supplemental procedures before implant surgery, including bone grafting or a sinus lift . Dr. Daigle will go over this at your consultation.

During the surgical phase, a small titanium implant is surgically placed into the jawbone to serve as the foundation for the replacement tooth. Over several months, the implant fuses with the bone in a process called osseointegration. Once the implant has firmly integrated, an abutment is attached to it, and a custom-made crown is placed on top, restoring the appearance and function of a natural tooth. After the dental implant surgery, patients may need to eat soft foods while the surgical site heals.
