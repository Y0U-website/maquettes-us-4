Bone Graft Materials Used in Sinus Lifts

The success of a sinus augmentation depends on the bone graft material used. Common options include:

  • Autograft: Bone taken from your own body (usually hip or chin). This is highly effective but requires an additional surgical site.
  • Allograft: Donor bone from a human tissue bank, processed for safety.
  • Xenograft: Bone sourced from animals (usually bovine), treated for compatibility.
  • Synthetic Graft: Man-made materials like calcium phosphate or hydroxyapatite.

Types of Sinus Lift Procedures

Traditional Sinus Lift (Lateral Window Technique)

This is the most common method, involving:

  • An incision in the gum tissue to access the jawbone.
  • Creating a small window in the bone to gently lift the sinus membrane.
  • Placing bone graft material in the space created.
  • Closing the incision and allowing the graft to integrate with the bone.

Osteotome Sinus Lift (Internal Lift)

This less invasive technique is used when only a small amount of bone is needed:

  • Our dentist near you accesses the sinus through the implant site.
  • The sinus membrane is gently lifted using specialized tools (osteotomes).
  • Bone graft material is added, and the implant may be placed simultaneously.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why would I need a sinus lift before getting dental implants?

When the bone in your upper jaw is too thin, often from tooth loss or a naturally low sinus floor, there is not enough structure to anchor an implant. A sinus lift adds bone material so the implant has a solid foundation.

How long is the recovery period after a sinus lift?

Most patients return to normal activities within a few days, but the bone graft itself needs 4 to 6 months to fully integrate before Dr. Daigle can place the dental implant.

Is a sinus lift procedure painful?

The procedure is performed under local anesthesia, so you will not feel pain during treatment. Post-operative discomfort is typically manageable with over-the-counter pain medication and resolves within a week.

Can a sinus lift and implant be placed at the same time?

In some cases where only a small amount of bone height is needed, Dr. Daigle can perform the sinus lift and place the implant in a single appointment. This depends on your individual anatomy and bone density.

What happens if I do not get a sinus lift when I need one?
