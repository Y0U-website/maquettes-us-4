Fixed and Removable Full-Arch Restorations

Fixed Full-Arch Implant Teeth

A fixed full-arch restoration remains connected to the dental implants. It is not removed at home, although a dental professional may remove it when maintenance or repair is required.

Fixed teeth can provide a stable restoration for eligible patients. However, careful cleaning beneath the restoration and around every implant remains essential.

Removable Implant-Supported Dentures

A removable implant-supported denture attaches to implants through specialized connectors. The implants help stabilize the denture, but the patient removes it for daily cleaning. This option may be appropriate for patients who want greater denture stability while retaining the ability to remove and clean the restoration at home.

How Full-Arch Implant Treatment Is Planned

Examination and Three-Dimensional Imaging

The dentists examine the teeth, gums, bite, existing restorations, and areas of missing bone. Digital X-rays and three-dimensional scans may be used to evaluate bone dimensions, implant positions, and nearby anatomical structures. Medical history, medications, smoking, and health conditions that could affect healing are also reviewed.

Removing Unsavable Teeth

Remaining teeth are not removed automatically. The dentists evaluate whether any teeth can be predictably preserved before recommending extractions. Teeth with severe decay, fractures, advanced bone loss, or other significant problems may need to be removed as part of the treatment plan.

Bone Grafting and Preparatory Care

Some patients require bone grafting or treatment for gum disease before or during implant placement. Others may have enough healthy bone to proceed without grafting. The need for preparatory care can only be determined after an examination and diagnostic imaging.

Implant Placement and Healing

Dental implants are placed in planned areas of the jaw to support the proposed restoration. The implants generally need time to integrate with the surrounding bone before supporting the final teeth. The number of implants and the healing period vary according to the restoration design, bone quality, implant stability, medical factors, and treatment complexity.

Designing the Full-Arch Restoration

Full-Arch Implants and the All-on-4 Approach

All-on-4 is a specific full-arch treatment concept in which a complete arch of replacement teeth is supported by four implants. Full-arch treatment is the broader category and may involve a different number or arrangement of implants. Not every patient is a candidate for an All-on-4 approach. Implant quantity and placement depend on available bone, bite forces, restorative design, medical considerations, and clinical findings.

Digital Technology for Coordinated Full-Arch Care

Dental Implants & Cosmetics uses digital X-rays, three-dimensional scanning, digital impressions, implant-planning systems, and smile-design technology when clinically appropriate. These tools may help the dentists:

  • Explain the treatment plan to the patient
