Dental Implants

If you’re not sure whether you qualify, our team will evaluate your bone structure and overall health to determine the best course of treatment. In some cases, additional procedures such as bone grafting or sinus lifts may be recommended before implant placement. The American Academy of Implant Dentistry outlines who makes a good candidate and what types of implants may be recommended.

  • Healthy gums and no active periodontal disease
  • Adequate bone to support the implant, or willingness to undergo grafting
  • Good oral hygiene habits

The Consultation Process

Step-by-Step Planning for Long-Term Success

During your consultation, we’ll take digital X-rays and 3D scans to assess your bone density and identify the best placement for the implant. We’ll also discuss your goals, timeline, and budget so we can tailor a solution that fits you. You’ll leave the visit with a clear understanding of your options and next steps.

What to Expect During Recovery

Healing and Final Restoration

After your implant is placed, it typically takes 3–6 months for the bone to heal and fully integrate with the implant (a process called osseointegration). During this time, we may provide a temporary restoration to maintain function and aesthetics. Once healing is complete, we attach the final crown, bridge, or denture.

Post-procedure discomfort is usually mild and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief. Our team will provide detailed instructions to support a smooth and successful recovery.
