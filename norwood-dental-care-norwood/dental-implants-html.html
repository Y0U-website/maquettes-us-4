What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small titanium posts that are shaped like screws and designed to replace the roots of your missing teeth. They’re crafted from titanium because the metal is biocompatible, meaning that it’s able to naturally fuse with your jawbone to provide blood flow and stimulation to it as well as offering your restoration and unmoving foundation to be attached to.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Receiving dental implants requires that you complete a four-step process, one that includes an initial consultation, dental implant surgery, osseointegration and abutment placement, and the final placement of your custom restoration. Dr. Michaels and our team are here to ensure each phase is successful. If you want to learn more about what you can expect in preparation for your new smile or how dental implants work in Norwood, keep reading or call us with any questions.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

During your consultation, Dr. Michaels will review your diagnostic images and discuss your oral health history and goals. Based on your discussion with Dr. Michaels and our team, we can walk you through all your treatment options, including whether dental implants are the right choice for you.

What we see when examining your mouth will help us determine whether you need additional preliminary care to create a healthier environment for your future implants. You may need treatment for gum disease, tooth extraction, or bone grafting.

Dental Implant Surgery

After reviewing your treatment plan with you, the next step is to schedule your dental implant placement. In some cases, we complete this in-house; in others, we may refer you to a local specialist. No matter how or where the procedure is performed, you will receive local anesthesia to numb your mouth before each dental implant is positioned. This requires opening your gums and inserting the implant to ensure optimal placement. When finished, your gum tissues will be closed, and protective healing caps will be put on top.

Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment

After your dental implant placement, you’ll be able to return to work within a day or two. However, your dental implants will fuse with your jawbone over the next 3 to 6 months (osseointegration). During this stage of treatment, you will need to follow the detailed instructions we provide. This is to ensure that you do not develop any complications.

After osseointegration has occurred, you’ll return to our office so your implant dentist in Norwood can attach small metal connector pieces, called abutments, to the tops of your dental implants. These will help hold your custom restoration in place once it is attached to your implant.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

The final step includes receiving your final restoration. When our team receives your custom crown, bridge, or denture from the dental laboratory, and after your gums have healed around your abutments, we will call you back in to secure your replacement teeth to your implants and complete your smile. We’ll make sure your bite feels comfortable and that you are pleased with the results.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

In order to determine if you’re a good candidate for dental implants in Norwood, we will need to review your dental history, take X-rays, complete a visual exam, and learn about your day-to-day routine. With all of this information, we can determine if your jawbone is strong enough to support dental implants, if your gums and any remaining teeth you have are healthy, and if you’re committed to taking good care of your smile at home. If that’s the case, then the answer will likely be yes!

Missing One Tooth

If you’re missing one tooth, but the rest of your teeth are healthy, then we will recommend a single dental implant! In this case, a titanium implant is placed into the jawbone. Once the two have fused together, we will secure a dental crown in place with an abutment. Since the restoration is made entirely from porcelain, the final result will look as natural as it feels.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Are you missing up to four teeth in a row? If so, then you should consider an implant bridge! Instead of relying on neighboring teeth to anchor restorations like traditional bridges, implant-retained bridges use two implants on either side to hold the prosthetic tooth or teeth in place. As a result, more of your natural smile is preserved in the process!

Missing All Teeth

Oftentimes, patients who are missing all of their teeth assume that traditional dentures are their only option. That’s not the case! With implant dentures, we place four to six implants strategically throughout the mouth. Then, we secure your custom restoration on top. This effectively replaces an entire row of teeth while offering unmatched stability and increased longevity.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Finally, eating your favorite foods is rarely an issue with dental implants. Tough or crunchy foods like steak or apples are easy to chew, so you can create a diet that matches your lifestyle. Just make sure to practice daily oral care to prevent plaque buildup on your surrounding gums.

Understanding the Cost of Dental Implants

It’s important to note that the amount you pay for implants will vary depending on your needs. For example, those who need an entire arch replaced instead of just one tooth are guaranteed to pay far more. Additionally, those who need preparatory treatments like bone grafts or sinus lifts will also see a higher cost. With that said, dental implants are considered to be a long-term investment that lasts for many decades. Compared to other tooth replacement methods, this makes them the most cost-effective solution for many individuals.
