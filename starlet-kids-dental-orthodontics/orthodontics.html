What Is Orthodontics in Sylmar?

Orthodontics is a specialized branch of dentistry focused on diagnosing, preventing, and treating dental and facial irregularities. This includes correcting crooked teeth, closing gaps, and fixing bite issues such as overbites, underbites, and crossbites. Treatment involves using appliances that apply gentle, sustained pressure to move teeth into their ideal positions over time.

For children and teens, orthodontic care often begins with an evaluation to monitor jaw growth and tooth eruption. Early intervention can sometimes guide development and reduce the need for extensive treatment later. When active treatment is needed, we offer a variety of options designed to fit different lifestyles, clinical needs, and aesthetic preferences.

Why Your Child Might Need Orthodontics

Orthodontic treatment addresses a wide range of functional and aesthetic concerns that can affect a child’s daily life.

Correcting Bite Misalignments

When the upper and lower jaws do not meet correctly, it can cause difficulty chewing, speaking, and even breathing. A misaligned bite also places uneven stress on certain teeth, leading to premature wear and potential jaw pain. Orthodontic treatment corrects these functional issues, creating a healthy, balanced bite.

Straightening Crowded or Crooked Teeth

Teeth that are crowded or overlapping are notoriously difficult to clean properly. This increases the risk of plaque buildup, tooth decay, and gum disease in those hard-to-reach areas. Straightening the teeth makes daily brushing and flossing much more effective, supporting better long-term oral hygiene.

Closing Unwanted Gaps

Significant spaces between teeth can trap food particles and affect the appearance of the smile. Closing these gaps improves both the function and the aesthetics of the mouth. It also prevents neighboring teeth from shifting out of their proper positions over time.

Boosting Self-Confidence

The appearance of a child’s smile has a profound impact on their self-esteem, particularly during the teenage years. Correcting visible alignment issues often leads to a significant boost in confidence. A child who feels good about their smile is more likely to share it freely.

What Happens If Orthodontics Is Delayed or Avoided?

Ignoring orthodontic issues can lead to a cascade of dental problems as a child grows. Crowded teeth that cannot be cleaned properly are highly susceptible to cavities and early gum disease. Severe bite misalignments can cause chronic jaw pain, headaches, and difficulty chewing certain foods.

In some cases, untreated alignment issues can even affect speech development and facial symmetry. The longer these problems are left unaddressed, the more complex and costly the eventual treatment becomes. Early evaluation and timely intervention are the best ways to prevent these long-term complications.

Traditional Braces

Traditional metal braces remain one of the most effective and reliable methods for correcting complex alignment issues. They consist of small brackets attached to the teeth, connected by a wire that is periodically adjusted to guide tooth movement. Modern braces are smaller, more comfortable, and more efficient than those used in the past.

Lingual Braces

For patients seeking a more discreet option, lingual braces offer the power of traditional braces without the visible hardware. The brackets and wires are custom-made to fit the back surface of the teeth, facing the tongue. This makes them virtually invisible from the outside while still providing precise, effective alignment correction.

Invisalign Clear Aligners

Invisalign uses a series of custom-made, clear plastic aligners to gradually shift teeth into place. The aligners are removable, allowing patients to eat their favorite foods and brush their teeth without interference. This option is highly popular among teens who want a flexible, nearly invisible orthodontic treatment.

FAQs About Orthodontics in Sylmar, CA

At what age should my child first be evaluated for Orthodontics in Sylmar?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends an initial evaluation by age seven. While treatment may not begin immediately, early assessment allows us to monitor growth and intervene at the optimal time.

How long does treatment with Orthodontics in Sylmar typically take?

The duration of treatment varies widely depending on the complexity of the case. Most patients wear braces or aligners for 12 to 24 months, followed by a period of wearing a retainer.

Are treatments for Orthodontics in Sylmar painful?

The placement of braces or aligners is not painful. Patients typically experience some mild soreness or pressure for a few days after adjustments or when switching to a new aligner, which can be managed with over-the-counter pain relief.

Will my child need teeth extracted for Orthodontics in Sylmar?

In cases of severe crowding, tooth extraction may be necessary to create enough space for proper alignment. Early intervention can often guide jaw growth and eliminate the need for extractions.

How often will we need to visit the office for Orthodontics in Sylmar?

Patients with traditional or lingual braces typically visit every four to eight weeks for adjustments. Invisalign patients may have slightly longer intervals between visits, depending on their specific treatment plan.

Can my child play sports while undergoing treatment for Orthodontics in Sylmar?

Yes, children can continue to play sports. We highly recommend wearing a custom orthodontic mouthguard to protect both the teeth and the appliances during physical activity.

How do we choose the right option for Orthodontics in Sylmar?

Dr. Partovi will evaluate your child’s clinical needs and discuss your lifestyle preferences during the consultation. We will recommend the most effective and appropriate options for your specific situation.

Will my child need to wear a retainer after Orthodontics in Sylmar?

Yes, wearing a retainer is a crucial part of the process. It holds the teeth in their new positions while the surrounding bone and tissue stabilize, preventing the teeth from shifting back.
