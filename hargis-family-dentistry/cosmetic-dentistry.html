Am I a good candidate for cosmetic dentistry?

Most adults in good overall oral health are candidates for one or more forms of cosmetic dentistry treatment available in Burlington, NC. We always complete a full exam first to ensure any underlying issues — such as decay or gum disease — are addressed before beginning cosmetic work.

How long do cosmetic dental treatments take?

The timeline depends on the specific cosmetic dental treatment — dental bonding can be completed in a single visit, while veneers and orthodontic treatment typically require two or more appointments spread over several weeks. Dr. Hargis will give you a clear timeline at your initial consultation.

What is cosmetic dental bonding, and how does it work?

Cosmetic dental bonding involves applying a tooth-colored composite resin to repair chips, cracks, gaps, or minor discoloration — sculpted and polished to blend seamlessly with your natural teeth. It is one of the quickest and most affordable cosmetic treatments we offer, often completed in just one visit.

What is the difference between cosmetic veneers and dental bonding?

Porcelain veneers are custom-crafted shells permanently bonded to the front of your teeth for a long-lasting, highly natural result, while dental bonding uses composite resin applied directly in the chair. Our team will help you decide which option suits your smile goals — patients seeking cosmetic dentistry veneers near Burlington, NC, will find both solutions available here.

Do you offer orthodontic options for cosmetic smile improvement?

Yes — for patients looking for a cosmetic orthodontist in Burlington, NC, we offer Invisalign clear aligner therapy to straighten teeth discreetly without traditional metal braces. Straighter teeth are often the foundation of a complete smile makeover.

Does cosmetic dental care affect the health of my teeth?

When performed correctly, quality cosmetic dental care is designed to enhance both the appearance and function of your smile without compromising tooth health. At Hargis Family Dentistry, we always take a conservative approach — removing as little natural tooth structure as possible.
