Am I a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Many adults with missing teeth can be considered for dental implants. The most important factors include healthy gums, adequate bone to support the implant, and good overall oral health.

If another dental problem is present, it may need to be treated before implant placement.

  • Can I Get Dental Implants if I Have Gum Disease? Active gum disease should be treated before dental implant surgery. Healthy gums and supporting bone are important for long-term implant stability.
  • What if I Have Been Missing Teeth for Years? You may still be a candidate for dental implants. Because bone can change after tooth loss, we may need to evaluate whether additional treatment is needed before implant placement.
  • Is There an Age Limit for Dental Implants? There is no single upper age limit for dental implants. Candidacy depends more on oral health, bone support, medical history, and the ability to heal properly.
  • Can I Get Dental Implants With Crooked or Crowded Teeth? Possibly. The available space, bite, and position of the surrounding teeth must be evaluated so the implant and final restoration can be placed correctly.
  • What if I Do Not Have Enough Jawbone? Some patients may need bone grafting or another treatment before implant placement. Your examination and imaging will help determine the appropriate approach.
  • Can I Get Dental Implants if I Have Cavities? Cavities and other active dental problems are generally treated before implant surgery so treatment begins with a healthier mouth.

What to Expect During Dental Implant Surgery

Dental implant treatment begins with careful planning. During implant placement, the area is numbed and the implant is positioned in the jawbone. The exact length of the appointment depends on how many implants are being placed and the complexity of your treatment. After placement, the implant is allowed to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone before the final restoration is completed.

Dental Implant Recovery & Care

Some swelling, tenderness, or sensitivity can occur after dental implant surgery. Follow the post-operative instructions provided by the dental team, keep the area clean as directed, and choose softer foods while the area is healing. Recovery varies by patient and by the type of procedure performed.

Dental IMPLANT Frequently asked questions

  • How Long Do Dental Implants Last? Dental implants are designed as a long-term tooth replacement option. Their longevity depends on oral hygiene, gum and bone health, regular dental care, and individual health factors.
  • Does Getting a Dental Implant Hurt? The area is numbed during implant placement. Some soreness or swelling can occur afterward, and your dental team will provide instructions for managing recovery.
  • How Long Is Dental Implant Recovery? Initial soreness often improves within the first several days, but the implant itself generally needs additional time to heal and integrate with the bone before the final restoration is completed.
  • What Can I Eat After Dental Implant Surgery? Soft foods that require minimal chewing are usually easier during the first part of recovery. Follow the specific diet instructions provided for your procedure.
  • Can Dental Implants Replace Dentures? Dental implants can be used to support full-arch tooth replacement for some patients, providing an option that is more stable than traditional removable dentures.
  • How Long Does the Dental Implant Process Take? Treatment time depends on the number of implants, your oral health, healing, and whether additional procedures are needed. We can provide a more specific timeline after your examination.
