Frequently Asked Questions

  • How long does a single tooth implant last? A single tooth implant is designed to be a long-term, often lifelong solution. The titanium implant fuses with your jawbone, creating a strong and stable foundation that can last decades with proper care. While the crown may eventually need replacement due to normal wear, the implant itself is built to be permanent. Regular dental check-ups, good brushing and flossing habits, and routine cleanings help maintain the health and longevity of your implant.
  • Is getting a dental implant painful? Most patients are pleasantly surprised by how comfortable the implant process is. During the procedure, your mouth is fully numbed, and many patients report feeling only gentle pressure, not pain. After placement, mild soreness or swelling is normal for a few days, similar to having a tooth extracted. Our team provides detailed aftercare instructions, and over-the-counter medication is usually all that’s needed for comfort. We also offer options to help anxious patients feel more relaxed throughout treatment.
  • How long does the implant process take from start to finish? The full implant process typically takes several months, depending on your individual healing timeline. First, the implant post is placed into the jawbone. Over the next 3 4 months, your bone naturally integrates with the implant during a process called osseointegration. Once healed, a custom-made crown is attached to complete your new tooth. Although it requires time, this step-by-step approach ensures the implant is strong, secure, and built to last.
  • How natural will my implant look and feel? Your implant crown is custom-designed to match the color, shape, and size of your surrounding teeth, blending seamlessly with your smile. Because the implant replaces the entire tooth structure, including the root, it feels stable and strong, just like a natural tooth. Patients often forget which tooth is the implant because the result looks so realistic and functions just like the real thing.
  • Am I a candidate for a single tooth implant? Most adults with a missing or failing tooth are good candidates for a single tooth implant. Ideal candidates have healthy gums, a fully developed jaw, and enough bone support in the area of the missing tooth. Even if bone loss has occurred, additional procedures, like bone grafting, can help prepare the site for a successful implant. At your free consultation, we use 3D imaging to evaluate your jawbone and determine the best treatment plan for your specific needs.
