Contact Us What are Dental Implants? Dental implants are titanium posts placed surgically into the jawbone beneath the gum line. The implant acts as an anchor for a replacement tooth. A connector called an abutment is placed on the implant, which holds the replacement tooth or a crown. Regarded as a safe and effective long-term solution for those who need to replace missing teeth, dental implants also preserve the jawbone and facial structure.

Who is a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Dental implants are an effective solution for individuals who have lost one or more teeth due to injury, decay, or disease. Ideal candidates for dental implants typically have good overall oral health, with sufficient jawbone to secure the implants and healthy gum tissue free of periodontal disease. It’s also essential for the candidate to be committed to good oral hygiene and regular dental visits. However, each person’s circumstances are unique, and a consultation with our dental professionals is always recommended before making any decisions.

Dental Implant Procedure

The dental implant procedure typically involves multiple visits to the dentist.

The first visit involves thoroughly examining your teeth, gums, jawbone, and medical history to ensure you’re a good implant candidate.

The next visit will require the surgical placement of the implant within the jawbone. In three to six months, the implant fuses to the bone in Osseointegration. This timeframe offers a suitable healing period before the final placement of the abutment.

The final visit involves the placement of the custom-made crown, which is aesthetically pleasing and provides a durable and sturdy tooth replacement.

Recovery and Maintenance of Dental Implants

Recovering from dental implant surgery depends on several factors, such as the number of implants, the complexity of the treatment, the patient’s age, and lifestyle habits. However, most people can manage their post-surgery pain with over-the-counter pain medication, while others need antibiotics to avoid infections. Maintaining dental implants is easy and involves regular oral care, including:

Brushing

Brush your teeth twice daily, removing the bacteria and particles of food that stay on your teeth and gums throughout the day.

Flossing

Floss your teeth at least once daily to remove the stubborn bacteria that winds up between your teeth, somewhere your toothbrush cannot reach.

Check-Ups

Be sure you visit our office every six months to ensure your smile is healthy and, your dental implant or implants are secured and are not suffering from any damage.
