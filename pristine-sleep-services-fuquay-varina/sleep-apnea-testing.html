Traditional Sleep Apnea Testing vs. At-Home Sleep Tests

In the past, diagnosing sleep apnea required spending the night in a sleep lab.Under close observation, you’d be studied by physicians as you slept. This can be intimidating for many patients—wearing multiple sensors and being watched can make sleep feel less natural, causing your results to be less accurate.

While the clinical setting is effective for getting highly detailed data, it can be difficult to receive the most accurate information and find the right diagnosis and treatment method.

How to Prepare for Your Test

Preparing for an at-home sleep apnea test is simple, but a few steps can ensure the best results:

Avoid caffeine or alcohol in the evening because both can interfere with your sleep and potentially affect your results.

Follow your usual bedtime routine to help your body relax and transition to sleep naturally.

Create a calm, comfortable sleep environment by minimizing potential disturbances, such as pets or noise.

Your care team will provide clear instructions and demonstrate how to use the test kit so you feel confident and ready.
