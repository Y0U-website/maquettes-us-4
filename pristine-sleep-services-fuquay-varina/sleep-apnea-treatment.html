Why Treat Sleep Apnea?

It’s a serious condition that impacts your health, energy levels, and quality of life.

Sleep apnea can lead to both short-term and long-term health risks if left untreated. Short-Term Health Effects There are many effects you’ll notice day-to-day when dealing with sleep apnea.

Sleep apnea interrupts your natural sleep cycle, leaving you exhausted and unable to concentrate. This can impact your work, studies, or daily responsibilities.

You’ll feel groggy even when trying to do activities you love, which can be upsetting and frustrating. That feeling can get worse over time. Sleep deprivation from apnea can lead to mood swings, irritability, and even anxiety or depression, affecting your personal and professional relationships.

You will also feel uncomfortable because of a lack of sleep.

Symptoms like morning headaches or a dry mouth often result from reduced oxygen levels and mouth breathing during sleep.

Long-Term Health Effects

The sleep disorder increases your risk of heart disease, stroke, and high blood pressure by placing excess stress on your cardiovascular system.

Disrupted sleep can also impair your body’s ability to regulate blood sugar, raising the risk of type 2 diabetes. The effects aren’t just physical, but mental too—sleep apnea can impact memory, focus, and cognitive function over time, potentially contributing to dementia or Alzheimer’s disease.

Treating sleep apnea helps protect your health and ensures better rest, reducing the risk of these complications. Schedule Now! Benefits of Treating Sleep Apnea By addressing sleep apnea, you can:

Stabilize your mood and improve relationships.

Reduce the risks of heart disease, diabetes, and other serious conditions.

CPAP Therapy

Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) machines are a widely trusted treatment for sleep apnea.

A CPAP device delivers a steady airflow through a mask, keeping your airway open throughout the night.

This prevents your airways from collapsing, which causes the interruptions sleep apnea is known for.

CPAP is most often used to treat severe sleep apnea because it is a tried and true way to treat the disease with decades of positive results.

Oral Appliance Therapy

Oral appliances offer a comfortable, non-invasive alternative to CPAP therapy.

These custom-fitted devices gently reposition your jaw or tongue to prevent airway blockages.

They are ideal for mild to moderate sleep apnea and provide:

Ease of Use

Portability

Comfort

Designed for a precise, personalized fit.

Oral appliances are typically used to treat mild to moderate sleep apnea and are effective when treating severe cases.

They are also helpful in preventing other issues, such as snoring and TMJ.
