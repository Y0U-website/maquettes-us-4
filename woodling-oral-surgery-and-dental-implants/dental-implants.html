Implant Anatomy A dental implant is a small, screw-shaped device made of biocompatible materials, such as titanium, ceramic or zirconia, that is placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. It consists of three main parts: the implant itself, an abutment, and a dental crown.

Dental implants are considered to be a highly successful and long-lasting option for replacing missing teeth, providing a durable and natural-looking solution that can help to improve oral health and quality of life.

Implant Abutment Crown Implant The implant is the portion that is surgically placed into the jawbone and serves as the replacement root for the missing tooth. Over time, the bone fuses to the implant in a process called osseointegration, creating a stable foundation for the replacement tooth.

Crown The dental crown is the visible part of the implant and is custom-made to match the size, shape, and color of the patient s natural teeth. It is attached to the abutment and serves as the replacement tooth, restoring both the function and appearance of the missing tooth.

Abutment The abutment is a connector that is attached to the implant and protrudes through the gums. It acts as a support for the dental crown.

Get Natural-Looking Replacement Teeth Restorative dentists custom-make the crown to match the size, shape, and color of your other teeth. The results are so natural-looking that it can be difficult for others to tell which tooth is the implant.

Preserve Jaw Bone When a tooth is missing, the jaw bone underneath can resorb over time. The implant post fuses to the jaw, providing the necessary stimulation during chewing to keep it healthy. Dental implants are the only tooth replacement option that offers this benefit.

Simple Care Routine Dental implants do not have any moving parts, and special rinses are not necessary. Simply practice good oral hygiene by brushing and flossing daily, just like with your other teeth. Regular dental checkups are also essential for maintaining your dental implant.

Implant Surgery Process

Getting dental implants typically requires two or three office visits, depending on the number and type of implants you need. During the first visit, your oral surgeon will review your health condition, take 3D scans, and discuss treatment and anesthesia options with you.

The actual dental implant surgery usually lasts between 1 to 3 hours. After administering the anesthesia, your oral surgeon will lift the gum tissue and place the implant(s) into the bone beneath. The post(s) integrate with the jaw bone while you heal, creating a firm and secure foundation for your replacement teeth.

If your implants are immediate loading, your crowns will be attached using the abutments the same day or a day after your initial surgery. With traditional implants, we may provide temporary teeth while the implant sites heal. In either case, you will leave with a beautiful new smile.
