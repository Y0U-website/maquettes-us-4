What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that’s surgically inserted beneath the surface of your gums, completely restoring the roots of your missing teeth. Because the metal is biocompatible, each implant is able to naturally fuse with the jawbone to provide the area with unmatched support and stability. Once you’re all healed up, we’ll be able to secure custom-made restorations to your dental implants, completing your smile!

Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

Dental implants require a surgical procedure, and how well you care for yourself in the days and weeks that follow will impact your results. This is why it’s so important to stick closely to your dental implant post-op instructions in Queen Creek. These guidelines help protect the implant site, support healing, and give your new tooth roots the best possible chance of integrating successfully. Our team at Queen Creek Complete Dental will review these instructions with you before you leave, but here s what to keep in mind.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

For the first few hours after your procedure, resting and protecting the surgical site are your most important jobs. To do this:

  • Keep your head elevated to reduce swelling and encourage circulation.
  • Bite gently on the gauze placed over the surgical site to control any bleeding, replacing it as directed.
  • Avoid touching the area with your fingers or tongue, and don t rinse your mouth with any force.
  • Apply an ice pack to the outside of your cheek in 20-minute intervals to help manage swelling during those first critical hours.
  • Don’t smoke, vape, or use straws, as these activities could dislodge the forming blood clot and disrupt healing.

Common Side-Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

It’s perfectly normal for there to be some discomfort, swelling, or even mild bruising after implant surgery. You may also notice some bleeding and soreness at the implant site over the first few days.

These side effects typically peak around day two or three before gradually improving. If these symptoms become distracting, over-the-counter pain relievers can help you manage. However, if things don’t improve or if they get worse let us know.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

For the first several days, stick to soft foods that require little to no chewing. Yogurt, mashed potatoes, eggs, smoothies, and warm soup are all tasty and nutritious options that will be easy to eat.

At the same time, avoid hard, crunchy, sticky, or chewy foods that could put pressure on the implant site or dislodge a blood clot. As you feel better, you can gradually reintroduce firmer foods as your comfort level allows.

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

A regular oral care routine is especially important during your recovery, but it needs to be handled carefully. Rinse gently with warm, salty water starting the day after surgery (but never swish aggressively). Brush your remaining teeth and floss as usual just be careful to avoid the implant area until it is cleared by our team.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Once your final restoration is in place, caring for your implants is very simple. Brush twice daily, floss carefully around the implant posts, and keep up with your regular checkups at Queen Creek Complete Dental. A water flosser is a great tool to add to your home oral care routine, as it can easily reach under your implant restoration to clean debris.

If you grind your teeth at night, we may also recommend a nightguard to protect your new teeth from unnecessary wear. Also, be sure to explore our full range of dental services to keep every part of your smile healthy for the long haul.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

The dental implant procedure has an incredibly high success rate of over 90 %, but that doesn’t mean your new teeth are invincible. Your prosthetics can potentially last for decades, but that’s only as long as you take the proper precautions to care for them correctly.

Continue reading to learn how you can help protect your restorations so you can keep making the most of their many unique advantages!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Your restorations aren’t made from the same organic materials as your teeth and gums, so you don’t need to worry that they’ll develop disease or decay themselves. With that said, it’s essential to keep your mouth clean to avoid common oral issues, such as cavities or periodontal disease, that can erode the tissues that hold them in place, causing them to fail.

To prevent this, you should brush and floss twice daily to remove plaque and bacteria, but be careful not to press so deeply into the pocket of your implant that you damage its protective seal.

Eat a Healthy Diet

This is the only treatment that has the potential to restore up to 80 % or more of your original bite force, so there are fewer dietary restrictions for dental implants than alternative solutions for tooth loss, like dentures or dental bridges.

That means you can eat a wider variety of wholesome foods to support your oral and overall health. For example, dairy products like milk, cheese, and yogurt have calcium for strong teeth and bones, and many citrus fruits and leafy greens are high in vitamin C to support your connective tissues.

Break Bad Habits

Did you know smokers are significantly more likely to experience implant failure? Tobacco products contain nicotine, which reduces your blood oxygen levels and flow while simultaneously suppressing your immune system. That means your body can’t deliver essential nutrients or infection-fighting cells to your mouth during recovery, increasing the risk of complications.

As a result, we might recommend quitting several weeks before your implant procedure and waiting until you’re fully recovered before resuming your habit, though quitting permanently is ideal.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Athletes and others who participate in team sports or other close-contact activities are prone to sustaining injuries that can chip, crack, break, or dislodge their teeth or restorations, and dental implants are no exception. You might also hurt them if you subconsciously grind your teeth at night with a condition known as bruxism.

Our team can provide a custom-fitted oral appliance, such as a nightguard or mouthguard, to protect your grin.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Seeing our team every 6 months for a routine dental examination and professional cleaning allows us to monitor your oral health and treat any areas of concern before they progress. We’ll also be able to track your recovery process after your implant procedure to ensure you’re healing as intended.

Dental Implant FAQs

Do Dental Implants Decay?

Dental implants are crafted from materials like titanium and porcelain, so they cannot decay. With that said, it’s still extremely important that you implement a solid oral hygiene regimen from flossing daily to getting a dental checkup and cleaning twice a year. If you don’t, then your natural teeth can decay, your gums can become infected, and your dental implant can loosen. In short, taking care of your smile benefits your oral health and overall health!
