FAQs

What is oral hygiene?

Oral hygiene refers to the practice of keeping your mouth, teeth, and gums clean and healthy through daily care routines and professional dental treatments. It includes brushing your teeth at least twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, flossing between your teeth, using mouthwash, and maintaining regular dental checkups. Good oral hygiene prevents plaque buildup, tooth decay, gum disease, and bad breath while supporting your overall health and wellbeing.

What can poor oral hygiene result in?

Poor oral hygiene can lead to serious dental problems including cavities, gum disease, tooth loss, and persistent bad breath that affects your confidence and social interactions. Advanced gum disease can cause painful infections, bone loss around your teeth, and may require expensive surgical treatments to correct. Additionally, poor oral health has been linked to serious systemic health conditions such as heart disease, diabetes complications, stroke, and respiratory infections.

How to maintain perfect oral hygiene?

Perfect oral hygiene requires brushing your teeth twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste, flossing at least once per day to remove plaque between teeth, and using an antimicrobial mouthwash. You should also maintain regular dental checkups every six months for professional cleanings and examinations, eat a balanced diet low in sugary foods, and avoid tobacco products. Following proper brushing and flossing techniques as demonstrated by your dental hygienist ensures maximum effectiveness of your daily routine.

How oral hygiene affects overall health?

Your oral health directly impacts your overall health because bacteria from your mouth can enter your bloodstream and contribute to serious medical conditions throughout your body. Poor oral hygiene increases your risk of heart disease, stroke, diabetes complications, respiratory infections, and pregnancy complications in expecting mothers. Maintaining excellent oral hygiene through regular professional care and effective home routines helps reduce harmful bacteria levels and supports your body’s overall health and immune system function.

Are tonsil stones caused by poor oral hygiene?

Tonsil stones can be influenced by poor oral hygiene, as bacteria and food particles that accumulate in your mouth can contribute to their formation in the crevices of your tonsils. While tonsil stones can occur even with good oral hygiene due to tonsil structure and other factors, maintaining excellent oral care helps reduce the bacteria and debris that contribute to their development. Regular brushing, flossing, tongue cleaning, and professional dental cleanings can help minimize the conditions that lead to tonsil stone formation.
