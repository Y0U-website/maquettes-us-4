Types of Dental Implants

Dental implants are a durable, natural-looking solution for replacing missing teeth. They act as artificial tooth roots, surgically placed into or supported by your jawbone, to anchor replacement teeth, bridges, or dentures. The type of implant that’s right for you depends on your oral health, jawbone density, and the number of teeth missing you need to be replaced.

Endosteal Implants

Endosteal implants are the most common type of dental implant and are surgically inserted directly into the jawbone. They resemble small screws, cylinders, or blades and serve as a foundation for crowns, bridges, or full-arch dentures.

Endosteal implants are ideal for patients with sufficient jawbone density and can support single-tooth replacements, multiple adjacent teeth, or entire arches. For patients who do not have enough natural bone, bone grafting can be performed to create a stable foundation.

Subperiosteal Implants

Subperiosteal implants are placed on top of the jawbone but beneath the gum tissue, using a metal framework that protrudes through the gums to support replacement teeth.

They are not anchored into the bone, making them suitable for patients who have insufficient jawbone or prefer not to undergo bone grafting procedures.

Subperiosteal implants are primarily used to stabilize dentures in patients with minimal bone structure, providing a reliable alternative when traditional implants are not feasible. While less common, they offer a practical solution for restoring function and appearance without invasive bone procedures.

Zygomatic Implants

Zygomatic implants are designed for patients with significant bone and gum loss, particularly in the upper jaw, and are anchored into the cheekbone (zygomatic bone) rather than the jawbone.

These implants allow for full-arch restorations without the need for extensive bone grafting, making them an option for patients ineligible for traditional implants. Zygomatic implants provide strong, stable support for dentures or bridges in complex cases, offering a long-term solution for patients with severe bone loss.

Single-Tooth and Multiple-Tooth Implants

Single-tooth implants are designed to replace one missing tooth by placing an implant into the jawbone, followed by an abutment and crown. For patients missing several consecutive teeth, an implant-supported bridge can replace multiple teeth using fewer implants, reducing the need for individual implants for each tooth.

Full-mouth or full-arch implants are used to replace all teeth in the upper or lower jaw, often anchoring a denture-like prosthesis to restore function and aesthetics across the entire arch.

All-on-4 and All-on-6 Implants

All-on-4 and All-on-6 implants are designed for patients missing most or all teeth in an arch. The All-on-4 system uses four strategically placed implants to support a full arch of replacement teeth, while the

The All-on-6 system uses six implants for additional stability. These procedures reduce the number of implants required, shorten recovery time, and provide a permanent, fully functional solution for full-arch restoration.

Mini Dental Implants

Mini dental implants (MDIs) are smaller than traditional implants, roughly the size of a toothpick or pencil lead, and are often used to stabilize lower dentures. They are ideal for patients with significant bone loss or limited jawbone availability.

Mini implants provide a less invasive alternative, with quicker recovery times, while preventing dentures from slipping during eating or speaking.

Immediate-Load Implants

Immediate-load implants, also known as “Teeth in a Day,” allow patients to receive a temporary crown on the same day the implant is placed. This approach is suitable for patients with sufficient bone density to support immediate loading, providing a functional and aesthetic temporary solution while the implant fully integrates with the jawbone. Once healing is complete, permanent crowns or prostheses are attached, offering a long-term restoration.
