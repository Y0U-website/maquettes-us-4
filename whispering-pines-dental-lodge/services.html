Periodontal Treatments & Therapy

The goal of periodontal treatments is to manage and treat gum disease by removing bacteria and plaque build-up from your teeth and below your gum line to help promote healthy gum tissue and prevent further bone loss.

There are a range of procedures, but we begin with a deep clean of your teeth and gums, starting with non-surgical methods such as scaling and root planning. Surgical options may require a referral to a periodontist.

Crowns & Bridges, Inlays, Onlays, & Tooth Colored Composite Fillings

A crown is a custom-made cap that fits over a damaged tooth. This restores the shape, size, strength and appearance of a tooth that is either discolored, has a large filling or has had a root canal.

A bridge is a fixed appliance that replaces one or more missing teeth. It is anchored to adjacent natural teeth or dental implants, and can replace one or several missing teeth.

Whether you need a crown or a bridge, we do not use any metals that include mercury or could cause an allergic reaction or unsightly black lines at the gum line.

Our crowns and veneers are handmade by artisans providing you with excellent quality and a beautiful esthetic. We do not farm anything out of the country as some less expensive providers do.

Dental Implants

There are several aspects to getting a dental implant. The damaged tooth must first be removed and heal. Depending on the tooth, it may require an oral surgeon. Next, the oral surgeon places the implant, and this procedure requires time to heal. Lastly, a permanent abutment and tooth are placed upon the implant. This last phase is known as the restoration process.

Dr. Lee provides all services related to implant restorations.

Different types of Dental “Cleanings”

Prophylaxis (Prophy) (D1110)

Prophylaxis is a medical term that refers to preventative measures taken to prevent or avoid disease or illness. In dentistry, a prophylaxis is designed for people with a healthy mouth and it specifically refers to a routine dental cleaning that aims to maintain oral health and to prevent dental problems.

During a dental prophylaxis, a hygienist (or dentist) will examine your gums and remove plaque and tartar above the gum line and polish your teeth.

Historically, his type of “cleaning” has been covered by insurance 100 % but this is changing. More and more policies require a co-pay, deductible, etc. Each insurance policy is different.

Also, additional services/products such as fluoride varnish are usually not covered for adults.

Scaling in the presence of gingival inflammation (D4346)

This procedure is used when a patient exhibits swollen, red and inflamed gums and moderate to severe bleeding. This condition is not healthy but it has not yet progressed to Periodontal disease. This procedure is aimed to restore healthy gums and to eliminate bleeding.

A hygienist (or dentist) will examine your gums and remove plaque and tartar above and slightly below the gum line and polish your teeth. As an adjunct to facilitate healing, a specialized product is applied to the teeth which will help eliminate sensitivity and gum inflammation. Oral hygiene instructions are given.

This type of cleaning may or may not be covered by insurance since it is considered “treatment” of a specific condition. It is NOT considered a routine dental cleaning.

Additional services/products are usually not covered by insurance.

Grossdebridement (D4355)

A Grossdebridement is needed when there is excessive plaque and tartar, often due to irregular dental visits. The tartar build up may or may not be accompanied by bleeding gums, persistent bad breath, tender or swollen gums, or signs of gum disease (Periodontitis).

The purpose is to eliminate plaque and tartar that interferes with a comprehensive oral examination and to reduce inflammation and gum disease.

A Grossdebridement is NOT considered a routine dental cleaning. It is a more intensive procedure that prepares the teeth for a subsequent regular cleaning or periodontal treatment. It is always followed by a subsequent cleaning. A hygienist (or dentist) often uses specialized instruments like ultrasonic tools and hand instruments to remove heavy build up above and below the gum line. Additional products are used to make the procedure more comfortable for the patient and to heal inflamed gums.

A Grossdebridement is considered “treatment” and may only be partially covered by insurance or not at all. Additional products/services are usually not covered by insurance.

Scaling and Root Planing (SRP) (D4341 or 4342)

Scaling and Root Planing is often referred to as “Deep Cleaning”. This dental procedure is used to treat periodontal disease. It involves removing plaque and tartar from above and below the gum line and smoothing the tooth roots to prevent further gum recession and infection.

This procedure is very precise and is carried out by Quadrant and often requires more than one visit. A hygienist (or dentist) utilizes specialized instruments like ultrasonic tools and hand instruments.

SRP is considered a first line treatment for periodontal disease by creating a clean environment, allowing the gums to heal and reattach to the teeth which will subsequently shrink periodontal pockets.

Local Anesthesia is typically used to numb the area during the procedure. Some discomfort or sensitivity may be experienced after the procedure.

SRP is considered periodontal treatment and may only be partially covered by insurance. Additional products/services such as local antibiotics may or may not be covered by insurance.

Periodontal Maintenance (D4910)

Periodontal maintenance is a specialized dental cleaning for individuals who have previously been diagnosed with and treated for periodontal disease (gum disease). It’s a step beyond routine cleanings and focuses on preventing the recurrence of gum disease by removing plaque and tartar buildup below the gum line, monitoring gum health, and providing oral hygiene instructions.

HERE’S A MORE DETAILED BREAKDOWN:

  • Periodontal disease is NOT curable, but manageable: While scaling and root planing (a deep cleaning) can treat gum disease, it’s not a permanent fix.
  • Bacteria can quickly return: After treatment, bacteria can quickly repopulate and potentially lead to a recurrence of the disease.
  • Regular maintenance prevents recurrence: Periodontal maintenance helps to control the bacteria and prevent the progression of gum disease.
  • Possible use of antimicrobial medications: In some cases, medications like Arestin may be applied to the affected areas to help control bacteria and to prevent further periodontal breakdown according to Whispering Pines Dental Lodge.
  • More frequent than routine cleanings: Periodontal maintenance is typically recommended every 3-4 months, whereas routine cleanings ( prophylaxis ) are usually done twice a year.
  • Frequency is tailored to the individual: The specific schedule will depend on the severity of the previous gum disease and how well the patient is responding to treatment.
  • Targeted for those with previous gum disease: Periodontal maintenance is for individuals who have had periodontal disease, while routine cleanings are for those with healthy gums.
