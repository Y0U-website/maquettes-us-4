Our Dental/Teeth Implants Services Include

Single-tooth implants are a specialized dental solution designed for the replacement of individual missing teeth. This procedure involves the surgical placement of a titanium implant into the jawbone, serving as an artificial tooth root. Single-tooth implants are ideal for individuals who have a single missing tooth.

All-on-4 implants offer a comprehensive solution for individuals with extensive tooth loss, providing support for a full arch of teeth with just four strategically placed implants. It maximizes bone support, offering a stable and efficient foundation. All-on-4 implants suit those requiring full upper or lower arch restoration.

Implant-supported dentures offer stability by securely attaching to dental implants surgically placed in the jaw, blending the convenience of dentures with enhanced support. Implant-supported dentures are ideal for those seeking enhanced stability and comfort compared to traditional removable dentures.

A full mouth restoration is an approach aimed at rebuilding and restoring all teeth in both the upper and lower jaws. This involves the strategic placement of various dental implants, along with other restorative procedures. Full mouth restoration is for individuals requiring a complete overhaul of both upper and lower teeth.

Dental bridges supported by implants provide a stable and durable alternative to traditional bridges. In this approach, dental implants act as secure anchors for the bridge, eliminating the need for support from natural teeth. Implant-supported bridges are suitable for individuals missing multiple adjacent teeth.

How are Dental Implants Attached to Your Mouth?

Tooth implants procedure is a surgical placement of the implant or implants in your jaw bone which usually requires a three to six months healing period before the implant restoration to replace the missing tooth or teeth. During this healing time, the bone grows in and around the titanium implant creating very strong support.

Tooth implants can be rejected. Usually, they are replaced with another implant of a slightly larger size. The rejection or “failure” rate is minimal, only 5 to 7 % of all implant procedures. You may usually go without wearing your dentures for one day to two weeks after implant placement. After the implant has “taken” and you have sufficiently healed, a very natural crown is placed on the implant.

When missing one tooth, your dentist may use a Flipper to fill the space. A Flipper is a false tooth to temporarily take the place of a missing tooth before the permanent crown is placed on the implant.

A Flipper can be attached via either a wire or a plastic piece that fits in the roof of your mouth. Flippers are meant to be a temporary solution while awaiting the permanent crown to be placed on your implant(s).

A procedure of building up the bone is known as Bone grafting . It is common with dental implants. The bone that is used is one of three types. The preferred bone to use is taken from other areas of your mouth or collected in a suction device as the drilling of the sites for dental implants occurs.

Sometimes the bone is taken from areas such as a hip (this requires an orthopedic surgeon and an operating room). The second source of bone can be taken from the same species (human) i.e. cadaver bone or another species like cow bone. The third source for needed bone is a synthetic type. This is the least preferred type of bone to be used for this procedure.
