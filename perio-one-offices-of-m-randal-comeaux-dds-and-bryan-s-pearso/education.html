Oral Hygiene

Practicing good oral hygiene means:

  • Your teeth are clean and free of debris
  • Gums are pink and do not hurt or bleed when you brush or floss
  • Bad breath is rare occurrence

To maintain good oral hygiene

TREATMENT Treatment involves care by a professional. Then follow-up procedures carried out by the patient at home—such as proper oral hygiene and antiseptic mouthwash.

TREATMENT There are a few options to replace missing teeth.

The most permanent treatments are dental implants and bridges.

Dentures and dental bridges are typically the best non-surgical treatment option. Bridges are permanently attached to dental crowns to close the gaps left by missing teeth, while dentures are removable restorations that replace missing teeth.

TREATMENT The best treatment is prevention by brushing and flossing at least twice a day. Once gingivitis has set in treatment involves care by a professional. Then follow-up procedures carried out by the patient at home—such as proper oral hygiene and antiseptic mouthwash.

TREATMENT Gum recession treatment greatly depends on the underlying cause. It starts by addressing any bacterial imbalance, removing plaque and tartar build-up and then treating the infection. Once gum tissue is healthy again further treatment may include:

  • Brush twice a day
  • Floss before brushing
  • Use an antiseptic mouthwash
  • Maintain a healthy diet
  • Stay away from tobacco products
  • Tender gums that may be painful to the touch
  • Bleeding from the gums when brushing or flossing
  • Bad breath
  • Swollen gums
  • Receding gums
  • Soft gums
  • TOOTH LOSS Tooth loss is common in children but very concerning in adults. CAUSES Poor Oral Hygiene
  • Gum Disease
  • Smoking and Tobacco Use
  • Drug Use
  • Poor Diet
  • Injury
  • Hypodontia
  • BLEEDING GUMS Bleeding gums are typically caused by inadequate plaque removal. Plaque contains germs which attack the healthy tissue around the teeth. This will cause the gums to become inflamed and irritated, which may cause them to bleed when brushing or flossing. This is called Gingivitis and is the first stage of gum disease. CAUSES The main cause is plaque build-up. However, there are a few other factors that can contribute to bleeding gums. Age
  • Medication
  • Poor oral hygiene
  • Poor nutrition
  • Clenching or grinding teeth
  • Tobacco use
  • Tooth removal and replacement for loose teeth
  • Bone reshaping, which includes smoothing a bone with dips to remove potential hiding spots for bacteria
  • Bone regeneration, a surgical procedure in which our periodontists perform bone grafting with natural or synthetic bone.
  • GUM RECESSION Gingival recession, or gum recession, is what happens when gum tissue is recessed and lowers its position on the tooth, exposing the roots of the teeth. CAUSES Bacterial disease
  • Overly aggressive brushing
  • Genetics
  • Abnormal tooth positioning
  • Grinding teeth
  • Injury
  • Poor oral hygiene
  • Teeth sensitivity
  • Extended tooth appearance
  • The tooth is a different color nearer the gum line
  • Soft tissue grafting
