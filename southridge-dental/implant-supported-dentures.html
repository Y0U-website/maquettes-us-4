How Implant-Supported Dentures Work

The implant-supported denture process is individualized, but treatment generally involves several stages:

  • Evaluation and treatment planning: Dr. Worke evaluates your teeth, gums, bite, available bone, health history, and goals for replacing missing teeth.
  • CBCT and digital planning: Southridge Dental uses CBCT and 3D imaging to evaluate dental anatomy and assist with implant planning. Dr. Worke also uses a TRIOS scanner for digital impressions and has approximately four years of experience with both technologies.
  • Planning the denture design: Dr. Worke discusses whether a removable implant-supported denture, fixed implant restoration, or another tooth-replacement option may be appropriate for your needs.
  • Dental implant placement: When appropriate, Dr. Worke surgically places the dental implants at Southridge Dental. Guided implant planning may be used to help transfer the digital treatment plan to the surgical procedure.
  • Healing and integration: The implants need time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone before they can provide support for the final restoration. Healing time varies based on the patient and treatment site.
  • Restorative planning: Once the implants are ready, digital impressions and other records may be used to plan the implant-supported denture and its connection to the implants.
  • Final fit and follow-up: The restoration is placed and evaluated for fit, bite, function, and comfort. Follow-up care is important for monitoring the implants, denture, and surrounding tissues.

What to Expect

Treatment begins with a comprehensive evaluation and discussion of your goals. Dr. Worke takes time to explain the option he believes is most appropriate while also discussing reasonable alternatives, so you understand your choices before proceeding.

Local anesthesia is used during dental implant placement to numb the treatment area. Some soreness, swelling, or tenderness may occur after surgery as the area heals. Recovery varies depending on the individual patient and the complexity of treatment.

After implant placement, the implants need time to integrate with the surrounding bone. Depending on your treatment plan, a temporary or existing denture may sometimes be used during portions of the healing process. Dr. Worke will explain what applies to your specific case.

Once healing is sufficient, the restorative phase can move forward. Digital impressions may be used to help plan the final denture and the way it connects to the implants.

Removable implant-supported dentures are taken out for cleaning as directed, while fixed implant restorations have different home-care requirements. Dr. Worke and our dental team will explain how to care for your specific restoration.

Care and Maintenance

Implant-supported dentures require ongoing home care and professional maintenance. The exact cleaning routine depends on whether your restoration is removable or fixed.

Maintenance needs can change over time. Components of an implant-supported denture may require adjustment, repair, or replacement as part of ongoing care.

At Southridge Dental, Dr. Worke and our team can show you how to clean your specific restoration and help you maintain the health of the implants and surrounding tissues.

  • Brush your gums, tongue, remaining teeth, and implant areas as directed.
  • Clean carefully around the implants and attachment areas.
  • Remove and clean a removable implant-supported denture according to your dentist&#x27;s instructions.
  • Use floss, interdental brushes, or other cleaning tools recommended for your restoration.
  • Keep regular dental visits so the implants, gums, bite, attachments, and denture can be monitored.
  • Contact the dental office if you notice looseness, discomfort, damage, or changes in the way the denture fits.
