How Dental Implant Placement Works: Consultation And Planning

Dental implant placement begins with a thorough evaluation and individualized treatment plan. Dr. Worke evaluates your teeth, gums, bite, available bone, health history, and the location of the missing tooth or teeth. These factors help determine whether an implant can be placed and whether any additional treatment should be considered beforehand.

Southridge Dental uses CBCT and 3D imaging to evaluate dental anatomy and assist with implant planning. Dr. Worke has approximately four years of experience using CBCT technology as well as digital impressions with a TRIOS scanner.

When appropriate, CBCT imaging and digital impressions can be combined for digitally guided implant planning. This technology provides detailed information that can help Dr. Worke evaluate the proposed implant location and plan the procedure according to the patient&#x27;s individual anatomy.

Dr. Worke takes time to review the recommended treatment with each patient and discuss appropriate alternatives so you can make an informed decision about your care.

Surgical Placement

When dental implant placement is appropriate, Dr. Worke can perform the surgical procedure at Southridge Dental rather than routinely referring the placement phase elsewhere.

Local anesthesia is used to numb the treatment area. The implant is then surgically positioned in the jaw according to the treatment plan. Depending on the individual case, guided implant technology may be used to help transfer the digital treatment plan to the surgical procedure.

Dr. Worke has completed approximately 150 hours of continuing education focused on dental implants. His advanced training includes Implant Seminars in Miami in 2022, ITC Implant Seminars in Guatemala in 2024, and multiday, hands-on education in the surgical placement of dental implants that involved performing procedures and demonstrating clinical proficiency.

After placement, you will receive instructions for caring for the surgical area during the initial healing period.

Healing And Integration

After a dental implant is placed, the surrounding bone needs time to heal around and integrate with the implant. This process is known as osseointegration and helps create a stable foundation for the future restoration.

The amount of healing time required varies depending on the implant location, available bone, overall health, the complexity of treatment, and other individual factors. Dr. Worke will monitor the implant during follow-up visits and determine when it is appropriate to move forward with the restorative phase.

During healing, patients should follow the postoperative instructions provided by Southridge Dental. These may include recommendations for oral hygiene, eating, activity, and caring for the surgical site.

Abutment And Final Crown

After the implant has healed sufficiently, the restorative phase can begin. An abutment may be used to connect the dental implant to the final restoration. Depending on the treatment plan, the implant may support a single dental crown, an implant-supported bridge, or an implant-supported denture.

Dr. Worke has completed approximately 100 dental implant restorations and provides both implant placement and restoration at Southridge Dental. Having both phases of implant care available within the practice when appropriate allows him to coordinate the surgical and restorative portions of treatment and maintain continuity throughout the process.

Digital impressions may also be used during restorative planning to capture information needed for the final implant restoration.
