How Implant-Supported Bridges Work

Treatment is individualized, but the implant-supported bridge process generally involves several stages:

Because Dr. Worke provides both implant placement and restoration in-house, he can coordinate the surgical and restorative phases of treatment within the same practice when appropriate.

  • Evaluation and treatment planning: Dr. Worke evaluates your teeth, gums, bite, available bone, health history, and the number and location of missing teeth.
  • CBCT and digital planning: Southridge Dental uses CBCT and 3D imaging to evaluate dental anatomy and assist with implant planning. Dr. Worke also uses a TRIOS scanner for digital impressions and has approximately four years of experience with both CBCT technology and digital impressions.
  • Preparing the treatment area: Depending on your individual needs, treatment may involve addressing damaged teeth, gum health, or the condition of the bone before implant placement. Dr. Worke will explain whether any additional treatment should be considered before proceeding.
  • Dental implant placement: When appropriate, Dr. Worke surgically places the implants at Southridge Dental. Guided implant planning may be used to help transfer the digital treatment plan to the surgical procedure.
  • Healing and integration: After placement, the implants require time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone. The length of this phase varies according to the patient and treatment site.
  • Digital impressions and restorative planning: Once the implants are ready for restoration, digital impressions and other records may be used to plan a custom implant-supported bridge.
  • Bridge placement and follow-up: The final bridge is attached to the implants, and Dr. Worke evaluates its fit, bite, and function. Ongoing professional care is important for monitoring the implants, restoration, and surrounding tissues.

Your Implant-Supported Bridge Options

The design of an implant-supported bridge depends on how many teeth are missing, where those teeth are located, the amount of available bone, your bite, and other individual considerations. There is not one implant configuration that is appropriate for every patient.

In some cases, multiple implants can be used to support a bridge replacing several adjacent missing teeth. Dr. Worke determines the appropriate number and position of implants based on the specific treatment area and the requirements of the planned restoration.

During your consultation, he will also discuss whether an implant-supported bridge is the most appropriate option or whether another solution should be considered. Depending on your needs, alternatives may include individual dental implants, a traditional dental bridge, a removable partial denture, or another implant-supported restoration.

Southridge Dental uses CBCT 3D imaging and digital impressions to help evaluate the treatment area and plan implant care. When appropriate, these technologies can also be incorporated into digitally guided implant planning.

What to Expect

Your implant-supported bridge treatment begins with a detailed examination and consultation. Dr. Worke believes patients should understand both the treatment he recommends and reasonable alternatives, so he takes time to explain the process before you decide how to proceed.

Local anesthesia is used during dental implant placement to numb the treatment area. Some soreness, swelling, or tenderness may occur afterward as the surgical sites heal. The amount and duration of discomfort vary by patient and the complexity of the procedure.

The implants then need time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone before they can support the final bridge. Treatment timelines vary, and Dr. Worke will monitor your progress to determine when the implants are ready for restoration.

Once healing is sufficient, the restorative phase begins. Digital impressions may be used to help plan the implant-supported bridge. The final restoration is designed based on your bite, the location of the missing teeth, and other individual considerations.

Long-term maintenance is an important part of implant care. Brushing, cleaning beneath and around the bridge as directed, keeping regular dental visits, and maintaining the health of the surrounding gums and tissues can help support the implant restoration over time.
