How Single-Tooth Dental Implants Work

The single-tooth dental implant process is completed in stages and is customized to the needs of each patient.

Because Dr. Worke provides both implant placement and restoration in-house, he can coordinate the surgical and restorative phases of a single-tooth implant within the same practice when appropriate.

Dr. Worke has completed approximately 150 hours of continuing education focused on dental implants. His advanced training includes Implant Seminars in Miami in 2022, ITC Implant Seminars in Guatemala in 2024, and multiday, hands-on training in the surgical placement of dental implants.

  • Evaluation and planning: Dr. Worke examines the missing-tooth area, surrounding teeth, gums, bite, available bone, health history, and treatment goals.
  • CBCT and digital planning: Southridge Dental uses CBCT and 3D imaging to evaluate dental anatomy and assist with implant planning. Dr. Worke also uses a TRIOS scanner for digital impressions and has approximately four years of experience with both CBCT technology and digital impressions.
  • Dental implant placement: When implant treatment is appropriate, Dr. Worke surgically places the dental implant at Southridge Dental. Local anesthesia is used to numb the treatment area. Guided implant planning may also be used when appropriate to help transfer the digital treatment plan to the procedure.
  • Healing and integration: After placement, the implant needs time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone. This process, known as osseointegration, helps establish a stable foundation for the final restoration. Healing time varies by patient and treatment site.
  • Abutment and custom crown: Once the implant is ready for restoration, an abutment may be connected to the implant, and a custom crown is created to replace the visible portion of the missing tooth. Digital impressions may be used during restorative planning.

What to Expect

Your treatment begins with an examination and discussion of your goals for replacing the missing tooth. Dr. Worke evaluates whether a single-tooth dental implant is an appropriate option and also discusses reasonable alternatives, including a traditional dental bridge when applicable.

Local anesthesia is used during implant placement to help keep the treatment area comfortable. Some soreness, swelling, or tenderness may occur after surgery as the area heals. The amount and duration of discomfort vary depending on the patient and the complexity of the procedure.

After placement, the dental implant requires a period of healing and integration before the final crown can be placed. Treatment timelines vary depending on the treatment site, available bone, healing response, and whether additional procedures are needed.

Once the implant is ready for restoration, digital impressions and other records may be used to plan the final crown. The restoration is designed based on the location of the missing tooth, your bite, surrounding teeth, and other individual considerations.

Ongoing care is important after treatment. Brush regularly, clean around the implant as directed, and maintain professional dental visits so the implant, crown, surrounding gums, and bite can be monitored.

If you clench or grind your teeth, Dr. Worke may also discuss whether an occlusal appliance or nightguard is appropriate for protecting your teeth and restorations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Single dental implants are used to replace individual missing teeth. A single-tooth dental implant typically includes an implant placed in the jaw and a custom restoration above it. Dr. Worke can evaluate the missing-tooth area and determine whether a single dental implant is an appropriate option for your needs.

A single dental implant is supported by an implant placed in the jaw, while a traditional dental bridge generally relies on neighboring teeth to support the replacement tooth. One potential advantage of a single-tooth dental implant is that the adjacent teeth may not need to be prepared to serve as the primary support for the restoration. A bridge may still be an appropriate option in some situations. Dr. Worke can explain the advantages and considerations of each based on your teeth, bone, bite, and treatment goals.

Healing time after dental implant placement varies from patient to patient. The implant needs time to integrate with the surrounding bone before it can support the final restoration. The overall timeline depends on factors such as the treatment location, available bone, healing response, health history, and whether additional procedures are required. Dr. Worke will monitor your progress and let you know when the implant is ready for restoration.

The final dental crown is custom designed with the surrounding teeth, bite, and smile in mind. Its shape, size, and appearance are planned to create a natural-looking replacement for the missing tooth. The final result depends on individual factors including the location of the implant, surrounding gum tissue, available bone, and the condition of nearby teeth. Dr. Worke will discuss the expected restorative outcome during treatment planning.

A single-tooth dental implant requires consistent home care and routine professional maintenance. Brush regularly, clean between the implant restoration and neighboring teeth, and follow the cleaning recommendations provided by your dentist. Regular dental visits allow Dr. Worke and our team to monitor the implant, crown, surrounding gums, bite, and overall oral health. If you notice discomfort, looseness, changes in your bite, or other concerns, schedule an evaluation.
