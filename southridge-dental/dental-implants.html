When Are Dental Implants Recommended?

Dental implants may be recommended for patients who have lost one or more teeth and want to explore a fixed or implant-supported tooth-replacement option. Dr. Worke evaluates your oral health, available bone, treatment goals, medical history, and other individual factors before recommending implant treatment.

Dental implants may be worth considering in situations such as:

Southridge Dental provides dental implants for patients in Inver Grove Heights and surrounding communities. A consultation with Dr. Worke can help determine whether implants are appropriate for your specific needs and what alternatives should also be considered.

  • One missing tooth that may be replaced with a single dental implant and crown.
  • Several missing teeth that may be replaced with an implant-supported bridge.
  • Loose or uncomfortable dentures that may benefit from additional stability provided by implants.
  • Missing teeth when you would prefer an option that does not depend on neighboring teeth for support.
  • Healthy gums and adequate bone to support an implant, or a situation in which additional treatment may help prepare the area for implant placement.

How the Dental Implants Process Works: 1. Consultation And Planning

Your dental implant treatment begins with an examination and personalized treatment planning. Dr. Worke evaluates your teeth, gums, bite, available bone, health history, and goals for replacing your missing tooth or teeth.

Southridge Dental uses CBCT and 3D imaging to evaluate dental anatomy and assist with implant planning. Dr. Worke also has approximately four years of experience using digital impressions with a TRIOS scanner. When appropriate, information from CBCT imaging and digital impressions can be incorporated into digitally guided implant planning. You will have an opportunity to review your options, expected treatment sequence, and alternatives before moving forward.

2. Comfortable Surgical Placement

When implant placement is appropriate, Dr. Worke can surgically place the dental implant at Southridge Dental. He has approximately four years of experience placing dental implants and has completed multiday, hands-on surgical implant training in addition to approximately 150 hours of implant-focused continuing education.

The implant is positioned according to your individualized treatment plan. Guided implant technology may be used when appropriate to help translate the digital treatment plan to the surgical procedure. Local anesthesia is used during treatment, and you will receive instructions for caring for the area afterward.

3. Healing And Integration

After placement, the implant needs time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone. This process, known as osseointegration, helps create a stable foundation for the future implant restoration.

Healing time varies by patient and treatment site. Dr. Worke will monitor your progress and explain when the implant is ready for the restorative phase. Additional appointments may be recommended based on how the area is healing and the type of restoration being planned.

4. Custom Restoration

Once the implant is ready to be restored, a custom dental restoration is created to replace the missing tooth or teeth. Depending on your treatment plan, this may be a single implant crown, an implant-supported bridge, or an implant-supported denture.

Dr. Worke has completed approximately 100 implant restorations and provides both implant placement and restoration at Southridge Dental. Keeping both phases of care within the practice when appropriate allows Dr. Worke to coordinate the surgical and restorative portions of treatment and maintain continuity throughout the process.

5. Follow-Up And Maintenance

Dental implants require ongoing home care and professional maintenance. Brushing, cleaning around the implant, routine dental visits, and following your dentist&#x27;s recommendations all play an important role in maintaining the health of the tissues surrounding an implant.

Southridge Dental monitors both the implant restoration and surrounding oral health during follow-up care. Dr. Worke and our team can also provide guidance on cleaning your specific implant crown, bridge, or implant-supported denture.

Pros & Cons of Dental Implants

✓ Pros

  • Can replace a single missing tooth, multiple teeth, or support certain dentures.
  • Provides stable support for an implant restoration.
  • Restorations can be designed to blend naturally with your smile.
  • A single implant does not require neighboring teeth to provide the primary support.
  • Can provide a long-term tooth-replacement option with appropriate care and maintenance.

✕ Cons

  • Requires a surgical procedure and a period of healing.
  • Adequate bone and healthy surrounding tissues are important considerations.
  • Treatment may take several months depending on healing and the complexity of the case.
  • Initial treatment costs may be greater than some alternative tooth-replacement options.
  • Dental implants are not appropriate for every patient, so an individualized evaluation is necessary.
