FAQ’s

A dental implant restoration is a procedure that involves placing a crown, bridge, or denture on a dental implant to restore the function and look of a missing tooth or teeth. The implant itself is a titanium post that is placed surgically into the jawbone, and the restoration connects to the top of the implant to replace the missing tooth.

With proper care, dental implant restorations can last for many years, often 10 to 15 years or longer. The life of the restoration depends on factors like oral hygiene, the placement of the implant, and your overall health. In some cases, the crown or restoration may need to be replaced after a period of time. Regular cleanings and check-up will help extend the life of the implant.

Dental implant restorations are commonly made from durable, aesthetic materials such as porcelain, ceramic, or zirconia. These materials are chosen because they closely resemble the look and feel of natural teeth, providing a seamless, natural appearance. For added strength, some restorations may also use metal or a combination of materials.

The process of placing a dental implant restoration typically involves two stages. First, the dental implant is placed into the jawbone through a surgical procedure. After the implant has fused with the bone (a process called osseointegration, which may take several months), a custom-made restoration is attached to the implant. The restoration could be a crown (for a single tooth), bridge (for multiple teeth), or denture (for replacing a full set of teeth).

The process of placing a dental implant restoration is typically not painful. The procedure is done under local anesthesia, so you won’t feel any discomfort during the surgery. Afterward, some swelling or mild discomfort may occur, but it can generally be managed with the help of over-the-counter pain relievers. The recovery time for implant restorations is generally minimal.

Caring for a dental implant restoration is quite similar to caring for original teeth. You must brush and floss regularly, paying specific attention to the area around the implant to control plaque buildup. Regular dental exams are important to track the health of the implant as well as the surrounding gums.

Yes, dental implants can be used to restore multiple missing teeth. If you have multiple missing teeth, a dental bridge or partial denture can be supported by dental implants to fill the gap. In some cases, full arch restorations can be supported by multiple implants, providing a stable, long-lasting solution for replacing several teeth.

Yes, dental implant restorations are designed to be as strong, if not stronger, than natural teeth. Since the implant is anchored straight into the jawbone, it provides a stable and much durable foundation for the restoration. The materials used for the restoration (such as porcelain or zirconia) are also very strong and resistant to wear.

The entire process of getting a dental implant restoration typically takes several months. This includes time duration for the implant to fuse with the bone (osseointegration), which can take 3 to 6 months. Once the implant is fully integrated, the restoration can be placed, which usually takes a few weeks to complete.
