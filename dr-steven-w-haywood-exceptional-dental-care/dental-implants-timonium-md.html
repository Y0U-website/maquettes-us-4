Am I a candidate for dental implants?

A full exam is the only way to know. Dr. Haywood reviews x-rays or a cone beam CT scan to check the quality and quantity of your bone, and goes over your health history. Active gum disease is treated first, and thin bone can often be built up with grafting. Smokers can have implants, though they are somewhat less successful. Read about single tooth implants .

How much do dental implants cost?

It depends on your case, so a fee is only quoted after your exam and scan. Simple cases cost less than complex ones. The number of implants, the kind of teeth they hold, whether grafting or a surgical guide is needed, and your choice of materials all affect the total. Implants can cost a little more up front than other options, but they are built to last. Book an implant consultation .

What is computer-guided implant surgery?

A cone beam CT scan builds a 3D picture of your jaw, and the implant is placed virtually in planning software first. A 3D-printed surgical guide then lets Dr. Haywood place the implant in that exact position. It matters most when bone is limited, a precise angle is needed, or several implants must line up. Not every implant needs a guide. Learn about guided implant surgery .

Can implants hold a denture in place?

Yes. Snap-on implant teeth attach to implants with snap connectors, so they stay secure but still come out for cleaning. The upper set usually sits on four or five implants and leaves the palate uncovered. The lower set needs at least two, though four or more give better stability, and more can often be added later. Read about snap-on implant teeth .

Can I get a full set of fixed teeth on implants?

Yes. Fixed full-arch teeth, sometimes called all-on-four or permanent dentures, are screwed or cemented onto at least four implants, and often more if bone is limited. They chew most like natural teeth and are the most costly option. Only the practice can remove them, so regular professional cleaning is part of the plan, and a spare set is always made. Read about fixed full-arch teeth .

Can a missing front tooth be replaced with an implant?

Yes, and it is what most patients choose here. Front implants take more care because the bone is often thinner, so smaller implants, custom ceramic posts and computer-guided surgery are almost always used. Grafting or orthodontics may be part of the plan, coordinated with any other work so your smile looks right. Read about front tooth implants .

Do I need an implant for every missing tooth?

Not always. An implant bridge uses two or more implants to hold three or more teeth joined together, which costs less than one implant for each tooth. It works well where back teeth have been lost or a few front teeth are gone. The trade-off is cleaning: you’ll need a floss threader, and food can collect under the bridge. Read about the implant bridge .

How long does it take, and what is recovery like?

A single tooth can be restored in as little as 6–8 weeks after surgery, and the whole process can take several months. Pain afterward is described as negligible. For the first day, eat soft food, drink plenty of fluids and avoid straws, smoking and alcohol. Leave the implant undisturbed while it heals, and avoid chewing directly on it.
