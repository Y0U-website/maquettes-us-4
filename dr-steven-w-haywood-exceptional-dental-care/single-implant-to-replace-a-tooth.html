Single tooth implant questions, answered

What is a single tooth implant made of?

Usually three parts: the implant “screw” that replaces the root of the tooth, the abutment (post) that supports the new tooth, and the crown, which is either screwed or cemented onto the post or the implant itself. Implants come in specific lengths and widths, sized for your particular need.

Am I a candidate for a dental implant?

That’s decided at a consultation, not from a webpage. A careful radiographic review checks whether there’s enough bone to support an implant, and a CBCT scan can show problems with bone quality or quantity. Gum health, your general health and smoking are all worth raising openly, since active gum disease needs attention first and implants are less successful in smokers. Request a consultation .

Are titanium or ceramic implants better?

Titanium implants are the most common and successful type. Ceramic and zirconia implants are also possible and are essentially the holistic implant of choice, though ceramic implants cost more than traditional ones. Dr. Haywood can explain which suits your mouth and your priorities.

How does an implant compare with a bridge or a partial denture?

A removable partial denture costs the least, but it comes in and out, usually has metal clasps and can affect how you taste food. A fixed bridge is attached to neighboring teeth and is harder to clean. A single implant replaces the whole tooth without affecting the teeth beside it, and in this office is regularly less expensive and more conservative than a bridge.

How long does the whole process take?

Implants need a healing time of at least 6–8 weeks after surgery before the tooth is restored, and full integration into the bone takes longer than that. From your first visit to your finished tooth, the entire process can take several months.

Does getting an implant hurt?

Pain after an implant procedure is described as negligible. You’ll leave with gauze over the site and instructions to follow: soft food and plenty of fluids for the first 24 hours, no exercise that day, and no straws, vigorous rinsing, smoking or alcohol, which can dislodge the protective blood clot. How do I look after the implant while it heals?

Leave it undisturbed: avoid chewing directly on it and don’t touch it with your tongue or fingers. From 24 hours, rinse gently with lukewarm salt water 3–4 times a day, and use ice bags for pain and swelling. Call the office if numbness lasts beyond 12 hours. After an upper implant or sinus graft, don’t blow your nose for 8 days and sneeze with your mouth open. See implant aftercare .

How long do dental implants last?

Current thinking is that implants may last a lifetime. They don’t decay and seem far less affected by gum disease than natural teeth. With proper planning, placement and maintenance, a single tooth implant can be a potential lifetime solution.
