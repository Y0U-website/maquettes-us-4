Front tooth implant questions, answered

Why are front tooth implants more complex?

Placing implants in the front is considerably more difficult, and can be more expensive, than placing back implants. The bone is often thinner and less dense, and the result shows every time you smile, so smaller implants, customized ceramic abutment posts and computer-guided implant surgery are virtually always used.

What causes front tooth loss?

Common causes include trauma such as a sports injury, fall or childhood accident; a tooth that never came in; failed root canals; teeth fractured by failed posts; severe periodontal disease; and extensive root decay from dry mouth or a high-carb diet.

Why does bone matter so much for a front implant?

The upper front jawbone can deteriorate rapidly if a tooth is removed incorrectly, if the socket isn’t preserved with grafting, or if a poorly placed implant leads to bone loss. Any of these can mean more surgery and a different implant, which is why bone is planned for carefully from the start.

My tooth has been missing for years. Can I still get an implant?

It’s worth finding out. When a tooth has been missing a long time, or never developed in that spot, orthodontics and bone grafting are considered as part of your individual treatment plan every time. A consultation with 3D imaging shows what your bone can support. Request a consultation .

Should I choose an implant, a bridge or a partial?

All three have been used for years to replace front teeth. In this office, implants make up 98 % of what patients want and receive to replace missing front teeth. An implant replaces the whole tooth without affecting the teeth beside it. See single tooth implants .

Will my front implant be planned in 3D?

Yes. A 3D CBCT scan shows where bone is and where it may need repair, the implant is placed virtually on the computer first, and a 3D-printed surgical guide carries that plan into surgery. See computer-guided implant surgery .
