Snap-on implant teeth questions, answered

What are snap-on implant teeth?

A removable set of teeth, full or partial, that is secured by implants in your jawbone. A snap connector joins the teeth to each implant, so the set stays anchored while you eat and speak but can still be taken out. The connectors are durable and offer a lower-cost option than fixed, screw-in implant teeth. See all dental implant options .

How many implants will I need?

In the upper jaw, usually 4 or 5 implants are placed. In the lower jaw, a minimum of 2 implants is used, spread apart so chewing forces are distributed over a larger area and the denture doesn’t rock. Four or more are often preferred in the lower jaw, to stop the teeth rocking forward or back and to keep food from collecting underneath while you chew.

Can I start with 2 implants and add more later?

Often, yes. Many people choose the 2-implant option for financial reasons. In many cases more implants can be added over time, so you can upgrade your implant denture when finances allow or when you want more stability.

How is this different from a regular denture?

Conventional dentures can cause sore spots on the gums, especially with harder foods or long wear. Anchoring the teeth to implants can relieve many of those difficulties, with stronger chewing and more comfort. The upper snap-on set is horseshoe-shaped and doesn’t fully cover the palate, while a conventional denture is bulkier and can change how you sense food temperature and flavor.

How do snap-on teeth compare with fixed implant teeth?

A full upper snap-on denture costs roughly 30 % less than a comparable fixed implant set of teeth. Fixed teeth are screwed in or cemented and can’t be removed to clean underneath, while snap-on teeth are removable. Dr. Haywood can walk you through both at a consultation. See fixed full-arch implant teeth .

Can implants hold a partial denture?

Yes. If you still have teeth that can be saved, they’re kept. Implants secure a partial better than the clips on a conventional partial, and there are no clips or wires to show in your smile. Clips also put stress on the teeth they grip, which can eventually cost you that tooth; an implant connection removes that risk.

What if I lose another tooth later?

With a conventional partial, losing a tooth can mean losing everything you paid for and starting over. With a snap-on implant partial, a tooth can simply be added, which makes it an easier way to plan ahead for changes that come with aging.

Which snap attachments do you use?

Upper snap-on dentures are made with snap connectors from two brands the practice likes: Locator attachments and Equator attachments. These premium attachments are chosen for long-term success.

What if I’m nervous about implant treatment?

Sedation options are available at the practice to help you feel calm and relaxed. Laughing gas is breathed through a nasal mask and wears off quickly enough for you to drive yourself home. Oral conscious sedation is a pill taken about an hour before your appointment; it stays in your system for several hours, so you’ll need a ride to and from the visit. See sedation dentistry .
