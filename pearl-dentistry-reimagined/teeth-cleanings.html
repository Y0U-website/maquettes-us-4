FAQs

Every six months for most people, which is why we build cleanings at that interval into your dental plan and schedule the next one before you leave. That rhythm is what keeps plaque and tartar from hardening into the kind of buildup that leads to gingivitis, decay and enamel erosion.

A cleaning is a single visit that covers more than the cleaning itself. Your hygienist starts with an exam of your whole mouth, then scales plaque and tartar from above and below the gumline, flosses around every tooth, polishes, and schedules your next visit before you leave. How long it runs comes down to how much buildup we find, since that’s what determines the kind of cleaning you need.

A regular cleaning is preventive. We clear plaque and tartar from your tooth surfaces above and below the gumline, then polish so buildup has a harder time returning. A deep cleaning, known clinically as scaling and root planing, is treatment rather than maintenance, used when gum disease has progressed and bacteria have settled into the pockets below the gumline. We decide which one you need from the extent of your buildup and the condition of your gums.

Brushing and flossing daily is the foundation, but once plaque hardens into tartar it bonds to the tooth and no toothbrush will lift it off. That’s the part we handle, scaling it away from above and below the gumline and then polishing off the residue your routine missed. Home care and professional cleanings aren’t alternatives to each other, they work together.
