What Is Airway-Centric Orthodontics?

Traditional orthodontics focuses primarily on straightening teeth and correcting your bite. Airway-centric orthodontics takes a broader approach. It evaluates how the size and position of your jaws affect your ability to breathe, sleep, and function — then treats the underlying structural issues, not just the cosmetic ones.

When a child’s jaw doesn’t develop properly, or when an adult’s airway is compromised by a narrow palate or recessed jaw, the effects can reach far beyond crooked teeth. Poor jaw development is linked to sleep-disordered breathing , chronic mouth breathing, snoring, obstructive sleep apnea, TMJ dysfunction , and even behavioral and focus issues in children.

At Hamada Orthodontics, Dr. Allison Hamada uses advanced diagnostics — including 3D imaging and airway assessment — to evaluate each patient’s jaw structure, tongue posture, breathing patterns, and facial development before recommending treatment. This whole-patient evaluation is what sets airway-focused orthodontic care apart from conventional approaches.

The goal isn’t just a straighter smile. It’s better breathing, better sleep, and a foundation for lifelong health.

Frequently Asked Questions About Airway Orthodontics in Metairie

What is airway-centric orthodontics, and how is it different from regular orthodontics?

Airway-centric orthodontics evaluates how your jaw structure, tongue position, and breathing patterns affect your overall health — not just the alignment of your teeth. At Hamada Orthodontics , Dr. Hamada assesses every patient’s airway function as part of the treatment planning process. This approach identifies underlying issues like mouth breathing, sleep-disordered breathing, and jaw underdevelopment that traditional orthodontics may overlook.

At what age should my child be evaluated for airway orthodontics?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends an orthodontic evaluation by age 7, and airway concerns can be identified even earlier. Because 80 % of jaw growth happens by age 7, early intervention gives Dr. Hamada the best opportunity to guide jaw development, expand the palate, and create space for proper breathing and adult teeth.

Will my child need braces if they get early airway treatment?

Early airway treatment (often called Phase 1 ) focuses on guiding jaw growth and correcting breathing patterns while your child is still developing. In many cases, this reduces the complexity of future orthodontic treatment — some children may need shorter treatment with braces later, and others may avoid certain interventions altogether. Dr. Hamada will outline a clear, individualized plan based on your child’s specific development.

What is palatal expansion, and does it hurt?

Palatal expansion uses a custom appliance to gently widen the upper jaw over time. For children, this process takes advantage of the natural growth plate in the palate, making it comfortable and effective. For adults, Dr. Hamada uses MARPE (miniscrew-assisted rapid palatal expansion), a minimally invasive technique. Most patients experience mild pressure for the first few days, but the process is well-tolerated.
