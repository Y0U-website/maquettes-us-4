Dental Cleanings

We’ll give you personalized advice on how to maintain your teeth at home with good nutrition and proper brushing and flossing techniques. Based on your oral health needs, we’ll also advise on how often you should return for dental cleanings. The standard is every six months, but patients at risk of gum disease and tooth decay may need cleanings more frequently.

  • Teeth cleaning: The dentist/dental hygienist uses special tools and skills to remove unhealthy deposits between teeth and just beneath the gum line. If you notice us spending more time on a particular region, it’s because of built-up deposits.
  • Polishing: The dentist will polish your teeth with a rotating brush and gritty toothpaste. This step removes surface stains and maintains a smooth, natural appearance. After cleaning, this is the right opportunity to talk to your dentist about teeth whitening or other procedures.

What are the benefits of dental cleanings?

A dental cleaning is more than a fresh-from-the-dentist feeling. Patients are surprised that the benefits go beyond the mouth. Here are the notable benefits of dental cleanings.

  • Prevention: Removing plaque and tartar protects your smile from cavities and gum disease.
  • Early detection: The dentist can detect oral issues at their earliest stage, allowing for timely treatment.
  • Lasting fresh breath: Cleanings remove harmful bacteria from hard-to-reach surfaces in the mouth, ensuring fresh breath.
  • A more confident smile: Removing surface stains with cleanings helps you maintain a brighter, more radiant smile.
  • It’s cost-effective: Preventive dentistry with routine cleanings avoids the need for costly and invasive procedures.
  • Enhanced systemic health: A healthy smile is a healthy you. Preventing dental problems like periodontal disease minimizes the risk of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, Alzheimer’s, and other systemic issues.

What if I skip dental cleanings?

Life can get busy, and sometimes cost and dental fear can make you fall behind with routine cleanings. Unfortunately, skipping regular cleanings can increase the risk of tooth decay, gum disease, bad breath, and other issues. These dental issues are painful and often require resolution with costly treatments.
