What Determines Your Candidacy and Number of Implants

No two Brooklyn patients arrive at a snap-in denture consultation with the same mouth. Some have been missing teeth for a decade and have much bone loss in the lower jaw. Others lost teeth recently and still have dense, healthy ridge structure. The number of implants you need, and whether you re a candidate at all, depends on a few measurable factors.

None of these factors can be judged accurately by a visual exam or a panoramic X-ray alone. A 3D CT scan confirms candidacy. This imaging measures bone density and volume in millimeters, maps out nerve locations, and reveals sinus positioning long before a drill touches the jaw. Practices across Brooklyn that offer in-house CBCT scanning can often determine implant number and placement in a single visit, rather than sending patients elsewhere and waiting weeks for answers.

  • Remaining bone volume: Height and width of the jawbone determine whether standard implants can be placed or whether shorter, wider, or angled implants are needed.
  • Number of missing teeth: A single arch with all teeth missing typically needs 2 to 6 implants for a snap-in denture, depending on the retention system chosen.
  • Which arch is involved: Lower jaws generally require fewer implants than upper jaws because upper bone tends to be softer and more porous, especially near the sinus cavities.
  • Whether grafting is likely: Long-term denture wearers often show ridge resorption that may require bone or sinus grafting before implants can be placed safely.

When Snap-In Dentures Are Not the Right Fit

Not every Brooklyn patient who walks into an implant consultation needs snap-in dentures. A dentist who understands implant dentistry will say so. If you are missing only one tooth or a few teeth scattered across the arch, a single dental implant or an implant-supported bridge is usually a more conservative and cost-effective solution. Snap-in dentures replace an entire arch of teeth using two to six implants as anchor points. Applying that system to a partial gap would mean removing healthy teeth or making a simple problem too complicated.

Some patients come in wanting a result that never comes out of the mouth at all. Snap-in dentures do snap onto implants securely, but they are still removable for cleaning and sleep. Patients from Bay Ridge to Bensonhurst who want a fixed, permanent feel often do better with an implant bridge or All-on-4 dental implants, which are anchored and only removable by a dentist. This matters for people with strong gag reflexes, public-facing careers, or a preference for a tooth replacement that mimics natural teeth as closely as possible.

There is also a simpler scenario: patients already wearing a conventional denture that fits well, stays in place, and causes no pain or embarrassment. If a current denture is doing its job, there is no clinical reason to recommend implants just because the technology exists. Recommending unnecessary surgery would go against the diagnostic standards that implant-trained dentists in Brooklyn are expected to follow.

  • Missing one or a few teeth: a single implant or implant bridge is often more appropriate than snap-in dentures
  • Wanting a completely fixed, non-removable result: implant bridges or All-on-4 dental implants may better match that goal
  • Currently wearing a well-fitting, comfortable denture with no complaints: no change may be needed
  • Consultation: Your dentist reviews medical history, examines remaining teeth or existing dentures, and discusses whether two or four implants per arch fits your bone density and budget.
  • Imaging: A 3D CT scan maps nerve position, sinus location, and available bone. This allows precise implant placement planning.
  • Implant placement: Titanium posts are surgically set into the jawbone, typically under local anesthesia with sedation options for anxious patients.
  • Healing period: Osseointegration, the process where bone fuses to the implant, generally takes three to six months depending on the arch and your bone quality.
  • Denture attachment: Once healing is confirmed, the denture is fitted with attachments that snap onto the implant abutments.
  • Fit and bite adjustment visit: The dentist checks bite alignment, pressure points, and snap tension. They make small adjustments so the denture feels stable and comfortable.
