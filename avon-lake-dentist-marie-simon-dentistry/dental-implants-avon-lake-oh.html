What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are titanium posts surgically placed into your jawbone to mimic the roots of natural teeth. They are an ideal solution for individuals with healthy jawbones who need tooth replacements. Unlike conventional dentures, which can slip or cause discomfort, dental implants remain fixed, allowing you to eat and speak naturally. Many patients prefer implants due to the stability and confidence they provide.

Exploring Your Options for Dental Implants in Avon Lake

When considering dental implants, it’s vital to understand the available options. At our office, we offer three primary types of dental implants: endosteal dental implants, subperiosteal dental implants, and zygomatic dental implants. Each type has unique benefits tailored to different dental needs.

Subperiosteal Dental Implants

Subperiosteal dental implants can be an alternative when there is insufficient jawbone height to support endosteal implants. These implants consist of a metal framework placed beneath the gum tissue but above the jawbone. It’s worth noting that, while subperiosteal implants may provide a secure way to hold dentures or crowns, they typically do not offer the same level of stability as endosteal implants. They are often recommended for patients who prefer a less invasive procedure.

Zygomatic Dental Implants

Zygomatic dental implants are the least common option and are primarily suitable for individuals with minimal jawbone structure due to extensive bone loss. Instead of being placed in the jawbone, these implants are anchored in the zygomatic bone (cheekbone), providing a strong foundation for dental prosthetics. Zygomatic implants are a complex solution tailored for advanced cases where conventional options may not be feasible. If you’re unsure about your eligibility, Dr. Marie Simon will provide a thorough evaluation and an honest assessment of your options.

The Benefits of Dental Implants

Whether you opt for endosteal, subperiosteal, or zygomatic dental implants, the benefits are substantial. Not only do dental implants look and function like natural teeth, but they also help maintain jawbone structure. This is crucial because missing teeth can lead to bone resorption, altering your facial structure over time. Implants also make it easier to eat a variety of foods, eliminating the worry about the fit and feel of dentures during meals.
