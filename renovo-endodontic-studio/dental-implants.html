Dental Implant FAQs

Considering dental implants, but have questions? You’re not alone. Many patients want to understand what’s involved before making decisions about their oral health. Below, we answer the most common questions about dental implants.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

With proper care, dental implants can last 25 years or longer. Many patients keep their implants for a lifetime. The titanium post fuses permanently with your jawbone through a process called osseointegration. Regular brushing, flossing, and dental checkups help maximize the lifespan of your implant.

Is Getting a Dental Implant Painful?

No, getting a dental implant is not painful during the procedure due to local anesthesia. Most patients experience little discomfort during implant placement.

Sedation options are available for patients who want additional relaxation. Any mild soreness after the procedure can typically be managed with over-the-counter pain relievers.

What Are the Downsides of Dental Implants?

Dental implants require a longer treatment timeline than bridges or dentures. The process takes several months to allow for proper bone fusion. Implants cost more upfront than other tooth replacement options.

Not everyone qualifies because sufficient jawbone density is required for successful placement.

Who Should Avoid Dental Implants?

Patients with uncontrolled diabetes or autoimmune conditions may face higher complication risks. Heavy smokers often experience slower healing and lower success rates.

Those who have had radiation therapy to the head or neck area need careful evaluation. A thorough assessment with 3D imaging helps determine your candidacy.

What Is the Difference Between an Implant and a Crown?

A dental implant replaces a missing tooth entirely, including the root.

A crown covers and protects a damaged tooth that still has a healthy root. Implants are standalone replacements anchored directly into your jawbone. Crowns are restorations placed over existing tooth structure.

What Happens During the Dental Implant Process?

The procedure begins with placing a titanium post into your jawbone. Over the next few months, the bone fuses with the implant, creating a stable foundation.

An abutment (connector piece) is then attached to the post. Finally, a custom-made crown is secured to complete your new tooth.
