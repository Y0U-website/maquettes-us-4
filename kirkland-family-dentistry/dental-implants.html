What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small titanium posts that act as artificial tooth roots. They’re placed into the jawbone to support a crown, bridge, or denture, creating a stable and long-lasting foundation for replacement teeth.

Unlike removable dentures, implants are fixed in place and function just like natural teeth. They restore your ability to chew and speak comfortably, while blending seamlessly with your smile. Because they integrate with your jawbone, they also help prevent bone loss and stop nearby teeth from shifting, protecting your oral health long-term.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants are a key part of restorative dentistry , offering more than just a fix for missing teeth. They provide a stable, long-lasting solution that brings back your ability to chew, speak, and smile with confidence. Whether you’re replacing a single tooth or restoring your full smile, implants support both your oral health and overall well-being.

Some of the key benefits of dental implants include:

  • Permanent and durable: Designed to last 15+ years — often a lifetime with proper care.
  • Low-maintenance: Easy to care for with regular brushing and flossing.
  • Natural look and feel: Blends seamlessly with your smile and functions like real teeth.
  • Improved chewing and speech: Eat your favorite foods and speak clearly without worry.
  • Boosts confidence: Restores your smile and helps you feel great about how you look.
  • Prevents bone loss: Stimulates the jawbone to maintain density and structure.
  • Protects facial structure: Reduces the risk of facial sagging caused by missing teeth.
  • Keeps teeth aligned: Prevents neighboring teeth from shifting into empty spaces.

Types of Dental Implants We Provide

We’re proud to offer a comprehensive range of implant options, including:

  • Single-tooth implants : Ideal for replacing just one missing tooth with a natural-looking crown.
  • Multiple-tooth implants : For patients missing several teeth, we provide custom solutions supported by two or more implants.
  • Full-mouth implants : We offer full-arch solutions that restore function and appearance for complete tooth replacement.
  • Endosteal implants : The most commonly used type, surgically placed into the jawbone for maximum stability.
  • Subperiosteal implants : Recommended for patients with insufficient jawbone who are not candidates for bone grafting.
  • Mini dental implants (MDIs) : Smaller-diameter implants ideal for stabilizing loose dentures, especially in cases of bone loss.
  • Immediate-load implants : Also known as “same-day implants,” these allow temporary teeth to be placed on the same day as your implant surgery.
  • All-on-4 implants : A full-arch restoration using four strategically placed implants to support a full set of replacement teeth.

Supporting Dental Implant Procedures in Kirkland, WA

After you choose your implant plan, our team carries out meticulous procedures to guarantee optimal placement and healing:

  • Surgical Placements : We perform guided implant surgeries using advanced imaging and techniques to place implants accurately and comfortably, minimizing recovery time and maximizing success.
  • Bone Grafts : For patients with insufficient jawbone density, our bone grafting procedures help rebuild and strengthen the bone, creating a stable foundation for your implants.
  • Sinus Augmentation Surgeries : When necessary, we safely elevate the sinus membrane and add bone material to prepare the upper jaw for implants, ensuring a secure and lasting fit.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

Choosing dental implants is an important decision, and it’s natural to have questions about the procedure, recovery, and costs. Below, we’ve compiled answers to some of the most common questions we receive to help you better understand what to expect and feel confident about your treatment.

Are dental implants painful?

Most patients experience only minor discomfort. We use local anesthesia and advanced techniques to keep you as comfortable as possible during and after the procedure. For patients who feel anxious about the procedure, we also offer sedation dentistry to help you relax throughout your treatment.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, dental implants can last 15+ years, often a lifetime. Regular check-ups and good oral hygiene are key.

How much do dental implants cost in Kirkland?

Dental implants typically range from $1,500 to $5,000 per implant (insurance dependent). The final cost depends on your individual needs, including the number of implants and whether you need procedures such as bone grafting or sinus lifts.

Are implants covered by insurance?

Coverage varies depending on your dental insurance plan. Some plans may cover a portion of the implant or related procedures. We’re happy to review your benefits and provide a breakdown of what’s covered.

How much are the monthly payments for dental implants?

We offer flexible financing options to fit your budget. Monthly payments will depend on your total treatment plan (number of implants, type of implant) and the financing terms you choose—many patients find payments manageable with our in-house or one of our third-party financing partners, Cherry, Sunbit, and Care Credit.

What are the different dental implant options?

We offer a variety of implant solutions, including:

Your dentist will help you choose the best option based on your goals and oral health.

  • Single-tooth implants
  • Multiple-tooth implants
  • Full-mouth implants
  • All-on-4
  • Mini dental implants
  • Same-day (immediate load) implants

How can I know if I’m a good candidate for dental implants?

Most healthy adults are candidates for dental implants. Ideal candidates have good gum health and sufficient jawbone. If you’ve experienced bone loss, procedures like bone grafting may still make implants possible. We’ll assess your case during your consultation.

How long does it take to get dental implants?

The full process from consultation to final crown typically takes 3 to 6 months, depending on healing time and whether supporting procedures (like bone grafting) are needed. Some patients qualify for same-day implants.
