How All-on-4 differs from traditional implants

Traditional implants typically require one post per missing tooth. All-on-4 uses only four implants per arch, angled to maximize bone contact in areas of higher bone density. This means most patients can avoid bone grafting and receive immediate-load temporary teeth at the surgical appointment, with final restorations placed once healing is complete (usually 3–6 months later).

The Dental Implant Timeline — From Consultation to Final Crown

One of the most common patient questions is how long do dental implants take from start to finish? The honest answer is that timeline varies based on your bone density, whether bone grafting is needed, and which restoration option you choose. Here’s what the typical implant patient experiences at Smile Center of Orlando.

Total timeline: typically 4–9 months from consultation to final restoration. Patients receiving All-on-4 can often leave the surgical appointment with a fixed temporary prosthesis the same day — though final restorations still require the osseointegration period.

  • Consultation 3D imaging (1 visit, ~60 minutes). We review your medical history, take a digital scan and CBCT 3D X-ray of your jaw, and discuss whether a single implant, multiple implants, or All-on-4 is appropriate. Insurance verification and treatment planning happens at this visit.
  • Bone graft or sinus lift if needed (3–6 month healing). Not every patient needs this. If your CBCT shows insufficient bone for implant integration, we may recommend a graft prior to placement. Healing time is generally 3–6 months before the implant can be placed.
  • Implant placement surgery (1 visit, 1–2 hours). The titanium implant post is placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia (with sedation options available). Most patients return to normal activity within 24–48 hours.
  • Osseointegration / healing period (3–6 months). During this stage, the implant fuses with your jawbone — a process called osseointegration. A temporary crown or removable replacement is often provided during this period.
  • Abutment crown placement (1–2 visits, 4–6 weeks apart). Once osseointegration is complete, the abutment is attached and the final crown (or bridge or denture) is fabricated and seated.

Bone Grafting Sinus Lifts — When Are They Needed?

For implants to integrate properly, there must be sufficient bone in the area where the implant post will be placed. Some patients — particularly those who have been missing teeth for a long period of time, or who have a history of periodontal disease — have experienced bone resorption and need additional bone before an implant can succeed.

When you may need a bone graft

  • You’ve been missing the tooth for more than 12 months and the jawbone has receded
  • You’ve had advanced gum disease that compromised the supporting bone
  • Your CBCT 3D imaging shows thin or low-density bone in the implant site
  • Trauma or extraction-related bone loss is documented

What a sinus lift involves

For upper-jaw implants — particularly molars in the back of the mouth — the maxillary sinus floor sometimes sits too close to the planned implant location. A sinus lift gently raises the sinus membrane and adds bone material to create space for the implant. Recovery is typically 4–6 months before the implant can be placed. This is a routine procedure when needed and is performed in-office under local anesthesia with sedation options.

Dental Implant Recovery Aftercare — What to Expect

Recovery from implant placement is generally less involved than patients expect. Most working adults return to normal activity within 1–2 days, though full healing and osseointegration takes longer.

The first 72 hours

  • Mild swelling and bruising are normal and usually peak around day 2–3
  • Pain is typically managed with over-the-counter ibuprofen — prescription pain medication is rarely necessary
  • Stick to soft, cool foods (yogurt, smoothies, room-temperature soups, scrambled eggs)
  • Avoid using a straw or smoking — both can disrupt clot formation
  • Cold compresses 15 minutes on, 15 off for the first 24 hours help control swelling

The first 2 weeks

  • Gradually reintroduce normal foods — avoid crunchy or chewy foods on the implant side
  • Rinse gently with warm salt water 3–4 times daily (do not use a vigorous mouthwash for the first 7 days)
  • Continue brushing normally elsewhere; brush gently around the implant site
  • A 1-week post-op check confirms healing is on track

Long-term implant care

Once your implant is fully restored, care it for it the same way you care for natural teeth: brush twice daily, floss daily (with an implant-friendly floss), and keep your professional cleanings on schedule. With proper care, dental implants frequently last 25+ years and can last a lifetime. Most insurance plans cover at least part of the cost of implant restoration — verify your benefits with our office before treatment begins.

With proper care, dental implants frequently last 25+ years — and many patients keep theirs for life. The titanium post itself is permanent; the crown or restoration on top may eventually need to be replaced after 15–20 years of use, depending on wear. Implants have a documented 95 %+ success rate when placed in patients with adequate bone and good oral hygiene.

Most adults missing one or more teeth are candidates for implants. The key factors are: (1) sufficient jawbone to support the implant — bone grafting can address insufficient bone in most cases; (2) healthy gums — periodontal disease must be treated first; (3) general health stable enough for minor outpatient surgery; (4) commitment to good oral hygiene during and after healing. Smokers can still receive implants but are advised to reduce or stop smoking during the healing window.

Most implant procedures are performed under local anesthesia — meaning you remain awake but feel no pain. Many patients also choose IV sedation or oral conscious sedation for added comfort, particularly for multiple implants or All-on-4 procedures. Sedation options are discussed at consultation based on your medical history and preference.

You’ll stick to soft, cool foods for the first 48–72 hours. Most patients can resume a normal diet within 7–10 days, avoiding chewing on the implant side until cleared by your dentist. After the final crown is placed (3–6 months later), you can eat anything you’d eat with a natural tooth.

The procedure itself is performed under local anesthesia (with sedation options available), so you should not feel pain during placement. Post-operative discomfort is generally mild — most patients describe it as less painful than a tooth extraction — and managed effectively with over-the-counter ibuprofen. Significant pain after the first 72 hours is unusual and should be reported to our office.

Traditional implants are 3–5 mm in diameter; mini implants are typically less than 3 mm. Mini implants are sometimes used to stabilize lower dentures or for patients with very limited bone, but they are less durable for long-term tooth replacement. For most single-tooth or All-on-4 cases, Dr. Altomare and Dr. Rezkalla recommend traditional implants for their superior longevity and load capacity.
