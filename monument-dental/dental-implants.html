What Are Dental Implants?

Advanced Care for Long-Lasting Tooth Replacement

Dental implants are artificial tooth roots, similar in shape to screws. When dental implants are placed in your jawbone, they bond with your natural bone and become a sturdy base for supporting one or more artificial teeth, known as dental implant crowns. Dental implants are a popular and effective permanent tooth replacement solution for people who suffer from missing teeth, failing teeth, or chronic dental problems.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Enhanced Stability for Everyday Eating and Speaking

Improved Appearance: Dental implants look and feel like your own teeth.

Enhanced Comfort: Dental implants eliminate the discomfort of removable dentures.

Speech Improvement: With poorly fitting dentures, teeth can slip and cause you to mumble or slur words. Dental implants help you speak without worry.

Durability: Implants are very durable and will last many years. With good care, many implants last a lifetime.

The Dental Implant Procedure

Receiving dental implants involves several steps and may require several months to complete. Here is a general overview of the process:

Initial Consultation: Our team will evaluate your dental and medical history to determine if you’re a good candidate for dental implants.

Surgical Placement: The implant is surgically placed into your jawbone. This is done under local anesthesia to ensure your comfort.

Osseointegration: Over the next few months, the implant will naturally bond with your jawbone in a process called osseointegration.

Abutment Placement: Once the implant is securely bonded, a small connector post, called an abutment, is attached to hold the new tooth securely.

Placement of Artificial Tooth: Finally, a custom-made crown is attached to the abutment, completing the restoration.

Are You a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Carefully Evaluated Options for Long-Term Success

Most individuals who are in good general health, have healthy gums, and have adequate bone to support an implant can consider dental implants for missing teeth or dental implants for failing teeth. However, certain conditions such as uncontrolled diabetes, cancer, or radiation to the jaw may affect implant success.
