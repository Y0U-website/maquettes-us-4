What happens before implant treatment begins?

Implant care starts with diagnosis, not a preset procedure. The team evaluates your goals, oral health, imaging, and remaining teeth before explaining suitable options and costs.

Who is a good candidate for dental implants?

A good candidate generally has one or more missing teeth, healthy gums, enough jawbone to support an implant or the ability to receive a bone graft, and medical health that allows normal healing. Candidates should be committed to daily oral hygiene and professional maintenance. Tobacco use and some medical conditions can affect healing, so Brighter Way confirms candidacy with an exam, health-history review, and imaging.

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants are intended to be a long-term solution. With healthy gum and bone tissue, consistent home care, and regular professional maintenance, the implant fixture may last for decades or even a lifetime. The crown, bridge, or full-arch restoration can wear and may need repair or replacement. Results vary with hygiene, tissue health, tobacco use, bite forces, medical health, and follow-up care.

How do dental implants help protect jawbone health?

An implant replaces a missing tooth root and integrates with the jawbone. This provides functional stimulation during chewing and helps preserve the bone around the implant compared with leaving the space empty. Existing bone loss may require grafting, and imaging helps the dental team evaluate bone quality and volume before treatment.

What is the difference between All-on-X and an implant overdenture?

All-on-X is a fixed full-arch restoration that is not removed by the patient. An implant overdenture connects to implants for stability but remains removable for daily cleaning. An evaluation is needed to determine which design may fit your anatomy, goals, maintenance preferences, and budget.
