Common Reasons For Dental Emergencies

Dental emergencies can happen at any time and anywhere. While there’s not an exhaustive list of what counts as a dental emergency, below are some of the most common urgent dental problems and what you can do to protect your mouth.

Knocked-Out Tooth

Dental trauma can be serious. The most severe of these instances is known as a tooth avulsion—or when a permanent tooth breaks at the root or is knocked out of its socket.

If this happens to you, take the following steps:

While we can’t always save your tooth, we do offer restorative dentistry treatments like implants and bridges to repair a missing tooth.

  • Handle the tooth with care, never touching the delicate root area
  • Gently clean the tooth with milk, saline, or clean water
  • Let it dry and carefully attempt to place it back in the socket
  • Ensure the tooth remains moist in the socket, a glass of milk, or your cheek
  • Seek emergency dental care right away

Cracked Teeth

If you bite down too hard on solid material or receive a blow to your mouth, face, or jaw, you might crack or break your teeth. This can be painful and dangerous. Cracked or broken teeth are vulnerable to bacterial infections.

Cracked teeth are not always visible, and not all cracks indicate an emergency. However, some symptoms should prompt you to seek emergency dental care as soon as possible. These include:

A cracked tooth may need to be removed or require a dental crown. We will find the right treatment plan for you.

  • Increased sensitivity to temperature
  • Pain while biting or chewing
  • Swelling and inflammation
  • Toothaches or jaw pain
  • Visible fractures

Broken or Chipped Teeth

Though your teeth are made of strong enamel, they may break or chip if you slip, fall, or hit your tooth on something hard. Broken or chipped teeth, however, are not always the result of dental trauma. Even grinding your teeth can cause dental injuries like chipped teeth.

As soon as you notice a chipped or broken tooth, seek repair from an emergency dentist. Be sure to take anti-inflammatory medications, use ice or a cold compress to reduce any swelling, and save any pieces of your tooth by keeping them moist and clean.

Loose or Missing Dental Restorations

Dislodged fillings can be painful since the inner layers of your tooth become exposed and sensitive to external stimuli and bacteria. Be sure to seek prompt emergency dental care if you have issues with a dental restoration.

Previously filled cavities can become loose or fall out completely. To prevent further damage to the exposed tooth, get the cavity refilled as soon as possible.

If you can, keep the loose filling or dental restoration. Your dentist may advise you to use temporary dental wax or putty on your tooth until you can repair the cavity.

Broken/Detached Crowns or Implants

Dental crowns are designed to last for 15 years or more. When the crown is compromised, the tooth below becomes highly susceptible to contamination and irritation. Dental implants can last a lifetime and an issue with a broken dental implant is often painful and serious.

If a crown is damaged or an implant breaks or falls out, call us immediately. We can instruct you on home care steps to take before you make it to our office.

It may be possible to re-bond or reattach the existing crown to your tooth, but in some cases, we may need to make a new crown. If your missing crown has led to an infection, you may need a root canal , an extraction , or a dental implant.

Infections & Dental Emergencies

Along with dental trauma, infections, and severe pain are common dental emergencies. Here’s what you need to know.

1 Toothaches

If your teeth hurt on occasion, you probably don’t need to worry. Feeling a little pain after biting into something too hard, getting something stuck in your teeth, or eating something very cold is normal.

If you have constant throbbing pain, swelling around the tooth, or an abnormal headache though, it’s probably time to visit the dentist.

When you visit Smart Dental Care for tooth pain, we will find the cause of the issue and treat your symptoms accordingly.

  • Tooth decay
  • A damaged filling
  • A fractured tooth
  • Abscessed tooth (an infection in the center of the tooth)
  • Infected gums
  • Eruption or impaction of wisdom teeth

2 Pulpitis (Root Canal Infection)

Pulpitis is a severe infection where the inner root of your tooth (called the pulp) becomes inflamed. Infections in the pulp can be highly painful and are accompanied by:

The proper steps moving forward will depend on how quickly you can receive treatment, the severity of the infection, and the condition of your oral health. Treatment may include a root canal, an extraction, or a filling.

  • Discomfort when eating or chewing
  • Sensitivity to hot or cold drinks
  • A foul smell or taste in your mouth
  • The presence of a fever or swelling in lymph nodes

3 Pericoronitis (Gum Pain or Swelling)

Persistent irritation or inflammation in your gums can make it difficult to chew, speak, or enjoy your favorite foods. Pericoronitis, the swelling of gum tissue, occurs when molars or wisdom teeth erupt.

Those who have poor dental hygiene, deal with food or other debris caught in soft tissues, and have other infections are prone to gum infections like gingivitis or periodontitis.

Gum pain and swelling are considered emergencies when accompanied by a fever, intense pain, difficulty chewing or biting, and severe swelling. If it is not an emergency, make sure to schedule a regular appointment with our dentists to see how we can treat your gum irritation.

4 Dental Abscesses

Most often caused by an untreated cavity or dental trauma, a dental abscess is a fluid-filled blister or pustule on the gums surrounding an infected tooth.

To fight against abscesses, your dentist may prescribe antibiotics to fight the infection, drain the blister to relieve pressure, or treat the affected tooth to remove the infection.

At-Home Treatment Tips

You might need to wait a bit before your dentist appointment, and these home remedies can help decrease the pain while you wait. Toothaches, broken teeth, and infections can be painful, but with these tips, your wait for an appointment doesn’t have to be uncomfortable.

Rinse with Warm Salt Water

If you have swollen gums, rinsing with warm salt water can help decrease swelling and provide pain relief. Saltwater is antibacterial, so it can also help with the infection. Plus, swishing it in your mouth can help release anything stuck in your teeth or gums.

To make warm salt water, mix about one teaspoon with about 8 ounces of warm water. Make sure that the water has cooled before you use it; it should be lukewarm.

Place an Ice Cold Compress

Use Painkillers

  • Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs): These include Advil, Ibuprofen, and Motrin.
  • Naproxen: These include the brand Aleve and other similar generic brands containing anti-inflammatory drugs.

Try Analgesic Gel
