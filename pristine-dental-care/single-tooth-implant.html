Implant Options for One or Several Missing Teeth

The same core technology can be tailored to how many teeth you are missing. During your consultation, Dr. Joseph will help you decide which approach fits your situation:

  • Single tooth implant: one post and one crown fill a single gap without altering the healthy teeth on either side.
  • Implant-supported bridge : a small number of implants anchor a fixed bridge to replace several teeth in a row.
  • Implant dentures : implants secure a full arch so a denture stays firmly in place instead of resting on the gums.

The Implant Placement Process

Replacing one tooth or several follows the same basic path. Here is what you can expect at our office:

  • Exam and planning: Dr. Joseph reviews your teeth, gums, and a 3D scan of your jaw to confirm you have enough healthy bone. If the bone is thin, a bone graft may be recommended first.
  • Implant placement: the post is positioned in the jaw at the planned site, then the area is closed so the bone can bond to it over the following months.
  • Abutment: once the jaw has healed, the abutment is attached to the post.
  • Final restoration: impressions are taken and your custom crown, bridge, or denture is crafted to match your surrounding teeth in shape and color, then secured for a natural result.

Planning Your Treatment with In-Office Cone Beam 3D Imaging

Dr. Joseph now plans every implant case using an in-office cone beam CT scanner. Unlike a flat, two-dimensional X-ray, a cone beam scan captures a detailed three-dimensional view of your jaw in only a few seconds, without sending you elsewhere for imaging. That complete 3D picture lets Dr. Joseph study the exact anatomy of your mouth before any treatment begins.

With this level of detail, we can measure the height, width, and density of your bone, map the position of nerves and sinuses so they are protected during surgery, and pinpoint the ideal location and angle for each implant. Planning the case in three dimensions ahead of time makes the procedure more precise and predictable, and having the scanner on-site means faster planning and fewer outside appointments for you.

How Long Do Implants Last?

With good oral hygiene and regular checkups, the implant post itself is built to last a lifetime. The visible crown, bridge, or denture it supports will experience normal daily wear and usually lasts 10 to 15 years before it may need to be refreshed, while the implant beneath it stays put.

Cost of Dental Implants

The cost of dental implant treatment depends on how many teeth you are replacing and whether any preparatory work, such as a bone graft, is needed. A single implant commonly ranges from around $1,000 to $4,000, and multi-tooth cases are quoted based on your custom plan. Many dental insurance plans cover part of the cost, and our team will walk through your coverage and payment options with you before you decide on anything.
