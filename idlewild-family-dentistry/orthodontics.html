Why Orthodontic Treatment Can Support Oral Health

Orthodontic treatment is often associated with improving the appearance of a smile, but properly aligned teeth may also support everyday oral health and function.

Every orthodontic case is different. The potential benefits, limitations, treatment time, and appropriate method depend on your individual tooth position, bite, oral health, and treatment goals.

  • Easier brushing and flossing when crowded or overlapping teeth are corrected
  • Improved bite function and chewing
  • More balanced contact between upper and lower teeth
  • Reduced areas where plaque may be difficult to remove
  • Reduced uneven wear caused by certain bite problems
  • Improved smile appearance and confidence for some patients

Traditional Braces

Traditional braces use brackets and wires to apply controlled forces that gradually move teeth into planned positions.

Braces may be considered for a variety of orthodontic concerns, including crowding, spacing, crooked teeth, and certain bite problems. Because braces remain attached to the teeth throughout active treatment, they can also be useful when precise or more involved tooth movement is needed.

Braces do require extra attention to brushing, flossing, and food choices because plaque and food particles can collect around brackets and wires.

  • Ability to address many types of tooth movement
  • Continuous treatment without removing an appliance
  • Options for patients who may not be suited to removable aligners
  • Regular professional monitoring throughout treatment

Clear Aligners

Clear aligners use a planned series of removable trays to move teeth gradually. Because the trays are removable and less noticeable than traditional brackets and wires, they can be an appealing option for qualifying teens and adults.

Potential benefits of clear aligners include:

Clear aligners are not appropriate for every orthodontic concern. Successful treatment also depends on wearing the aligners for the amount of time prescribed by your dentist.

  • A less noticeable appearance during treatment
  • Removal for meals
  • Easier access for normal brushing and flossing
  • No traditional brackets or wires

Retainers

After active orthodontic treatment, teeth can gradually shift if they are not supported in their new positions. Retainers are designed to help maintain alignment after braces or clear aligner treatment.

Depending on your needs, a fixed or removable retainer may be recommended. Following your prescribed retainer schedule is an important part of protecting your orthodontic results over time.
