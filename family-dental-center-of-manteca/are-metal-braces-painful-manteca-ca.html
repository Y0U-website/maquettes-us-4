Do metal braces hurt when they are first placed?

The process of placing braces is not painful. Brackets are bonded to the teeth, and wires are attached to begin guiding movement. No drilling or injections are needed. Most patients feel mild soreness later that day or over the next few days as the teeth begin to respond to gentle pressure. This usually feels like tenderness rather than sharp pain. Soft foods, cool drinks, orthodontic wax, and over-the-counter pain relief can help patients stay comfortable during the first adjustment period.

Why do braces feel sore after adjustments?

Braces move teeth by applying steady, controlled pressure. During adjustment visits, wires may be tightened or changed to allow the teeth to continue shifting into better positions. Soreness after these appointments is normal because the teeth and surrounding tissues ll

are adapting to the new pressure. For most patients, this discomfort fades within a few days. Modern braces use improved materials and techniques, so treatment is often easier than patients imagine before beginning care.

Can metal braces irritate my cheeks or lips?

Yes, brackets or wires may rub against the cheeks, lips, or tongue, especially when braces are new. This irritation usually improves as the mouth adjusts. Orthodontic wax can be placed over bothersome brackets to create a smoother surface. Warm salt water rinses may also soothe irritated areas. If a wire feels sharp or loose, patients should contact our office so the team can make a simple adjustment and restore comfort.

Are metal braces more painful than other orthodontic options?

All orthodontic treatments use pressure to move teeth; some soreness can occur with metal braces. Metal braces may feel more noticeable at times because they are often used for moderate to severe crowding, spacing, overbites, underbites, and other bite concerns. However, they are highly effective, affordable, durable, and predictable. Many patients choose traditional braces because they can correct complex alignment problems.

How can I make braces treatment more comfortable?

Patients can reduce discomfort after adjustments by eating softer foods, such as soup, pasta, yogurt, eggs, smoothies, or mashed potatoes. Avoiding very hard, sticky, or crunchy foods also helps protect brackets and wires. Good brushing and flossing are important because healthy teeth and gums respond better during treatment. Regular adjustment visits allow the team to monitor progress, prevent avoidable problems, and keep treatment moving as smoothly as possible.
