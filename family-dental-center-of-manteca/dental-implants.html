As a dentist at Family Dental Center of Manteca, I’m happy to shed some light on dental implants and their function. Think of dental implants as artificial tooth roots, which are similar to screws in shape. These implants, when placed in your jawbone, bond with your natural bone and provide a strong base to support one or more artificial teeth, also known as crowns.

Getting implants is a process that requires multiple visits spanning several months. The first step is the surgical placement of the implant into the jawbone. Over the following months, the implant and the bone bond together to form a solid anchor for your artificial tooth. During this healing period, you can wear a temporary tooth replacement over the implant sites.

Once the implant has bonded with the jawbone, a small connector post, or an abutment, is attached to the implant to hold the new tooth securely. To create your new tooth or teeth, we take impressions of your teeth and create a model of your bite. The new tooth or teeth are designed based on this model. A replacement tooth, known as a crown, is then attached to the abutment.

Dental implants don’t just look and feel like natural teeth, but they function like them as well. With your implants securely in place, you can eat, speak, and smile with confidence. They also help to maintain your facial structure by preventing bone loss in the area of missing teeth.

What happens if I don’t replace my missing tooth?

Leaving a gap untreated can lead to serious issues over time, including:

Implants respond to these effects by preserving bone and maintaining proper dental alignment.

  • Bone loss : The jawbone beneath a missing tooth collapses without stimulation, altering facial structure.
  • Teeth shifting : Adjacent teeth can drift into the space, causing bite misalignment.
  • Functional decline : Chewing efficiency diminishes, which may strain other teeth and lead to digestive problems.

How long is the recovery time after getting implants?

Recovery isn’t a single event—it unfolds in stages:

Most patients resume normal activities within a day or two and enjoy fully functional results after 3–6 months.

  • Surgery day : Expect mild discomfort or swelling, peaking within 48 hours.
  • Initial healing : 7–14 days for soft tissue recovery.
  • Osseointegration : The implant fuses with the jawbone over 3–6 months.
  • Restoration placement : Once integration is confirmed, the final crown or prosthetic is affixed.

How safe and predictable is the dental implant procedure?

Dental implant therapy is considered a highly successful, evidence-based treatment:

  • Safety protocols : Our team employs sterile surgical techniques and CBCT (3D) imaging to plan precise implant placement, avoiding critical anatomical structures and minimizing risk.
  • Experience matters : The high success rate reflects meticulous planning, surgical skill, and well-controlled aftercare.

How do dental implants compare to dentures or bridges?

Although upfront costs are higher, implants often offer greater long-term value.

  • Longevity : Implants can last decades with good oral hygiene, while bridges and dentures may need replacement every 7–10 years.
  • Bone preservation : Implants stimulate the jawbone, preventing resorption—unlike dentures and traditional bridges.
  • Stability & comfort : Implants feel and function like natural teeth, avoiding slippage or adhesives required with dentures.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, implants can last 25+ years—and often, a lifetime. Key elements that contribute to promoting longevity include:

  • Excellent oral hygiene : Daily brushing and flossing around the implant and prosthetic.
  • Regular check-ups : 6-month cleanings and exams allow early detection of issues like peri-implantitis.
  • Lifestyle habits : Avoiding tobacco and addressing bruxism (teeth grinding) with a nightguard all support success.

Why are implant-supported dentures better than conventional dentures?

Stability changes everything. Traditional dentures rest on the gums and can shift, slip, or require adhesives. Implant-supported dentures are anchored directly into the jawbone with titanium posts. That means no movement. No messy adhesives. No fear of slipping while speaking or eating. Dental implants also help preserve jawbone density, which conventional dentures cannot.

Will I eat everything I want?

In most cases, yes. Implant-supported teeth restore your chewing power to nearly 100 %, allowing you to enjoy most of your favorite foods. That means you can enjoy steak, apples, and crunchy vegetables that are often difficult with traditional dentures. While implants don’t have any food restrictions per se, it’s essential to reduce the intake of hard items like ice.
