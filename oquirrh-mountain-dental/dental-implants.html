Dental Implants

  • They look and feel natural. The crown is color-matched to blend with your existing teeth, and most patients forget they even have one.
  • They protect your jawbone. When a tooth is missing , the underlying bone begins to deteriorate. An implant stimulates the bone the same way a natural root does.
  • They do not affect neighboring teeth. Bridges require grinding down adjacent healthy teeth. Implants stand completely on their own.
  • They support overall oral health. A complete smile is easier to clean and maintain, reducing your risk of gum disease and other complications.

What to Expect During the Implant Process

The dental implant process is completed over several months, and each step is carefully planned to ensure long-term success.

  • Placement: During your first procedure, a small incision is made in the gum and the titanium implant is inserted into the jawbone. Multiple implants can be placed at the same appointment when needed. The gum tissue is then sutured and allowed to heal.
  • Osseointegration: Over the next three to six months, the jawbone gradually fuses with the implant in a process called osseointegration. This creates the stable foundation your restoration depends on. During this period, temporary crowns can be placed so you can eat, speak, and smile normally.
  • Restoration: Once healing is complete, an abutment is attached to the implant. This small connector piece serves as the base for your permanent crown. An impression is taken, and your custom restoration is created to match your smile precisely. When it is ready, the crown is attached and your new tooth is complete.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

Are dental implants painful?

Most patients experience minimal discomfort during placement thanks to local anesthesia. Post-procedure soreness is typically manageable with over-the-counter pain relief and resolves within a few days.

How long does the entire process take?

The full treatment, from placement to final restoration, generally takes three to six months. Much of that time is healing rather than active treatment.

Does insurance cover dental implants?

Coverage varies by plan. Our team is happy to help you review your benefits and explore financing options so cost does not stand in the way of your care.

Am I a good candidate for implants?

Most adults in good general health who have sufficient jawbone density are candidates. Dr. Enslow or Dr. Nelson will evaluate your situation during a consultation and recommend the best path forward.
