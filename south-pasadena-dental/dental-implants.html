What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are modern tooth-replacement foundations made from medical-grade titanium. Instead of sitting on top of the gums like traditional dentures, the implant post is placed directly into the jawbone, where it naturally bonds with the surrounding bone.

  • Implant Post : The titanium root is placed into the bone.
  • Abutment : The connector that supports the restoration.
  • Crown : The naturally looking tooth designed to blend with the rest of your natural teeth.

Advantages of Choosing Dental Implants

Dental implants offer a modern tooth-replacement option. They offer strength, comfort, and aesthetic benefits that removable dentures or dental bridges cannot.

Teeth implants are anchored directly into the bone, giving them the stability of a natural tooth. This lets you bite and chew without the shifting that happens with removable restorative options. Most patients return to enjoying a full variety of foods comfortably.

Missing teeth lead to gradual bone loss. A tooth implant stimulates the bone and helps keep it healthy, which also maintains your facial structure.

Each dental implant has a custom crown that matches your other teeth. Maintenance is simple as there are no special cleaning aides or adhesives. You brush and floss, just like you do with your natural teeth, and preserve a healthy, natural-looking smile.

Dental implants are a significant investment because they last for many years. With routine maintenance, they can last a lifetime. Fewer replacements or repairs mean a healthy and more affordable choice in the long run.

Who Can Consider Dental Implants?

Dental implants offer a lasting solution for those who have lost their teeth. At South Pasadena Dental, Dr. Tsibel examines your oral health to ensure your eligibility for dental implants.

Dental implants are suitable in situations where:

  • The surrounding teeth are at risk of moving due to missing teeth, which affects the bite and alignment.
  • Traditional dentures are loose, uncomfortable, or irritating your gums, and a more secure dental restorative solution is required.
  • A tooth beyond repair due to the damage, decay, or an infected root has to be replaced entirely.
  • You have one or more missing teeth that affect your confidence and appearance, even if chewing function hasn’t been significantly compromised.
  • Bone loss is a concern, as dental implants help preserve the jawbone and facial contours.
  • You experience difficulty eating or speaking due to missing or loose teeth, which tooth implants can restore.

Dental Implant Journey: Four Steps to Restoring Your Smile

A dental implant placement follows a simple four-step process to keep you comfortable and give you a natural-looking result. Our dental implant process involves:

We check your mouth, review your medical history, and discuss your ideal smile. A 3D CBCT scan evaluates your bone and determines whether it is sufficient to support a dental implant. That way, we can make our treatment plan for you.

During the procedure, a titanium post is carefully placed into your jawbone under local anesthesia for your comfort. A small incision is made in the gum tissue to allow precise placement of the post, which is then closed to begin the healing process.

Recovery time takes three to six months. During this time, the jawbone fuses with the tooth implant, a process called osseointegration. It provides strength for chewing and bone health in the area where the tooth root originally was.

An abutment will be attached after the initial healing. From there, impressions will be made for your custom crown, bridge, or denture. Once fitted, your brand-new restoration completes your smile.

Specialty Dental Implant Services and Treatments

Single Tooth Replacement

This procedure restores one missing tooth without affecting the health of adjacent teeth. A single titanium post is placed in the bone to serve as the new root. After healing, a porcelain restoration is attached, creating a permanent, lifelike tooth.

All-on-4 Dental Implants

All-on-4 dental implants is an innovative technique that replaces an entire arch of teeth using just four strategically positioned teeth implants. The back dental implants are angled to maximize contact with existing bone, which often eliminates the need for bone grafting. This approach allows for a fixed, full-arch prosthesis to be attached on the same day as implant placement in many cases. Patients appreciate the efficiency of this treatment and the ability to leave with functional teeth.

All-on-X Dental Implants

All-on-X dental implants provides a customized solution for full arch restoration, using anywhere from four to eight implants depending on your jaw structure and bone density. Our dentist evaluates your specific anatomy to determine the ideal number and placement of dental implants needed for long-term success. This flexibility ensures that each patient receives a personalized treatment plan designed to provide maximum stability and durability for their new teeth.

Implant-Supported Dentures or Overdentures

For patients missing most or all of their teeth, implant-supported dentures offer enhanced stability and comfort. These dentures are anchored by a small number of strategically placed dental implants, preventing the slipping, shifting, and irritation common with traditional dentures. This secure foundation restores confidence in everyday activities like eating and speaking, providing a more natural feel and improved quality of life.

Preparatory Procedures for Dental Implant Success

The jawbone may require strengthening to ensure the long-term stability of dental implant posts. Our dental practice uses state-of-the-art tools and utilize safe procedures to create the necessary, stable foundation for your new smile.

Bone Strengthening Techniques

  • Bone Augmentation : Add bone material (synthetic, donor, or self) into weak or thin spaces. This activity induces natural bone fusion, establishing a solid foundation for the tooth implant.
  • Sinus Membrane Elevation : Mostly required for teeth implants in the upper back area. This slightly elevates the sinus membrane for vertical space. This space allows for bone grafting and the insertion of longer, more stable dental implants.
  • Ridge Reshaping : If the jawbone is too narrow, this widens the ridge. Graft material is added to ensure sufficient width and volume to securely hold the dental implant.

Advanced Healing Treatments

  • Guided Bone Regeneration (GBR) : A barrier membrane is placed over the bone graft to protect it. This isolates the area, allowing slower-growing bone cells to mature properly and form a strong anchor.
  • Concentrated Healing Factors (PRF/PRP) : A small blood sample is processed to isolate growth factors. Applied to the site, it accelerates healing, promotes bone integration, and helps reduce swelling and recovery time.

Post-Procedure Care for Your Tooth Implant

Proper care after dental implant placement ensures successful integration and a faster recovery. Following these simple guidelines helps keep your new tooth implant healthy and stable in the long term.

  • Apply a cold compress for 15-minute intervals to minimize swelling and discomfort.
  • For the first week, consume only soft, cool foods. Do not chew on the side where the surgical site is.
  • Avoid smoking for at least 72 hours, as it severely hinders the healing process.
  • Do not use straws and do not spit to preserve the blood clot.
  • Avoid touching or disturbing the surgical area. After 24 hours, you may gently resume brushing your other teeth.
  • Begin warm saltwater rinses 24 hours post-surgery to keep the area clean.
  • Take all prescribed drugs as directed by our dentist.
  • Attend all follow-up dental appointments regarding implant integration.

Daily and Professional Care for Teeth Implants

For the success of restored smiles, long-term consistency becomes the order of the day. Our team offers expert advice and professional cleaning for the longevity of your teeth implants.

Follow these simple, effective steps every day to protect your dental implants:

  • Gently brush all surfaces with a soft toothbrush, using a non-abrasive toothpaste twice daily.
  • Floss daily with either SuperFloss, interdental brushes, or a water flosser to clean under the crown and between teeth.
  • Gurgle with an alcohol-free antibacterial mouthwash to reduce bacteria and maintain fresh breath.
  • Avoid chewing on very hard foods, ice, candies or chewing gum to prevent damage to the crown or the implant.
  • Never use your teeth to open packages or bite objects.
  • Consider using a nylon-coated instrument rather than metal picks for cleaning near the dental implant.
  • Visit our dental office every 3 to 6 months for routine maintenance, as recommended by our dentist.

What to Do If Your Tooth Implant Fails

Even though dental implant failure is rare, a prompt response is important when issues arise. The following are the steps to take:

  • Watch for persistent pain or swelling around the dental implant site long after the initial healing period.
  • Notice if the crown or tooth implant feels loose or shows any unexpected mobility. This is an emergency.
  • Contact us immediately if you notice any concerning symptoms so we can address them promptly.
  • Do not chew food on the affected side to avoid placing pressure on the compromised titanium post.
  • Never try to move, adjust, or tighten the dental implant or its crown yourself.
  • Treatment involves antibiotics, deep cleaning, or bone grafting, depending on the diagnosis.
  • If removal is necessary, we will create a custom plan for healing and future replacement.

What Factors Determine the Total Cost of Dental Implants?

  • Treatment Complexity : Replacing one tooth is simpler and less expensive than multiple dental implants for a bridge or a full mouth restoration.
  • Component Quality : The three main parts of the tooth implant are priced based on the quality and specialized nature of the materials used, ensuring long-term success.
  • Need for Pre-Treatment : Sometimes the jawbone must be prepared before dental implant placement. Procedures such as bone grafting or a sinus lift for upper-jaw implants increase the total cost and treatment time.
