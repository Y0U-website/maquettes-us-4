Gingivitis is the first stage of gum disease. It’s mild and reversible with good brushing, flossing, and professional care. Periodontitis is the next stage. It damages the bone and tissue that hold your teeth in place.

Our periodontal treatment team at McGregor Dental in Fort Myers, FL checks for these signs at every visit and recommends scaling and root planing when pockets get too deep for a regular cleaning to reach.

  • Gum pockets deeper than 4 millimeters
  • Gums pulling away from your teeth
  • Bad breath that toothpaste won’t fix
  • Loose teeth or a change in your bite

Regular Cleaning vs. Scaling and Root Planing vs. Periodontal Maintenance

These three treatments sound alike, but they solve different problems. A regular cleaning removes surface buildup. Scaling and root planing removes buildup below the gumline. Periodontal maintenance keeps your gums stable after treatment.

McGregor Dental in Fort Myers, FL walks you through which one fits your gum health during your exam.

The Deep Cleaning Teeth Procedure Step by Step

The deep cleaning teeth procedure happens in two parts. Your dentist treats one section of your mouth at a time, called a quadrant.

Your hygienist removes plaque, tartar, and bacteria from your teeth and roots. Most offices, including McGregor Dental, use an ultrasonic scaler. This tool uses vibration and water to break up hardened deposits. A hand scaler removes any spots the ultrasonic tool misses.

Once your roots are clean, your dentist smooths out rough spots. Smooth roots give bacteria fewer places to hide. This step also helps your gum tissue reattach to your tooth.

Some Fort Myers, FL patients ask about laser-assisted scaling and root planing. A laser can target infected tissue with less bleeding than hand instruments alone. McGregor Dental will tell you if this option fits your case during your exam.

Is Scaling and Root Planing Painful?

Most patients feel pressure, not pain, during scaling and root planing. Your dentist numbs the treatment area first with a local anesthetic.

McGregor Dental in Fort Myers, FL typically treats one quadrant per visit. This keeps your whole mouth from going numb at once, so you can eat and talk normally after you leave. Some patients ask for numbing gel before the injection. This step makes the process more comfortable.

What to Expect After Your Deep Cleaning

Most patients return to work or normal activities the same day. Expect mild gum tenderness for one to two days.

Call McGregor Dental in Fort Myers, FL if bleeding doesn’t stop or swelling gets worse.

  • Rinse with an antimicrobial mouthwash as directed
  • Stick to soft foods for 24 hours
  • Brush gently around treated areas
  • Skip hot or spicy foods until sensitivity fades

What Happens If You Skip Scaling and Root Planing

Untreated gum disease gets worse, not better. Bacteria continue to eat away at the bone that holds your teeth. Left alone, periodontitis leads to loose teeth, receding gums, and eventual tooth loss. Waiting also raises your odds of needing more visits or a referral to a periodontist for advanced care. Early treatment at McGregor Dental in Fort Myers, FL stops this damage before it reaches that point.

Scaling and root planing carries few risks. Some patients notice mild bleeding, gum tenderness, or tooth sensitivity for a few days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SRP dental treatment the same as a regular cleaning?

No. A regular cleaning cleans above the gumline. SRP dental treatment cleans below it, where regular tools can’t reach.

How many visits does scaling and root planing take?

Most patients need two to four visits. McGregor Dental in Fort Myers, FL treats one quadrant per appointment.

Does dental insurance cover scaling and root planing?

Many plans cover part of the cost under codes D4341 and D4342. McGregor Dental checks your benefits before treatment.

How often will I need scaling and root planing?

Most patients need it once per affected area. After treatment, periodontal maintenance every three to four months keeps your gums healthy.

Can scaling and root planing reverse gum disease?

It manages periodontitis and stops it from getting worse. Gingivitis, the earlier stage, often improves with treatment and good home care.
