How Orthodontic Treatment Works

Orthodontic tooth movement relies on precise, light forces applied over months to guide teeth into new positions. Here is a step-by-step overview of the process patients often experience:

  • Initial consultation and evaluation that reviews goals, bite concerns, and health history.
  • Records and planning with photos, digital scans or impressions, and X-rays to map tooth movement.
  • Active treatment using braces or aligners with small adjustments to guide progress.
  • Periodic checkups to monitor movement, change wires or trays, and support hygiene.
  • Retention phase with custom retainers to help new positions stabilize long term.

Your Orthodontic Experience

Most orthodontic plans range from about twelve to twenty-four months, though minor corrections may finish sooner and complex cases can take longer. The first few days after starting or adjusting braces may bring tenderness, which typically improves with time and over-the-counter comfort options. Clear aligners bring similar short adjustment periods at the start of each new tray.

Daily care plays a major role in success. Brushing after meals, flossing with threaders or interdental cleaners, and using fluoride toothpaste help protect enamel. With braces, avoiding very hard, sticky, or chewy foods can reduce breakage and keep treatment on schedule. Aligners should be worn as directed, usually twenty to twenty-two hours per day, and removed only for eating, drinking anything other than water, and cleaning.

After active treatment, retainers keep teeth from drifting. Consistent retainer wear is essential because teeth can shift throughout life. Your plan will explain how long to wear retainers and how to care for them.
