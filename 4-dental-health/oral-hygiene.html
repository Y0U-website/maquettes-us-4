Oral Hygiene

  • Gum disease prevention with healthier, less inflamed gums.
  • Fresher breath by reducing odor-causing bacteria.
  • Early detection of issues before they become painful or costly.
  • Brighter teeth by polishing surface stains during cleanings.
  • Stronger enamel with the benefits of professional fluoride treatments.

How Professional Cleanings Work

Health Review And Exam

A visit typically starts with a brief medical and dental review, followed by an exam to identify areas of concern.

Plaque And Tartar Removal

The dental hygienist removes soft plaque and hardened tartar from tooth surfaces and along the gumline.

Polishing, Flossing, And Fluoride

Teeth are polished to smooth surfaces and lift minor stains, then flossed to clear debris. Many patients receive a fluoride treatment to help remineralize enamel.

What to Expect

Most cleanings are comfortable and take about an hour. Mild sensitivity can happen if gums are tender, but this usually improves as oral hygiene habits strengthen.

How often to get dental cleanings depends on your risk level. Many adults benefit from visits every six months. People with gum disease or dry mouth may need more frequent appointments.

Daily Home Care Tips

  • Brush twice a day for two minutes with a soft-bristled brush and fluoride toothpaste.
  • Floss once daily or use interdental brushes to remove plaque between teeth.
  • Angle the brush at 45 degrees to the gumline and use short, gentle strokes.
  • Limit frequent snacking and sip water to reduce acid exposure.
  • Consider a fluoride or alcohol-free mouthrinse if recommended at your visit.
  • Replace your toothbrush or brush head every three months or sooner if worn.

Did You Know?

Consistent oral hygiene habits at home, combined with professional care, form the foundation of lifelong dental health.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most people do well with cleanings every six months. Higher-risk patients, including those with gum disease or dry mouth, may need visits every three to four months.

Plaque is a soft, sticky film of bacteria that forms daily. Tartar is hardened plaque that only a dental professional can remove during a cleaning.

A cleaning removes surface stains and can make teeth look brighter. Deeper whitening usually requires a dedicated whitening treatment.

Many studies show electric brushes remove more plaque, especially along the gumline. A manual brush can work well if used with proper technique for two minutes.

Fluoride helps remineralize enamel and lowers cavity risk. It is considered safe at recommended levels in toothpaste and professional treatments.

Healthy gums lower inflammation in the mouth, which may support overall wellness. Consistent oral care complements medical care for better health outcomes.
