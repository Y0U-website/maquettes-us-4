What Are Dental Implants?

Your natural teeth are made up of two parts: the crown, which is the white part you can see when you smile, and the roots, which are hidden underneath the gumline. Traditional tooth replacement options only replace the crowns of missing teeth, which leaves a lot to be desired in terms of stability. Dental implants, on the other hand, consist of three parts to replace the entire tooth: an implant post, an abutment, and a final restoration.

The small titanium implant post is inserted directly into the jawbone to mimic the roots of the missing tooth. Once there, it bonds with the surrounding bone tissue to form a strong foundation for the final restoration on top of it. This custom-made crown, bridge, or denture is secured in place via the abutment and crafted out of high-quality materials to look just like a natural tooth or teeth. Rebuilding a tooth from the roots up like this gives you a smile that looks, feels, and functions just like natural!

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Dental implants are considered the gold standard of tooth replacement for a good reason. Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The first step on your journey toward a complete and fully functioning smile is a consultation with our dentist. During this appointment, they will conduct an oral examination, review your dental health and medical history, ask about your goals to determine whether this treatment is right for you, and make sure you understand how dental implants work in Coppell. Based on the findings, they may need to perform some preliminary procedures (gum disease therapy, tooth extractions, bone grafting) to prepare your mouth.

Dental Implant Surgery

Our dentist can complete the entire dental implant process in-house to ensure a top-notch result for your smile. In preparation for implant placement, they numb the area to ensure you’re completely comfortable during the procedure. Then, they will make small incisions in the gums, insert the implants at precise angles and locations in the jawbone, and suture your gums shut. Afterward, they will place a protective cap over the posts to keep your mouth safe.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

After approximately 3-6 months, the dental implant will integrate with your jawbone via a natural process called osseointegration. Once that step is complete, you’ll return to the office for a second smaller surgery to place the abutments on top of the posts. While you heal, your final restorations will be made using impressions taken of your mouth.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

A trusted dental laboratory will craft each dental implant restoration so it fits perfectly inside your mouth. When it’s ready, we’ll call you back to the office and attach your crown, bridge, or denture to your abutments. Your implant dentist in Coppell will make any final adjustments to ensure your new smile is exactly what you wanted. You’ll be ready to enjoy your newly rebuilt grin that same day!

Dental implants can have a positive impact on your health. Here are some of the ways that your body will benefit:

Advanced Dental Implant Procedures

Dental implants are an excellent solution to replace one, multiple, or all your missing teeth. They have the potential to last for a lifetime, but their long-term success relies on having a strong foundation. As a result, you may need advanced procedures before undergoing your placement surgery, like a bone graft in Coppell. Although this will add another step to your treatment plan, it’s time well spent to ensure your new smile has the framework it needs to thrive.

Bone Grafting

Bone loss and missing teeth often go hand-in-hand. After you lose a tooth, your underlying jawbone is not being stimulated by the root. As a result, you’ll slowly lose bone density. In some cases, bone loss can be a result of advanced gum disease. No matter the reason, if you don’t have sufficient bone, your jaw won’t be able to support the implant posts.

Bone grafting will add thickness to your jaw to accommodate the dental implants. A small piece of bone is taken from another area in your body or from a donor and surgically grafted onto a specific location of your jaw. Over the next several weeks, the graft will integrate with your jaw to strengthen your bone to support your dental implants for many years to come with the right aftercare.

Sinus Lifts

Your maxillary sinuses are located on either side of your nose above your molars. Sometimes, there may not be enough space between your upper jawbone and the sinus cavity. Inserting an implant would cause the post to protrude into the sinus cavity, leading to the failure of your procedure. A sinus lift in Coppell can solve this problem.

A sinus lift increases the space between your bone and the sinus cavities to make you a suitable candidate for dental implants. A small oval is cut into your bone to allow the sinus cavity to be lifted upward. A bone grafting material is inserted into the hole to hold your sinus in place. Over the next several weeks, your jawbone will fuse with the graft. After adequate healing has occurred, you can be scheduled for your implant placement surgery.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

It’s always important that you give yourself a few days to rest after surgery. This is why we recommend you take one to two days off from work or even three or four if you’re having multiple teeth replaced.

The anesthesia you receive will not wear off immediately, so you’ll need someone to escort you home. Once you are no longer experiencing the effects of anesthesia, you can expect to feel some soreness, so taking an over-the-counter pain reliever is recommended. Also, if your implant dentist in Coppell prescribed any medications, make sure to take them as instructed.

If you smoke, do not engage in this activity for at least 24 hours. It is strongly advised, however, that you try to quit before receiving dental implants, as it can disrupt the healing process and make osseointegration difficult, if not impossible.

Common Side Effects

Once your dental implant surgery is complete, it’s normal to feel slight discomfort and soreness around the implant site. You may experience swelling, bruising, and minor pain or bleeding, but these symptoms are regular. In fact, they can usually be managed with pain medication that we prescribe, and we may also give you antibiotics to fight off infection.

Diet

We use self-dissolving stitches so you won’t have to worry about taking them out, but you should follow a strict soft-food diet after surgery. And, when you have to chew, be sure to do so on the opposite side of your mouth. In the first few months of recovery, you’ll need to stick to the following types of food:

  • Pasta
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Yogurt
  • Ice cream
  • Pudding
  • Warm soups
  • Mashed potatoes

Health & Oral Hygiene

The day after your oral surgery, you are free to brush your teeth like normal, only you’ll need to be careful when cleaning around your surgical sites. The same can be said for flossing. Instead of using mouthwash that contains alcohol, rinse with warm salt water two to three times a day. This flushes out any potentially harmful bacteria and debris.

Maintaining a healthy smile is what will help to keep your new teeth thriving in the coming years.
