About Full Mouth Dental Implants

Full mouth implants support fixed dentures for replacing all your teeth when they go missing. The implant itself is a titanium or ceramic screw that functions like a tooth root. Typically, four or six implants support an upper or lower teeth arch. After placing the implants inside your jawbone surgically, Dr. Richard will wait three to six months for them to fuse with surrounding bone tissue before attaching your new teeth.

Full mouth restorations offer a permanent solution to replacing lost teeth as they’re supported by artificial tooth roots that have integrated with your jaw. Implant surgery has a 95 % success rate in replacing teeth that have gone missing. Here’s what you can expect from implant dentures:

  • Esthetics: Porcelain restorations closely resemble the color and translucency of natural teeth, giving your smile an authentic-looking appearance. Dr. Richard can create custom dentures that restore your smile’s original form and beauty.
  • Functionality: With implant dentures, biting and chewing will come naturally as artificial tooth roots anchor your teeth in place. You’ll be able to eat a balanced diet that includes crunchy, crispy, and chewy foods. By improving your chewing, implant dentures make it easier to digest your food to get more nutritional value from your meals.
  • Strong jawbone: Tooth loss often leads to bone loss and a sunken jaw, as there are no tooth roots to stimulate bone growth in your jaw. Implants function like tooth roots to prevent bone loss and keep your jawbone healthy and strong.
  • Easy maintenance and care: Unlike traditional removable dentures, which need special care, implant dentures can be brushed and flossed just like real teeth. Brushing, flossing, eating a healthy diet, and doing routine dental cleanings will keep your new teeth in good health in the long term.
  • Permanency: Implant-supported dentures are designed for long-term use. With proper care, the titanium implants can last a lifetime, as they have firmly integrated with the surrounding bone tissue. Your porcelain dentures can have a lifespan of 20+ years. Together, they give you a strong, pleasing, and enduring smile.
