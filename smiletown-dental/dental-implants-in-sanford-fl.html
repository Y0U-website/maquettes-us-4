Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants in Sanford

The complete dental implant process typically takes between 3 to 6 months from start to finish, though the timeline can vary based on your individual needs and treatment plan. Here’s what to expect:

Initial Consultation & Planning (1-2 weeks): Your first visit includes comprehensive examination, 3D imaging, and treatment planning.

Implant Placement Surgery (1 day): The surgical procedure to place the implant usually takes 1-2 hours per implant. Most patients experience initial healing within 7-10 days.

Osseointegration Phase (3-6 months): This is the most important phase where your jawbone fuses with the titanium implant. While this takes several months, you won’t need frequent office visits—your body does the healing work naturally.

Abutment Placement (1-2 weeks): After complete integration, we attach the abutment connector piece. Minor healing time is needed before the final step.

Final Restoration (2-3 weeks): Your custom crown is fabricated and permanently attached, completing your new smile.

If you require additional procedures like tooth extraction or bone grafting, the timeline may extend by 3-6 months to allow for proper healing before implant placement. While the process requires patience, the end result is a permanent, natural-looking tooth that’s worth the wait.

Most adults who are missing one or more teeth are excellent candidates for dental implants. The ideal candidate meets these criteria:

Age Requirements: You should be over 18 years old with a fully developed jawbone. There’s no upper age limit—many patients in their 70s and 80s successfully receive implants.

Adequate Bone Density: Your jawbone needs sufficient volume and density to support the implant. If bone loss has occurred, bone grafting procedures can rebuild the foundation necessary for successful implant placement.

Good Oral Health: Healthy gums free from periodontal disease are essential. Any gum disease must be treated before implant surgery.

Overall Health: You should be in reasonably good general health. Certain conditions like uncontrolled diabetes, active cancer treatment, or severe osteoporosis may require additional evaluation and management before proceeding.

Non-Smoker or Willing to Quit: Smoking significantly increases the risk of implant failure. We strongly recommend quitting or reducing tobacco use before and after implant surgery.

Commitment to Oral Hygiene: Success depends on maintaining excellent oral hygiene and attending regular dental check-ups.

Even if you’ve been told you’re not a candidate in the past due to bone loss or other concerns, modern advances in implant dentistry—including bone grafting, sinus lifts, and mini implants—have made it possible for more people than ever to benefit from dental implants.
