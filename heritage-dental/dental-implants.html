Dental Implants

A new root, not just a new tooth

A dental implant is a small titanium post that’s placed into your jawbone to act as an artificial tooth root. Over a few months the bone fuses to the post — a process called osseointegration — creating a stable foundation. A custom abutment and crown are then attached on top, giving you a replacement that looks, feels, and chews like the real thing.

Because the implant stands on its own, it doesn’t rely on grinding down the healthy teeth next to it the way a traditional bridge does. It’s the option we most often recommend when a single tooth needs replacing and the surrounding teeth are healthy.

Good first step

A consultation and exam. We’ll check the health of your gums and jawbone, talk through your options honestly, and only recommend an implant if it’s genuinely the right fit.

How a dental implant works

Most of the timeline is healing — the active appointments are shorter than people expect.

  • Planning & imaging. We examine your gums and bone and plan exactly where the implant should go.
  • Placement. Under local anesthesia, the titanium post is placed into the jawbone and the gum is closed over it.
  • Healing (osseointegration). Over roughly three to nine months, your jawbone fuses to the post. Initial recovery from the surgery itself usually takes about a week.
  • Abutment & crown. Once healed, a connector (abutment) is attached and a custom crown — color-matched to your teeth — is secured on top.

How implants help your dental health

Implants do more than fill a gap — they protect the structures around the missing tooth.

  • Help preserve jawbone and prevent the bone loss and sunken look that can follow tooth loss.
  • Restore natural chewing strength and clear speech.
  • Stand alone without grinding down the healthy teeth on either side.
  • Can also anchor and stabilize a bridge or denture.

You may be a good candidate if

An exam is the only way to know for sure, but implants tend to be a good fit when:

  • You have enough healthy jawbone and healthy gums to support the post.
  • You’re in good general health and your jaw has finished growing (generally adults).
  • Conditions like active gum disease or uncontrolled diabetes are managed, and ideally you don’t smoke or vape — these affect healing.
