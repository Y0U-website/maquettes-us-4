What is a Dental Crown?

A dental crown is a custom restoration placed over a damaged or weakened tooth. It restores the tooth’s shape, strength, and appearance while helping it function normally during chewing and speaking. Crowns are commonly used after root canal treatment, large fillings, fractures, or advanced wear. They may also support dental implants or improve the appearance of uneven or discolored teeth.

Modern crowns are designed to match surrounding teeth closely, which helps maintain a balanced and natural-looking smile. Teeth tend to appreciate structural support. They rarely file complaints afterward. Our crowns are made from high-quality porcelain or porcelain fused to gold for exceptional strength and a natural appearance. With proper care, they can last 20–30 years—one of the longest-lasting treatments in dentistry.

Types of Dental Crowns

Porcelain crowns provide a natural appearance and blend well with nearby teeth. They are commonly used for visible teeth and cosmetic restorations.

Zirconia crowns offer strong durability and resistance to wear. They work well for molars and patients who place heavier pressure on their bite.

Ceramic crowns provide a balance of aesthetics and function. They are metal-free and suitable for many front and back teeth restorations.

Implant-supported crowns replace missing teeth by attaching securely to a dental implant. This restores bite function while maintaining spacing and alignment.

Signs You May Need a Dental Crown

Crowns cover and protect the visible portion of the tooth while restoring strength and bite support. Treatment also helps prevent additional wear or structural damage over time.

  • A cracked or fractured tooth
  • Severe tooth decay
  • Pain while chewing

  • A tooth weakened after root canal treatment
  • Large failing fillings
  • Tooth discoloration or cosmetic damage

The Crown Process – Two Simple Visits

Visit 1 – Preparing the Tooth

  • We remove any decay and shape the tooth for the crown.
  • An impression is taken to create a custom-fit crown.
  • A temporary crown is placed to protect your tooth until the final crown is ready. .

Visit 2 – Placing the Crown

  • The temporary crown is removed.
  • The custom crown is fitted, adjusted, and permanently cemented in place.
  • You leave with a tooth that looks beautiful and functions naturally.

How Long Do Dental Crowns Last?

Dental crowns can last many years with proper care and regular dental visits. Longevity depends on several factors, including oral hygiene habits, bite pressure, grinding, and crown material.

Brushing, flossing, and routine examinations help maintain both the crown and the supporting tooth underneath. Teeth operate continuously. Maintenance helps them stay cooperative.
