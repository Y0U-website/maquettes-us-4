How Scaling and Root Planing Works

The procedure is divided into two parts. Scaling is the careful removal of plaque and hardened tartar from the tooth surface, including the areas below the gumline where bacteria collect. Root planing smooths out the surfaces of the tooth roots, which makes it harder for bacteria to reattach and helps your gums heal and reconnect to the teeth.

Depending on the extent of the buildup and the condition of your gums, the treatment may be done in one or two visits. Local anesthesia is used to keep you comfortable throughout the process. Most patients experience mild tenderness for a day or two afterward, but this is typically easy to manage with over-the-counter pain relievers.

After the procedure, we will set up follow-up visits to monitor your healing and recommend a periodontal maintenance schedule to keep your gums healthy going forward.

  • Stops the progression of gum disease at its early stages
  • Reduces gum inflammation, bleeding, and sensitivity
  • Eliminates the bacteria that cause bad breath and infection
  • Helps preserve your natural teeth by protecting their supporting structures
  • Creates a healthier foundation for ongoing preventive care

Tips

Caring for Your Gums After Deep Cleaning

After scaling and root planing, your gums need time to heal. A few simple steps will help you recover comfortably and get the best results from your treatment.

With good habits and consistent maintenance visits, many patients see dramatic improvements in gum health within just a few weeks.

  • Brush gently twice a day and floss daily, being careful around the treated areas.
  • Rinse with warm salt water a few times a day to soothe discomfort and promote healing.
  • Avoid hard, crunchy, or spicy foods for the first day or two after treatment.
  • Stay consistent with your at-home care and keep every follow-up appointment.
  • Schedule regular periodontal maintenance cleanings, typically every three to four months.
