  • Why is Fluoride Important for My Teeth? Fluoride helps to remineralize weakened enamel, making teeth more resistant to decay. It also reduces the activity of bacteria that cause cavities, promoting overall oral health.
  • Who Should Get Fluoride Treatments? Fluoride treatments are beneficial for people of all ages. They are particularly recommended for: Children, as their developing teeth are more susceptible to cavities.
  • Adults with a history of frequent cavities or weakened enamel.
  • Individuals with conditions like dry mouth, gum disease, or those undergoing orthodontic treatment.
  • How is Fluoride Applied? Fluoride is applied as a gel, foam, or varnish directly to the teeth using a brush, tray, or rinse. The procedure is quick, painless, and typically takes only a few minutes.
  • How Often Should I Get Fluoride Treatments? Most dentists recommend fluoride treatments every six months during routine cleanings. However, individuals with a higher risk of tooth decay may benefit from more frequent applications.
  • Is Fluoride Safe? Yes, fluoride is safe when used as directed. Professional treatments contain a controlled amount of fluoride, ensuring its effectiveness while minimizing any risk.

BITE GUARDS

Bite guards, otherwise known as occlusal guards, night guards or mouth guards, are recommended to patients that suffer from teeth grinding or clenching in their sleep. Properly adjusted bite guards can relieve muscle tension that derives from teeth grinding.

Bite guards also act as a great form of protection for those participating in sports activities. This custom fitting oral device protects your teeth, tongue, gums and cheeks from any unexpected trauma that may occur.

TMJ SCREENING

At Smile St. Pete Dentistry, TMJ screenings are an essential part of our comprehensive dental exams. Whether or not you have a TMJ Disorder, Dr. Lowry will be sure to set you up for success in regards to prevention and management.

TMJ is the abbreviated term for the temporomandibular joint. This joint can be described as a hinge that connects your lower jaw to your skull, allowing you to freely move your mouth, talk, yawn, and chew food. When individuals experience problems within the jaw and facial muscles around it, this can lead to temporomandibular joint disorder (TMD).

TMJ FAQ’S

  • What Happens During a TMJ Screening? During the screening, your dentist will: Examine your jaw for pain, tenderness, or restricted movement.
  • Listen for clicking or popping sounds when you open or close your mouth.
  • Assess your bite and look for signs of teeth grinding or clenching.
  • Take X-rays or other imaging, if necessary, to get a closer look at the jaw joint.
  • Why is TMJ Screening Important? MJ disorders can lead to chronic pain, difficulty eating, and other complications if left untreated. Early detection allows for effective treatment and can prevent further damage to your jaw and surrounding structures.
  • How Can TMJ Disorders Be Treated? Treatment options vary based on severity and may include: Lifestyle changes (e.g., stress management, avoiding hard foods)
  • Physical therapy or exercises for the jaw
  • Custom nightguards to prevent teeth grinding
  • Medications for pain or inflammation
  • In severe cases, surgical intervention
  • What Causes TMJ Disorders? TMJ disorders can be caused by several factors, including: Teeth grinding or clenching
  • Jaw injury or trauma
  • Arthritis in the joint
  • Misaligned teeth or bite issues
  • Stress, which can lead to tension in the jaw muscles
  • How Often Should I Get Screened for TMJ Issues? It’s a good idea to include TMJ screening as part of your routine dental check-ups, especially if you have a history of jaw pain or other symptoms.
  • Can TMJ Disorders Affect Other Parts of the Body? Yes, untreated TMJ disorders can lead to headaches, neck and shoulder pain, earaches, and even posture problems due to muscle tension and strain.
