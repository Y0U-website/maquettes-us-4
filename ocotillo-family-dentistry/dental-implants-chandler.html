Dental implant timelines for Chandler patients

Most Chandler implant questions come down to two things: how long , and how many visits . The honest answer depends on whether the tooth is still in your mouth, whether the bone underneath has held its shape, and whether one tooth or a whole arch is being replaced. Here’s how the four most common paths schedule out at our Riggs Rd office.

Path 1 · Working adult, single cracked molar

Single-tooth implant, intact bone — 4 to 5 months

Total chair time: ~3 hours across 3 visits.

  • Visit 1 (60 min): Free 3D CBCT consult, written plan, benefits verified.
  • Visit 2 (~90 min): Extraction (if needed) and implant placement the same day. Most patients drive themselves home.
  • Healing window (3–4 months): Osseointegration — the bone fuses to the titanium post. No restrictions on normal eating after week one.
  • Visit 3 (30 min): Final crown seated. Done.

Single-tooth implant with bone graft — 7 to 10 months

The graft is what makes implants possible on sites where the body has already started reabsorbing bone — usually 1+ years after a tooth was lost.

  • Visit 1: CBCT confirms bone loss at the site, plan and pricing.
  • Visit 2: Graft placed (socket preservation or block graft, depending on volume needed).
  • Healing (4–6 months): Graft integrates and matures. No daily impact on you.
  • Visit 3: Implant placed once the graft is solid.
  • Healing (3–4 months): Osseointegration.
  • Visit 4: Final crown seated.

All-on-4 with same-day temporary teeth — 5 to 6 months

Most All-on-4 patients schedule the surgical day around a long weekend so they can rest at home for 48 hours before easing back to work.

  • Visit 1 (~90 min): CBCT, full-arch planning, written all-in price for both arches if needed.
  • Surgical day (~4–6 hours, with sedation available): Any failing teeth extracted, four implants placed, fixed temporary bridge attached the same day. You leave with teeth — not a gap, not a removable.
  • Healing (4–5 months): Soft diet for the first 8–10 weeks; the temporary bridge stays in place.
  • Final delivery (1–2 short visits): Permanent fixed bridge fabricated and seated.

Implant case across the snowbird season — 2 to 3 AZ visits

If timing slips, we coordinate the final restoration with your summer-home dentist using our CBCT scan and digital impressions — no need to extend your stay. We’ve handled dozens of these handoffs for Sun Lakes patients.

  • October–November (arrival): CBCT consult and any extractions or grafts. The healing window runs through the winter while you’re in AZ.
  • January–February: Implant placement. Healing continues through the spring.
  • March–April (before you leave): Final crown or fixed bridge seated. You head north with the case closed.

How much does a dental implant cost in Chandler?

Most single-tooth implants at our Chandler office land between $3,500 and $4,500 all-in — that’s the implant itself, the abutment, and the final crown. The spread is real and depends on whether you need a bone graft, a sinus lift, or extraction first. The free 3D CBCT consult gives you the exact number for your tooth before you commit a dollar, and we walk through it line-by-line so there are no surprise charges later.

How long does the dental implant process take?

From first consult to final crown, most Chandler implant cases run 4 to 6 months. The implant goes in during a ~90-minute visit, the bone heals around it for 3–4 months (osseointegration — this is what makes the implant last decades), then we seat the final crown in a single 30-minute visit. For straightforward front-tooth cases we can sometimes attach a temporary crown the same day so you never walk around with a gap.

Am I too old for a dental implant?

Almost never — and this is the single most common question we hear from our Sun Lakes patients. Healthy 75- and 85-year-olds heal implants beautifully. What matters far more than age is bone density at the implant site and a short list of medications (certain bisphosphonates, blood thinners, and immunosuppressants). The 3D CBCT scan answers the bone question in one visit, and we coordinate the medication question directly with your physician — no phone-tag for you.

Does insurance or Medicare cover dental implants?

Traditional Medicare does not — but many Medicare Advantage plans now include a dental implant benefit ($1,000–$3,000 typical), and PPO plans usually pay a portion of the crown and abutment even when they exclude the implant fixture itself. We verify your specific plan before the consult so you walk in knowing the out-of-pocket number. FSA and HSA dollars qualify, and CareCredit / Sunbit stretches the balance into a monthly payment most patients barely notice.
