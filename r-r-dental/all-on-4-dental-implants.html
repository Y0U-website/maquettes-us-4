Who is a Good Candidate for All-on-4?

To qualify for All-on-4 implants, you must be missing most or all of your teeth or be a current denture wearer. You also need enough jawbone density to support the implants. For patients lacking sufficient bone density, your dentist can perform a bone graft to rebuild the jaw for dental implant placement.

Smokers are not good candidates. Smoking increases your risk of oral health problems such as gum disease, which could lead to complications and the implants failing. We highly recommend quitting smoking if you’re considering an All-on-4 procedure.

The best way to know if All-on-4 is right for you is through a consultation. Your dentist will evaluate your oral health, bone support, medical history, treatment goals, and comfort needs before making a recommendation.

How the All-on-4 Process Works

Step 1: Consultation and Digital Evaluation

During your first visit, we talk through the current state of your teeth, what you want to improve, and what you’re hoping the final result will feel and look like. We examine your mouth, review your dental and medical history, and use digital imaging to evaluate your bone, gums, bite, and overall oral health. If you currently have dentures, failing teeth, or older dental work, we assess how those factors affect your treatment plan.

This is also the time to talk about concerns such as dental anxiety, healing, timeline, budget, and maintenance. We want you to leave the consultation with a clear understanding of whether All-on-4 makes sense for you.

Step 2: Personalized Treatment Planning

Once we confirm candidacy, we build a plan around your smile, anatomy, and goals. This is where the details matter. We determine:

Some patients have enough bone for a straightforward path to treatment. Others may need additional planning depending on bone levels, gum health, or the condition of their existing teeth. We’ll design a customized treatment plan to make sure the end result looks natural, feels stable, and functions well over time.

  • Whether one arch or both arches need treatment
  • Whether any teeth need to be removed first
  • How the implants should be positioned for support and stability
  • What your temporary and final restorations may involve
  • What your recovery and follow-up schedule should look like

Step 3: Implant Placement

On the day of treatment, the area is prepared, and implants are placed in carefully selected positions within the jaw. The goal is to provide strong support for a full arch of replacement teeth while efficiently using the available bone.

If damaged or failing teeth are still present, extractions may be completed as part of the process. Once the implants are in place, the next step is supporting the arch with a temporary restoration when appropriate for your case.

This stage is when many patients feel the most nervous, but careful planning, clear communication, and a supportive team go a long way. Our office focuses on making the experience feel organized, calm, and as comfortable as possible.

Step 4: Temporary Teeth and Early Healing

Many patients receive a temporary fixed set of teeth during the early healing phase. The temporary prosthesis will sit on top of the implants while they heal. This means you don’t have to spend time without teeth while the implants are still bonding with the bone.

During healing, your mouth needs time to recover, and your implants need time to stabilize. You will receive instructions on eating, cleaning, and protecting the area. Soft foods are usually recommended early on, and follow-up visits help us monitor healing and make sure everything is progressing as expected.

Step 5: Final Restoration and Long-Term Care

After healing is complete, the final full-arch restoration is placed. Your final teeth will be designed to look natural, feel secure, and help restore confidence when you eat, speak, and smile. We also review how to care for your new smile at home and how often to return for maintenance.

FAQs About All-on-4 Dental Implants

What are All-on-4 dental implants?

All-on-4 is a full-arch tooth replacement treatment that uses four strategically placed dental implants to support a fixed set of replacement teeth. It is designed for people who need to replace most or all of the teeth in one arch.

Who is a good candidate for All-on-4?

Good candidates are often patients with significant tooth loss, loose dentures, or failing teeth who want a more secure long-term option.

Will I have teeth while I heal?

Patients receive a temporary fixed set of teeth during the healing phase. You will wear the temporary teeth for three to six months before the permanent bridge is added.

Is All-on-4 painful?

Dental implant placement is less invasive than other procedures and typically includes sedation. You will also take anti-inflammatory medication before surgery to help prevent swelling and discomfort. You may have soreness during recovery, but careful planning, clear aftercare instructions, and a supportive team help make the experience easier.

How long do All-on-4 dental implants last?

Dental implants have an approximate lifespan of 25+ years. Longevity depends on factors such as daily home care, overall health, bite forces, regular dental visits, and the condition of the restoration over time.

How much do All-on-4 dental implants cost in Hicksville?

All-on-4 costs approximately $28,000 per jawline, but costs vary depending on your specific case. Factors that impact cost include the number of arches being treated, whether teeth need to be removed, the complexity of planning, and the materials used for the restoration.

What happens at an All-on-4 consultation?

The dentist evaluates your oral health, jawbone, medical history, current teeth or dentures, and smile goals. From there, R+R Dental can explain whether All-on-4 is a strong fit or whether another implant option may make more sense.

How do I care for All-on-4 implants while I heal?

R+R Dental will give you specific instructions for cleaning, eating, and follow-up visits. Good home care and routine checkups are important for protecting the implants and the restoration.

How do I know if All-on-4 is better than dentures for me?

All-on-4 may be a good fit if you want a fixed option and are missing most or all teeth in an arch. Dentures may still be the right option for some patients. A consultation is the best way to compare comfort, cost, timeline, maintenance, and long-term goals.
