FAQs

Who is a good candidate for All-on-4 implants?

Good candidates are those with significant tooth loss or those struggling with traditional dentures. A healthy jawbone or bone assessment is required to ensure implants will have adequate support for long-term success.

Is the All-on-4 procedure painful?

The All-on-4 procedure is performed with sedation and local anesthesia, ensuring minimal discomfort. Post-surgery discomfort is typically mild and can be managed with prescribed pain relief. Most patients report little to no pain.

How long do All-on-4 implants last?

With proper care, All-on-4 implants are designed to last many years, with most patients enjoying them for over 20 years. Regular dental check-ups and good oral hygiene help maintain the implants’ longevity.

Can All-on-4 implants prevent bone loss?

Yes, dental implants, including All-on-4, stimulate the jawbone just like natural teeth, helping to prevent bone loss that typically occurs with missing teeth. This promotes better long-term oral health and stability.

What is the difference between All-on-4 and dentures?

Unlike removable dentures, All-on-4 implants are fixed, providing stability and long-lasting support. They allow for better chewing function, improved speech, and a more natural appearance without the discomfort or inconvenience of dentures.

How soon after surgery can I eat?

You may be able to eat soft foods within a few days after surgery. Immediate provisional teeth are placed to help restore function, while initial healing may require you to avoid harder or sticky foods for a few weeks.

Do I need bone grafting?

One of the key advantages of All-on-4 is that it often eliminates the need for bone grafting. By using angled implants, the procedure maximizes available bone in the jaw, making it suitable for patients with moderate bone loss.

How long is the recovery for All-on-4?

Recovery time varies by patient, but initial healing takes a few days. Full osseointegration, where the implants bond with the jawbone, can take several months. Most patients can resume normal activities within a week.
