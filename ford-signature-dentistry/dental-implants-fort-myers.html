Three Clear Paths for Replacing Missing Teeth

Implant Crowns

An implant crown replaces one missing tooth without altering the healthy teeth beside it. The implant acts as the root, and the crown is designed to match your surrounding teeth in shape, shade, and proportion. It is the most conservative way to replace a single tooth and keep the rest of your smile untouched.

Implant Bridges

An implant bridge replaces several missing teeth in a row using implants instead of grinding down neighboring teeth for support. It restores stable chewing across the gap and is planned for cleanability, comfort, and a natural appearance. For many patients in Fort Myers and Naples, it is a fixed, long-term alternative to a removable partial.

Full Arch Dental Implants

Full arch dental implants replace an entire upper or lower set of teeth with a fixed restoration anchored on several implants. It is a stable, non-removable answer for failing teeth or a loose denture. Accurate fit across a full arch depends on precise planning, which is where our digital workflow matters most.

Dental implants work best when the whole mouth is planned together, not one gap at a time. The goal is not only to fill a space, but to protect your bite, preserve the teeth you still have, and build a result that stays stable for years.

Replacing Missing Teeth From the Root Up

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth with a small titanium post, which then supports a crown, a bridge, or a full arch of teeth. Because the implant is anchored in the bone, the replacement tooth stays fixed in place and does not shift, slip, or rely on the teeth around it.

A single implant crown may be all that one gap needs. Larger cases may call for an implant bridge or full arch dental implants when several teeth are missing or failing together. Every plan starts with the same question: what will keep this mouth stable for the long-term?

Dental Implant Questions, Answered

What are dental implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that replaces the root of a missing tooth. Once it integrates with the bone, it can support a single crown, a bridge, or a full arch of teeth. Implants are used to replace one tooth, several teeth, or a whole upper or lower set, and they stay fixed in place rather than resting on the gums like a denture.

The right choice depends on how many teeth are missing, your bone support, and your long-term goals, which we review together at a consultation in our Fort Myers office.

What makes full arch dental implants fit accurately?

Full arch accuracy comes from precise planning, not guesswork. Accurate fit is what makes a full arch restoration comfortable and stable over the long-term.

How long do dental implants last?

With good care, the implant post itself can last for decades, and many patients keep theirs for life. The visible crown or bridge on top may eventually need replacement due to normal wear, much like any dental restoration. Longevity depends on your bite, your home care, and keeping up with regular maintenance visits.

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

Most patients who are missing one or more teeth, or who have a tooth that cannot be saved, are candidates for implants. The main factors are the health of your gums, your available bone, and your overall health. A consultation lets us evaluate these and tell you honestly whether an implant is the right option for you.

Does getting dental implants hurt, and what is recovery like?

Most patients are surprised by how manageable the process is. Placement is done with anesthetic so you are comfortable during treatment, and any soreness afterward is usually mild and short-lived. Because the case is planned in advance, the process tends to be smoother and more predictable than patients expect.

What is the difference between an implant crown, an implant bridge, and full arch implants?

An implant crown replaces one missing tooth with a single implant. An implant bridge replaces several teeth in a row using implants at each end instead of relying on natural teeth. Full arch dental implants replace an entire upper or lower set of teeth on several implants. The difference comes down to how many teeth are missing and how they are supported.
