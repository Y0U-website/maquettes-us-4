What Cosmetic Dentistry Actually Means

Cosmetic dentistry improves the appearance of your smile, including color, shape, size, alignment, and how your teeth relate to your face. At a high level, it combines function with aesthetics to create results that look natural, feel comfortable, and hold up over time.

Patients in Fort Myers, Naples, and across Southwest Florida often want more than a whiter smile. They want a transformation designed around their individual face. That level of planning is what separates refined cosmetic dentistry from cosmetic dentistry that looks obvious.

Questions Answered

How much does cosmetic dentistry cost in Fort Myers or Naples?

Cost varies by treatment type, number of teeth, and case complexity. Bonding may be a modest investment, while a full arch of porcelain veneers is a larger commitment. A consultation provides the clearest plan and fee. Financing through Cherry and CareCredit may be available.

How long does cosmetic dentistry last?

Porcelain veneers typically last 10–15 years and can last longer with proper care. Crowns can also last 10–20 years or longer with proper planning and maintenance. Individual results vary. Bonding often needs touch-ups sooner. Whitening longevity depends on diet and habits. Bite stability, material selection, and home care all matter.

Will my cosmetic work look natural?

Natural results depend on planning, material quality, and execution. At Ford Signature Dentistry, tooth shape, facial proportions, color, and gumline balance are considered before treatment begins. The goal is the best version of your smile, not an obvious dental look.

Are veneers better than bonding?

Veneers and bonding solve different problems. Bonding works well for isolated chips, small gaps, and minor shape corrections. Veneers are stronger, more stain resistant, and better for comprehensive changes. Your consultation determines which option makes sense.

How do I know if I need veneers, crowns, or something else?

Veneers are usually for healthy teeth needing cosmetic change. Dental crowns are used when a tooth needs full coverage or structural reinforcement. Many complex cases use both. The decision is based on the condition of each tooth.

Cosmetic Concerns We Address

Chipped or Cracked Teeth

Chips and cracks may be corrected with bonding, veneers, or crowns depending on severity.

Worn or Short Teeth

Teeth worn by grinding or age can be rebuilt with veneers, crowns, or a full occlusal plan.

Small or Undersized Teeth

Teeth that look too small relative to the face can be proportioned with veneers or bonding.

Uneven or Asymmetrical Teeth

Teeth that look uneven in shape, size, or proportion can often be improved with veneers, bonding, or a comprehensive smile design plan.

Tooth Discoloration

Staining that does not respond to whitening may be addressed with veneers or porcelain crowns.

Old or Discolored Dental Work

Outdated crowns, bonding, or mismatched restorations can be replaced as part of a smile makeover.
