Accessibility

These tips are adapted from accessibility guidance provided by the U.S. Social Security Administration.

  • Use keyboard navigation to move through screens and page elements.
  • Increase text size in your browser or device settings.
  • Use screen magnification tools to enlarge page content.
  • Adjust background and text colors for better contrast.
  • Make your mouse pointer larger or more visible, if supported by your device.

Mouse and Keyboard Alternatives

If using a mouse or keyboard is difficult, speech recognition software may help you navigate websites using voice commands. Programs such as Dragon allow some users to move through websites, complete forms, and interact with digital content through spoken instructions.

Support for Deaf or Hard-of-Hearing Visitors

Some website content may include video or audio features. The following settings can help visitors access captions, adjust sound, and improve the media-viewing experience.

Closed Captioning

Closed captioning allows users to view visual transcripts of audio content in videos. If a video is hosted on YouTube or another video platform, caption settings may be available within the video player.

Volume Controls

Most computers, tablets, and mobile devices include volume settings. Video and audio players may also have their own volume controls. Adjusting both your device volume and the media player volume may help improve the listening experience.
