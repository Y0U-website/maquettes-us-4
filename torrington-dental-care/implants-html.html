What Are Dental Implants?

There are two parts of your teeth: the visible crown that sits above the gumline and the hidden root that anchors the tooth into the jawbone. Other tooth replacement options like dental bridges and dentures only replace the crown of missing teeth. Without the root, these restorations tend to leave a lot to be desired in terms of stability and comfort. Dental implants are revolutionary because they replace the entire structure of a missing tooth to mimic it in practically every way.

The 4 Step Dental Implant Process

The dental implant process takes place across multiple treatment stages over the course of several months. That may seem like a long time, but the end result is worth it! Here at Torrington Dental Care, our team of professionals has all the training and expertise necessary to perform the entire treatment right here in our office. We can thereby spare you the hassles of visiting another practice for part of your procedure.

Exactly how dental implants work in Torrington varies from case to case. In general, though, patients must go through four main stages of treatment: consultation, implant placement surgery, osseointegration/abutment placement, and restoration.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

Dental Implant Surgery

Once we have determined that you are a good candidate for dental implants in Torrington, the implants themselves must be surgically placed within the jawbone. Some practices must refer patients to an outside specialist for this portion of the treatment, but our team can handle the surgery right here in our office. This spares you from the inconveniences and uncertainties of dealing with an unfamiliar practice.

Our team will use local anesthesia and possibly sedation to ensure you are comfortable, and then your dentist will make one or more small incisions in the gums to access the jawbone. Then, the implants are inserted into the jaw at pre-determined locations, and the gums are closed around them.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

Once the dental implants are successfully situated, they will need a period of time (typically a few months) to bond with your natural tissue. This step is called osseointegration. It is what enables the implants to serve as prosthetic tooth roots.

Following osseointegration, you may undergo a second minor surgery. The implants are exposed, and healing caps are placed on them. Later, after your gums have had time to heal, abutments are placed. Abutments are small connector pieces that serve to secure implant restorations in place. (Some patients receive abutments at the same time as their initial implant surgery.)

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

Restoration is the final stage of the dental implant process, and it is the one that patients most look forward to. After the abutments are in place, we take detailed impressions of your mouth. Those impressions serve as the basis for designing your custom crown, bridge, or denture. After a laboratory fabricates your restorations, we attach them to your abutments. Once we verify their fit and you are satisfied with their appearance, you can go off and enjoy your beautiful new smile.

Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

Following your dental implant surgery, you will have to go through a recovery period. Do not worry. Below, we will walk you through what you can expect to experience while your mouth is on the mend. We will also provide some practical dental implant post-op instructions in Torrington.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Within a few hours after your surgery, blood clots should form at your surgical sites. They are an important part of your healing process, so you should take care that you do not accidentally dislodge them or prevent them from forming. Here are a few practical things you can do to protect them:

  • Do not spit. Use tissues instead or simply swallow excess saliva.
  • Avoid the use of drinking straws.
  • Do not smoke.
  • Resist any temptation to touch your surgical sites with your tongue or fingers.

Common Side Effects

It is common for patients to experience some side effects after getting dental implants in Torrington, including:

If you notice any severe, unusual, or prolonged side effects following your surgery, contact your implant dentist in Torrington right away.

  • Intermittent bleeding. Gauze and light pressure should be able to manage this. It usually subsides after several days.
  • This can continue for a week or longer, but cold compresses should help to keep it to a minimum.
  • General discomfort. Getting adequate rest and taking pain medication can promote your comfort.

Diet

At first, you should stick to a no-chew diet, which can include things like yogurt, mashed potatoes, applesauce, and even ice cream. Later, you can progress to a fork tender diet, which means you can eat soft foods like non-crusty breads, pasta, and meatloaf. When you feel up to it, you can resume fairly normal eating habits, but you should continue to be careful around crunchy and hard foods so you do not irritate your surgical sites.

Health & Oral Hygiene

You can brush and floss your teeth the day after surgery, but exercise extreme caution around your surgical sites. Rinsing with warm saltwater can help to keep swelling and bacterial buildup to a minimum. If you have a prescription mouthwash, be sure to use it as directed. If you choose to use a nonprescription mouthwash, opt for a product without a high alcohol content.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Once your restorations are attached to your dental implants, you might notice some gum sensitivity and excess saliva production, but these should subside fairly quickly. You will be able to use your new teeth on the same day you receive them! If you have any questions about how to care for them, let us know.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants in Torrington are designed to last a lifetime. However, they can only live up to their potential if you treat them well. Below, you will find some practical guidelines that explain how oral hygiene, a healthy diet, and other practical measures can help your restored smile to stand the test of time.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Oral hygiene plays a big role in preventing bacteria from attacking the tissue around your dental implants. To maintain a clean mouth, you should:

  • Brush thoroughly twice a day . Be sure to clean the backs of your teeth and the area along the gumline.
  • Floss daily . If you are not sure how to floss properly, our team will be happy to coach you.
  • Follow hygiene instructions that are specific to your restoration . For example, you might need to remove your implant denture for proper cleaning.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Break Bad Habits

Some habits that could adversely affect your dental implants include:

  • Tobacco use. This can lead to dry mouth, an increased risk of infections, and a weakened immune system. Every effort you make to quit is well worth it!
  • Using your teeth as a tool to open packages. Instead, use scissors or other means to open stubborn packages.
  • Chewing on hard objects. Gnawing on things like ice and pen caps could damage your implants’ restorations.

Protect Your Dental Implants

If you play sports, you should wear a mouthguard to protect both your oral tissues and your dental implants from damage. A custom mouthguard from a dentist is superior to any that you can buy over the counter.

Do you often wake up with a sore jaw? If so, you might grind your teeth at night. A custom nightguard can separate your top and bottom teeth and prevent them from damaging one another.
