What to Do in an Orthodontic Emergency

No matter how big or small your orthodontic emergency is, all you need to do is follow these easy steps:

  • Step 1: Call our office. We do everything to see emergency patients as quickly as possible, so call us right away so we can make room for you in our schedule. One of our experienced team members will guide you over the phone on how to handle your emergency until you can reach us.
  • Step 2: See Dr. Isaak Yelizar. Your emergency orthodontist Dr. Yelizar will see you right away when you arrive. After carefully evaluating your situation and gathering any necessary data, such as X-ray images, he will explain his findings to you.
  • Step 3: Get the care you need! In many cases, Dr. Yelizar will be able to handle your orthodontic emergency right away. In some cases, such as you need a replacement piece for your orthodontics, we may need to schedule you for a follow-up appointment before you go.

The Most Common Orthodontic Emergencies

Are you in significant pain? Are your braces broken? Are you concerned about a new symptom you’ve noticed during your treatment? Orthodontic emergencies come in all shapes and sizes, from small inconveniences to major issues. Even if you aren’t sure if your situation qualifies as an emergency, we encourage you to give us a call anyway. We will help you assess your symptoms over the phone and walk you through the next steps you should take. Below, you’ll find some of the most common orthodontic emergencies we treat.

Every Orthodontic Emergency Is Different

Keep in mind that since every person’s smile and orthodontic treatment differ, no two orthodontic emergencies truly look the same. But this isn’t a reason to worry; even the direst circumstances can be treated, and most of the time, these situations aren’t too severe to begin with. In any case, the first thing we’ll do once you arrive is quickly and carefully examine your situation, to determine the extent of the damage and what will be required to treat you.

Keys to Preventing Orthodontic Emergencies

While you can count on our team to be here for you should you ever experience an orthodontic emergency, we’d prefer it if you never ran into one of these scary situations in the first place! Avoiding an orthodontic emergency is an easy task if you practice a few healthy habits and simple precautions. Practicing good oral hygiene, following your treatment plan, and visiting us for regular checkups will all go a long way in keeping your smile safe and your treatment on track!

Prioritize Your Check-In Visits

Throughout your teeth-straightening journey, you’ll come into our office periodically. If you have traditional braces, then we’ll use these visits to adjust your brackets and wires and assess the condition of each. In short, these routine visits help prevent orthodontic emergencies like broken wires and loose attachments.

Maintain Good Oral Hygiene At Home

Remember, orthodontic emergencies can extend beyond broken brackets and loose wires. To keep your teeth and gums healthy throughout your teeth-straightening journey, it’s of the utmost importance that you consistently brush, floss, and rinse with mouthwash. If you’ve fallen off-track because you’re having trouble cleaning around your braces, try using special oral hygiene products, like an interproximal toothbrush.

Abide by the Dietary Restrictions

One of the top reasons patients end up at our office due to an orthodontic emergency is because they did something that caused a bracket, wire, or attachment to snap. So, don’t eat chewy, crunchy, and sticky foods during your teeth-straightening journey (i.e., caramel candies, tortilla chips, raw nuts). It’s also important to limit your intake of foods and drinks with added sugar since they can result in a cavity or two developing.

Wear a Mouthguard During Sports

All too often, patients wear protective gear like kneepads and helmets but completely forget to protect their teeth (and braces) from harm. We recommend having a custom mouthguard made for that very reason: to protect your smile from accidental bumps, rogue balls, and bad falls.

Important note: You should wear a mouthguard even if you participate in a non-contact sport like skateboarding or surfing.

Quit Unhealthy Dental Habits

In short, even habits that seem harmless can increase your risk of experiencing an orthodontic emergency. That’s why we encourage our patients to avoid habits like biting their nails, chewing on ice cubes, or using their teeth as tools to open things.

Orthodontic Emergency FAQs

How Should I Sleep with Tooth Pain?

From time to time, it’s normal to experience some soreness and pressure from your braces. The good news is that there are several things you can do to alleviate it, like eating only soft foods, sipping on cool water, and using a cold compress for 10 minutes at a time. If the discomfort you’re experiencing is stemming from a sharp bracket or wire, then cover it with some dental wax as well. If all else fails, you can also take OTC pain medication about 30 minutes before bed (just make sure to follow the directions on the label!).

Is It Safe to Use A Toothpick?

If you have something stuck in your brackets or lodged between your teeth, you might be tempted to reach for a toothpick. However, this often results in the debris getting pushed further into position. Plus, swallowing a toothpick can be deadly, so we don’t recommend using them.

Tip: Rinse your mouth with warm saltwater! This should help loosen the debris. Then, you can use your floss of choice to gently remove it. If that doesn’t work, call our dental team instead of reaching for something sharp.

What Does Chronic Bad Breath Mean?

Chronic bad breath often stems from one of two things: diet or oral hygiene. For that reason, we recommend first eliminating pungent foods from your diet (i.e., pickles, onions, fish) as well as prioritizing your at-home dental care regimen. If that doesn’t seem to do the trick, then don’t hesitate to bring it to our attention at your next check-in visit. That way, we can take a look at your teeth and gums to determine if an untreated oral health problem is the culprit.

Should I Worry About a Chipped Tooth If It Doesn’t Hurt?

Although a same-day visit to your dentist’s office may not be necessary, it is a good idea to give them a call and share what happened. From there, they can determine the urgency of the situation and schedule an appointment with that information in mind. Remember, even if you aren’t experiencing any pain, the tooth could develop an infection or break further. In other words, scheduling an appointment now could save you from a painful dental injury later.

Should I Visit the Emergency Room First for Orthodontic Emergencies?

Hospitals don’t usually have dentists or orthodontists on-staff. That’s why we don’t recommend going to the ER if you have a broken bracket, a loose wire, or a damaged retainer. Of course, if you are experiencing any potentially life-threatening symptoms, like uncontrollable bleeding, then that certainly warrants a call to 911 or a visit to the ER. After you get the medical attention you need, give us a call so we can address any damage to your braces and get your orthodontic treatment back on track.

Toothache

Is a toothache an emergency? It’s incredibly normal to experience some discomfort throughout your orthodontic treatment. For example, you may feel aching and soreness when biting down for a few days after getting your braces tightened or moving on to the next set of aligners. However, your toothache is considered a dental emergency if you experience any of the following:

How you should handle toothaches: Don’t hesitate to give us a call if your toothache hasn’t gone away after multiple days. We’ll make an appointment for you at your earliest convenience to identify the cause of your pain. Depending on your situation, we may recommend contacting your regular dentist for an appointment.

In the meantime, you can manage your discomfort with over-the-counter pain medication and cold compresses. Make sure you rinse with salt water to kill any bacteria from a potential infection and floss to remove stuck debris.

  • Pain that doesn’t go away
  • Worsening discomfort
  • A tooth is starting to look discolored
  • Sharp pain when biting down
  • Lingering sensitivity

Loose Bracket

Is a loose bracket or wire an emergency? Not always, mainly because it doesn’t typically cause pain. However, you should still seek treatment as soon as possible to ensure your orthodontic treatment stays on track. Contact our office as soon as you can to schedule an emergency appointment!

How you should handle a loose bracket or wire: Call our orthodontic office for advice if a bracket is loose. We will let you know if the bracket can be fixed at your next scheduled visit or if you should come in sooner than that. If the bracket is still attached to the wire, you should leave it in place and put wax on it if needed for comfort. If the bracket is bothering you and can be removed easily, place it in an envelope and save it to bring to your next appointment.

Broken Wire

Is a broken bracket or wire an emergency? Since a broken wire can harm your soft oral tissues, you will need urgent orthodontic care to protect your smile and get it back on the right track. You should never try and resolve the situation on your own, as that can simply lead to further damage. Instead, contact us right away for personal assistance.

How you should handle a broken bracket or wire: If possible, collect any pieces that have broken off and bring them with you to your upcoming orthodontic appointment. While you wait for your appointment, cover any sharp edges to the best of your abilities with dental wax or sugar-free gum.

Poking Wire

Is a poking or protruding wire an emergency? Yes! It needs to be addressed right away. Otherwise, a poking or protruding wire can irritate your soft tissues and potentially lead to an infection. You should never feel like you have to suffer through this type of soreness. Make sure you give our office a call and schedule an appointment with our emergency orthodontist to relieve your pain.

How you should handle a poking or protruding wire: You can use a Q-tip or a clean pencil eraser to carefully move the protruding wire back into the proper position. In addition, you can apply orthodontic wax or sugar-free gum over the wire to keep it from damaging your cheeks, lips, or tongue. When you get to our office, we’ll help you put it back into place and ensure your treatment is working as intended.

Mouth Sores

Are mouth sores or irritation an emergency? Once you start wearing braces, you may notice your brackets can easily rub against your lips, tongue, or cheeks. As a result, your soft tissue can lead to irritation, sores, and inflammation. Of course, this is a normal side effect that will naturally go away when you adjust to your treatment. After a few weeks, you should feel more comfortable with your braces.

How you should handle mouth sores or irritation: Don’t hesitate to contact us if it seems like your sores aren’t getting better or they’re becoming more painful. Our team will check for an infection and put a game plan in place to get you out of pain. We’ll get your smile back to normal in no time!

In the meantime, you can rinse your mouth with warm water to ease discomfort. Dental wax can also be used to cover your brackets and prevent further irritation. It’ll create a barrier between your braces and soft tissues.

Broken Ligature

Is a broken ligature an emergency? It’s easy to put ligatures (rubber bands) back in if they fall off. They’re an essential part of your orthodontic treatment because they ensure your wire stays attached to your braces. If they happen to break, you need to contact our office right away to ensure your treatment stays on track.

How you should handle a broken ligature: Try using sterile tweezers to carefully put a broken ligature back in place. If needed, you can even use a cotton swab or pencil eraser to bend it back into the right position. Don’t hesitate to make an appointment with our emergency dentist if nothing is working. We’ll gladly assist you and resolve your situation quickly!

Soft Tissue Cuts

Is a cut lip, cheek, or tongue an emergency? Your braces may cut your soft tissues if you sustain a blow to the face. While these injuries can bleed a lot, they’re not typically considered an emergency. However, we can stitch the wound closed if needed. If your bleeding hasn’t stopped after 5-10 minutes, head to your local emergency for assistance.

How you should handle a cut lip, cheek, or tongue: Carefully clean the area with a wet washcloth or rinse your mouth out. In addition, apply pressure to the area to control bleeding. You can also take over-the-counter pain medication and use cold compresses to relieve your discomfort.

Something Stuck Between Teeth

Is having something stuck between the teeth an emergency? Everyone has had something stuck between their teeth at some point in time. It’s not uncommon for this issue to occur when wearing traditional metal braces! While you do not need to see your orthodontist every time this happens, you should have the proper instruments on hand to clean your braces carefully and effectively.

How you should handle something stuck between your teeth: You can try using dental floss or a proxy brush to dislodge food debris stuck in between your teeth. They’re both great ways to get something out from between your teeth or braces! Rinsing with warm water can also help remove the stuck item. Whatever you do, never use a sharp object like a toothpick as it can do more harm than good. If you can’t get the item out yourself, don’t hesitate to give us a call for assistance.

Broken/Lost Aligner or Retainer

Is a broken or lost aligner or retainer an emergency? Not always! However, you should still seek help as soon as possible. Failing to do so may cause you to potentially get off track and delay your treatment. Make sure you contact us within a few days of losing or breaking your aligner or retainer.

How you should handle a broken or lost aligner or retainer: The last thing you should do is continue wearing your lost or broken aligner or retainer. It could potentially move your teeth into the wrong positions. Instead, retrace your steps and try looking for the lost appliance. You can search areas like countertops, between couch cushions, in the trash, in your purse, or your pockets.

Don’t forget to contact us and explain your situation. We’ll help you decide whether or not you should have your missing or broken set replaced.

Loose Teeth

Are loose teeth an emergency? Rest assured it’s completely normal for your teeth to feel slightly loose while they’re being repositioned. It’s only a matter of concern if your teeth feel particularly loose. In that case, something may be wrong, like gum disease.

How you should handle loose teeth: Do not play or overuse your loose teeth! Instead, practice good oral hygiene to fight off the bacteria that could be weakening the tissue. You can schedule an emergency appointment with our team to ensure everything is okay. We’ll take X-rays to monitor the health of your jawbone and determine which treatment option is best to get your smile on the right track.
