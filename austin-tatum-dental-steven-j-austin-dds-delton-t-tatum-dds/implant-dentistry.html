Single Tooth Implant Restorations

A single implant replaces one missing tooth without affecting neighboring teeth. Unlike a bridge, which requires grinding down adjacent teeth for support, an implant stands independently. We place the titanium post, allow it to integrate with your bone, then attach a custom crown that matches your natural teeth in color, shape, and size. The result looks and functions just like the tooth you lost. Single implants are ideal when you want to preserve your remaining healthy teeth.

Implant-Retained Bridges

When you’re missing several adjacent teeth, implant-retained bridges offer a permanent solution. Instead of relying on natural teeth for support, the bridge attaches to two or more implants. This provides superior stability, preserves bone in the areas where teeth are missing, and looks completely natural. Implant bridges don’t require adhesives or removal for cleaning you care for them like natural teeth.

Implant-Retained Partials and Dentures

For patients missing most or all of their teeth, implant-retained dentures provide stability that traditional dentures can’t match. The denture snaps onto implants (typically 2-4 per arch), eliminating slipping, clicking, and the need for messy adhesives. You can eat a wider variety of foods, speak more clearly, and feel confident that your teeth will stay in place. These can be designed as removable (for easy cleaning) or fixed (permanent).

Snap-On Dentures for Comfort and Convenience

Snap-on dentures attach securely to implants via small connectors but can be removed for cleaning. They offer the stability of implants with the convenience of a removable prosthesis. Most patients find them far more comfortable than traditional dentures because they don’t cover the roof of your mouth and don’t rely on suction or adhesives. They stay put during eating and speaking but come out easily when you need to clean them.

Advanced Technology for Precision Implant Placement

Advanced technology shows us the exact position of nerves, sinuses, and bone structure before we begin. Digital scanning leads to more predictable results, shorter procedures, and better healing. We also use 3D printing to create custom surgical components when needed for complex cases.

3D CT Imaging and Digital Planning

Our cone beam 3D scanner provides detailed views of your jawbone, allowing us to plan implant placement with accuracy that traditional x-rays can’t provide. We can see bone density, measure available space, and avoid vital structures like nerves and sinuses. This reduces complications and improves long-term success. Digital planning means we know exactly what we’re doing before the procedure begins, making the surgery more efficient and predictable.

3D Printing for Surgical Guides

For complex cases, we use 3D printing to create custom surgical guides and components. While we rarely use fully guided implant surgery, we have the technology available when precision demands it. These custom guides improve placement accuracy and can reduce procedure time. The result is better outcomes with less guesswork.

Bone Grafting and Sinus Lift Procedures in Amarillo, TX

Sometimes the jawbone needs strengthening before implants can be placed. Bone grafting adds material to areas where bone has deteriorated, creating a solid foundation. Sinus lifts create space in the upper jaw when the sinus cavity is too close to where the implant needs to go. Ridge augmentation reshapes the bone after tooth loss to improve implant positioning. We handle these procedures with careful attention to safety and comfort, using 3D imaging to plan and monitor the process.

Bone Grafting for Stronger Implant Support

Bone grafting is common when teeth have been missing for a while or when bone loss occurred due to infection or trauma. We place grafting material (either synthetic or natural) in the area where the implant will go, then wait several months for it to integrate before placing the implant. The grafted bone provides the stability implants need for long-term success. We monitor healing closely and use advanced materials that promote predictable results.

Sinus Lift and Ridge Augmentation

A sinus lift creates room in the upper jaw for implants by gently lifting the sinus membrane and adding bone underneath. It’s necessary when your sinus cavity sits too low for standard implant placement. Ridge augmentation reshapes the jawbone to improve width or height, typically after tooth loss has caused bone resorption. Both procedures are performed with precision using 3D imaging guidance. Recovery is manageable, and these procedures significantly expand who can successfully receive implants.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants in Amarillo, TX

Who is a good candidate for dental implants?

Most adults with missing teeth are candidates for implants. You need healthy gums, adequate bone density, and good overall health. Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking can affect healing, but many patients with these factors still receive implants successfully with proper management. The best way to know if implants are right for you is a consultation where we evaluate your bone structure, oral health, and medical history.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, dental implants can last decades often a lifetime. The titanium post itself rarely fails once it’s integrated. The crown, bridge, or denture attached to it may eventually need replacement due to normal wear, but the implant itself remains. Success depends on good oral hygiene, regular dental checkups, and avoiding habits like grinding that can stress the restoration.

Do dental implants hurt?

Most patients report minimal discomfort during and after implant placement. We use local anesthesia during the procedure, and sedation is available if you prefer. Afterward, any soreness is usually manageable with over-the-counter medication. Many patients are surprised by how comfortable the process is. Advanced technology and careful technique make modern implant surgery much less invasive than it used to be.

What if I don’t have enough bone for implants?

Bone grafting or sinus lifts can create the foundation you need. These procedures are common and have high success rates. We evaluate your bone using 3D imaging and discuss whether grafting is necessary during your consultation. While it adds time to the overall treatment, it makes implants possible for patients who wouldn’t otherwise be candidates.

Are implant-supported dentures better than traditional dentures?

Yes, for most patients. Implant-supported dentures stay secure without adhesives, allow better chewing function, preserve bone, and feel more natural. They don’t cover the roof of your mouth (in many cases), which improves taste and comfort. Traditional dentures can work well initially, but they often become loose over time as bone resorbs. Implant-supported options provide long-term stability and prevent the bone loss that causes dentures to fit poorly.
