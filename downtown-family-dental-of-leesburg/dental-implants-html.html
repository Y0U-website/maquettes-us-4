Benefits of Dental Implants

Lifestyle Benefits

  • Your self-confidence will dramatically increase because your smile will once again be complete.
  • You’ll be able to drink your favorite hot beverages (i.e., coffee, tea) and worry less about damaging your prosthetics (unlike dentures that can become easily warped).
  • With dental implants, there is no need to worry about what foods you can and can’t eat. These prosthetics offer a greater bite force and chewing power, making it easy to consume harder, denser food options.
  • Your dental implants will never slip or fall out, unlike dentures. Their surgical placement in your jaw creates a more stable and durable foundation that acts just like regular teeth.

Health Benefits

  • Dental implants in Leesburg do not irritate the soft tissues like fixed bridges or dentures. Instead, they mimic the natural tooth structure, allowing them to fit comfortably within the socket, not impacting nearby healthy teeth.
  • Your implants will stimulate the alveolar bone and jawbone, allowing you to embrace a more youthful appearance and avoid a potential facial collapse that is often caused by jawbone deterioration. Denture-wearers and those with fixed bridges may still be susceptible to this common problem.
  • By replacing your missing teeth, you’ll increase your chances for a longer lifespan simply because you’ll minimize common overall health problems that are linked to tooth loss (i.e., cardiovascular disease, diabetes, osteoporosis, etc.).
  • Cleaning and maintaining your dental implants is far easier than if you were to opt for dentures or a fixed bridge. The reason is that your implants work just like regular teeth, so you do not need to remove them to ensure a proper clean.

Long-Term Benefits

  • You’ll enjoy a smile that is designed to last a lifetime as long as you commit to properly caring for and maintaining your new and existing teeth and gums.
  • With dental implants, you’ll not only save time but also money. How? You’ll never need denture adhesives, complex cleaning solutions, or appointments designed for relining and replacing prosthetics.

Who Can Dental Implants Help?

After the posts have been placed and the jawbone has encompassed them, dental implants can be used to replace any number of missing teeth. No matter your tooth loss situation, dental implants can help you regain your smile’s former glory by keeping your restorations firmly grounded.

Missing One Tooth

For a single tooth, your dentist will insert one dental implant into the socket and jawbone. After a few months and it’s fully healed, a high-quality dental crown will be placed on top. The artificial tooth will look and function just like a natural tooth so well that you may even forget which tooth is the prosthetic one!

Missing Multiple Teeth

To replace two or more lost teeth in a row, a bridge can be anchored to two dental implants instead of healthy teeth, which allows you to preserve and retain the natural teeth you have left for much longer. If your missing teeth are spread throughout your mouth, a couple of implants can be used to secure a partial denture.

Missing All of Your Teeth

Even if you have suffered total tooth loss, dental implants can still provide a strong smile. Using only four to six strategically placed implants, your dentist can provide a solid foundation for a full denture. By attaching your prosthetic to implants, you won’t have to experience the embarrassment of a slipping denture when you eat or speak.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

After your dental implant surgery, you shouldn’t touch the forming blood clot; that would just delay or reverse your recovery. Instead, please stick to the following rules:

  • For a few days post-surgery, keep your fingers and tongue away from the surgical site(s).
  • Avoid using tobacco products during the first 24 hours after surgery.
  • Don’t use drinking straws while you recover; their suction can dislodge a blood clot.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

Most patients have mild side effects after dental implant surgery. In many cases, these include:

Remember that the effects above are temporary; they quickly fade over time. With that said, please tell us if they persist in your case.

  • General Discomfort Once your surgery’s anesthesia wears off, your treatment site may ache. Use store-bought (or doctor-prescribed) pain relievers to relieve symptoms.
  • Intermittent Bleeding If your implant site bleeds after placement, please apply gauze and light pressure as needed.
  • Facial or Gum Swelling The face and gums can swell during the first 72 hours after surgery. In that case, apply a cold compress or ice pack.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

You should follow a soft food diet for a few days after surgery. After all, chewing a tough meal too soon could dislodge your blood clot; you’d then face recovery delays.

Example foods to include in this diet are:

Most patients can resume a regular diet once they feel up to it. Still, try to limit your intake of crunchy and tough foods going forward. You also shouldn’t chew directly on the implant site(s).

  • Yogurt
  • Soft Cheese
  • Scrambled Eggs
  • Smoothies
  • (Non-hot) Soup
  • Pasta
  • Pudding
  • Mashed Potatoes
  • Sweet Potatoes
  • Ice Cream

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Your mouth must be cleaned daily, regardless of your recovery from implant surgery. That means you should use the following tips while you heal:

  • When brushing your teeth twice daily, be careful of the surgical site.
  • Rinse your mouth with salt water twice (or thrice) daily to clean the treatment site. If you’d like, you could also substitute a prescription mouthwash for the salt water.
  • Avoid mouthwashes with a high level of alcohol (i.e., Scope, Listerine, etc.).

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Your treatment’s hardest part is over when your crown, bridge, or denture is attached to your implant. From that point onward, the most you’ll feel is some minor (and easily managed) gum sensitivity. You shouldn’t experience swelling, bleeding, or demanding recovery steps.; you can just enjoy your restored smile once the attachment is done!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Your implant is made from a durable material, like resilient porcelain, that doesn’t get cavities like your natural teeth. However, did you know that the leading cause of failure is a pervasive form of gum disease called peri-implantitis?

To keep your restorations in good condition, you must continue brushing and flossing twice daily to remove plaque and bacteria that contribute to common oral issues, like cavities and periodontal problems.
