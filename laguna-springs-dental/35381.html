Factors That Affect Implant Longevity

An implant s lifespan comes down to the individual, including oral hygiene habits, lifestyle choices, and overall health.

Oral Hygiene Habits

Hygiene is one of the most significant factors affecting an implant s longevity. If you don t brush and floss consistently, you can develop gum disease, leading to implant failure. Be sure to brush at least twice daily, floss once, and visit your dentist for routine checkups and teeth cleanings.

Smoking

Smoking can greatly impact the success of dental implants; in fact, it is one of the most significant risk factors for implant failure.

The reason is that smoking compromises healing by reducing blood flow and oxygen delivery to oral tissues. This not only delays healing but also increases the risk of infection. Smoking can also affect bone density, which impacts the implant s stability.

Overall Health

Another factor affecting a dental implant s lifespan is general health. Chronic illnesses like diabetes can also interfere with healing and may increase the risk of implant failure. Be sure to discuss any underlying medical conditions with our dentist so he can make the appropriate recommendations.

Dental Implant Benefits

Dental professionals often consider implants the gold standard for tooth replacement. If you look at their benefits, it’s easy to see why!

High Success Rate

According to the American Academy of Implant Dentistry , the success rate for implants over ten years is about 95 percent, making them a highly effective and reliable tooth replacement.

Long-Term Tooth Replacement

Tooth implants are designed to last many years even a lifetime with proper care. They are a cost-effective long-term solution when you compare them to options such as dentures or bridges that need to be replaced or repaired over time.

Looks and Feels Natural

An implant looks, feels, and functions most like a natural tooth. We custom design a prosthetic tooth like a dental crown to match the shape and shade of existing teeth for a seamless appearance.

Restores Comfortable Chewing

Chewing comfortably can be difficult when you have missing teeth or a removable denture. Because it’s so stable, an implant tooth functions like a natural tooth, so you can eat whatever foods you like.

Preserves Tooth Structure

Unlike a dental bridge that involves other teeth, an implant is a standalone solution that requires no alteration of healthy tooth structure. This helps preserve natural tooth structure and promotes better oral health.

Maintains Jawbone Health

When you lose a tooth, the bone that supported it starts to deteriorate without a tooth root. A dental implant is placed directly into the jawbone and acts as a replacement root to stimulate bone regeneration and prevent further bone loss.

Replaces One Tooth or Multiple Teeth

We can replace a single tooth or multiple teeth with dental implants. Dr. Albik can replace multiple teeth with an implant-supported bridge or even a full arch of missing teeth with a technique called All-on-4 .

Who Can Get a Dental Implant?

You should generally be healthy and have sufficient bone mass in the area we want to place the implant. Even if there isn’t enough bone quantity, Dr. Albik will explain and guide you which route to take to augment the bone. This procedure involves building up bone in the area over several months until it’s strong enough to support the implant.

What’s the Process for Getting an Implant?

  • Initial consultation The first step determines if you are a good implant candidate and involves an exam, x-rays, and 3-D cone beam scans of your teeth and jaws.
  • Treatment plan You will receive a personalized care plan outlining the number and type of implants and additional procedures, such as tooth extraction.
  • Implant placement Dr. Albik uses your cone beam scans and x-rays as a guide to place your implant in the optimal location in your jaw.
  • Healing period Implant healing and osseointegration typically take several months. During this period, bone and tissue around the implant post heal and fuse with it, becoming part of your dental anatomy.
  • Abutment and crown placement After healing, an abutment is added to the implant post. Your custom crown is then attached to the abutment. We design your crown to match the color and shape of your natural teeth for a natural-looking, functional replacement tooth.
