On this page What are Dental Implants? Procedure Benefits Candidates Recovery Results FAQs Consultation A Lasting Investment What are dental implants? Dental implants are small, biocompatible posts surgically placed in the jawbone to act as artificial tooth roots . Over time, the implant fuses with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration, creating a strong and stable foundation for a dental crown, bridge, or denture.

We offer titanium and ceramic dental implants to meet each patient’s needs and preferences. We also offer zygomatic dental implants for patients with significant upper jaw bone deterioration and all-on-four implants to replace a full upper or lower arch of missing teeth. Read on to learn more about these implant options, or schedule your personal consultation with Dr. Jamie or Dr. Tan of Yeo Family Dental Group to receive your personalized dental implant treatment plan.

  • Permanent solution for replacing missing teeth
  • Natural-looking and functional implants that mimic the look and feel of your own teeth
  • Improved chewing ability and speech
  • Enhanced facial structure and support for a more youthful appearance
  • Long-lasting results with proper care

Dental Implant Treatment Options

Dental implants can be used in several ways, depending on how many teeth are missing, your jawbone health, and the type of restoration that best supports your smile. At Yeo Family Dental Group, Dr. Tan or Dr. Jamie will evaluate your teeth, gums, bite, and bone structure to recommend the right approach for your needs.

Single Dental Implants

A single dental implant is often the best option when one tooth is missing . The implant replaces the tooth root, and a custom crown is attached to restore the look, feel, and function of a natural tooth. This option also helps protect nearby healthy teeth because it does not rely on them for support.

Full Arch Dental Implants

Full arch dental implants are used to restore an entire row of teeth with an implant-supported restoration. This option may be a good fit for patients with significant tooth loss, uncomfortable dentures, or dental issues affecting many teeth.

Zygomatic Dental Implants

Zygomatic dental implants may be considered for patients with significant bone loss in the upper jaw. These longer implants anchor into the cheekbone rather than relying only on the jawbone, which may help some patients avoid bone grafting. Dr. Tan or Dr. Jamie can determine if this advanced option is appropriate after reviewing your imaging and oral health.

Titanium Dental Implants

Titanium is the most popular material for dental implants due to its durability, relative affordability, and biocompatibility with your natural bone and gum tissue. They have excellent osseointegration capabilities, meaning they integrate well into the jaw bone after they are placed. Titanium implants are associated with a high rate of long-term success and a low incidence of complications.

In rare cases, titanium implants can corrode when they react with other metal fixtures in the mouth. However, they have a long-term success rate of 94 to 97 percent and remain the industry standard for dental implant materials. Learn more about titanium implants on our blog.

Zirconia/Ceramic Dental Implants Zirconia implants have been used since 1987, providing an alternative to titanium implants. They are also referred to as ceramic implants because zirconia is a ceramic non-metal material. While titanium allergies are rare, a small percentage of people who are allergic to titanium can benefit from zirconia implants.

Because zirconia has a very low metal content, these implants also have a lower risk of corroding. The zirconia material is white, so there is less chance of the posts showing through the gums, which can occasionally happen with titanium implants. One disadvantage of zirconia implants is a higher risk for fracture. Learn more about ceramic dental implants on our blog.

State-of-the-Art Care

What is the dental implant procedure like?

The dental implant procedure is a carefully planned process that restores a missing tooth from the root up. Every detail matters, from the health of your gums and jawbone to the final shape and color of your crown. At Yeo Family Dental Group, Dr. Tan and Dr. Jamie use advanced dental technology, thoughtful planning, and personalized care to make the experience feel comfortable, precise, and easy to understand.

For patients considering dental implants in Marina Del Rey , as well as nearby communities like Playa Del Rey, Venice, Culver City, and Santa Monica, the goal is simple: a replacement tooth that looks natural, feels secure, and supports your long-term dental health.

1. Consultation and Personalized Planning

Your first visit begins with a detailed evaluation of your teeth, gums, bite, and jawbone. We’ll talk through your goals, review your oral health , and use digital imaging to get a clear picture of the structure beneath your smile. This helps your dentist evaluate bone density, identify important anatomical details, and plan your implant placement with precision.

Dental implants are often part of a larger plan for a healthier, more confident smile. For some patients, that may include preventive care, cosmetic dentistry, teeth whitening , full mouth reconstruction, or other services that support both function and appearance.

If your jawbone has weakened due to gum disease, tooth loss, or multiple missing teeth, a small bone graft may be recommended before implant surgery. This creates a stronger foundation for the implant and helps support a lasting result. During this appointment, we’ll also review your treatment options, discuss cost and dental insurance questions, and create a personalized plan that reflects your unique needs, dental issues, and cosmetic goals. At Yeo Family Dental Group, every recommendation is made with your comfort, dental health, and long-term results in mind .

2. Implant Placement

During dental implant surgery , local anesthesia or sedation is used to help you stay relaxed and comfortable. Your dentist places the implant into the jawbone, where it acts like an artificial tooth root . This small post becomes the foundation for your future crown and helps restore the feel and function of a natural tooth .

In some cases, a temporary crown may be placed at this appointment, allowing you to leave with a more complete-looking smile while your permanent restoration is being made. Your dentist will let you know if this option makes sense for your treatment plan. Our focus is to make implant placement feel smooth, stress-free, and carefully guided from start to finish.

3. Healing and Osseointegration

After placement, the implant needs time to bond with the surrounding bone. This process is called osseointegration , and it is what gives quality dental implants their strength and stability. Healing usually takes several months, depending on your gum health, bone quality, and the number of implants placed.

During this time, we’ll provide clear instructions to help you care for your mouth and protect the implant site. Follow-up visits allow us to monitor your progress and make sure your gums, bone, and implant are healing beautifully. This step also supports preventive care by helping us catch concerns early and keep your mouth moving toward optimal oral health .

4. Abutment Placement

Once the implant has fully integrated with the jawbone, an abutment is attached. This small connector sits between the implant and the final crown. It helps secure the restoration and creates the right shape for a comfortable, natural-looking result .

Your gums may need a short period of healing around the abutment before your final crown is placed. This step helps ensure your replacement tooth fits well, protects nearby healthy teeth, and blends with the surrounding smile.

5. Custom Crown Placement

The final step is placing your permanent dental implant crown . Your crown is custom-made to match the color, shape, and size of your nearby teeth, creating a replacement that looks polished, balanced, and natural. Once attached, the implant crown is fixed in the mouth, unlike removable dentures or bridges.

With your new implant in place, you can chew, speak, and smile with greater comfort and confidence. Dental implants restore the function of natural teeth, improve chewing ability, and make it easier to enjoy a wider range of foods . With proper care, regular checkups, and support from an experienced dental practice, permanent dental implants can help protect your oral health and keep your smile strong for years.

A Well-Supported Recovery

Dental Implant Recovery

After receiving dental implants in Marina Del Rey, patients can expect a healing process that is usually manageable with the right care. Recovery time varies based on your oral health, gum health, bone quality, and the number of implants placed, but most patients return to normal daily activities within a few days. Dr. Tan or Dr. Jamie will provide clear aftercare instructions to support comfortable healing and long-term implant success.

Fulfill Your Goals for a Beautiful Smile Dental Implant Results Once your dental implant surgery is complete and your restoration is placed, you can enjoy a beautiful, natural-looking, and fully functional smile for years. Just like natural teeth, dental implants require good oral hygiene and regular dental checkups . With diligent care, your dental implants can last a lifetime.

  • The first few days: Mild swelling, tenderness, bruising, or discomfort is normal after dental implant surgery. These symptoms often peak within the first 48 to 72 hours, then gradually improve with rest, medication, cold compresses, and a soft food diet. Avoid strenuous exercise, smoking, alcohol, and drinking through a straw while the area begins to heal.
  • Eating after dental implant surgery: Stick with soft, nourishing foods during the early healing period, such as yogurt, eggs, mashed potatoes, oatmeal, pasta, soft fish, and warm soup. Avoid chewing directly on the implant site until your dentist says it is safe. As healing progresses, you can slowly return to more of your regular foods.
  • Caring for your implant site: Keep your mouth clean, but be gentle around the surgical area. Follow your dentist’s instructions for brushing, rinsing, and protecting your gums. Avoid touching the implant site with your fingers or tongue, and attend follow-up appointments so we can monitor your healing and make sure the implant is bonding well with the jawbone.
