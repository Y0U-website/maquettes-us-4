What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants themselves are small, titanium posts that are surgically placed below the gumline to anchor a prosthetic in the mouth. This way, the restoration won’t have to rely on natural suction, adhesive, or existing teeth to stay securely in place. Due to their biocompatible abilities, they’re also able to fuse with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration, which helps them provide the bone structure with the necessary support and blood flow to keep it from deteriorating.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

At Claybrooke Dental of Gahanna, our dental team will begin by going over your oral health and medical history. This will give us a clear view of whether you currently show signs of gum disease, tooth decay, or jawbone loss. Although most patients are eligible to receive dental implants, it may not be possible to move forward with surgery right away. The consultation will determine if you require additional preliminary treatments (i.e., gum disease treatment, tooth extraction, bone grafting, etc.) before having your new teeth put in place. If necessary, this will set your mouth up for success.

Dental Implant Surgery

Dr. Odenweller and Dr. Buller will make sure you are referred to a local implant dentist in Gahanna who will place your new prosthetics with your safety and comfort in mind. Administering local anesthesia to numb your mouth, they will carefully place each implant post into the designated area of the gumline and jawbone so that ample support is provided once the custom restoration is put in place. After positioning each post, they will close your gum tissue and place protective healing caps over the areas for adequate healing.

Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment

The next phase of treatment is osseointegration. While healing at home, your implant posts must fuse with your jawbone to ensure long-lasting placement. This can take 4-6 months. Once you are fully healed from this portion of treatment, you will have the metal abutments that attach your restoration to your implant put into place. This will require a small procedure that will likely take a few weeks for you to heal properly.

It is also at this time that we will take a dental impression of your mouth so that trusted lab technicians can craft your custom restoration.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

The final phase of treatment is receiving your customized dental restoration. This can be a crown, bridge, or denture depending on the number of teeth you need to have replaced. This will be determined during your initial consultation, as you and a member of our team go over the number of missing teeth you have and what will be required to replace them. Once everything is in place, you will be able to begin enjoying life with your new smile.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Your new dental implants represent an investment of time, effort, and finances that can restore your beautiful smile as well as your confidence, so it only makes sense that you’ll want to do anything you can to make them last for as long as possible. Luckily, there are a few simple measures you can take to keep your dental implants and the gums and jawbones supporting them healthy and strong. Here are a few tips on how to keep your new teeth in great shape for life from the team at Claybrooke Dental of Gahanna.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While dental implants cannot become infected, the gums and jawbones supporting them still can, and gum disease is one of the leading causes of implant failure. To keep your newly restored smile in great shape, be sure to brush, floss, and use antibacterial mouthwash every day to eliminate food debris and sugary residues that can shelter and feed harmful oral bacteria.

Eat a Healthy Diet

While dental implants work just like real teeth when chewing, it’s still important to eat a healthy diet to preserve your oral health. Be sure to get plenty of protein, calcium, phosphorus, and vitamins C and D to help your implants form a strong and durable bond with your jawbone while keeping your gums, soft tissues, and teeth healthy.

Break Bad Habits

Bad habits can set the stage for dental implant disaster. To ensure that your implants stay healthy, abstain from chewing on non-food items and using your teeth to open packages or bottles, as these can damage your appliances. Tobacco use and excessive alcohol consumption are both great ways to inhibit your gums’ ability to heal and fight infection, so kick these habits as soon as possible.

Protect Your Dental Implants

It’s important to protect your dental implants from damage. If you play sports or ride a bike, be sure to wear a mouthguard to cushion your smile against falls and blows to the face. If you grind your teeth when you sleep, we offer customized nightguards that you can wear to bed to ensure that your natural and artificial teeth receive superior protection.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

We love seeing our patients, so remember to have at least two dental exams and cleanings at our Gahanna office every year. This allows our dentists to keep an eye on the state of your oral health while correcting problems in their early stages and advising you about oral hygiene practice, and these appointments play a key role in ensuring that your implants stay in great condition.

Dental Implant FAQs

Are There Any Food Restrictions with Dental Implants?

Fortunately, there are very few, if any, food restrictions once you have your dental implants placed. Because your implants look and feel like regular teeth, you won’t have to worry about them accidentally slipping or falling out; however, it is important to avoid hard, crunchy foods. The reason is that these particular items can crack, chip, or fracture a tooth, both natural and artificial.

Does It Hurt to Have Dental Implants Placed?

Just like any other type of surgical procedure, the placement of dental implants will require some anesthesia. This will enable you to relax and feel no pain whatsoever during the surgery. It will be necessary for one or more small incisions to be made in your gums in order to place the implants. Once you are at home healing, you can expect some soreness and swelling to occur; however, pain medication or over-the-counter products can help to reduce or alleviate some of the discomfort.

Is It Necessary to Have a Bone Graft or Sinus Lift?

Depending on the status of your jawbone density, you may or may not need a bone graft or sinus lift. It will be up to Dr. Todd, Dr. Odenweller, or Dr. Buller to determine if your bone is strong enough to withstand the pressure of a dental implant and its restoration. If not, depending on the location of the missing tooth, you will need to have bone minerals placed into the socket to allow the additional bone to grow and become durable. This process can take several months to heal prior to moving forward with your dental implant placement.

How Do I Care for My Dental Implants?

Taking care of your dental implants is an easy process. You will not need any special tools or products to keep them clean. In fact, all you need is your toothbrush, toothpaste, dental floss, and mouthwash. Maintaining good oral hygiene is imperative with dental implants. Although the implant and restoration cannot decay, the natural teeth surrounding it can. Should you develop tooth decay or gum disease, this can negatively impact your dental implants and cause them to fail.

What Can Cause Dental Implants to Fail?

Although these cases aren’t common, dental implants can typically fail due to two reasons: failed osseointegration and peri-implantitis. If your jawbone is unhealthy or you don’t have a sufficient amount of tissue to heal over the metal post(s), it can lower the chances of successful treatment. Peri-implantitis a form of gum disease can develop due to poor oral hygiene and cause damage to both the gums and bones supporting your implant. There are various other reasons this treatment can fail, so you’ll want to speak with your dentist on ways to minimize your risks.

Are Dental Implants Safe?

When placed by a trained professional, and if you have good oral and overall health, dental implants are an incredibly successful and safe treatment. Even if you have low jawbone density that might risk failure, your dental team will do everything they can to address the issue with additional procedures, such as bone grafting. Should you have conditions that could cause complications during surgery or recovery, such as diabetes or cancer, you’ll be able to discuss it with your dentist to determine the best and safest way forward for restoring your smile.

Can I Get Dental Implants If I Smoke?

Those who smoke or chew tobacco typically have a higher rate of unsuccessful dental implants. Not only does using these products increase the risk of dry mouth, but it can also delay your healing process and disrupt osseointegration. Smoking may also lead to dry socket a painful condition where the blood clot forming over the implant site is dislodged. While this might be a good opportunity to quit this habit altogether, you should hold off at least two weeks before your surgery and wait two to three months afterward.
