What Are Implant Dentures?

Traditionally, dentures sit directly on the gums and rely on natural suction to keep them in place. Implant dentures are different; they stay anchored to the jaw with the help of strategically placed implant posts. This gives them improved stability as well as enhanced chewing power.

Note that there are two main types of implant dentures you can get based on your specific needs: fixed and removable.

Fixed/Permanent Implant Dentures

A fixed implant denture stays in your mouth at all times. This can make maintenance easier, as you’ll be able to brush and floss your prosthesis similarly to your natural teeth. That said, it’s important to note that fixed implant dentures usually call for a greater number of implant posts.

Removable Implant Dentures

You can take a removable implant denture out of your mouth whenever you want. Since this type of implant denture often requires fewer implant posts, it’s often a suitable option for patients who have lower levels of bone density in their jaws.

The Implant Denture Process

Once it has been confirmed that you are a suitable candidate for implant dentures (and any necessary preliminary treatments have been completed), you will be referred to a local oral surgeon to have the implant posts surgically placed in your jaw. After the posts have fused with the bone around them, metal connector pieces called abutments can be attached. Then our team will take impressions of your mouth in order to design the final denture. The completed prosthesis will be anchored to the implant posts via the abutments.

Am I a Candidate for Implant Dentures?

You may qualify for implant dentures if:

In order to know for sure whether implant dentures are right for you, you will need to schedule a consultation with our team. We’ll evaluate the state of your mouth and your overall health history to determine your candidacy for dental implant surgery.

  • You’ve lost most or all of the teeth in one or both arches.
  • You’re in good oral health overall.
  • You have plenty of bone density in your jaw.
  • You are able to maintain good oral hygiene habits once your implant posts have been placed.

Benefits of Combining Dentures & Dental Implants

Some of the benefits you’ll be able to enjoy with dentures supported by dental implants include:

  • A More Varied Diet: Implant dentures have enough chewing power to handle many of the foods that you might have trouble eating with traditional dentures.
  • Prevention of Bone Loss: The stimulation from your implant posts will help prevent bone loss in your jaw.
  • Longevity: As long as they’re properly cared for, implant dentures can last for decades.
