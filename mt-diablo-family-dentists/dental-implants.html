The Implant Timeline, Step by Step

First, the consultation and 3D scan. Dr. Jain examines your mouth, takes a CBCT scan, reviews your health history, and maps out a written treatment plan with exact costs. Next comes placement: in a single visit under local anesthesia, the titanium post is placed into the jawbone. The procedure is usually quicker and more comfortable than patients expect — many compare it favorably to having a tooth pulled.

Then your bone does the work. Over the next three to six months, the bone fuses to the implant through osseointegration. You&#x27;ll live your normal life during this phase; we can provide a temporary tooth so you&#x27;re never left with a visible gap. Once the implant is solid, Dr. Jain attaches the abutment and takes impressions for your custom crown. About two weeks later, the finished crown is secured — and you walk out with a complete, fully functional tooth.

  • Step 1 — Consultation and CBCT 3D scan: exam, planning, and a written quote
  • Step 2 — Implant placement: one visit under local anesthesia, mild soreness for a few days
  • Step 3 — Osseointegration: 3–6 months while bone fuses to the post (temporary tooth available)
  • Step 4 — Abutment and impressions: the connector is placed and your crown is designed
  • Step 5 — Final crown: your new tooth is attached and adjusted for a perfect bite

Why Implants Are Worth It: Success Rates and Bone Health

The benefit patients don&#x27;t see — but feel years later — is bone preservation. Your jawbone stays strong only when tooth roots stimulate it. After a tooth is lost, the bone in that spot begins to shrink, which can shift neighboring teeth, change your bite, and over time give the face a sunken appearance. Because an implant transmits chewing force into the bone just like a natural root, it&#x27;s the only replacement option that actually stops this bone loss.

Bridges and dentures replace the visible tooth but do nothing for the bone underneath. That&#x27;s why waiting on an implant has a real cost: the longer a gap sits empty, the more bone resorbs, and the more likely you&#x27;ll need grafting before an implant can be placed.

Caring for Your Implant — and How Long It Will Last

One of the best things about implants is that caring for them is refreshingly ordinary: brush twice a day, floss daily, and keep up with professional cleanings and checkups. Implants can&#x27;t get cavities, but the gum and bone around them still need protection — a gum infection around an implant (called peri-implantitis) is the main threat to its long-term success.

With that basic care, the titanium post is designed for long-term use, since it fuses directly to your bone — though how long it actually lasts depends on your oral health, home care, and regular checkups. The crown on top is a wear item, like a natural tooth&#x27;s enamel, and may need replacement after 15 to 25 years of chewing. At your regular visits we check the implant, the crown, and the surrounding gum so small issues never become big ones.

For the first few days after placement, we&#x27;ll ask you to stick to soft foods, avoid the surgical site while brushing, and skip smoking and drinking through straws. Most patients manage any soreness with over-the-counter pain relievers and are back to their routine within a day or two.

Dental Implant Costs: Implant vs. Bridge vs. Denture

An implant costs more upfront than a bridge or partial denture, but it&#x27;s usually the better long-term value: implants don&#x27;t decay, don&#x27;t sacrifice neighboring teeth, and rarely need replacing — while bridges typically last 10–15 years and partial dentures often need relining or remaking every 5–8 years.
