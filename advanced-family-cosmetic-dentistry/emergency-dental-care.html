After you have suffered a dental emergency, it is normal to feel concerned and unsure of what your next steps should be. Our dental team is here to help and can ease your stress and pain. Contact us today for all of your emergency dental needs.

  • Bleeding: If your gums are bloody or there is blood on your toothbrush or in the sink, this could be a sign of a dental emergency that should be treated by a dentist.
  • Loose or Missing Teeth: If you have a knocked-out tooth, a tooth that is partially dislodged, or a loose tooth, it is time to go see an emergency dentist.
  • Swelling: If you are experiencing extreme swelling in your gums, mouth, or face, you could be experiencing a dental emergency and should seek the help of a professional.
  • Infection: Signs of infection may include fever, inflammation in the mouth or face, or a dental abscess. A dental abscess is a pus-filled infection in the pulp of your tooth and must be treated by a dental professional.
  • Broken or Loose Restorations: If you have a broken or loose filling, crown , or bridge , you should schedule an emergency dentist appointment.
  • Jaw Pain: If you are suffering from jaw pain that is accompanied by clicking, popping, or difficulty opening and closing your mouth, this could be a sign of temporomandibular joint disorder (TMJ) and should be treated by an emergency dentist.

Caring for Your Dental Health Post-Emergency

Experiencing a dental emergency can be stressful, but once the immediate issue is resolved, recovery and aftercare are just as vital to one’s health. Taking the right steps after an emergency treatment not only helps speed up the healing process but also prevents future complications. Whether you have had a tooth fixed, an infection treated, or any other urgent dental care, knowing how to care for your dental health afterward will make a world of difference.

Some simple but effective ways to ensure a smooth recovery and keep your smile healthy for the long haul, include the following:

Caring for yourself properly after a dental emergency is just as important as seeking the help of an emergency dentist to fix the issue. Our team is happy to answer any question you may have regarding your care instructions and will ensure you leave our office confident in your ability to properly care for your mouth post-emergency.

  • Keep Your Mouth Clean: Ensuring you are keeping your mouth clean is one of the most important emergency post-op dental care tips to remember. After a dental emergency is resolved, your dentist may recommend that you avoid brushing your teeth for a period, instead recommending that you rinse your mouth with warm saltwater or any other mouthwash your dentist provides to help keep your mouth clean.
  • Eat Soft Foods: After a dental emergency, it is normal to experience some pain and discomfort in your mouth. Your dentist will likely recommend that you follow a soft diet that does not require too much chewing.
  • Avoid Smoking and Alcohol: Smoking and drinking alcohol can slow down the healing process after a dental emergency. For this reason, these activities should be avoided until your dentist says otherwise.
  • Follow Instructions:
  • Your dentist will provide you with specific instructions on how to care for your teeth and mouth after a dental emergency. You should follow these instructions carefully to ensure you heal properly and quickly.
  • Watch Out for Signs of Complications: If you are feeling more pain than normal or you notice abnormal discoloration around the surgery area, you should contact your dentist immediately.
