Dental Implants: Your Solution for Smile Perfection

Missing teeth can affect your confidence and oral function. At Sunrise Dental Brooklyn, our team of dental professionals provides durable, natural-looking dental implant solutions to restore your smile.

Our services are designed to improve both the function and appearance of your teeth, helping you eat, speak, and smile with renewed confidence. Using the latest technology and advanced techniques, we offer a full range of implant services, including:

We also provide preparatory procedures like bone grafting and sinus lifts to ensure your implants are placed with precision and care, leading to long-lasting results. Our goal is to create a personalized plan that gives you a complete and confident smile.

Dental implants are a long-lasting, natural-looking solution for missing teeth. They consist of a small titanium post that is surgically placed into your jawbone, acting as a new tooth root. A custom-made artificial tooth, designed to match the look and color of your natural teeth, is then attached to this post. Because they are permanently anchored, implants offer a secure and comfortable alternative to dentures.

Most healthy adults are great candidates for dental implants. You should have good overall health, healthy gums, and enough bone density in your jaw to support the implant. Implants are not recommended for children whose jaws are still growing.

Certain health conditions, like uncontrolled diabetes or leukemia, can slow down the healing process and may make you a less-than-ideal candidate. However, even if you have insufficient jawbone from missing teeth for a long time, our team can often help. We may be able to perform a bone grafting procedure , such as a ridge augmentation, to build up the bone and prepare the area for a successful implant.

Orthodontics is a dental specialty focused on correcting issues with crooked teeth, bad bites (malocclusions), and jaw alignment. While many people think of orthodontics as a cosmetic solution for kids, modern treatments like Invisalign have made it a popular choice for adults and teens as well.

Here are some common issues that orthodontics can fix:

Properly aligned teeth are not just for looks; they are crucial for your overall oral health. Misaligned teeth can make it difficult to chew, eat, and even speak properly. They also create hard-to-reach areas that are difficult to clean, which increases your risk for tooth decay and gum disease . Additionally, an improper bite can cause the protective enamel on your teeth to wear down and put extra stress on your gums and jawbone.

The days of only having one option for braces are long gone. Today, there are several effective treatments available, all working to correct misalignments and give you a straighter smile.

Clear Braces: A Discrete Choice

Clear aligners are a popular, discreet alternative to traditional braces. They are custom-made, clear trays that fit snugly over your teeth. Brands like Invisalign® have made them a top choice for adults and teens who want to straighten their teeth without the look of metal braces. The aligners are removable, so you can take them out when you eat, drink, brush, and floss.

Clear aligners are a great option for most adults and teens. However, they may not be suitable for younger patients whose teeth and jaws are still developing. Our team will perform a thorough examination and discuss your goals with you to determine the best orthodontic treatment for your unique needs.

Our team has special training in Invisalign treatments. If you’re interested in clear aligners, contact us to schedule a consultation.

  • Custom Aligners : Our team of dental professionals will take impressions of your teeth, which are then used to create a series of aligners. You’ll wear each set for a specific period (typically one to two weeks) before moving on to the next. For the best results, you’ll need to wear your aligners for 20-22 hours a day.
  • Progress Checks : You’ll have periodic checkups with our team, usually every six weeks, so we can monitor your progress and make sure your treatment is on track.
  • Completion : Most treatments take 12-18 months. Afterward, we may recommend a retainer to wear at night to keep your teeth from shifting back to their original positions

Root Canals

A root canal is a common procedure used to save a tooth that has infected or damaged pulp—the soft tissue inside your tooth that contains nerves and blood vessels. In the past, a tooth with this kind of damage would often have to be extracted. During the procedure, our team removes the infected pulp and seals the tooth to prevent further infection, allowing you to keep your natural tooth.

The pulp can become infected or damaged for several reasons, including:

You might need a root canal if you’re experiencing symptoms such as a mild to severe toothache, especially when chewing or biting down. Other signs include:

A root canal is typically completed in one or two appointments. The main goal is to clean out the damaged pulp and fill the tooth with a protective material. This saves your tooth and allows you to use it without pain.

  • A deep cavity or a large filling
  • A cracked, chipped, or broken tooth
  • A tooth abscess (a pus-filled pocket around the root)
  • Repeated dental procedures on the same tooth
  • Sensitivity to hot or cold foods
  • Red or swollen gums near the affected tooth
  • A pimple-like bump on your gum
  • Discoloration of the tooth

Sleep Apnea

Sleep apnea is a common but serious condition where your breathing repeatedly stops and starts while you sleep. This is often caused by the soft tissues in your throat collapsing and blocking your airway. People with sleep apnea often snore loudly or make choking sounds during the night.

If left untreated, sleep apnea can lead to serious health problems, including:

There are two main types of sleep apnea:

  • Stroke
  • High blood pressure
  • Heart attack and irregular heartbeat
  • Diabetes and depression
  • Excessive daytime drowsiness
  • Obstructive sleep apnea: The most common form, caused by a physical blockage in the airway.
  • Central sleep apnea: A less common type that happens when the brain fails to send the right signals to the muscles that control breathing.
  • Lifestyle changes: Simple adjustments like changing your sleeping position or losing weight can sometimes help.
  • CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure): A machine that delivers a constant flow of air through a mask, keeping your airway open.
  • Oral appliances: Custom-fitted devices that look like a mouthguard and help to support your jaw, keeping the airway clear.
