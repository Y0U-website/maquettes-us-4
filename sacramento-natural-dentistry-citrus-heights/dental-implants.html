Zirconia Dental Implants in Citrus Heights, CA

A dental implant is a biocompatible fixture placed within the jaw to serve as an artificial tooth root. It provides a stable base for a replacement tooth after one has been lost or removed. Once healing occurs, a custom-made restoration is secured to the implant, allowing the area to function as part of your natural bite. Unlike removable tooth replacements, an implant remains fixed in place and is designed to integrate with the surrounding bone under proper clinical conditions.

Missing teeth can affect chewing efficiency, bite balance, and long-term oral stability. Over time, neighbouring teeth may shift into the empty space, and the jawbone in that area may gradually lose density.

Because an implant replaces the root structure as well as the visible portion of the tooth, it provides structural support within the bone. Treatment is recommended based on functional and clinical considerations rather than appearance alone.

Removable dentures sit on the gums and are taken out for cleaning. While appropriate in many cases, they do not replace the underlying tooth root.

A traditional bridge relies on adjacent teeth for support and may require reshaping those teeth to anchor the restoration.

A dental implant, by contrast, is placed directly into the jawbone and functions independently. The appropriate option depends on bone levels, gum health, medical history, and patient preferences. A detailed assessment is necessary before making a recommendation.

Not every patient is an immediate candidate. In some cases, additional procedures may be discussed. In others, alternative treatment options may be more suitable.

Implant treatment is typically completed in stages. The implant post is first placed in the jawbone. A healing period follows to allow the bone to integrate with the implant surface. Once stability is confirmed, a custom restoration is attached.

The timeline varies depending on individual healing patterns and whether additional procedures are required. Your dental provider will explain each phase and expected recovery in advance.

Dental implants require ongoing maintenance similar to natural teeth. Daily brushing and flossing help maintain healthy gum tissue around the implant. Regular dental examinations allow for monitoring of bone support, bite alignment, and tissue health.

Although implants do not develop cavities, the surrounding structures can become inflamed if plaque accumulates. Consistent home care and professional follow-up are important for long-term stability.
