How Should You Care for Dental Implants?

It is essential to maintain a high level of commitment to at-home dental hygiene. You must also be committed to maintaining regular dental visits. Make sure to schedule professional exams and dental cleanings every 3-6 months or as directed by your dentist.

At each visit, Dr. Fu will compare the data collected on the periodontal chart, x-rays, and physical exam to identify any changes or concerns at the early stage. The goal is to maximize the service life of your dental implant restorations like your favorite car.

If not taken care of consistently and adequately through a daily home hygiene regimen and routine maintenance visits to the dentist, implants can develop problems. The earliest signs of potential concerns include pain, redness, and bleeding of the gum tissues around your dental implants, and your restoration may feel loose. This soft tissue condition, known as mucositis, is similar to gingivitis around the natural teeth. Gum inflammation can be successfully treated and reversed if identified in its early stages.

The primary purpose of implant maintenance is to prevent the onset of gum inflammation and, if needed, stop the progression from peri-implant mucositis to peri-implantitis. Similar to advanced gum disease, this devastating condition can lead to bone loss. Unfortunately, this is not reversible and may eventually lead to the failure and removal of the implant.

Daily care for your dental implants is similar to that of your natural teeth. Make sure to keep your implant restoration clean and plaque-free by brushing and flossing at least twice daily. Gently brush around the implant paying attention to all its sides, and make sure you brush your teeth after every meal.
