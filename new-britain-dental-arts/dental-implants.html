Types Of Dental Implants

There are a few different ways we can replace missing teeth. The option that’s best for you will depend on how many teeth you’re missing, where those teeth are located, and the health of your jawbone.

Single Dental Implant

As the name suggests, a single dental implant is a one-for-one tooth replacement option where we use one implant to replace one missing tooth. We can use this method in a number of instances, ranging from when you’re missing one tooth to when you’re missing a number of teeth throughout your mouth.

Implant-Supported Bridge

Using an implant-supported bridge allows us to close up gaps caused by multiple missing teeth in a row. With this option, we place dental implants on either side of the gap left by missing teeth. The bridge itself is an appliance consisting of a dental crown on each end and false teeth in between. Once we bond the crowns to the implants, the bridge will restore your smile and help you regain confidence. The implants will also prevent further bone loss in the jaw.

Implant-Supported Dentures

We can use implant-supported dentures to replace an entire arch of missing teeth on the upper or lower jaw. With this restoration, we strategically place implants along your arch and fit them with an abutment that allows your denture to snap into place. If we determine implant-supported dentures are best for you, know that your denture will be made to fit your mouth comfortably and securely.

Dental Implant Recovery

Because we place the implants below the gumline, we must make incisions. It’s important to care for your mouth during the recovery process so you don’t experience infection or interrupt the fusion process for the implant. If you experience discomfort or swelling, apply a cold compress to your cheek. You may also take over-the-counter medications to reduce your discomfort. Use a warm saltwater rinse to clean your mouth. Soft foods should be on your entire menu for at least a few days; foods that are especially crunchy, hard, or chewy can cause damage to your newly placed implant.

As we work through the entire dental implant process, our team will advise you on the best care for your mouth. We encourage you to ask questions along the way, too.
