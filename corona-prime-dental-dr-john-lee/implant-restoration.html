What Is Implant Restoration?

Implant restoration refers to repairing or replacing the visible portion of a dental implant after the implant post has already been placed in the jawbone.

The implant itself serves as the artificial root, while the restoration replaces the visible tooth structure.

These restorations are custom-made to match the appearance and function of natural teeth.

  • Implant crowns
  • Implant-supported bridges
  • Implant-supported dentures

When Is Implant Restoration Needed?

Implant restoration may be required for several reasons.

Dentists evaluate the condition of the implant and surrounding tissues to determine the appropriate restoration.

  • Replacement of a worn or damaged implant crown
  • Repair of a loose implant restoration
  • Upgrading an older implant restoration
  • Restoring implants placed by another dental provider
  • Completing the final restoration after implant placement

Types of Implant Restorations

Several types of restorations can be used depending on the number of missing teeth and the type of implant placed.

Implant Crown

An implant crown replaces a single missing tooth and is attached to the implant through an abutment.

This restoration closely resembles a natural tooth in both appearance and function.

Implant-Supported Bridge

Implant-supported bridges replace multiple missing teeth by attaching artificial teeth to two or more dental implants.

This option provides increased stability compared to traditional bridges.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Implant-supported dentures attach securely to dental implants, providing improved stability compared to removable dentures.

  • Reduced denture movement
  • Increased chewing ability
  • Greater comfort and confidence

Implant Restoration FAQ

What is the difference between a dental implant and an implant restoration?

The dental implant is the titanium post placed in the jawbone, while the implant restoration is the crown, bridge, or denture attached to the implant.

How long do implant restorations last?

Implant crowns and restorations typically last 10 to 15 years or longer with proper care.

Can an implant crown be replaced?

Yes. If an implant crown becomes damaged or worn, it can be replaced without removing the implant post.

Is implant restoration painful?

The procedure is typically comfortable, and local anesthesia may be used if necessary.

Can you restore implants placed by another dentist?

Yes. Many dental offices provide implant restoration for implants placed by other providers.
