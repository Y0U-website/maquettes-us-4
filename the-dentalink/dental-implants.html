Improve Your Life With Teeth Implants

Although dental implants have been around in one form or another for hundreds of years, in the last few decades implant technology has grown by leaps and bounds. Today, teeth implants are generally made from medical-grade titanium and are surgically placed into a patient’s jaw.

Dr. Link will design your implant procedure beforehand, then use a 3-D printer to create a detailed model to guide your surgery. Afterward, he can secure a dental restoration to your implants that could potentially last for several decades! Your options for implant restoration may include:

  • Single Implant Crowns – A porcelain or zirconia crown anchored to an implant will replace a single missing tooth. Using our CEREC system, we can design your new crown and make it with our own milling station located at our other dental practice across town.
  • Implant-Supported Bridges – We can use two or more implants to secure a sturdy bridge, replacing several missing teeth in a row. You’ll enjoy having a complete smile as well as renewed biting power.
  • Implant-Supported Dentures – Implants can anchor a set of dentures, making your new teeth more stable and comfortable than traditional removable dentures could ever be. They will secure to a bar that is in turn screwed to your implants.
  • Fixed Full Arch Replacement Teeth – Using a minimal number of implants (usually four), we can attach a complete arch of replacement teeth. They will look natural and give you back virtually all your biting and chewing capacity.
  • Same-Day Smile – You could potentially receive your new implants and replacement teeth in just one appointment – without waiting several months in between.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

What are dental implants, and why are they considered one of the best tooth replacement options?

Dental implants replace both the visible tooth and its root, creating a stable, natural-feeling solution for missing teeth. A titanium implant fuses with the jawbone and supports a custom crown , bridge , or denture . Unlike removable options, implants help preserve bone, restore chewing ability, and prevent nearby teeth from shifting. With proper care, they can provide a durable, attractive smile that supports your long-term oral health and confidence.

Am I a candidate for dental implants if I’ve been missing teeth for years?

Many patients who have lived with missing teeth for years are still candidates for dental implants. During your consultation, your dentist will evaluate your jawbone, gum health, and overall medical history to determine the best approach. Even if bone loss has occurred, there may still be solutions available, including mini dental implants for qualifying patients. A personalized evaluation is the best way to learn which treatment option fits your smile.

Is getting a dental implant painful, and what is recovery like?

Most patients are surprised by how comfortable dental implant treatment is. The procedure is performed using local anesthesia, and many people experience only mild soreness afterward. Some swelling or tenderness is normal for a few days and is usually manageable with medication and home care instructions. While the implant continues bonding with the jawbone over the following months, many patients return to work and regular daily activities within a day or two.

Can dental implants replace multiple missing teeth or full dentures?

Yes. Dental implants are extremely versatile and can substitute one missing tooth, several teeth, or an entire arch. A single implant supports an individual crown, while multiple implants can anchor bridges or implant-supported dentures for greater stability. Some patients may also qualify for a fixed full-arch restoration supported by only a few strategically placed implants. Your dentist will recommend the option that best suits your oral health and smile goals.

How long do dental implants last with proper care?

Dental implants are designed to be a long-lasting investment in your smile. The implant itself can often last for decades when supported by healthy gums and consistent oral hygiene. Daily brushing, flossing, and routine dental checkups are essential to maintaining their success. While the crown or denture attached to the implant may eventually need replacement because of normal wear, the implant foundation is built for exceptional durability.

What is the difference between traditional and mini dental implants?

Traditional dental implants are larger and typically require adequate jawbone for support. Mini dental implants have a smaller diameter and may be recommended for patients with reduced bone volume or those seeking a less invasive procedure. They often involve a shorter healing period while still providing reliable support for certain restorations. Your dentist will determine which option is appropriate based on your anatomy, oral health, and treatment goals.

How soon should I replace a missing tooth with a dental implant?

It’s generally best to replace a missing tooth as soon as possible. Waiting too long can allow neighboring teeth to shift and may lead to gradual bone loss in the jaw, making future treatment more complicated. Scheduling an implant consultation early helps your dentist evaluate your options before additional changes occur. Prompt treatment can protect your oral health while restoring your ability to smile, speak, and chew comfortably.
