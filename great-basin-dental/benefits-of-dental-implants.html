Benefits of Dental Implants Reno, NV

Losing a tooth can affect more than your smile. A missing tooth may make it harder to chew certain foods, change the way you speak, or leave you feeling self-conscious when you smile. Fortunately, you don’t have to live with those changes permanently. Dental implants in Reno, NV offer a long-term way to replace missing teeth while restoring much of the function and appearance of natural teeth.

Unlike a traditional bridge or removable denture, a dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth as well as the visible portion. This allows the replacement tooth to attach securely to your jawbone. For many patients, that stability is one of the biggest advantages of choosing dental implants. Great Basin Dental recommends dental implants as the ideal choice for tooth replacement if a patient is eligible.

Dental Implants Look and Feel Natural

One of the main benefits of dental implants is how closely they can resemble natural teeth. The implant itself sits beneath the gumline, while a custom-made dental crown is placed on top to complete the restoration. Your dentist can design the crown to complement the size, shape, and color of your surrounding teeth.

Because the implant is anchored in the jaw, it doesn’t need to be held in place with adhesives or clasps. Once the treatment is complete and the area has healed, you can smile, talk, and eat without constantly thinking about your replacement tooth.

Improved Ability to Eat and Chew

A missing tooth can make some foods more difficult to eat. You may find yourself chewing on one side of your mouth or avoiding foods that are crunchy, firm, or chewy.

Dental implants provide a stable foundation for replacement teeth. Unlike removable dentures that can shift while you eat, an implant-supported restoration is secured in place. This can make everyday meals more comfortable and allow you to enjoy a wider variety of foods with greater confidence.

Dental Implants Help Preserve Jawbone

Your teeth do more than help you chew. Their roots also stimulate the surrounding jawbone. When a tooth is lost, that stimulation disappears, and the bone in that area can gradually shrink.

Dental implants are designed to replace the missing tooth root. The implant is placed into the jawbone, where it can provide the stimulation associated with a natural tooth root. Preserving the bone can help maintain the structure of your jaw and support the surrounding teeth.

They Can Help Protect Your Remaining Teeth

A gap left by a missing tooth can affect the teeth around it. Neighboring teeth may gradually shift into the open space, potentially changing your bite and making it harder to keep certain areas clean.

Replacing the missing tooth can help maintain the spacing of your smile. An implant-supported crown fills the gap without requiring the neighboring teeth to be filed down for support, as may be necessary with some traditional dental bridges.

Dental Implants Are Designed for Long-Term Use

Another significant advantage of dental implants is their durability. With proper care and regular dental visits, dental implants can provide a long-lasting tooth replacement solution.

That doesn’t mean an implant is maintenance-free. You still need to brush, floss, and attend routine dental appointments. Taking care of your gums and the surrounding teeth is an important part of protecting your investment and keeping your smile healthy.

Improved Confidence in Your Smile

Tooth loss can affect how comfortable you feel in social situations. You might hesitate to smile for a photograph, cover your mouth when talking, or worry that other people will notice a missing tooth.

Replacing a missing tooth with an implant can help you feel more comfortable showing your smile. Since the restoration is designed to blend with your natural teeth and remain securely in place, you don’t have to worry about the replacement tooth slipping out while you’re talking or laughing.

Dental Implants Can Replace One or Multiple Missing Teeth

Implants aren’t limited to replacing a single tooth. Depending on your needs, they can also be used to support other types of restorations for patients who are missing several teeth or an entire arch.

For patients with multiple missing teeth, implant-supported bridges or dentures may provide greater stability than traditional removable options. Your dentist can evaluate your oral health, jawbone, and goals to determine which approach makes sense for you.

Although dental implants offer many advantages, they aren’t automatically the best choice for every patient. Your dentist will need to evaluate the health of your gums and jawbone and consider factors such as your medical and dental history. Some patients may need periodontal treatment or a bone graft before implant placement.
