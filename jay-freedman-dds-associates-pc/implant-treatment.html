What Is Implant Treatment?

Dental implants are artificial tooth roots, usually made from titanium, which are surgically inserted into the jawbone. These implants fuse with the bone over time, providing a stable base to support replacement teeth. Once the implant has integrated into the bone, an abutment and crown are placed on top, creating a lifelike restoration that blends in seamlessly with natural teeth.

Implants can replace a single tooth, multiple teeth, or even an entire set of upper or lower teeth, offering great flexibility in treatment options. Due to their durability, function, and appearance, they have become the gold standard for tooth replacement. Contact us to learn more.

The Benefits of Implant Treatment

There are numerous benefits to choosing implant treatment over traditional methods like dentures or bridges. These include:

By choosing implant treatment in Abington, PA, patients gain access to these benefits with the support and expertise of our local professional.

  • Natural Look and Feel : Implants are designed to look and function like natural teeth, giving patients a confident, authentic smile.
  • Longevity : With proper care, implants can last a lifetime, making them a highly durable solution.
  • Preservation of Jawbone : Implants stimulate the jawbone, preventing the bone loss that typically occurs when a tooth is missing.
  • Enhanced Functionality : Unlike dentures, implants are fixed in place so patients can eat, speak, and chew without worrying about shifting or discomfort.
  • Protection of Adjacent Teeth : Traditional bridges often require neighboring teeth to be ground down, but implants do not rely on adjacent teeth for support, preserving their structure.

Who Is a Candidate for Implant Treatment?

While dental implants offer a versatile solution, they are not suitable for everyone. Generally, candidates should have healthy gums, adequate bone density in the jaw, and be free from chronic illnesses that may impair healing. For those with insufficient bone, advanced techniques such as bone grafting can often prepare the area for implants, expanding eligibility.

During an initial consultation, our dentist in Abington, PA, will evaluate your oral health and overall medical history to determine if implant treatment is right for you. He will also take X-rays or 3D scans to assess the jawbone and surrounding structures, ensuring a personalized approach to treatment.

The Implant Treatment Process

Initial Consultation and Planning

The process begins with a comprehensive examination, including jaw imaging. Our dentist in Abington, PA, will discuss your goals and outline the steps involved in the procedure.

Implant Placement

Once a treatment plan is established, the implant will be surgically placed into the jawbone. This is typically done under local anesthesia, though sedation options are available for patients who may need additional comfort during the procedure.

Healing and Osseointegration

A healing period is required after the implant is placed. During this time, the implant will undergo a process known as osseointegration, where it fuses with the jawbone. This integration is crucial, ensuring the implant is secure and can support restoration. This phase can take several weeks to a few months.

Abutment Placement and Crown Fitting

Once the implant is securely integrated, an abutment will be attached to it. This abutment will serve as the base for the final crown. The custom crown will then be placed on the abutment, completing the restoration and giving the patient a fully functional and natural-looking tooth replacement.

Post-Treatment Care for Implants

Caring for implants is much like caring for natural teeth. Good oral hygiene practices, including brushing and flossing, will help maintain the health of the implant and surrounding gums. Regular check-ups with our dentist in Abington, PA, ensure the implant remains healthy and stable.

In some cases, a simple restorative approach, like a dental crown, may be used to complement the implant, especially if surrounding teeth need to be restored. This combination of implant and restorative dentistry supports aesthetics and function, ensuring a comprehensive treatment plan.

Alternatives to Implant Treatment

While dental implants are a preferred solution, they may not be suitable for everyone. Alternatives include dentures and fixed dental bridges. However, these options often lack the longevity, stability, and natural appearance of implants. Unlike implants, dentures can sometimes feel bulky and require adhesives to stay in place, while bridges often require altering neighboring teeth.
