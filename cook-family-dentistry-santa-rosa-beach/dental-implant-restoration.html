Restoring the Crown on a Healthy Existing Implant

Repair the visible part of your implant without replacing the post

A dental implant has three parts: the titanium post anchored in the jawbone, the abutment that connects to the post, and the crown that sits on top. Over time, the crown or abutment can loosen, fracture, chip, or wear down, even when the implant post itself is still healthy and well integrated with the bone. Many patients believe a problem with their implant crown means they need a whole new implant, but that is rarely the case.

At Cook Family Dentistry, Dr. Cook uses CBCT 3D imaging to verify that your implant post and surrounding bone are still in good shape, then plans a new crown or abutment to fit the existing implant. For travelers visiting Santa Rosa Beach and the surrounding communities , we offer prompt appointments and clear communication with your home dentist. If the implant post itself has failed, we will be honest with you and discuss full implant replacement options.

What Is Dental Implant Restoration ?

How we repair or replace the crown on an existing implant

Dental implant restoration is the process of repairing or replacing the prosthetic parts of a dental implant, specifically the abutment and the crown, while keeping the existing titanium implant post in place. It is the right choice when the implant body has fused with the bone successfully (a process called osseointegration), but the visible crown has been damaged, lost, or has aged out of its useful lifespan.

The treatment is not the same as placing a new implant. There is no surgery on the bone, no healing period of several months, and no need to graft. In most cases, Dr. Cook can unscrew or remove the old crown, take a digital impression of the abutment with the Primescan scanner, and either cement or screw a new crown into position. Many patients are surprised by how straightforward the visit is compared with their original implant placement.

Treatment Timeline

Restoring an existing implant usually takes just 1 to 2 short visits with little to no recovery. Here s what to expect during the process.

  • Treatment Time Typically 30 to 60 minutes per visit
  • Visits Needed Usually 1 to 2 visits depending on the lab workflow
  • Healing Time Minimal, no surgical recovery required when the post is healthy
  • Imaging Used CBCT 3D scan to confirm implant integrity and bone level
  • Expected Lifespan A well-made implant crown can last 10 to 15 years or longer

Broken or Cracked Crown

Chipped Crown

Worn Down Crown

Aesthetic Mismatch

Older crown no longer matches the color of surrounding teeth

Aging Restorations

Implant crowns 10 or more years old that are due for replacement

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implant Restoration

Dental implant restoration is the right path when the titanium implant body is still healthy but the visible crown or abutment needs attention.

Dr. Cook will review your CBCT scan, exam, and history before recommending a crown only, an abutment update, or a full implant replacement, whichever protects your long-term oral health.

  • Adults with a loose, broken, chipped, or worn crown on an existing implant
  • Patients whose original implant placement healed well and shows no signs of infection
  • Snowbirds and visitors needing a same-week implant repair while in the area
  • Patients with healthy gums and adequate bone level around the implant
  • Anyone with an implant crown 10 or more years old that is showing signs of wear
  • Patients with active infection, abscess, or peri-implantitis around the implant
  • Implants with visible mobility, bone loss, or fractured posts that may need removal
  • Untreated severe gum disease that should be stabilized first
  • Pregnancy in the first trimester (we often delay elective restorations until later if possible)

Your Implant Restoration Appointment , Step by Step

Dr. Cook uses digital impressions and 3D imaging to confirm implant integrity, then delivers a precise new crown - often completing your restoration in just one to two comfortable visits.

Exam and CBCT Imaging

Dr. Cook reviews your implant with CBCT 3D imaging to confirm the post and bone are healthy.

Crown Removal

Dr. Cook carefully removes the existing crown or abutment, preserving the implant post.

Digital Impression

Dr. Cook captures a 3D scan of the abutment and surrounding teeth with the Primescan digital scanner.

Lab Crafted or Same-Day Crown

Dr. Cook places a temporary if needed, then receives a custom new crown shaded to match your smile.

Final Crown Placement

Dr. Cook checks the fit, bite, and color, then cements or screws the new crown into place.

Common Side Effects

Side effects are usually mild and short lived. After your visit you may notice:

  • Mild gum tenderness around the implant for a day or two
  • A brief sense that your bite is slightly different, which Dr. Cook can adjust quickly
  • Light sensitivity to cold or hot in nearby natural teeth for a few days
  • Minor soreness if the area was numbed for the appointment

Rare Risks & Complications

In rare cases, an underlying issue with the implant post may need a more involved repair, and Dr. Cook will discuss your options honestly.
