FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Check out these frequently asked questions, or call us to speak with our team.

What are the signs that my child may need orthodontic treatment?

If your child shows one or more of the following signs and symptoms, they may be a good candidate for orthodontic treatment:

Our team is happy to work directly with you and your child to assess these symptoms before they become severe issues over time

  • Underbites
  • Crossbites
  • Crowded teeth
  • Excessively gapped teeth
  • Extra or missing teeth
  • Excessive thumb, finger, or pacifier sucking

What are the benefits of children’s orthodontics?

Early orthodontic treatment can be beneficial when a developing issue is likely to worsen over time or lead to more complicated and lengthy treatment later on. The goal of children’s orthodontics is to identify and improve—or even eliminate—these problems early, allowing the teeth and jaw to grow in alignment and preserving space for future adult teeth.

What causes orthodontic problems in my child?

While many orthodontic problems are inherited, some develop over time due to common habits like thumb sucking, finger sucking, or excessive pacifier use. Other factors such as mouth breathing, dental disease, abnormal swallowing, poor dental hygiene, early or late loss of baby teeth, and dental accidents can also affect your child’s teeth and jaw alignment. No matter the cause, our team will cater to your child’s unique needs and teach them healthy habits for a lifetime of optimal oral health.

What age should my child be seen by an orthodontist?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends that children have their first orthodontic evaluation by age 7—or earlier if any concerns arise. By this age, enough permanent teeth have typically erupted to give the orthodontist a clear view of how the teeth and jaws are developing. This early insight allows for a personalized approach to care, helping to guide your child’s smile as they grow.

Do all children benefit from early orthodontic treatment?

Not always. Some dental and jaw concerns are best treated during adolescence, once all permanent teeth have come in. If treatment isn’t indicated yet, Dr. Nova will enroll your child in our complimentary observation program to monitor their development until the time is right to start.

What is the orthodontic observation program?

Our observation program is a free service designed to monitor your child’s dental and jaw growth over time. With checkups every 6 to 12 months, Dr. Nova tracks the eruption of permanent teeth and any developing bite or alignment issues. This proactive care helps ensure that if treatment is needed later, it begins at the ideal time for the best outcome.

Does early interceptive treatment prevent the need for braces later?

Not always. Phase 1 treatment focuses on addressing specific early concerns—like crossbites, severe crowding, or jaw growth issues—but most kids still need Phase 2 treatment during adolescence. Early intervention can reduce the complexity and length of future treatment by creating space and improving function early on.

How much does children’s orthodontics cost?

The cost of children’s orthodontics is different for each patient. Factors that may affect the cost include the severity of your child’s teeth misalignment, appointment and visits, insurance type, and more. The best way to find out how much you’ll pay for your child’s care is to schedule a consultation with your orthodontist.

The types of children&#x27;s orthodontics

Palatal Expander Palatal expanders are metal orthodontic appliances placed on the roof of your child’s mouth to widen the palate over time. These appliances expand narrow palates while aligning the upper teeth and jaw before it fully develops.

Partial Braces Made from medical-grade stainless steel, some Phase 1 treatments require a limited number of brackets and wires to gently move select teeth into better alignment. Over time, this targeted approach can lead to noticeable improvements in your child’s smile and dental development.

Habit Appliances Habit appliances are devices designed to help gently discourage habits like thumb sucking and tongue thrusting—supporting healthy jaw development and proper tooth eruption as your child grows.

Space Maintainer A space maintainer is a dental appliance used to hold space open when a baby tooth is lost too early. It helps prevent nearby teeth from shifting into the empty space, which can block the path for the permanent tooth to come in. Holding a space open is much easier than trying to regain lost space later on!

Headgear Headgear is an external appliance used in select cases to help guide jaw growth—supporting proper bite alignment and facial development during your child’s orthodontic treatment.

Invisalign In some cases, Phase 1 treatment can be successfully done using Invisalign First clear aligners. These custom-made trays gently guide developing teeth into better positions and can be a comfortable, removable alternative to braces—especially for younger patients who are eager to be good helpers during treatment!

Retainers

Once treatment is complete, we create personalized retainers to maintain your child&#x27;s beautiful new smile. We offer both digital scan options for maximum comfort and same-day retainers when needed. These custom-fitted appliances are designed specifically for your child&#x27;s unique dental structure, ensuring long-term stability of their orthodontic results.

If treatment is recommended, we’ll review the proposed plan in detail and answer any questions so you feel confident about the next steps.

Starting Phase 1 Treatment

If Phase 1 treatment is recommended after the consultation, the next step is to schedule your child&#x27;s braces or appliance placement appointment. At this visit, we’ll place any appliances—such as partial braces, a palatal expander, or Invisalign First aligners—and go over instructions for care and hygiene.

Follow Up Appointments

After your child’s braces or appliance is placed, we’ll schedule regular adjustment visits to monitor progress and make any necessary adjustments to their braces. These appointments help ensure everything is moving as planned. We’ll also provide tips for keeping teeth clean and comfortable. Most Phase 1 treatments are completed within 9-12 months.
