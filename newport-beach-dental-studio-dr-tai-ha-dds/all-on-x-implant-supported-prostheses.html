All-on-X Dental Implants in Newport Beach

All-on-X dental implants in Newport Beach can replace an entire arch of failing or missing teeth with a fixed implant-supported restoration that is designed specifically for your face, bite, and smile.

At Newport Beach Dental Studio, Dr. Tai Ha approaches full-arch implant treatment as a comprehensive restorative case rather than simply placing a set of teeth on implants.

The implant positions, bite, tooth proportions, lip support, smile line, facial appearance, materials, and final prosthesis all need to work together. Therefore, we carefully plan the case digitally and coordinate treatment with the surgical and laboratory team before surgery takes place.

What Is the All-on-X Procedure?

All-on-X is a full-arch dental implant concept in which several implants support a fixed prosthesis replacing all of the teeth in the upper jaw, lower jaw, or both.

The “X” means the number of implants is not automatically the same for every patient.

Depending on bone volume, anatomy, implant stability, bite forces, prosthetic design, and the surgical plan, an arch may be supported by four, five, six, or another appropriate number of implants.

The goal is to place the implants where they can support the planned final restoration—not simply to use the fewest implants possible.

All-on-4 vs. All-on-X: What Is the Difference?

“All-on-4” describes a full arch supported by four implants. “All-on-X” is a broader term because the number of implants is selected according to the individual case.

For example, Dr. Ha and the surgical team may decide that five or six implants provide a better restorative plan for a particular patient.

Therefore, we do not start by deciding how many implants you should receive. We first design the desired prosthesis and evaluate the available anatomy. Implant number and position follow from that plan.

Who May Be a Candidate for Full-Arch Dental Implants?

All-on-X is generally considered for patients whose existing teeth can no longer be predictably maintained or for patients who already have extensive tooth loss.

We Do Not Recommend Removing Healthy Teeth Just to Do All-on-X

Full-arch implant treatment is a major decision because teeth that are removed cannot be replaced biologically.

If individual teeth can be maintained predictably with reasonable treatment, preserving natural teeth may still be the better option.

However, when most or all of the teeth in an arch have a poor prognosis, repeatedly repairing one failing tooth at a time may no longer make sense.

Our goal is to evaluate the entire mouth and recommend the treatment that makes sense for the overall condition rather than automatically recommending full-arch implants.

Can You Get Teeth the Same Day as Implant Surgery?

In many appropriately selected All-on-X cases, a fixed temporary prosthesis can be attached to the implants on the day of surgery or shortly afterward.

This is commonly called immediate loading or “teeth in a day.”

However, same-day fixed teeth are not guaranteed. The implants need adequate stability, and the surgical and restorative conditions must support immediate loading.

If those conditions are not met, a different temporary restoration or healing approach may be safer.

Your Same-Day Teeth Are Usually Temporary

The teeth delivered around the time of surgery are generally not the final zirconia prosthesis.

Instead, the temporary restoration allows the implants and surrounding tissues to heal while you begin living with the new tooth position, bite, smile, and overall design.

This healing period is useful because we can evaluate esthetics, speech, tooth position, hygiene access, and function before creating the final prosthesis.

Our Approach: Design the Smile Before the Surgery

At Newport Beach Dental Studio, we believe the final prosthesis should guide the surgical plan—not the other way around.

Before surgery, we collect records that may include digital scans, photographs, three-dimensional imaging, bite information, and facial references. These records allow us to plan the teeth in relation to your lips, face, smile line, and functional bite.

We can then coordinate the implant positions around the prosthetic design. This restorative-driven approach helps the surgical and dental teams work toward the same final result.

Why We Often Use Zirconia for the Final Teeth

After the implants have healed, the temporary restoration is replaced with the definitive prosthesis.

High-strength dental zirconia is frequently used for final full-arch restorations because it can provide strength, polishability, color stability, and lifelike dental anatomy.

However, material selection is individualized. The best restorative material depends on the bite, available restorative space, implant positions, opposing teeth, esthetic requirements, and other clinical factors.

Are All-on-X Teeth Permanent?

Patients often use the word “permanent” because the prosthesis is fixed in the mouth and is not removed each night like a conventional denture.

A more accurate term is fixed implant-supported prosthesis .

The restoration is secured to the dental implants and is normally removed only by a dental professional when maintenance, repair, or clinical access requires it.

The implants and prosthesis can function for many years, but neither should be described as guaranteed to last for life.

All-on-X vs. Traditional Dentures

Traditional Removable Denture

A conventional denture rests primarily on the gums and is removed by the patient. Movement, changes in fit, and reduced chewing efficiency can occur over time.

Fixed All-on-X Prosthesis

A full-arch prosthesis is secured to dental implants. It does not rely only on the gums for support and is not removed by the patient during normal daily use.
