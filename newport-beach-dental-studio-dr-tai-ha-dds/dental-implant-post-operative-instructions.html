Dental Implant Post-Operative Instructions

Proper dental implant aftercare helps protect the surgical site while your gums and bone begin healing around the implant.

Some bleeding, swelling, bruising, tenderness, and soreness can occur after dental implant surgery. Your recovery will depend on the number and location of implants, whether teeth were removed, whether bone grafting was performed, and your individual health.

If your surgeon or Dr. Ha gave you instructions that differ from the general information below, follow the instructions provided specifically for your surgery.

The First 24 Hours Are Important

The surgical site is beginning the initial healing process during the first day.

Avoid vigorous rinsing, forceful spitting, disturbing the area with your fingers or tongue, and unnecessary pressure directly over the implant.

Drink from a cup rather than using a straw, and choose foods that require minimal chewing.

Dental Implant Aftercare Instructions

Light bleeding or blood-tinged saliva can occur after surgery. If you were given gauze, place it directly over the surgical area and maintain gentle, steady pressure as instructed. Avoid repeatedly removing the gauze to check the site. If bleeding is heavy or does not improve with pressure, contact the surgical office.

Apply a cold pack to the outside of the face near the surgical area for short intervals during the first 24 to 72 hours as needed. Do not place ice directly against the skin. Swelling and bruising can occur even when healing normally.

Follow the medication schedule provided by your dentist or surgeon. Depending on your health history, over-the-counter ibuprofen, acetaminophen, prescription medication, or a combination may be recommended. Do not exceed the labeled or prescribed dosage.

During early healing, choose foods that require minimal chewing and avoid placing direct chewing pressure over the implant site. Gradually return to firmer foods according to your comfort and the instructions provided for your specific surgery.

Drink adequate fluids throughout the day. Use a cup rather than a straw during the early healing period because strong suction can disturb a fresh surgical site.

Continue cleaning the rest of your mouth, but be gentle near the surgical area. Do not aggressively brush directly across fresh stitches, graft material, or the implant site unless you have specifically been instructed to do so.

Avoid forceful rinsing during the initial healing period. Beginning after the first day, gentle warm salt-water rinsing may be recommended. If you were prescribed an antimicrobial rinse such as chlorhexidine, use it according to your instructions.

Avoid strenuous exercise, heavy lifting, and activities that substantially raise your heart rate during the first few days. Increased physical activity can contribute to bleeding, throbbing, and swelling.

Tobacco, nicotine, smoking, and vaping can impair blood flow and interfere with soft-tissue and bone healing. Avoid them during the healing period. Smoking is also associated with a higher risk of dental implant complications and failure.

Alcohol can interfere with healing and may interact with pain medications, sedatives, or antibiotics. Avoid alcohol while taking medications that should not be combined with it and during the immediate surgical recovery period.

If antibiotics or other medications were prescribed, follow the instructions on your prescription. Antibiotics are not automatically required after every implant procedure, so only take them when prescribed for you.

Follow-up visits allow your dental team to evaluate the incision, gum tissue, implant site, grafting when applicable, and overall healing before progressing to the next phase of treatment.

What Should I Eat After Dental Implant Surgery?

For the first few days, choose foods that are easy to eat without significant chewing or pressure on the implant site.

If You Have a Temporary Tooth on the Implant

A temporary crown may sometimes be placed on an implant immediately after surgery for appearance.

That does not automatically mean the implant is ready for normal chewing forces.

If you were instructed not to bite on the temporary restoration, follow that instruction carefully while the implant integrates with the surrounding bone.

Patients receiving an intentionally immediately loaded implant restoration or full-arch prosthesis may receive different dietary instructions from their surgeon.

How Should I Brush After Implant Surgery?

Good oral hygiene is important, but the surgical area should not be traumatized.

Continue brushing the teeth away from the surgical site normally. Near the implant, stitches, or bone graft, use the technique and timing recommended by your surgical team.

Do not aggressively scrub fresh incisions or try to pull the lip or cheek excessively to inspect the area.

If you were given a prescription rinse, use it according to the prescribed directions rather than rinsing more frequently in an attempt to make the site heal faster.

Do Not Judge Implant Healing Only by How the Gum Looks

The surface gum tissue can appear healed before the implant has fully integrated with the bone underneath it.

Osseointegration generally takes substantially longer than the initial soft-tissue healing period. For this reason, do not begin chewing aggressively on an implant simply because the gum feels comfortable.

Pain Medication, Aspirin & Blood Thinners

Only take pain medication that is appropriate for your medical history and has been recommended by your dentist, surgeon, physician, or pharmacist.

Aspirin may contribute to bleeding in some surgical situations. However, if you take aspirin, an anticoagulant, or another blood-thinning medication because it was prescribed by a physician, do not stop it on your own.

Any change to a medically necessary medication should be coordinated with the clinician who prescribed it and your surgical provider.

Antibiotics After Dental Implant Surgery

Not every dental implant patient requires postoperative antibiotics.

If an antibiotic was prescribed, take it exactly as directed. Do not take leftover antibiotics from another procedure or another person’s prescription.

If you develop a suspected medication reaction, contact your healthcare provider. Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction, such as difficulty breathing or rapidly developing swelling of the face or throat, require emergency medical attention.

If Bone Grafting Was Performed

Your implant procedure may have included a bone graft.

Small particles of graft material may occasionally be noticed in the mouth depending on the type of graft and surgical technique. However, you should not manipulate or intentionally disturb the grafted area.

Follow any additional brushing, rinsing, dietary, membrane, or denture instructions provided by your surgeon because grafting procedures vary considerably.

If a Sinus Lift or Sinus Graft Was Performed

Implants in the back of the upper jaw sometimes involve the maxillary sinus. If you underwent a sinus lift or sinus graft, you may receive additional sinus precautions.

These instructions may include avoiding nose blowing and activities that create excessive sinus pressure. Follow the specific sinus precautions given by your surgeon rather than relying solely on these general implant instructions.
