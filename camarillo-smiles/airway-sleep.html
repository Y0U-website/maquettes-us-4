Airway & Sleep Medicine

Common Questions Airway & Sleep FAQ Can my dentist really help with sleep apnea? Yes. Dentists trained in airway medicine play a critical role in diagnosing and treating sleep-disordered breathing. We offer home sleep tests, oral appliances, airway expansion (Vivos), laser therapy (NightLase), and myofunctional therapy, all of which are evidence-based treatments for snoring and sleep apnea. We also coordinate with sleep physicians when medical management is appropriate.

  • Diagnosed or suspected sleep apnea
  • CPAP intolerance or non-compliance
  • Waking up exhausted despite a full night s sleep
  • Daytime fatigue, brain fog, or difficulty concentrating
  • Children with Airway Concerns Mouth breathing at rest or during sleep
  • Snoring, teeth grinding, or restless sleep
  • Bedwetting beyond typical ages
  • Attention, focus, or behavioral concerns
  • Crowded teeth or underdeveloped jaw
  • Do I need a referral to be seen for airway concerns? No referral is needed. You can schedule a new patient appointment or request an airway-specific consultation directly. We screen all new patients for airway and sleep concerns as part of our comprehensive exam. If you already have a sleep study report from your doctor, bring it and we can review it together.
  • Is airway treatment covered by insurance? Coverage varies. Oral appliances for sleep apnea are often covered under medical insurance (not dental), especially when accompanied by a sleep study and diagnosis. Laser treatments, Vivos appliances, and myofunctional therapy may be partially covered depending on your plan. Our team will review your benefits and explain your options before any treatment begins.
  • How long does airway treatment take? It depends on the treatment. NightLase consists of three 20-minute sessions spaced 3 weeks apart. Oral appliances are typically delivered within 2–3 weeks. Vivos palate expansion is a longer commitment, usually 12–24 months, but addresses the root cause structurally. We ll give you a clear timeline at your consultation.
  • Do you treat children for airway issues? Absolutely, and early intervention makes a significant difference. Many children with chronic mouth breathing, snoring, bedwetting, or behavioral concerns have an underlying airway issue. We see children of all ages and offer age-appropriate treatments including palate expansion, frenectomy (tongue/lip tie release), and myofunctional therapy referrals.
  • What s the difference between a CPAP and an oral appliance? A CPAP machine delivers pressurized air to keep the airway open. It is highly effective when used consistently, but roughly 50 % of patients abandon it within a year due to discomfort. An oral appliance repositions the lower jaw to mechanically open the airway during sleep, and is quieter, portable, and more comfortable for most patients. For mild to moderate sleep apnea, oral appliances are an excellent CPAP alternative. We can discuss which option fits your diagnosis and lifestyle.
