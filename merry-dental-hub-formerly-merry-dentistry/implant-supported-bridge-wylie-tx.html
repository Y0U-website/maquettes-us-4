Choosing the Right Implant Solution

Wylie patients come to Merry Dental Hub having been quoted everything from a single implant to All-on-4, often without a clear explanation of why. Here’s the honest clinical breakdown for each situation — so you arrive at your consultation already informed.

Single Tooth Implant

The ideal permanent solution for one missing tooth — anywhere in your mouth

One lost tooth — whether from decay, fracture, or gum disease — often starts a cascade: adjacent teeth drift, the opposing tooth over-erupts, and bone at the gap begins dissolving within weeks. A single implant stops all of that. At Merry Dental Hub, Dr. C places a titanium post, attaches a custom abutment, and seats a porcelain or zirconia crown matched to your surrounding teeth. Patients from Murphy and Sachse consistently tell us they forget which tooth was replaced.

Implant-Supported Bridge

Replace 2–4 consecutive missing teeth without grinding healthy adjacent teeth

For two to four consecutive missing teeth, an implant bridge hits a practical sweet spot: more bone preservation than a traditional bridge, less cost than individual implants per tooth. At Merry Dental Hub, Dr. C places two titanium posts at the anchor points, allows osseointegration over 3–6 months, then cements a fixed porcelain bridge across the span. The two post sites prevent the bone hollowing that would otherwise happen at each missing tooth location. Rowlett and Garland patients choose this route regularly because the provisional bridge keeps them functional throughout healing.

Implant-Supported Partial Denture

Replace multiple non-consecutive missing teeth with implant stability

When missing teeth are scattered across different areas of the jaw, a partial denture supported by implant posts is often the most practical answer. Dr. C places 2–4 titanium posts as anchors; the removable partial snaps or magnetizes into place with enough retention to behave like a fixed restoration during meals and conversation. Richardson and Lavon patients who’d worn conventional partials for years tell us the stability change is immediate and dramatic — no shifting, no clasps visible at the gumline, no anxiety in public.

All-on-4® Full Arch Implants

Replace all teeth on one jaw — with just 4 implants and often same-day teeth

For Wylie and St. Paul patients facing full-arch tooth loss, All-on-4 offers something no other treatment can: a complete, functional smile placed on the same day as implant surgery. Dr. C uses 3D CBCT imaging to plan the exact angle and depth of each of the four titanium posts — maximizing bone contact with available ridge structure and, in most cases, bypassing the need for bone grafting entirely. A provisional full arch of 10–14 teeth is attached the same afternoon. After 3–6 months of healing, the final zirconia bridge is seated. Patients eat solid food within days.

Full Mouth Implant Reconstruction

Complete restoration of both upper and lower arches simultaneously

Full mouth reconstruction treats both arches as a single unified project — which matters because upper and lower jaw must work in precise harmony to achieve a functional, lasting bite. Dr. C plans both arches together before a single post is placed, using 3D CBCT to map bone density, nerve pathways, and sinus anatomy. Most Wylie patients in this category complete treatment in 6–12 months, with costs spread naturally across the phased timeline. The end result is a smile and bite that functions like natural teeth — maintained only with a water flosser and regular cleanings at Merry Dental Hub.

A single implant addresses one missing tooth: one post, one crown, done. It’s the right answer when all other teeth are healthy and the goal is to restore function and preserve bone at one specific site. All-on-4 is a completely different scale — four implants placed at strategic angles across an entire jaw to support a fixed bridge of 10–14 teeth, often with a provisional bridge seated the same day as surgery. Wylie patients who’ve been struggling with loose dentures on one arch are typically the best All-on-4 candidates. The 3D CBCT determines which applies to your specific bone anatomy.

The titanium post — the part that fuses to your jawbone — is designed to last a lifetime. Clinical data shows 95 %+ survival at 10 years, and implants placed in the 1960s are still functioning in patients today. The crown or bridge sitting on top of the post has a shorter functional lifespan: single-crown restorations typically last 15–25 years; All-on-4 full arches commonly last 15–20+ years before needing refurbishment of the prosthetic. Merry Dental Hub patients who don’t smoke, maintain 6-month cleanings, and water-floss daily regularly exceed these benchmarks.
