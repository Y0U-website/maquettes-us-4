What is a Scaling and Root Planing?

When is Scaling and Root Planing Needed?

Scaling and root planing may be recommended if you exhibit signs of gum disease, such as:

The Scaling and Root Planing Procedure At Dublin Corners Dental, we prioritize your comfort and well-being during the scaling and root planing procedure:

  • Gum Inflammation: Red, swollen, and tender gums are early indicators of gum disease, known as gingivitis.
  • Gum Recession: A receding gum line exposes the tooth roots, making them vulnerable to decay and sensitivity.
  • Gum Pocket Formation: Deep pockets between the gums and teeth can trap food debris and bacteria, leading to infection and bone loss .
  • Persistent Bad Breath: Foul breath that doesn’t improve with regular oral hygiene could be a sign of gum disease.
  • Thorough Examination: Before the treatment, we conduct a comprehensive dental examination, including X-rays, to assess the extent of gum disease and determine the best approach.
  • Local Anesthesia: To ensure your comfort during the procedure, we administer local anesthesia to numb the treated area.
  • Dental scaling: Our skilled dental hygienist uses specialized tools to carefully remove plaque, tartar, and bacteria from above and below the gumline.
  • Root Planing: The tooth roots are gently smoothed to remove rough areas where bacteria tend to accumulate, promoting gum healing and reattachment.
  • Post-Treatment Care: We provide you with detailed post-treatment instructions and may schedule follow-up visits to monitor your gum health and progress.
  • Gum Disease Treatment: Scaling and root planing effectively targets and treats gum disease, preventing further damage and tooth loss.
  • Gum Rejuvenation: The procedure helps restore the health of your gums, reducing inflammation and promoting tissue healing.
  • Improved Oral Health: Healthy gums are essential for maintaining the stability of your teeth and preserving your smile.
  • Prevention of Systemic Health Issues: Gum disease has been linked to various systemic health problems, and scaling and root planing can help reduce these risks.

Treatment Areas for Scaling and Root Planing

  • For gum disease: Chronic periodontitis is an advanced gum disease that destroys the tissues and bones supporting teeth. If untreated, it can lead to tooth loss.
  • For periodontal disease: Gum disease, or periodontal disease, is a chronic condition caused by bacterial infections and inflammation of the gums. It starts as gingivitis and can progress to periodontitis, which affects the tissues and bone supporting the teeth.

Frequently Asked Questions Why is scaling and root planing necessary? The procedure is necessary for combatting gum disease, gum recession, inflammation, pocket formation, or bone and/or tooth loss. It works to restore and prevent further damage.

When is scaling and root planing recommended?

Dentists recommend scaling and root planing for those who show signs of gum disease such as swollen, red, or tender gums. It is also often recommended for those who have receeding gums, gum pockets, or persistent bad breath.

How does scaling and root planing help with gum inflammation?

Scaling and root planing remove plaque, tartar, and bacteria to reduce the source of inflammation, allowing the gums to heal and return to a healthy state.

Can scaling and root planing reverse gum recession?

While it cant fully reverse gum recession, scaling and root planing can prevent further recession and promote gum reattachment to the tooth.

What is the recovery like after scaling and root planing?

After your scaling and root planing procedure, you will follow specific instructions for oral hygiene. These instructions may include using a special mouthwash or taking a prescribed antibiotic for healing. Follow-up treatments are also scheduled to monitor healing.
