Single Tooth Implants, From Root to Crown

A single tooth implant replaces a missing tooth from root to crown, so it looks, feels, and works like the natural tooth you lost. One missing tooth changes more than how you smile. It affects how you chew, how you speak, and starting almost immediately, the bone in your jaw. Dr. Matthew Collins uses precise techniques and proven implant systems to deliver permanent results that fit right in with your smile.

The Implant Post

A small titanium screw placed in the jawbone. It acts as the new root and fuses with your bone over three to six months.

The Abutment

A small connector that attaches to the top of the implant post and holds the crown in place above the gum line.

The Crown

A custom porcelain crown shaped and shaded to match your surrounding teeth. This is the visible part of your new tooth. Learn more about our implant-supported crowns .

The Most Permanent Way to Replace a Missing Tooth

A dental bridge or partial denture can replace a missing tooth, but only an implant replaces the root too. That difference matters a lot for your long-term oral health.

Looks Natural A custom crown is shaded and shaped to match your other teeth. Most people can’t tell which tooth is the implant.

Feels Natural Eat what you want. Bite into an apple, chew a steak, smile in photos. An implant works just like the tooth it replaced.

Preserves Your Jawbone Without a root, the jaw starts receding right away, and most of the width loss happens in the first three to six months rather than slowly over years. The implant post stimulates the bone the way a natural root does.

Protects Your Other Teeth Unlike a traditional bridge, an implant doesn’t require us to file down healthy neighboring teeth.

Built to Last With proper care, the implant post can last decades, often a lifetime. The crown typically lasts 10 to 15 years.

Easy to Care For Brush and floss like you would a natural tooth. No special routines, no removable parts.

From First Visit to Final Smile

A single tooth implant is a multi-step process spread across several months. Most of that time is healing, not treatment. Here’s what to expect.

Consultation

3D imaging, exam, and a clear treatment plan with cost.

Implant Placement

The titanium post is placed under local anesthesia in one visit.

Healing

Three to six months for the implant to fuse with your jawbone.

Abutment & Impression

Abutment placed and a digital impression taken for your custom crown.

Final Crown

Your custom porcelain crown is attached, completing your new tooth.

Most Adults Qualify for a Single Tooth Implant

During your implant consultation , Dr. Collins reviews the four factors that determine implant success.

Healthy Gums Your gums need to be free of active periodontal disease. If gum disease is present, we treat it first with periodontal therapy before placing an implant.

Adequate Jawbone Implants need enough bone to anchor into, and the jaw starts giving that bone back as soon as a tooth is gone. Because most of the loss happens in the first few months, older gaps more often need a bone graft first. We coordinate that referral, then place the implant once the site is ready.

Overall Health Most patients are healthy enough for an implant. Uncontrolled diabetes, smoking, and certain medications can affect healing, so we review your full medical history.

Good Aftercare Habits Implants last when you take care of them. Daily brushing, daily flossing, and routine dental cleanings keep the implant and the gum tissue around it healthy.
