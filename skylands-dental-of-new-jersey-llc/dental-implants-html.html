What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small titanium rods designed to replicate the roots of your missing teeth – the part hidden beneath the gums. After the implants have been placed in the jaw during a minor surgical procedure, they begin to bond with the surrounding bone and tissue, creating a firm foundation for the prosthetic teeth that are secured on top. Currently, dental implants are the only option that recreates the whole structure of missing teeth, which makes them the closest thing to having your old teeth back.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

The process of replacing missing teeth with dental implants typically takes months to complete. Our team can walk you through the various steps involved in order to help you form an accurate idea of what you can expect. The treatment won’t be exactly the same for every patient, but it can generally be broken up into four main parts: the initial consultation, the implant placement surgery, osseointegration, and the delivery of the final restoration.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

First of all, our team will need to confirm that you’re a good candidate for dental implants. This can be done during a consultation at our office; we can take a close look at your smile and evaluate the relevant facial structures to determine whether implant posts can safely be placed. This will also help us figure out exactly how many implant posts you will require and where they will need to be inserted into the jaw.

Before moving on to the next step, you may need to have certain preliminary procedures performed. For example, bone grafting might be required to reverse any bone loss that you have experienced due to your missing teeth; this ensures that there’s enough bone density for the implant posts.

Dental Implant Surgery

After the initial consultation and the necessary preparations comes dental implant surgery. For the convenience of our patients, we’re able to handle this step in-office. The procedure involves making an incision in your gums to expose the underlying bone. The implant posts will be inserted into the jaw at precisely chosen positions and angles that were determined ahead of time. Then the gums are sutured shut, and special caps are placed on the implants to keep them safe while your mouth heals.

Naturally, we’ll do everything we can to keep you comfortable during the surgery. Before we begin, a local anesthetic will be applied in order to numb your mouth. You might feel pressure during the procedure, but you’ll experience little to no pain.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Osseointegration is a natural process that lets your dental implants form a lasting bond with your jawbone. This process typically takes 3 to 6 months, and it’s an essential step for ensuring that your dental implants last as long as possible. Once osseointegration has been successfully completed, we can attach a metal connector piece called an abutment to each of your dental implant posts.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

An impression of your mouth can be taken in order to design a crown, bridge, or denture that matches the rest of your smile. Once it is ready, you’ll return to our office for the final part of the dental implant process. Using the abutments placed in the previous step, the prosthesis will be carefully attached to your dental implant posts in order to recomplete your smile. We can make any last-minute adjustments that might be needed to ensure that your new teeth function as they should.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Dental implants can help practically any healthy adult replace any number of missing teeth. As long as you have enough healthy jawbone mass (or can get a bone grafting procedure in order to reach that point), there’s a good chance that you’re a candidate for dental implants. After meeting with Dr. Kakkar, he can let you know which of the following treatment options would be best for you.

Missing One Tooth

A single implant can be vertically placed between two natural teeth and fitted with a dental crown on top. Not only does an implant crown support itself without relying on surrounding teeth, but the crown also blends in with your smile as a whole.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Rather than placing one implant for every missing tooth, we can position a small number of posts at strategic points throughout the mouth to hold a dental bridge or partial denture in place. Compared to their traditional counterparts, these solutions are much sturdier and more reliable.

Missing All Teeth

Tired of your current denture? It rarely takes more than eight implants to bring back an entire row of teeth with an implant denture, which makes eating easier and never slips out of place. We even offer All-on-4 dental implants, which require only four posts per arch.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

The lifespan of dental implants depends on the health and lifestyle choices of the patient. To ensure that dental implants last, it is important to brush, floss, and rinse with a mouthwash on a daily basis. You should be attending regular cleanings and checkups and be sure to avoid foods that are particularly hard or sticky. With proper maintenance, dental implants can last upwards of 30 years. This is several times longer than the average traditional bridges and dentures.

Does Getting Dental Implants Hurt?

The jawbone doesn’t have very many nerve endings, and your mouth will be numbed with a local anesthetic before the start of the procedure. You will also most likely be sedated, so you shouldn’t feel any pain while the surgery takes place. However, you may feel some soreness for a few days afterward. This can be managed with recommended over-the-counter and prescribed pain relievers as well as cold compresses. If your discomfort worsens instead of improving after two to three days, give us a call!

Will I Have to Take Off from Work for Dental Implant Surgery?

Most patients need to take one or two days off work to get dental implants, but if you have a job that is physically demanding, you should probably take at least three to four days off. This is because heavy exercise can divert blood from the implant site and slow down healing. Before your procedure, Dr. Kakkar will be able to give you a more specific recommendation for your unique case.

Can I Get Dental Implants If I Have Rheumatoid Arthritis?

Depending on your bone density and the health of your gums, you may still be able to get dental implants even if you have rheumatoid arthritis. Because treating rheumatoid arthritis often involves taking immunosuppressant drugs which can affect bone healing and increase your risk of infection, you may need to talk to your doctor about other options in the weeks before and after your surgery. Dr. Kakkar will evaluate your oral and overall health to determine whether or not they are right for you.
