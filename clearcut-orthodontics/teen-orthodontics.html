Questions about teen clear aligner treatment

When should my teen start clear aligner treatment?

Most teens begin treatment between the ages of 11 and 16, once most permanent teeth are in place. Earlier is generally better — the teenage growth window allows for more efficient tooth movement. A complimentary consultation with Dr. Skopek will determine the right timing for your teen’s specific case.

Are clear aligners a good option for teens?

For most teens, yes — and at ClearCut, it’s the only option we offer. That’s not a limitation; it’s a deliberate choice. The exclusive focus on clear aligner treatment means Dr. Skopek has treated every level of complexity with aligners, including cases that other practices consider too complex. If your teen is a candidate, they’re getting the most experienced clear aligner specialist in the Midwest.

Will treatment interfere with sports or activities?

No, and clear aligners are actually a great fit for athletes. Your teen can leave their aligners in during play, where they add a layer of protection for the teeth, or wear a mouthguard right over them. There are no brackets to worry about breaking, no wires to irritate gums, and no emergency visits when something goes wrong mid-season. For athletes in particular, clear aligners are the clear choice.

How long does teen treatment take?

Most teen cases at ClearCut are completed within 6 to 18 months, depending on complexity. Your teen’s exact timeline will be confirmed during the complimentary consultation — and with Skopek Remote built into treatment, progress is tracked consistently, so there are no timeline surprises.

What if my teen doesn t wear the aligners consistently?

Consistency is essential — aligners need to be worn 20 to 22 hours per day to stay on track. At ClearCut, Skopek Remote allows Dr. Skopek’s team to track wear progress between visits. If compliance drops, we catch it early and address it before it impacts outcomes. We also have an honest conversation with teens about why consistency matters — because understanding the why makes a bigger difference than reminders.
