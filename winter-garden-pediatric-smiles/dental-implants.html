The Dental Implant Procedure

IMPLANT

  • The jawbone is assessed to prepare for the procedure.
  • A titanium post is securely inserted to replace the tooth root.
  • The implant/screw integrates with the jawbone, creating a strong foundation for the new tooth.

ABUTMENT

  • A dental implant abutment is attached, linking the metal post and the crown.
  • This secure connection guarantees the new tooth remains firmly in position.

RESTORATION

  • A custom-crafted crown is engineered.
  • The crown is screwed into place.
  • The finishing touch of your dental implant is that it perfects your smile’s appearance.

From single implants to full-arch restorations, we’ve got you covered.

A single implant is a dental implant that is used to replace a single missing tooth.

An implant bridge is a dental restoration that replaces two or more missing teeth. This type of bridge involves two or more dental implants placed into the jawbone and topped with custom-made dental crowns.

Full Arch, All-X Over Dentures-Fixed and Removable

Full-arch dental implants replace an entire arch of teeth (either upper or lower).

The All-X Over Dentures system is a type of full arch implant that involves four to six dental implants placed in the jawbone, topped with a custom-made denture.

FAQ About Dental Implants

  • What are dental implants? Dental implants are the best way of replacing a missing tooth. It consists of a small post, usually made of titanium, which is inserted into the jawbone. An abutment is placed on top of the dental implant, which connects it to a replacement tooth. Dental implants can hold a prosthetic tooth or bridge.
  • Who is a good candidate for dental implants? Most people are good candidates. Dental implants can replace one, multiple, or all of your teeth. Talk to your dentist about your dental needs.
  • What are the advantages of dental implants? Dental implants behave like natural teeth. They can last a lifetime, prevent jaw deterioration, and restore a person’s ability to chew. Dental implants will also improve your nutrition and help you feel more confident about your smile.
  • What are the disadvantages of dental implants? Like any minor oral surgical procedure, there is a risk of infection, inflammation, and pain. But your dentist will discuss how these can be managed for your specific situation. If there is no bone to place the implant, bone and gum grafting procedures may be required, which can increase treatment costs. However, the upfront investment can pay off in the long term.
  • How much do implants cost and does insurance cover the cost? The cost of replacing a single tooth with a dental implant is almost the same as replacing it with a regular fixed bridge. Dental implant treatment may qualify for some insurance coverage, but situations vary. The cost is determined by patient needs, bone quantity, and region. Our dentist will make an assessment based on your unique needs.
  • What is the success rate of dental implants? Each patient’s health and oral habits are different, and impact the success rate of a dental implant. Dental implants have reported success rates above 90-95 percent for a healthy individual with good oral hygiene.
  • Do dental implants last? Unlike natural teeth, dental implants are not susceptible to dental diseases such as decay (caries). However, the health of the gums is vital to sustainable implant success. Dental implants must be maintained with regular patient home care as well as professional cleanings and exams. Each patient is unique, and success relies on diagnosis and planning, medical history, and a variety of other factors.
  • What is All-on-X? This full-mouth tooth replacement is a specialized dental implant procedure where as few as four implants can replace an entire arch of teeth, eliminating the need for a mouth full of dental implants.
  • Are dental implants removable like dentures or do they stay in your mouth? No, dental implants are fixed solidly in the bone and allow teeth to be replaced in a manner that is closest to natural teeth.
  • How do dental implants help if I have full dentures or partial dentures? Implants are primarily used in patients who wear full dentures or partial dentures. They can provide retention and support for a removable implant overdenture that snaps onto the implants. This stability reduces the patient’s dependency on denture adhesives/denture glue. Dental implants can be used for a fixed denture, where a patient’s dentures are fixed/bolted to the implants through titanium components. Only the dentist can remove this from the mouth.
