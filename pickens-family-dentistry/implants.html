How the Dental Implants Process Works

Dental implants are a great option to replace and repair missing teeth, but is a dental implant the right choice for you? While Dr. Pickens and the friendly staff at Pickens Family Dentistry can answer questions like this one and more, we also want to provide you with the basics of hte dental implant process.

Teeth implants are generally a two-stage process that involves placing the dental implant itself and then adding the replacement tooth on top of it. Adding the replacement tooth is often called restoring your implant.

Let’s look at the two main steps of the dental implant process:

  • The dentist places the implant. First, the implant itself is placed into the jaw. The implant is a small metal post that, after being placed, will naturally fuse with your jawbone. This placement is done during a surgical procedure while the patient is under anesthesia. After the procedure, your jaw will be left to heal for a couple of weeks. In some cases, a temporary replacement tooth is used while the implant heals completely.
  • The dentist adds the replacement tooth. After the dental implant heals, the replacement tooth component is added. This tooth goes on top of an abutment on the dental implant. Your replacement tooth, usually a crown, is created in a lab to match the color of your other teeth. It will be completely customized to match your smile!

Accelerated Treatment and Guided Implant Surgery

With guided implant surgery, multiple dental implants can be placed more efficiently and accurately. While placing one dental implant is relatively straightforward, the treatment gets more complicated when jawbone loss occurs or when a patient needs more than one implant.

Guided implant surgery involves the use of 3D models and computer software to scan the patient’s mouth. Then, the dentist and his staff can better plan an individualized surgical approach to ensure the correct and easy placement of the implants.
