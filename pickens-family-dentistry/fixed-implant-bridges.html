What Is the Difference Between Traditional Bridges and Implant Bridges?

Both implant-supported bridges and traditional fixed bridges are a way to replace a missing tooth.

Fixed implant bridges are an alternative to a traditional fixed bridge. What is a dental bridge? With a traditional bridge, a replacement tooth is supported on either side through a concrete-like adhesive. With this method, the bridge is attached to the adjoining, healthy teeth. This creates a “bridge” where the new replacement tooth can sit.

With a fixed implant bridge, however, dental implants are used to secure the replacement teeth to provide even more support. The bridge will be placed on two implants, making the entire bridge capable of filling the spots of three teeth.

Types of Fixed Bridges

Above we discussed the advantages of a fixed implant bridge, but the team at Pickens Family Dentistry also provides other bridge procedures. We will figure out what type of fixed bridge suits your dental health needs best.

Here are some of the basics about the various kinds of fixed bridges:

  • Resin-bonded fixed bridge: A resin-bonded fixed bridge is the most typical and traditional type of fixed bridge. With this method, a dental crown is attached to the surrounding teeth using resin. This acts as a “bridge” to place the replacement dental crown.
  • Cantilever bridge: A cantilever dental bridge is similar to a resin-bonded fixed bridge except there is only one anchor tooth, instead of two. These aren’t as common because they only work well for the front of the mouth.
  • Dental implant-supported bridge: With a dental implant-supported bridge, the dental crown is supported and attached to dental implants. The dental implants are placed and then three new replacement teeth are added on top of the implants.
