How Can I Fix Teeth That I’ve Lost?

Losing teeth can be very stressful. Whether you lose the tooth from a traumatic injury or from a dental disease, most people don’t want to live with a missing tooth. In fact, leaving a missing tooth alone can cause damage to the surrounding teeth and to your gums and jaw.

There are various options to fix a missing tooth. Some of the most common include dental crowns, dental bridges, and dental implants. Our implants are often recommended because they are so long-lasting.

How Single Tooth Dental Implants Work at Our Dental Office

With single-tooth dental implants, the process is straightforward. Here’s what you can expect from single-tooth dental implants at Pickens Family Dentistry:

If you have questions about the tooth implant process, don’t hesitate to reach out to one of our friendly staff! Contact Pickens Family Dentistry for an appointment!

  • A consultation: First, you’ll get a consultation with Dr. Pickens to determine a treatment plan.
  • Placing the implant: During your first appointment, the implant, which is a titanium post, will be surgically placed and left to heal. A temporary placement tooth can be placed on top.
  • Affixing the replacement tooth: After a few months, the implant will heal and function like a regular tooth root. At this appointment, Dr. Drue Pickens places a permanent replacement tooth.

Types of Dental Implants

While teeth implants are excellent for replacing a single missing or damaged tooth, we also provide other options to assist more patients.

Some of the other types of dental implants we offer include:

Find out more about options for replacing missing teeth by reaching out today.

  • Multiple tooth implants: If you have more than one missing tooth, multiple implants are an option to restore the function of your mouth.
  • Full mouth implants: When patients have lost most or all of their teeth, full mouth implants can repair the mouth. Usually, full-mouth implants are a type of denture that involves placing just a few implant posts and attaching a permanent denture.

Over-Dentures and Implants

As mentioned above, full-mouth implants can restore multiple missing teeth. Implant-supported dentures are sometimes called overdentures. During this treatment, implants are placed strategically into the gums. Then, an arch, or denture, is fitted on top of the implants.

With this procedure, only a few implants need to be placed to support an entire arch of missing teeth.
