FAQs

A dental implant has a titanium post that replaces the root of a missing tooth. This screw-like post is surgically inserted into the jawbone, where it fuses with the surrounding bone through a process called osseointegration. An abutment is then placed over the post to connect it to your final restoration, which may be a crown, bridge, or denture.

During your initial evaluation, Dr. Elvi Roci will inspect your oral health, bone density, and other factors to determine your eligibility. Adequate jawbone is essential, since the post must fuse with the bone to provide support. If you have significant bone loss, we offer bone grafting procedures to build up the jawbone before implant placement.

Bridges and dentures only replace the visible part of the tooth. Implants replace both the root and the crown. Because the post integrates with the jawbone, it provides a stable base, stimulates healthy bone formation, reduces bone loss, and allows you to eat, speak, and smile with complete confidence.

The complete dental implant process typically takes 3 to 6 months. This includes surgical placement of the implant post, a healing period for osseointegration, and final placement of the custom crown. Some patients may require additional procedures like bone grafting, which can extend the timeline. Dr. Elvi Roci will walk you through a timeline for your specific case.

With proper care and regular dental visits, dental implants can last a lifetime. The implant post itself is made from biocompatible titanium that fuses permanently with your jawbone. The crown attached to the implant may need replacement after 10 to 15 years due to normal wear and tear.
