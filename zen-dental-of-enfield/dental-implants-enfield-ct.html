How the Dental Implant Process Works

Phase 1 Implant Placement A small titanium post is precisely placed into the jawbone where the missing tooth once was. This post acts as an artificial root. The surrounding bone then gradually fuses with it over the next 4–6 months — a process called osseointegration.

Phase 2 Healing & Temporary Restoration Once the implant has integrated with the bone, a temporary crown or abutment is placed while your final restoration is being crafted. This phase typically takes 4–6 weeks.

Phase 3 Final Restoration Your permanent crown, bridge, or denture is placed and adjusted for a precise fit and natural bite. The result looks and functions like a natural tooth — and with proper care, it can last a lifetime.

Dental Implants FAQ

How long do dental implants last? With proper care, dental implants can last 20–30 years or a lifetime. The crown on top may need replacement after 10–15 years with heavy wear, but the implant itself is designed to be permanent.

Are dental implants painful? Most patients are surprised by how manageable the procedure is. Local anesthesia is used throughout, and any post-procedure soreness is typically mild and resolved within a few days with over-the-counter pain relief.

How much do dental implants cost in Enfield, CT? The cost of a single implant typically ranges from $3,000–$5,000 depending on the complexity of your case and whether preparatory procedures like bone grafting are needed. Zen Dental offers financing and a current $500 new patient discount to help make treatment affordable.

How long does the implant process take? From initial placement to final crown, the process generally takes 6–9 months. The majority of that time is the natural healing and osseointegration phase — not active treatment time.

Can implants be done in one day? All-on-4 and certain single implant procedures can be completed with a same-day provisional tooth, so you don’t leave without a smile. Full final restorations still require healing time.

Do implants work if I’ve had bone loss? Often, yes. Bone grafting can rebuild the jaw to support an implant. Mini implants also require less bone density. We’ll evaluate this at your consultation.

Does Cigna cover dental implants? Cigna Dental plans vary. Some cover a portion of the implant crown or the underlying procedures. Our team will review your specific Cigna plan and explain your benefits clearly before treatment.
