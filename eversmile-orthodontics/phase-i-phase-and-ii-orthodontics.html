How Long Does Phase I Treatment Last?

Every child’s needs are unique, but most Phase I orthodontic treatment lasts between 6 and 18 months. Dr. Davis carefully monitors growth during this time to make sure changes are effective and lasting.

What Appliances Are Used in Phase I Orthodontics?

Children may use several different appliances in interceptive orthodontics, depending on their needs. These may include:

  • Palatal expanders
  • Partial braces
  • Space maintainers
  • Habit appliances
  • Retainers

Will My Child Still Need Braces After Phase I?

Yes. While early orthodontics sets the foundation for healthy growth, most children still benefit from Phase II braces or aligners once their permanent teeth have erupted. The good news is that Phase I often makes Phase II treatment shorter and simpler.

What Are the Benefits of Phase I Orthodontics?

Starting orthodontic treatment early offers long-term benefits, including:

  • Shorter treatment time during Phase II
  • Reduced risk of extractions or jaw surgery
  • Improved facial balance and airway development
  • Stronger self-esteem during critical growth years

What is Phase II Orthodontic Treatment?

Phase II orthodontics is comprehensive treatment that takes place once most permanent teeth are in. This stage focuses on aligning teeth, fine-tuning bite function, and creating a healthy, confident smile. Phase II treatment typically lasts 18–24 months, depending on each patient’s needs.

When Does Phase II Orthodontics Begin?

Most patients begin Phase II braces between ages 11 and 15, after the permanent teeth have erupted. Timing may vary, but Dr. Davis monitors growth closely to ensure treatment starts at the ideal stage.

What Options Are Available in Phase II?

At Eversmile Orthodontics, we offer a variety of modern treatment options to meet every need, including:

  • Damon Ultima Braces for precise, efficient results
  • Ceramic braces for a more aesthetic option
  • Invisalign and Spark clear aligners for flexibility and comfort

What Happens After Phase II Treatment?

After Phase II is complete, patients receive retainers to protect their results and prevent shifting. We emphasize lifetime retention so your smile stays healthy, balanced, and beautiful long after treatment ends.

How Do Phase I and Phase II Differ?

Together, these stages create long-term stability and healthy function.

  • Phase I orthodontics : Early interceptive treatment, focused on growth and foundation.
  • Phase II orthodontics : Comprehensive alignment and bite correction during adolescence.

Is There a Rest Period Between Phases?

Yes. After Phase I, most children enter a monitoring period while their permanent teeth continue to erupt. Dr. Davis schedules regular visits to ensure proper development until it’s time to begin Phase II.
