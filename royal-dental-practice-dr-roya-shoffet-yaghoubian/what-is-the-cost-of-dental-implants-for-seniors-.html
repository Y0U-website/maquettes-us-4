1 Number of teeth that need to be replaced

Implants are a versatile way to replace teeth. They may be recommended to support and replace one tooth, a few teeth, several teeth, or all of the teeth in the upper and/or lower dental arch(es). A single-tooth implant generally costs less than multiple-tooth implants, because more components are involved. Multiple implants may also require additional and more extensive surgical sites/procedures, which adds to the cost of treatment.

2 Type of appliance

The number of implants and restorations is not the only factor that contributes to the cost of treatment. When choosing from different implant systems, some options for dentures or full-arch restorations cost more than others. For instance, removable dentures that snap stably onto the implants via special connectors generally cost less than non-removable dentures or full-arch options that are fixed to implants in the jawbone.

4 Insurance

Seniors may have government-subsidized, employer-sponsored, and/or private insurance plans. Some of these plans do not cover any portion of the treatment process. Other plans may cover a portion of treatment deemed “medically necessary,” or restorative work such as a percentage of the cost of a crown. As always, coverage varies considerably and is also subject to things like annual maximums and lifetime caps.
