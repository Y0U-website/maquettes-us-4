Our Tooth Pain Treatment Process

1. Emergency Intake

Tooth pain assessment begins the moment you contact us. Our team collects your pain history, symptom duration, and intensity level. We schedule your appointment based on urgency.

2. Clinical Examination

Your dentist performs visual inspection, percussion testing, and palpation of the affected area. We check for swelling, discoloration, and mobility. Cold testing and electric pulp testing determine nerve vitality.

3. Diagnostic Imaging

Digital radiographs reveal decay depth, bone loss, and abscess formation. Complex cases may require 3D cone beam imaging. These images guide treatment planning for your tooth pain.

4. Diagnosis and Treatment Options

We explain the cause of your dental pain in clear terms. You’ll receive a written treatment plan with costs and timelines. We answer all questions before proceeding.

5. Pain Relief Treatment

Treatment varies based on diagnosis. Options include dental fillings, root canal therapy, tooth extraction, or incision and drainage. We administer appropriate anesthesia and complete treatment efficiently.

6. Follow-Up Care

You’ll leave with post-treatment instructions and prescribed medications if needed. We schedule follow-up appointments to monitor healing and ensure your tooth pain has resolved completely.

FAQs About Tooth Pain

What causes tooth pain?

Tooth pain results from several conditions. Dental decay reaching the nerve causes sharp or throbbing pain. Cracked teeth, gum disease, dental abscesses, and exposed tooth roots all produce dental discomfort. Grinding habits and sinus infections can also trigger tooth pain symptoms.

When should I see a dentist for tooth pain?

Seek dental care immediately if tooth pain lasts more than two days, includes facial swelling, or accompanies fever. Severe throbbing pain, difficulty swallowing, or pus discharge requires emergency treatment. Mild sensitivity can wait for a regular appointment, but persistent pain needs professional evaluation.

Why does my tooth pain get worse at night?

Tooth pain intensifies at night because lying down increases blood flow to your head. This elevated pressure aggravates inflamed dental pulp. Fewer distractions at night also make you more aware of dental discomfort. Nighttime tooth pain often indicates pulpitis requiring treatment.

How do dentists diagnose tooth pain?

Dentists use multiple diagnostic methods. Visual examination reveals obvious decay or damage. Percussion testing identifies cracked teeth. Thermal testing checks nerve response. X-rays show decay depth, bone loss, and abscess formation. These combined findings pinpoint your tooth pain source.

Can tooth pain go away on its own?

Tooth pain rarely resolves without treatment. The underlying cause typically worsens over time. A toothache that suddenly disappears may indicate nerve death, not healing. The infection remains and can spread. Professional dental treatment addresses the root cause of your tooth pain.

Does tooth pain always mean extraction?

Tooth pain does not automatically require extraction. Root canal therapy saves most painful teeth by removing infected pulp while preserving tooth structure. Fillings treat decay-related pain. Extraction becomes necessary only when teeth are severely damaged or fractured beyond repair.
