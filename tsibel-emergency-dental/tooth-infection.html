What is a Tooth Infection?

A tooth infection is a bacterial infection that occurs inside the tooth or in the surrounding tissues. It is a localized collection of bacteria that has taken hold within the dental pulp, the soft inner core of the tooth containing nerves and blood vessels, or in the space between the tooth and gum.

Signs You Need Tooth Infection Treatment

Tooth infection symptoms range from mild discomfort to severe systemic illness. Recognizing these warning signs prevents dangerous complications.

Persistent Throbbing Pain

Pulpitis creates constant, pulsating pain that worsens when lying down. The discomfort radiates along the trigeminal nerve to your jaw, ear, and temple. Over the counter analgesics provide minimal relief because the infection continues spreading through dental pulp tissue.

Facial Swelling

A dental abscess produces visible swelling in the cheek, jaw, or submandibular region. Cellulitis develops as infection penetrates fascial planes. Ludwig’s angina, a bilateral submandibular space infection, constitutes a medical emergency requiring immediate intervention.

Fever and Malaise

Systemic infection triggers immune response. Body temperature exceeds 100.4°F. You experience fatigue, chills, and general unwellness. These symptoms indicate bacteria have entered your bloodstream, a condition called bacteremia that demands urgent tooth infection treatment.

Foul Taste and Drainage

A draining fistula or parulis appears on gum tissue near the infected tooth. Purulent exudate tastes metallic and smells unpleasant. This sinus tract provides temporary pressure relief but signals active periapical abscess requiring professional drainage.

Tooth Sensitivity and Discoloration

Necrotic pulp tissue causes the affected tooth to darken. Extreme sensitivity to hot and cold temperatures indicates irreversible pulpitis. The tooth may feel elevated in its socket due to periapical inflammation pushing against the surrounding bone.

Our Tooth Infection Treatment Process

Tooth infection treatment follows a systematic protocol designed for complete bacterial elimination and tissue healing.

Emergency Assessment

Your visit begins with a focused examination. We test pulp vitality using thermal and electric stimulation. Percussion and palpation identify the source tooth. Digital radiographs reveal periapical radiolucency indicating abscess formation.

Pain Management

Profound anesthesia precedes all procedures. We administer local anesthetic after the area numbs from topical benzocaine. Patients with severe infections sometimes require supplemental techniques like intraosseous injection for complete comfort.

Infection Drainage

Incision and drainage releases purulent material trapped in soft tissue. We establish drainage through the tooth by creating endodontic access or through gingival tissue via stab incision. Rubber drain placement maintains the opening for continued drainage over several days.

Source Elimination

Definitive treatment removes infected pulp tissue. Root canal therapy cleans and shapes the canal system using rotary nickel titanium files. Sodium hypochlorite irrigation dissolves organic debris. Calcium hydroxide medicament destroys remaining bacteria between appointments.

Restoration and Follow Up

After infection resolves, we seal the root canal system with gutta percha. A crown protects the treated tooth from fracture. Follow up radiographs at six months confirm periapical healing and bone regeneration.

FAQs About Tooth Infection Treatment

What causes a tooth infection?

Tooth infections develop when bacteria penetrate the dental pulp through deep cavities, cracked enamel, or failed restorations. The bacteria multiply in the pulp chamber, kill the nerve tissue, and spread through the root apex into the surrounding bone. Periodontal disease also allows bacteria to infect teeth through gum pockets.

When should I seek emergency care for a tooth infection?

Seek immediate tooth infection treatment if you develop facial swelling, difficulty swallowing, fever above 101°F, or trouble breathing. These symptoms indicate spreading infection requiring urgent intervention. Any dental pain lasting more than two days warrants professional evaluation.

How does a dentist treat an infected tooth?

Treatment depends on infection severity and tooth condition. Root canal therapy saves teeth with viable structure by removing infected pulp and sealing the canal system. Extraction becomes necessary when teeth are non restorable. Antibiotics alone cannot cure tooth infections because they cannot penetrate the necrotic pulp tissue where bacteria reside.

Can a tooth infection heal without treatment?

Tooth infections never resolve spontaneously. The infection may become chronic with periodic flare ups, but bacteria remain in the tooth and surrounding bone. Untreated dental abscesses eventually destroy bone, spread to adjacent teeth, or cause systemic illness including sepsis.

Does tooth infection treatment hurt?

Modern anesthetic techniques provide complete numbness during procedures. You feel pressure but not pain. Post treatment discomfort responds well to ibuprofen and acetaminophen. Most patients report significant pain reduction within 24 hours of drainage and antibiotic therapy.
