What are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small, titanium post that acts as an artificial root. It is securely placed into the jawbone and provides a stable foundation for a replacement crown. The full restoration includes three parts: the post (implant body), the connector (abutment), and the final custom tooth (crown).

The titanium post fuses with your natural jawbone through a process called osseointegration. This creates strong stability, allowing the dental implant to function like a natural tooth root. It also helps prevent bone loss, keeping your face and jawline healthy in the long term.

Dental Implant Process: 4 Simple Steps to a New Smile

Dental implant placement involves using a small titanium post that serves as an artificial root to replace a lost natural tooth root. This procedure is completed in four simple steps, ensuring both a comfortable experience and a beautiful, lasting smile.

  • Initial Consultation Your journey begins with a comprehensive oral health evaluation, which includes a review of your medical history and a discussion of your smile goals and objectives. We use a 3D Cone Beam CT (CBCT) scan to measure your jawbone’s density and volume. This important step confirms your eligibility for a dental implant procedure and finalizes a personalized treatment plan tailored to your specific needs.
  • Dental Implant Surgery Our oral surgeon places the titanium post into the jawbone using local anesthesia for a painless procedure. A small gum incision is made to allow precise dental implant placement. The gum is then stitched closed, marking the beginning of the healing phase.
  • Healing and Integration Over a period of 3 to 6 months, osseointegration takes place that allows the bone in your jaw to fuse with the titanium implant. This bond provides solid stability, allowing the implant to withstand everyday chewing. At the same time, healing prevents bone deficiency that happens when a tooth root is missing.
  • Final Restoration Once healing is complete, a small connector called an abutment is attached above the gumline. We take detailed impressions to craft your custom crown, bridge, or denture, ensuring it matches the natural appearance of your teeth. The final restoration is then secured, completing your new, functional smile.

Who Needs Dental Implants and Why?

The dental implant procedure is an effective solution for adults who have lost one or more teeth and require long-term replacements. Good oral health, strong gums, and sufficient bone structure are essential for successful dental implant placement. Even in cases of bone loss, newer grafting techniques make teeth implants a safe, effective option.

People facing the following dental issues can benefit from teeth implants:

  • Missing Teeth : A tooth gap makes nearby teeth shift and misalign your bite. A dental implant replaces the root, keeping your smile stable and functional.
  • Poor Dentures : Loose dentures slip or cause sores. Dental implants hold them firmly in place, eliminating the need for denture adhesives and providing greater comfort and stability.
  • Weak or Infected Roots : When a root is damaged beyond repair, a tooth implant replaces it after extraction, restoring chewing strength and oral health.
  • Cosmetic Concern : Even small gaps affect confidence. Dental implants replace missing teeth with natural-looking restorations that bring back a complete, balanced smile.
  • Jawbone Loss : Losing a tooth root stops bone stimulation. Implants act like tooth roots, preserving bone and preventing facial deterioration.
  • Difficulty Chewing or Speaking : Missing or loose teeth affect speech and eating. Tooth implants restore bite strength and clarity, allowing you to eat and speak with ease.

Advanced Dental Implant Treatment

Our advanced dental implant treatment replaces missing tooth roots with durable, screw-like posts that secure new teeth that look and function like natural ones. We deliver these cutting-edge solutions with precision and care to help you achieve a confident, healthy smile.

Single Tooth Implant

This treatment replaces one missing tooth without altering the adjacent healthy teeth. A single titanium post is placed in the jaw to serve as a tooth root, and a custom porcelain crown is then attached for a seamless, permanent replacement.

Multiple Teeth Replacement (Implant-Supported Bridge)

When you have several missing teeth in a row, a dental implant bridge uses two or more implants to anchor a fixed set of replacement teeth. This avoids placing stress on remaining natural teeth and provides exceptional stability for chewing and speaking.

Full Mouth Dental Implants

Full-mouth dental implants permanently restore an entire upper or lower arch of missing teeth using several strategically placed posts, typically 6 to 8. The fixed restoration is non-removable, delivering the highest level of stability, chewing function, and a completely natural appearance.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Implant-supported dentures, also known as snap-in dentures, secure removable full dentures onto a smaller number of implants, typically four. The dental implants keep your dentures firmly in place, so they won’t slip or move around. This makes eating and speaking much more comfortable than with regular dentures.

Same Day Dental Implants

This modern treatment enables some patients to receive their dental implants and temporary new teeth in a single visit. You leave the office with a smile on your face that day. It provides immediate cosmetic and functional results, although the final, permanent restoration is attached after the jawbone has fully healed.

What Determines the Final Price of Your New Smile?

The total cost depends on many factors and materials specifically applicable to your case. Some of these factors include:

Number of Missing Teeth and Implant Type

The price primarily depends on the number of teeth to replace. Generally, one missing tooth requires one dental implant and a dental crown, while several missing teeth require a different form of treatment. A full-arch restoration (usually called an All-on-4 or All-on-6 implant procedure) requires more materials and surgical time than a single tooth implant. Another price consideration is whether the prosthetic is a crown, fixed bridge, or secured denture.

Pre-Surgical Procedures and Case Complexity

Your jawbone must be strong enough to support the tooth implant. When a tooth root is gone, the bone naturally shrinks over time. Therefore, you need additional procedures, including:

Your total cost comprises three main components, each playing an important role in determining the longevity of your implant.

  • Bone Grafting : If your bone is too thin or soft, this step becomes necessary and adds to the total cost and treatment time.
  • Sinus Lift : Occasionally required for dental implants in the upper back teeth to create enough bone space for grafting.
  • Extractions : Removing a damaged or infected tooth before dental implant placement incurs an additional charge.
  • Implant Post : This is the titanium screw placed in your jawbone. We use strong, high-quality titanium or zirconia to ensure safety and long-term strength.
  • Abutment : This small connector attaches to the top of the implant post. Custom-made abutments help your new tooth fit naturally and blend seamlessly with your natural teeth.
  • Crown/Restoration : This is the visible porcelain or zirconia tooth that is placed on top of the implant, blending naturally with your smile. Its cost depends on the chosen material’s durability and appearance.

The Long-Term Investment: Why Dental Implants Are Worth It

Though the initial price is higher than that of bridges or dentures, dental implants are the smarter long-term choice.

Over time, avoiding replacements and repairs makes implants a truly cost-effective option.

  • Longevity and Durability : Tooth implants have a success rate of over 90 % after 10 years and can last a lifetime, unlike bridges, which typically last 5–15 years and require replacement.
  • Preserving Jawbone : The titanium post stimulates the bone, preventing the shrinkage and facial collapse caused by missing roots.
  • Protecting Healthy Teeth : Teeth implants stand alone without grinding nearby teeth, unlike traditional bridges.
