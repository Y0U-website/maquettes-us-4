Initial Consultation

The consultation is where it all begins. Before you can receive implants, we need to confirm that you’re a suitable candidate by evaluating your oral and overall health. We’ll also need to consider your medical history as well as your lifestyle; any possible risk factors that could affect the chances of success (such as gum disease or jawbone damage) need to be addressed before the procedure starts. Also, this initial visit gives us a chance to scan your jaw to see if additional procedures like bone grafting or a sinus lift might be needed.

Dental Implant Surgery

Dr. Majidian perform implant placement in-house. The goal of the procedure is to place the implant post directly into the jaw. After applying a sedative or anesthesia to keep you relaxed and comfortable, we’ll make an incision in the gums to expose the jawbone. We’ll then create several holes in strategic locations; the implant posts will be inserted into the holes. (The exact number of implants you get depends largely on how many teeth you’ve lost.) Finally, we’ll suture the gums closed and let the healing process begin.

Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

The stimulation from your implants encourages the jaw to form new bone tissue. Eventually, the bone will fuse with the metal. This process is called osseointegration, and it normally takes several months. Since the implant effectively becomes part of the jaw, it can provide an extremely stable foundation for your new teeth.

Once osseointegration is complete, we’ll need to perform another surgery to attach a piece called an abutment to your dental implants. This is what will hold your restoration in place. Dr. Majidian will open the gums again to place an abutment on each post. It should only take a couple of weeks for the gums to heal after this step.

Delivery of Final Restoration

Implants can support individual crowns, dental bridges, and even full dentures. We’ll take an impression of your mouth and send the information to our dental lab. They’ll use it to create false teeth with the exact color, size, and shape you need to maintain a functioning, natural-looking smile. Once it’s ready, we can schedule an appointment to place it in your mouth. This is the final step you’ll need to complete before you can enjoy a beautiful full grin again.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants involve placing a small titanium post into the jawbone to resemble the natural root of a tooth, and this provides the foundation for a new crown, bridge, or denture. Does this approach to replacement really bring advantages compared to other options? Absolutely! There are certain benefits you simply won’t get with traditional dentures or bridges. From their stability and reliability to their natural appearance, you can quickly see why dental implants are often the most recommended replacement option available.

Day-to-Day Benefits

Because the new teeth are firmly anchored to the bone, patients are able to eat basically whatever they like without having to worry about slippage or breakage. As a result, you can feel confident to let your smile show in front of those around you! Implants are always restored using custom-made porcelain restorations, giving them an appearance that seamlessly blends into any smile.

With dentures, you need to soak your replacement teeth overnight to keep them in good condition; however, implants require the same basic oral hygiene as natural teeth, which is easier to integrate into your daily routine.

Health Benefits

Implants not only replace missing teeth but protect the jawbone from shrinking as well. This is common after tooth loss, but the implant roots actually stimulate blood flow and growth in the area to keep the bone strong and healthy.

Being able to eat a varied diet full of fibrous, nutritious vegetables and other foods opens the door to better overall health. In other words, dental implants can help you enjoy a higher, healthier qualify of life.

Long-Term Benefits

Unfortunately, many patients are deterred by the initial cost of dental implants compared to the cost of traditional dentures and bridges. However, when you factor in that these other options have to be replaced every few years, dental implants can become the smarter choice financially. With just basic oral hygiene, implants can be trusted to last for multiple decades on end, potentially giving you the best deal in the long run.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most patients with missing teeth qualify for dental implants, but they must meet certain criteria first: they need to have an overall healthy mouth without infection, and their jawbone must be dense and strong enough to support the implant. After giving you an oral exam and taking X-rays of your jaw, your dentist can determine whether or not dental implants would be a good option for you. If they are, we can help you decide which restoration would be the best fit for your needs depending on the extent of your tooth loss.

Missing One Tooth

In this situation, we would need only a single implant to complete your smile. A dental implant crown can be placed between two healthy teeth without affecting them at all. The implant post connects the crown directly to the jawbone, just like a real tooth. This is a much more conservative approach compared to a traditional bridge in which the surrounding teeth have to be shaved down to support the prosthetic.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Regardless of where they are within the arch, a small number of implant roots, usually two, can be used to support multiple crowns, a partial denture, or even a bridge that replaces up to three teeth in a row. With a firm foundation attached to the jawbone in strategic places, implant restorations have a stronger fit, more natural appearance, and have been shown to last much longer than regular dental prosthetics.

Missing All Teeth

Even if you’ve suffered total tooth loss, we have an implant solution that can restore your smile. For patients missing all of their teeth along a single arch, we do not have to replace each one with an individual implant. Instead, we can be much more efficient and use just four to six implants to secure a full denture. Implant dentures are not only more stable than regular ones, but they also help a patient recover much more of their bite strength.

Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

After your implant surgery, our team will give you post-op instructions to follow while your mouth is recovering. It will take about 3 to 6 months for your mouth to heal completely, but any pain or discomfort that you notice will subside significantly after just 5 days; you’ll normally be able to return to work in just a couple of days. In the meantime, it’s important that you follow these instructions to keep your mouth comfortable and your implant safe during the healing process.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

After the surgery is performed, a blood clot will form around the surgery site. The purpose of a blood clot is to prevent excessive bleeding and protect the mouth during the initial healing process. It’s important to make sure that the blood clot stays in place while your gums are still recovering, so avoid any actions that could dislodge it. This includes spitting, smoking, drinking with straws, or touching the surgery site with your fingers or your tongue.

Common Side-Effects

It can sometimes be hard to tell the difference between normal side effects of oral surgery and symptoms that are pointing towards a serious issue. The following side effects are considered normal after implant surgery:

If any of these symptoms become severe or last longer than expected, call our office immediately.

  • Gradual and intermittent bleeding that can be stopped by pressing a clean piece of gauze onto the surgical site.
  • Swelling that typically lasts 2 or 3 days (although sometimes it can last over a week).
  • General discomfort that can be reduced with pain medication.

Diet

During the initial healing process, you should eat only soft foods. Good choices include mashed potatoes, soup (as long as it’s not too hot), scrambled eggs, pasta, yogurt, and ice cream. You should be able to eat normally again once the pain has subsided enough. However, your implant will still be in the process of joining with the jawbone, so you should continue to avoid chewing directly on the surgery site until you’ve received the final restoration.

Health & Oral Hygiene

You can brush your teeth normally after implant surgery, but you need to be very gentle with the implant post. Exercise caution while brushing around the implant site. Rinsing with salt water two to three times every day to keep the area around the implant site clean.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Prosthetic teeth can be attached to your implants once the posts have fused with the jawbone. This may result in minor sensitivity, but it’s nothing that can’t be managed with pain medication. There isn’t normally any swelling or bleeding after restoration attachment, so call us immediately if there are any unusual symptoms. Once you’ve received the final restoration, you’ll be able to put your new smile to use right away.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are considered the gold standard when it comes to tooth replacement options. They’re the only restoration treatment that not only rebuilds pearly whites from the roots up but can also prevent and revert jawbone loss by promoting strong bone tissue. While you can expect to enjoy your new teeth for several decades to a lifetime, their lifespan will depend entirely on your oral health and how well you take care of them. Keep reading to learn several ways to practice solid dental implant care in Fargo.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Your dental implants can’t develop cavities, as they’re constructed out of titanium alloy and don’t contain the same organic tissue as your natural teeth. This doesn’t mean the rest of your smile will be immune to dental decay, however. For this reason, you’ll need to maintain good oral hygiene every day to keep issues at bay. Neglecting to clean your smile can increase your risk of serious problems like gum disease, which can lead to implant failure. Be sure to brush twice a day, floss daily, and rinse with mouthwash frequently to keep your mouth strong and healthy.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Since your dental implants will be directly anchored to your jawbone, you’ll be able to regain 80 % or more of your biting power. This means you can enjoy most of your favorite foods like you could with your natural teeth. That said, you’ll still need to be somewhat careful about the meals you choose to munch on. Firstly, you’ll want to eat foods like low-fat dairy, lean proteins, and other treats containing plenty of calcium and vitamin C to encourage healthy gum and bone tissue.

Break Poor Oral Habits

If you want your new pearly whites to last as long as possible, you’ll need to protect them by putting a stop to certain oral habits. To make the most of your dental implants, be sure to:

  • Keep away from chewing on hard objects - You’ll want to watch out for meals that are too hard or sticky, as they can damage or wear down your restorations. You should also avoid chewing on ice, pencils, fingernails, and other tough items that can end up loosening your metal posts. Try chewing on sugar-free gum instead!
  • Never use your teeth as tools If you tend to use your teeth to open packages, bottle caps, or even hold grocery bags, you can end up damaging your dental implants later on. Make sure to keep the appropriate tools handy to avoid temptation.
  • Quit smoking Stopping this bad habit can significantly decrease your risk of dental implant failure as well as other serious oral health issues, such as gum disease and oral cancer.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Though your dental implants will be durable, they won’t be indestructible. If you enjoy physical activities where there’s a chance of getting hit in the face, ask your dentist about custom-made sports mouthguards. These appliances can provide the cushion and protection your teeth need to remain safe and intact. If you tend to grind your teeth at night, then you can ask us about nightguards for bruxism. We’ll be more than happy to recommend the best products to help your specific dental needs.

Even if you’ve rebuilt your missing teeth, you should still schedule regular dental checkups and cleanings every six months with our team. We’ll be able to monitor your oral health and address any developing problems we might find. Not only will this help you avoid or stop dental issues early on, but it’ll also allow us to ensure that your dental implants remain in pristine condition for as long as possible. We’ll also provide you with personalized preventive care and thoroughly clean your smile so that you can enjoy your renewed pearly whites for the rest of your life!

What Causes Dental Implants to Fail?

  • Failed osseointegration: If the titanium of a dental implant fails to adequately bond with the patient’s jawbone, it may not have the stable hold it needs to remain functional.
  • Peri-implantitis: Usually caused by poor oral hygiene, this condition is the result of the gum and jawbone tissues supporting an implant becoming infected, which can cause the device to loosen.
  • Injury: Blows to the face or the stress of frequent grinding can break or work an implant loose from the jawbone.
