Dental Implants: How They Work

This implant treatment is designed to mimic your natural teeth. When you need tooth replacement, a post can be secured into the jawbone to act as the root of the tooth.

It takes a few months for the post to become secure. Then, an implant dentist will attach the restoration on top of the post and abutment to replace missing teeth.

For a single-tooth replacement, a crown can be secured to the dental implant. Or, if you need to replace multiple teeth, your implant dentist might recommend dental bridges or implant-supported dentures . What disqualifies you from dental implants?

While dental implants are an excellent solution for most patients missing one or more teeth, certain health factors can affect your eligibility.

Insufficient jawbone density is one of the most common concerns, though bone grafting can often address this. Uncontrolled diabetes, active and untreated periodontal disease, heavy tobacco use, and certain autoimmune conditions may increase the risk of implant failure and typically need to be managed before treatment can proceed.

Patients currently undergoing radiation therapy to the head or neck may also need to postpone implant placement. Some medications, particularly bisphosphonates used to treat osteoporosis, require careful evaluation due to their effects on bone healing.

The good news is that many of these factors are treatable or manageable, meaning implants may still be possible once the underlying condition is addressed. What is the best age to get dental implants?

There is no single ideal age for dental implants, but there are important considerations at both ends of the spectrum. On the younger side, implants are generally not recommended until the jawbone has finished growing, which typically occurs around age 18 for women and the early 20s for men.

Placing an implant before growth is complete can result in the implant becoming misaligned as the surrounding bone continues to develop. On the other end, there is no upper age limit. Patients in their 70s, 80s, and even 90s have successfully received dental implants with excellent outcomes.

What matters most is not your chronological age but your overall health, bone quality, and the stability of any chronic conditions. If you are in good general health and have adequate bone support, or are a candidate for bone grafting, dental implants can be a viable option at nearly any adult age.

Should a 70 year old get dental implants?

Absolutely, in many cases. Age alone is not a barrier to dental implant treatment. Many patients in their 70s and beyond are excellent candidates for implants and go on to enjoy years of improved function, comfort, and confidence.

The key factors in determining candidacy are overall systemic health, bone quality, and the management of any chronic conditions such as diabetes, heart disease, or osteoporosis. A healthy 70-year-old with adequate bone density and well-managed health is just as likely to have a successful implant outcome as a younger patient.

In fact, dental implants can be particularly life-changing for older adults who have been struggling with loose or uncomfortable dentures, as implants provide a stable, secure foundation that restores the ability to eat, speak, and smile with confidence.

The Benefits of Tooth Implants

Why should you choose a tooth implant over other types of cosmetic dentistry services? If you want to restore your smile, compare your options, and you will see that dental implants offer the most benefits:

Durability

Implants are strong and can last a lifetime when you take care of them. You are investing in a life-long tooth restoration that will look good for many years in the future.

Function

A dental implant looks and feels like a natural tooth. The crown or bridge is carefully designed to fit your smile so other people won’t know you had tooth restoration treatments.

Bone Loss

If a tooth is missing, then there is a high risk of bone loss in the area since the root of the tooth is no longer in the jawbone. Placing an implant is a good way to preserve the bone so you can avoid bone loss complications in the future.

Smile Restoration

There’s no question that dental implants are an effective way to restore the smile. You’ll love the way your teeth look and feel after the implant treatment is finished.

Why are dental implants the most popular option for permanent tooth replacement?

If you are looking for the longest-lasting dental restoration, then dental implants are the best solution. A good implant looks, feels, and acts like a natural tooth. The result is an aesthetic smile that functions normally. You can smile, speak, and eat with confidence. Once your implant is in place, you should treat it like a natural tooth by brushing and flossing daily to prevent complications in the future.

What types of dental implant repair services are available?

In the unlikely event that the implant fails, then it might not be possible to repair it. Instead, the implant might need to be removed and replaced. If the restoration on top of the implant needs to be repaired, then the crown, bridge, or denture can easily be fixed.

How do full mouth dental implants work?

If you are considering full mouth dental implants, then the dentist will first complete a thorough examination to determine if you are a good candidate for this treatment. These implants are secured in the jawbone using surgical techniques. Typically, 4 – 6 implants are necessary for each jawbone arch. Once the implants have healed, then permanent or removable restorations can be placed on top of the implants. The implants function as the tooth roots and create a foundation to stabilize the dentures or bridges.

How should I choose the right dental implants dentist?

When you need dental implants, the most important decision you will make is choosing a dentist with experience in this type of treatment. Dental implants require specialized training beyond the skills learned in a general dentistry program. Look for a dentist who has training in implant dentistry specifically.

What are denture implants and how do they work?

If you need dentures to replace all of the teeth in your mouth, then implants can be part of the treatment plan to hold the dentures in place. This solution is beneficial because the denture fit is more comfortable and stable compared to traditional dentures. Instead of using adhesives or suction, the dentures are secured on the implant foundation.
