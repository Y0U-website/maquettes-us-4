How Many Dental Implants are Needed for Dentures?

The number of dental implants required can vary depending on the patient’s jawbone health, the type of denture, and the desired level of stability.

We will determine the ideal number based on your bone structure, bite alignment, and goals. During your consultation at Dens Dental Studio, we will take detailed scans and impressions to design a specialized treatment plan for your needs.

  • Lower dentures often require at least two implants for retention, though four implants can provide greater stability.
  • Upper dentures typically require four to six implants because the bone density in the upper jaw is often less than in the lower jaw.
  • Full-arch fixed dentures, which are not removed by the patient, may need as many as six to eight implants for long-term support.

Dental Implant Procedure

The process of getting implant-supported dentures involves several steps to ensure long-lasting results:

For some patients, immediate-load implants may allow for the placement of a temporary denture on the same day as surgery, reducing the overall treatment timeline.

  • Consultation and Evaluation: We begin with a thorough examination, including digital X-rays or 3D scans, to assess bone density and oral health. If bone loss is significant, a bone graft may be recommended before implant placement.
  • Implant Placement Surgery: Dental implants are surgically placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia or sedation for comfort. The implants act as artificial tooth roots, providing a strong foundation for your denture.
  • Healing Period (Osseointegration): Over the next three to six months, the implants fuse with the surrounding bone in a process called osseointegration. This step is essential for ensuring the stability of your future denture.
  • Attachment of the Denture: Once healing is complete, custom abutments are placed on the implants. The denture is then secured to these abutments, either in a removable snap-in style or as a fixed bridge.
  • Final Adjustments: We make sure your bite feels natural and comfortable, adjusting as needed to achieve optimal fit and function.

Potential Considerations

While implant-supported dentures are an excellent solution for many, there are factors to consider:

  • Surgical Procedure: Implant placement requires oral surgery, which includes a healing period.
  • Bone Health Requirements: Patients with significant bone loss may need a bone graft before implants can be placed.
  • Denture Lifespan: While implants can last decades with proper care, the denture itself may need replacement after several years due to normal wear.
