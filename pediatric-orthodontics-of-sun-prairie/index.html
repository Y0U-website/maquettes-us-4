The practice

Many traditional orthodontic clinics focus solely on aesthetics and won’t evaluate your child until age 6 or 7. By taking a proactive approach starting as early as age 3, we can correct the root causes of crooked teeth—like narrow palates and restricted breathing—while jaw bones are highly adaptable.

Orthodontic issues aren’t just about crooked teeth. We look beyond the surface—early jaw and airway development manifests in many everyday behaviors.

Resting with their mouth open during the day or sleeping with open lips at night.

Tossing, turning, snoring, or grinding teeth during sleep due to narrow airways.

A lack of spacing between front baby teeth usually indicates a narrow jaw frame.

Persistent thumb-sucking, finger-sucking, or tongue-thrust habits that alter palate shape.

Difficulty pronouncing certain consonant sounds or a persistent lisp.

Top teeth biting inside the bottom teeth or upper jaw appearing too small.
