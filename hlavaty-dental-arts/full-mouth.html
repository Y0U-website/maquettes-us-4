Full Mouth Reconstruction vs. All-on-4 Full-Arch Restoration

Patients often wonder whether they need full mouth reconstruction or an All-on-4 full-arch restoration . While there is overlap, these are distinct approaches.

All-on-4 replaces an entire arch of teeth (upper, lower, or both) with a fixed prosthesis supported by four strategically placed implants. It is designed specifically for patients who have lost — or are about to lose — most or all of their teeth in one or both arches.

Full mouth reconstruction is broader in scope. It may include All-on-4 as one component, but it can also combine dental implants , crowns, bridges, veneers, bone grafting , and other procedures to address a wider range of structural and cosmetic concerns. Reconstruction is tailored to preserve and restore what you have, rather than replacing an entire arch.

Dr. Hlavaty will help you understand which approach — or combination — best serves your goals during your consultation.

Frequently Asked Questions About Full Mouth Reconstruction

What is the difference between full mouth reconstruction and a smile makeover?

A smile makeover focuses primarily on the cosmetic appearance of your teeth, while full mouth reconstruction addresses both function and aesthetics. Reconstruction is typically recommended when there are underlying structural, health, or bite issues that need to be resolved alongside any cosmetic improvements.

How long does full mouth reconstruction take?

Treatment timelines vary depending on the complexity of your case and the procedures involved. Some plans may be completed in a few months, while more extensive cases involving dental implants or bone grafting may take a year or longer. Am I too old for full mouth reconstruction?

Age alone does not disqualify you from full mouth reconstruction. What matters most is your overall health and the condition of your oral tissues. Many patients in their 60s, 70s, and beyond successfully undergo reconstruction and enjoy a significant improvement in comfort and quality of life.

Does full mouth reconstruction require dental implants?

Not necessarily. While dental implants are a common component, your treatment plan may include crowns, bridges, veneers, or other restorative procedures depending on your needs. Dr. Hlavaty designs each plan around your specific oral health goals.
