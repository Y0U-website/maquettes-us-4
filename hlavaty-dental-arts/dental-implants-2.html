Dental Implants vs. Dentures

Patients often weigh dental implants against dentures when considering tooth replacement. Both serve an important purpose, but they differ in meaningful ways.

Traditional dentures rest on the gums and are removable. They can restore appearance and basic function, though some patients experience shifting, discomfort, or difficulty eating certain foods. Over time, the jawbone beneath dentures may gradually lose volume because it is no longer stimulated by tooth roots.

Dental implants, by contrast, are fixed in place and actively help maintain bone health. They do not require adhesives or nightly removal. Many patients find that implants feel more like their natural teeth and offer greater confidence in daily activities. For patients who currently wear dentures, implant-supported options can provide a significant upgrade in stability and comfort.

The best choice depends on your oral health, bone density, lifestyle, and personal preferences. Dr. Hlavaty takes the time to walk you through every option so you can make an informed, pressure-free decision.

What Happens When You Delay Replacing a Missing Tooth

A missing tooth affects more than your appearance. When a tooth is lost, the surrounding bone begins to resorb — gradually losing volume and density because it no longer receives stimulation from a root. Over time, this bone loss can compromise neighboring teeth, alter your bite, and change the contours of your face.

Adjacent teeth may also shift into the open space, creating alignment issues and increasing the risk of decay or gum disease. The longer replacement is delayed, the more complex — and potentially costly — treatment may become. Early evaluation with an experienced implant dentist helps preserve your options and may simplify your path to restoration.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

Most adults with good general health and adequate jawbone density are candidates for dental implants. Conditions such as bone loss, gum disease, or certain medical factors may require additional evaluation. Dr. Hlavaty uses 3D CT imaging during your consultation to assess your bone structure and determine the best path forward.

With proper care and regular dental visits, dental implants are designed to last decades — and many patients enjoy them for a lifetime. The titanium post integrates with your jawbone, creating a stable foundation that helps maintain long-term results.
