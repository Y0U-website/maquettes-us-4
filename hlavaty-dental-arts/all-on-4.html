All-on-4 vs. Full Mouth Reconstruction — Which Is Right for You

Patients exploring full-arch solutions often wonder how All-on-4 compares to a comprehensive full mouth reconstruction . The right choice depends on your clinical situation and goals.

All-on-4 is designed specifically for patients who need to replace all teeth in one or both arches. It uses four implants per arch to support a single fixed prosthesis, and it is often possible even when moderate bone loss is present — potentially avoiding the need for bone grafting .

Full mouth reconstruction is a broader treatment approach that may combine individual implants, crowns, bridges, veneers, and other restorative procedures to rebuild the entire mouth. It is typically recommended when a patient still has some healthy teeth worth preserving, or when concerns extend beyond tooth replacement to bite alignment, jaw function, or aesthetics across both arches.

During your consultation, Dr. Hlavaty will review your 3D imaging, discuss your priorities, and recommend the approach that delivers the strongest long-term outcome for your specific situation.

What to Expect During Your All-on-4 Procedure

Understanding the treatment timeline helps you feel prepared and confident at every stage.

Consultation and 3D CT-Guided Planning

Your journey begins with a comprehensive evaluation. Dr. Hlavaty uses digital 3D CT scanning to map your jawbone density, nerve pathways, and sinus anatomy in precise detail. This imaging allows him to plan the exact position, angle, and depth of each implant before treatment begins — reducing surgical time and improving predictability. You will also discuss your health history, aesthetic goals, and any questions about sedation options to ensure your comfort throughout the procedure.

Implant Placement and Temporary Restoration

On the day of surgery, any remaining compromised teeth are extracted, and four titanium implants are placed into the jawbone according to your computer-guided plan. When the clinical conditions support it, a temporary fixed prosthesis is secured to the implants that same day. Sedation is available at every level — from nitrous oxide to IV sedation to general anesthesia — so you can choose the comfort level that is right for you.

Final Prosthesis and Long-Term Care

Over the following three to six months, your implants integrate with the surrounding bone through a process called osseointegration. Once healing is complete, Dr. Hlavaty replaces the temporary prosthesis with your final restoration — a custom-designed, durable arch crafted for natural appearance and lasting function. Regular follow-up visits and daily home care keep your All-on-4 restoration performing at its best for years to come.

What Happens When You Delay Full-Arch Treatment

Postponing treatment for complete or near-complete tooth loss carries real consequences. Without the stimulation that tooth roots or implants provide, the jawbone continues to resorb — losing volume and density over months and years. This progressive bone loss can eventually make implant placement more complex, potentially requiring grafting procedures that add time and cost.

Beyond the bone, delaying treatment affects daily quality of life. Difficulty chewing leads many patients to avoid nutritious foods, which may impact overall health. Speech changes, social self-consciousness, and the constant management of ill-fitting dentures take a cumulative toll. The sooner you pursue a stable, long-term solution, the more bone you preserve and the simpler your treatment path is likely to be.

Am I a candidate for All-on-4 dental implants?

Most adults who are missing all or most of their teeth in one or both arches may be candidates for All-on-4 implants. The angled placement of the posterior implants often allows treatment even when some bone loss has occurred. How long do All-on-4 implants last?

The titanium implants that anchor an All-on-4 restoration are designed to last a lifetime with proper care. The fixed prosthesis attached to those implants typically lasts 15 to 20 years or longer before it may need replacement. Regular follow-up visits and consistent home care help maximize the longevity of your restoration.

What is the recovery time for All-on-4?

Most patients return to light daily activities within a few days of their procedure. You will follow a soft-food diet for several weeks while the implants integrate with your jawbone. Full healing and osseointegration typically take three to six months, at which point your final prosthesis is placed.

Do I need bone grafting for All-on-4?

One of the key advantages of All-on-4 is that it is specifically designed to work with available bone. The two posterior implants are placed at an angle to engage denser bone areas, which often eliminates the need for bone grafting. However, some patients with significant bone loss may still benefit from a grafting procedure , which Dr. Hlavaty will determine during your 3D CT evaluation.
