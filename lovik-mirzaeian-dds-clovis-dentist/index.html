When a tooth is missing, the bone underneath slowly shrinks. An implant stimulates the bone the same way a natural root does, helping prevent bone loss and the sunken look that can come with it.

Unlike removable dentures, implants stay firmly in place. You can enjoy the foods you love and speak naturally, without worrying about slipping or clicking.

For a single missing tooth an implant is usually the better option, because it replaces the root as well as the crown and does not require cutting down the healthy teeth beside the gap. A bridge makes more sense when those neighbouring teeth already need crowns, when there is not enough bone for an implant, or when you need it finished quickly.

Both work. The right answer depends on the teeth either side of the gap, your bone, your timeline and your budget. Here is how to think about it.

An implant is a titanium post placed into the jawbone where the root used to be. The bone grows onto it over a few months, then a crown is attached. Nothing is done to the teeth on either side.

A bridge spans the gap. The teeth on both sides are prepared, meaning healthy enamel is removed, and a single unit of three crowns is cemented across, with the middle one sitting over the gap.
