Understanding Dental Implants

What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are robust, long-term solutions for missing teeth. Composed of a titanium post that integrates with the jawbone, a connecting abutment, and a custom crown, implants are designed to look, feel, and function like your natural teeth.

How Do Dental Implants Work?

The process involves implanting a titanium post directly into the jawbone, which over time fuses with the bone through a process known as osseointegration. This foundation provides unparalleled stability and durability for the replacement tooth or teeth.

Dental Implant Restorations

Dental Crown

A dental crown used in single-tooth implants is custom-made to match the color and shape of your surrounding teeth, effectively restoring and enhancing your smile without affecting adjacent teeth.

Dental Bridge

For those missing several teeth, an implant-supported bridge provides a durable solution that restores functionality and aesthetics. The bridge is anchored by implants, offering a fixed alternative that feels natural.

Implant-Supported Denture

Consider implant-supported dentures for a secure fit that improves speech, eating, and confidence. These dentures attach to strategically placed implants, ensuring they remain stable without the need for adhesives.

The Benefits of Dental Implants

Durability and Longevity

Implants are a long-term investment in your oral health, designed to last many years and provide functionality superior to other tooth replacement options.

Improved Oral Health and Comfort

Unlike traditional bridges or dentures, implants do not require altering adjacent teeth, preserving more of your natural tooth structure and improving oral health overall.

Exploring Types of Dental Implants

Endosteal Implants

The most commonly used type, these implants are placed directly into the jawbone and provide a strong, stable base for artificial teeth.

Single-Tooth Implant

Perfect for replacing one missing tooth without impacting surrounding teeth, ensuring a seamless addition to your smile.

Mini Dental Implant (MDI)

Great for patients needing less invasive options or who have minimal bone mass. MDIs are smaller but effective for stabilizing dentures.

Subperiosteal Implants

These rest on top of the jawbone but under the gum. They are an alternative for patients unable to undergo traditional implant placement.

Zygomatic Implants

These are less common and are anchored in the cheekbone rather than the jawbone, used primarily when there is significant bone loss.
