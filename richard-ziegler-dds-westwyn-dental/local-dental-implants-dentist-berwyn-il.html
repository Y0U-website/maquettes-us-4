Exactly What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium rod designed to serve as the root of a lost tooth. Titanium is biocompatible, meaning it works well with your bone. It’s topped with a small abutment and a porcelain crown to complete your brand-new tooth.

How Do Dental Implants Work for Missing Teeth?

Once the titanium post is in your jawbone, the bone fuses around it to lock it in. This process is called osseointegration. A dental implant can be a stand-alone solution for one missing tooth. Or multiple implants can support dentures or a bridge.

Is a Dental Implant Right for Me?

You need to have overall healthy teeth and gums to be a good fit for implants. Our team uses 3D X-ray imaging to ensure your bone density supports the implant. If there’s already been some bone loss, we may be able to perform a bone graft to rebuild the area. And if you have gum disease, that’ll need to be treated first.

How Does the Dental Implant Process Work?

Here’s the step-by-step process of getting a dental implant:

  • Consultation and 3D imaging to create our treatment plan.
  • Surgical placement of the titanium post.
  • Healing time while your bone fuses to the titanium post.
  • Abutment and crown placement as the final step.

How Long Does It Take to Get a Dental Implant?

Start to finish, it can take anywhere from four to nine months. The biggest chunk of time is osseointegration. That’s the part where your bone bonds to the titanium post. But if you need a bone graft first, you’ll add a few more months.

Why Are Dental Implants Better Than Bridges and Dentures?

Implants offer benefits you can’t get with conventional bridges and dentures.

  • Dental implants are a permanent fixture. They’ll last decades if you take good care of your teeth.
  • An implant stimulates your jawbone.
  • We don’t need to touch your natural teeth to place a dental implant.
  • Eat whatever you want, just like before.
  • Brushing and flossing are the same as with your real teeth.

What’s Recovery Like After Getting a Dental Implant?

After the implant surgery, you’ll have a little soreness around the site—maybe minor swelling. But tenderness and swelling go away after a few days. We recommend eating soft foods during the first week and avoiding anything crunchy. You should keep your mouth clean by rinsing with saltwater.
