The practice

Learn About the I-19 Pathway Most kids get dental visits. Very few get a plan. At every appointment, three things happen.

A 12-month-old gets a lip- and tongue-tie screen. A 6-year-old gets sealants on her first permanent molars. A 7-year-old gets a documented orthodontic evaluation, the age the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends. Different child, different milestone, same plan.

Intraoral photos, x-rays when indicated, and clinical notes go into your child’s chart at every visit. The next dentist who sees them, even if that’s the same of us six months from now, picks up exactly where this visit left off. Nothing has to be re-discovered.

Before you leave, you get a written summary of what we did today, what we’re watching, and what we expect to evaluate at the next visit. No guessing between appointments about whether something is normal or something to ask about.

A lip and tongue tie screen before their first birthday. Sealants the year the first permanent molars arrive. An orthodontic evaluation at seven, when a crossbite is still simple to correct. Every year has something.

That’s the difference between a child who has been to the dentist and a child who is on a pathway.
