The I-19 Pathway™

Three things, and only one of them is information.

Your child’s mouth is the more complicated machine, and it is the one you cannot replace. It is also the only one of the two that is changing shape every year on a schedule nobody handed you. Crowded teeth. Crossbites. Airway issues. Cavities between baby teeth. Lip and tongue ties that affect feeding and speech. None of those show up all at once. They develop over years, and by the time they are obvious, treatment is more complex, more expensive, and harder on your child. The hardest part is that most parents did not know there was something to watch for.

That is not a criticism of anybody’s dentist. It is a criticism of the paperwork. So we changed the paperwork.

02 Every visit Three things happen, every time. This is the operating definition of a pathway. It is what separates it from a recall list, and it is why your child’s plan moves forward at every appointment, even with a different team member, without you having to remember what was said two years ago.

  • "Everything looks good"
  • "See you in six months"

What is appropriate for their age, not a generic exam.

A twelve month old gets a lip and tongue tie screen. A six year old gets sealants on the first permanent molars the year they arrive. A seven year old gets a documented orthodontic evaluation. Different child, different milestone, same plan.

Photographs, so the next visit starts where this one ended.

Intraoral photos, radiographs when they will change what we do, and clinical notes go into your child’s chart at every visit. Nothing has to be re-discovered six months later, and nothing depends on somebody remembering.

In writing, in your portal, before you would think to ask.

A member of our team writes a summary of that visit in plain English and attaches it to the record you can open at home. What we did, what we are watching, and what we expect to look at next time.

One team, one roof.

Most pediatric practices refer out for orthodontics, surgery and complex treatment. We do not. Prevention, orthodontics and complex treatment happen in this building, on the same chart. See what we do here .

A protocol, not a program.

The pathway is how every visit in this office is structured, for every patient. It is not a package, there is nothing to enroll in, and there is nothing extra to buy.

Lasting Smiles

The first visit, by the first birthday.

At home: Wipe the gums after the last feeding of the day, and start brushing the moment the first tooth appears.

Visits: A first visit by age one, then we set the rhythm together.

If your child has a tooth, or is about to turn one, this is the visit to book.

  • Lip and tongue attachments that can interfere with feeding
  • The first teeth as they come in, and the gums around them
  • How your child is feeding, and what happens at night
  • A tongue tie tight enough to affect feeding now and speech later
  • Early white spots on the front teeth, the first visible stage of decay

Getting comfortable, in your lap.

At home: Move away from the bottle at sleep times. Water only after brushing.

Visits: Short, friendly visits, mostly on your lap.

One year olds do great here. The visit is shorter than you think.

  • Eruption pattern and spacing of the front teeth
  • Signs of bottle or sippy cup decay
  • Lip and tongue attachments, again, now that more has come in
  • Decay patterns that follow a bottle at naps or overnight
  • Anything that makes eating or speaking harder than it should be

The back teeth arrive.

At home: Brush for them, not with them. Two minutes, twice a day, and you do the work at this age.

Visits: Every six months for most children at this age.

Two is a great age to get a baseline, before anything is urgent.

  • The first molars and the surfaces between them
  • Habits, including thumb, finger and pacifier
  • How brushing is going at home, honestly
  • Decay starting between teeth that have just closed their contacts
  • Grinding, and whether it is the normal kind

A full set of baby teeth.

At home: Tell us if your child snores or sleeps with their mouth open. Parents rarely think to mention it, and it matters.

Visits: Every six months, and your child can usually sit in the chair alone now.

If your three year old snores or mouth breathes, mention it when you call.

  • All twenty primary teeth and the bite they make together
  • Speech patterns, and whether the mouth is part of the story
  • Airway signs: mouth breathing, snoring, restless sleep
  • An airway concern noticed now changes how the next ten years unfold
  • Habits that are starting to move teeth rather than just being habits

The first big-kid visit.

At home: Start flossing where the back teeth touch. That is the one place brushing cannot reach.

Four is when visits start feeling like a routine instead of an event.

  • A full cleaning and comprehensive exam
  • Spacing between the baby teeth, which predicts crowding later
  • Habits that are still going, and whether they need a plan
  • No spacing between baby teeth, an early signal that permanent teeth will be crowded
  • Decay between the back teeth, where a toothbrush does not reach

A watching year.

At home: Keep doing what you are doing. Check their brushing at night even when they insist they have it.

Nothing new starts at five, which is exactly what we want to be able to tell you.

  • The same full exam and cleaning
  • Any loosening front teeth, and whether the timing is normal
  • Brushing technique, now with your child doing more of it
  • Permanent teeth getting ready underneath, and whether there is room

The first permanent molars come in.

At home: Watch for the new back teeth. Most parents never notice them arrive because nothing falls out first.

Visits: Every six months, and this is usually when sealants go on.

If your child is six, the next thing on the pathway is sealants on those first permanent molars.

  • The first permanent molars, which arrive behind the baby teeth without anything falling out
  • Whether those molars are ready to be sealed
  • The front teeth starting to change over
  • Deep grooves in a brand new molar, which is the most cavity prone it will ever be
  • Permanent front teeth coming in behind the baby teeth

The orthodontic evaluation year.

At home: Nothing yet. This is the year to get the evaluation on paper so the timing is not left to chance.

Visits: Every six months, plus a documented orthodontic evaluation this year.

Seven is the year the evaluation happens. Most children need watching, not braces.

  • Jaw relationship, crowding, and crossbites, documented
  • Whether the permanent teeth have room to come in
  • Airway and breathing patterns as the face grows
  • A crossbite that is far simpler to correct while the palate is still growing
  • Crowding that is manageable now and surgical later
  • Permanent teeth that are missing, impacted, or coming in the wrong direction

Front teeth settle in.

At home: If your child plays a contact sport, ask us about a mouthguard. The permanent front teeth are in now.

Eight is a good year to sort out a sports mouthguard before you need one.

  • How the new permanent front teeth are sitting
  • Whether last year s evaluation is still reading the same way
  • Decay risk, which changes as diet and independence change
  • A bite that is drifting rather than settling

A watching year, with one exception.

  • Continued jaw growth and arch width
  • The canines, which are still years away but already moving
  • Airway and habit patterns
  • Arch width, because some corrections depend on growth that has not finished yet

The back baby teeth start to go.

  • The change from baby molars to permanent premolars
  • Space, and whether it is being lost as teeth come out
  • Second permanent molars getting ready behind the first
  • Space closing after a baby tooth is lost early, which crowds the permanent tooth behind it

Records, and the real orthodontic conversation.

  • A full picture of where the permanent teeth are and where they are going
  • Whether treatment should start now, later, or not at all
  • The second permanent molars as they arrive
