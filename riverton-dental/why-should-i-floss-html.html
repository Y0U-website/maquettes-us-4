Why Should I Floss? What Happens…

The C-shape is the whole secret. Straight up-and-down "snapping" misses the tooth surfaces and irritates gums.

  • Gently guide it between teeth with a rubbing motion — never snap it down into the gums.
  • At the gumline, curve it into a C-shape against one tooth and slide it gently under the gum until you feel light resistance.
  • Scrape up and away from the gum a few times, then re-curve around the neighboring tooth and repeat.
  • Unwind to a fresh section as you go.

Hate String Floss? These Count Too

The best interdental cleaner is the one you’ll actually use tonight. Start with whichever tool you’ll stick with.

  • Floss picks — less ideal angles than string, but vastly better than nothing, and great for building the habit.
  • Water flossers — excellent, especially with braces, bridges, implants, or dexterity issues. Research shows they reduce gum inflammation effectively.
  • Interdental brushes — tiny bottle-brushes for wider gaps; often better than floss for those spaces.

Making It Stick

Attach flossing to something you already do (right before brushing at night), keep picks visible — on your nightstand, in the car, at your desk — and start with a "just one tooth" rule on lazy nights. Momentum usually does the rest. Miss a night? Irrelevant. Consistency over weeks is what your gums respond to, not perfection.
