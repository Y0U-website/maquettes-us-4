Dental implants are titanium posts that replace the root of a missing tooth. Once placed in the jawbone, they provide a secure foundation for a custom dental crown, bridge, or full-arch prosthetic. Unlike removable options, implants are permanent and fixed, offering the closest feel and function to your natural teeth. ‍ ‍ Key Benefits of Implants: Prevent bone loss and maintain facial structure
  • Look, feel, and function like natural teeth
  • No need to alter healthy surrounding teeth
  • Improved chewing and speaking ability
  • Long-lasting — often decades with proper care

The Dental Implant Process

Step 1: Consultation & 3D Imaging

We use digital X-rays and 3D scans to evaluate bone density and plan your implant placement. We’ll review your goals and determine if you’re a good candidate.

Step 2: Implant Placement

The titanium post is surgically placed into your jawbone. Most patients report minimal discomfort, especially with sedation options available for anxiety or complex cases.

Step 3: Healing & Integration

Over 3–6 months, the implant integrates with your bone in a process called osseointegration. We may place a temporary crown or custom denture during this time, depending on the case.

Step 4: Final Restoration

Once healing is complete, we attach a custom dental crown or prosthetic that matches your natural teeth in color, shape, and function.

Frequently Asked Questions

How painful is getting a dental implant?

Most patients experience minimal discomfort, similar to a tooth extraction. We use local anesthesia and offer sedation dentistry to ensure a relaxed experience.

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants can last 20+ years, and often a lifetime with good care and regular dental checkups.

What is the best age for dental implants?

Adults of any age can receive implants once jaw development is complete, typically by the early 20s. Seniors can also benefit as long as they meet health criteria.

How long do you go without teeth when getting implants?

You won&#x27;t have to go without teeth. We often place a temporary crown or denture during the healing period so you can smile and eat comfortably.
