Ceramic Braces

These braces are fixed onto the outer surfaces of your teeth for the entire treatment. They have clear or tooth-colored brackets made from a ceramic material. Thin wires are attached to the brackets. These may be held in place by virtually invisible clear elastics. Alternatively, the brace brackets may be self-ligating, so elastics are not necessary.

Lingual Braces

These braces are also fixed but attached to the inner surfaces of the teeth, so they are far less visible. The brackets are made from metal and can be custom-made to be thinner and more comfortable. Wires are attached to the brackets.

Lingual braces can be a good option in certain circumstances, but they can be trickier to keep clean and tend to be more expensive than other options.

Clear Aligners

Invisalign clear aligners are probably the least obtrusive option and are extremely popular. The clear plastic aligner trays are almost invisible and minimally disruptive to everyday life, as they are removable for meals and oral care. After treatment, patients may need teeth retainers to maintain their results.

The aligner trays are custom-made from BPA-free plastic and are specifically designed to be smooth and more comfortable to wear.

Invisible Braces Step by Step

Invisible braces process starts with your consultation and continues through treatment and progress checks. Each stage is planned around your teeth, bite, and treatment goals, with a focus on comfort, precision, and predictable results. Here’s what you can expect along the way.

Step One – Consultation and Smile Evaluation

Your dentist or orthodontist will look at your teeth, bite, and overall oral health and talk with you about what you would like to change. You’ll also discuss your treatment goals and the invisible braces options that may work best for your needs and lifestyle. These can include clear aligners, ceramic braces, or lingual braces. If invisible braces are a good fit for you, your provider will recommend a treatment approach based on your specific situation.

Step Two – Digital Scanning and Treatment Planning

Next, your teeth are scanned digitally or impressions are taken to create a detailed 3D model of your smile. Your orthodontist uses specialized software to plan how each tooth needs to move and map out the different stages of treatment. In many cases, you can even see a preview of what your expected results may look like before treatment starts.

Step Three – Creating Your Custom Appliance

Once your treatment plan is ready, your invisible braces are made specifically for you. With clear aligners, a series of custom aligners is manufactured to fit your teeth and gradually move them into position. If you choose ceramic or lingual braces, the brackets and wires are customized and placed carefully to provide the precision and comfort your treatment requires.

Step Four – Active Treatment and Progress Checks

After your invisible braces are fitted, you’ll wear them according to your orthodontist’s instructions. Clear aligners are typically worn for 20–22 hours each day, while ceramic and lingual braces stay fixed in place throughout treatment. You’ll also have regular follow-up appointments so your provider can check how your teeth are moving and make adjustments when needed. As treatment continues, your smile will gradually become straighter, and you’ll start to see the changes for yourself.

Benefits of Choosing Invisible Braces

Invisible braces can offer the following benefits.

  • Treatment is far more discreet since the braces or aligners are almost invisible.
  • Invisible braces can treat a wide range of orthodontic problems, including those that are more complex.
  • These systems use the very latest technologies to achieve excellent results more comfortably.
  • Clear aligners can offer greater comfort during treatment since they do not use brackets or wires that can irritate or cause sore spots on cheeks and lips.

Who Is a Suitable Candidate for Invisible Braces?

Usually, invisible braces are recommended for teens and adults who would otherwise feel very self-conscious about wearing more visible metal braces that are fitted onto the outer surfaces of teeth.

Anyone considering orthodontic treatment needs to have reasonable dental health. It is less suitable for people who may have issues with gum disease or tooth decay, as teeth need to be strong enough to be removed safely through the gum and bone tissue.

A commitment to good oral hygiene is essential during any orthodontic treatment, especially where fixed braces are used. Failure to keep braces or aligners clean can increase the risk of oral disease.

Finally, excellent compliance is essential when using removable aligners. These need to be left in almost all of the time, preferably only removed for meals and oral care.
