What are Dental Implants

A dental implant is a replacement not just for your tooth, but also for your tooth’s root. As a foundation for permanent replacement for your teeth, implants are integrated to your bone like natural teeth. They’re placed in the area where you’re missing a tooth or teeth and help to preserve bone deterioration that often follows tooth loss. Implants help to:

  • Increase or restore oral aesthetics
  • Restore your mouth to optimal function
  • Prevent complications that can occur from missing teeth
  • Stabilize the surrounding gum tissue, bone and teeth

Why Consider Dental Implants in Cary

Here are just a few of the benefits they provide:

  • Improved speech — Since implants won’t slip around in your mouth like dentures, you’ll be able to speak clearly.
  • Better comfort and convenience — Implants are comfortable and convenient. They become a part of you, so you don’t need to worry about removing them like you would dentures.
  • Natural appearance — You’ll love that implants provide a natural appearance. They feel and look like real teeth.
  • Improved oral health — Implants can be a great for your oral health because you don’t need to alter other teeth for support as you do with bridges. Implants also make it easier to clean your teeth, which can improve your oral hygiene.
  • Easy chewing and eating — Dentures can slide around and make it tough to eat. Since implants function similar to natural teeth, you’ll enjoy eating again.
  • Durable and long-lasting — Implants are an investment, but they’re durable and restore function if well maintained.

What to Expect with Dental Implant Treatment

It generally takes a few appointments over a period of months to complete dental implant treatment. The site needs to be prepared, then the implant will be placed into your bone with a special healing cap. Next, the implant needs to heal so it offers a stable foundation for the replacement tooth.

When the site is completely healed and ready, the crown (new tooth) can be placed. Once that is accomplished and the implant is complete, you’ll enjoy the optimal shape and function of your new tooth while the implant protects, stabilizes and preserves your surrounding tissue.

  • Initial consultation and exam
  • Removal or extraction of damaged tooth if needed
  • Bone grafting if needed
  • Placement of dental implant
  • Crown placed onto the dental implant post

Caring for Your New Implanted Teeth

Maintaining good oral health is always important and once your implants are in place, it’s essential to care for them properly to ensure their success and longevity. If not properly cleaned and managed it may lead to dental surgery . Caring for your implants is very similar to caring for natural teeth.

You’ll need to brush your dental implants just like you do your natural teeth. Brush both below and at the gum line. You also need to floss or use an interdental brush between teeth and implants.

With regular brushing and flossing, you can prevent plaque buildup. This is critical because plaque buildup has the potential to cause gum inflammation. This can result in implant-related gum disease that, if not treated, could result in the loss of your implant.

FAQ People Often Ask

What is an abutment used for in dental implants?

An a butment is inserted into the dental implant and the crown is then attached to finish the implant process.

How do I choose between dental implants and rebuilding the existing tooth with dental crowns?

Having a natural tooth saved with a dental crown placed on top would be preferred but for badly damaged or infected teeth, a dental implant will be a permanent replacement for a missing tooth.
