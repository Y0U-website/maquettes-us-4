What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are comprised of two main parts in order to replace the entire structure of a missing tooth. The first part is the dental implant post, which is typically made of biocompatible material called zirconia. The second part is a custom-made restoration that is carefully designed to blend in seamlessly with your remaining healthy teeth. Depending on how many teeth you’re missing, this restoration could be a crown, bridge, partial, or denture. The two components are fastened together via a small connector piece called an abutment.

Unlike other tooth loss solutions that only replace the visible portion of a missing tooth or teeth, dental implants can rebuild your smile both above and below the gumline. The dental implant post is inserted directly into the jawbone to replace the roots of a missing tooth. Not only does this provide your new teeth with lifelike comfort and stability, but it also protects your healthy teeth and keeps your jawbone healthy. The custom-made restoration on top will be precisely crafted out of dental porcelain, which is renowned for how much it resembles the appearance of natural tooth enamel.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Unlike other tooth replacement options, getting dental implants is a multi-step process that’s completed over the course of several months. While this may seem like a long time, it’s important to keep in mind that the results have the potential to last a lifetime. During your consultation, we’ll go over your entire treatment plan so you’ll know exactly what to expect. In general, there are four main stages: the consultation, the surgery, osseointegration, and placement of the final restoration.

After your dental implant placement surgery, you’ll need to wait three to six months before moving on to the next step of your treatment. While it will only take you a few days or weeks to recover from your procedure, your jawbone will need much more time to heal. Over the coming months, a process known as osseointegration takes place, where the dental implant and bone tissue fuse together.

Once you’re done healing, you’ll have a strong, stable foundation for your new replacement teeth. However, before your restoration can be placed, you’ll need to undergo one more minor surgery to expose the top of the dental implant and have a small connector piece called an abutment placed.   • A confident smile. The restorations on top of dental implants are usually crafted out of dental-grade porcelain, which is almost identical to natural teeth. Onlookers will notice your beautiful smile, not that you have had some dental work done.

Health Benefits

The benefits of dental implants near Las Colinas extend beyond improving daily quality of life. They offer some outstanding health advantages as well, including:

  • Jawbone preservation. Dental implants are the only tooth loss solution that replaces the roots of missing teeth. This stimulates circulation in the jawbone, which prevents it from deteriorating and shrinking away. They can therefore prevent the sunken facial appearance that often accompanies tooth loss.
  • Protection for oral tissues. Traditional fixed bridges require the removal of healthy tooth structure for support, while dentures place pressure on your gums, which can cause irritation. On the other hand, dental implants are fully self-supporting. You won’t need to worry about tooth sensitivity or gum sores.
  • Systemic health benefits. Research indicates an association between tooth loss and an increased risk of several diseases . Because dental implants are the best form of tooth replacement, it is reasonable to believe that they may help to lower such risks.

What Is a 3D Cone Beam CT Scanner?

A 3D cone beam CT (computed tomography) scanner is an advanced type of X-ray. It uses cone beam radiography to create detailed three-dimensional images of your teeth, dental structures, sinus cavity, jawbone, and nerves in your face. To use it, you’ll sit down and place your chin on the designated resting space. Then the machine will rotate completely around your head in a matter of moments, taking 150-200 images at various angles. A special software is then used to combine all these images into a digital 3D model of the inside of your mouth.

3D Cone Beam Scanner Vs Digital X-Rays

Digital X-rays give us tremendous insight into the well-being of your teeth and jaws, and are ideal for spotting issues like cavities and small cracks. However, they are only two-dimensional images and are somewhat limited in what they can show. A CT scanner provides a far more detailed picture of your mouth that can be rendered into a 3D model on our computers, allowing us to inspect your oral structures from every side and truly understand how they work together. Plus, the cone beam scanner shows more of your anatomical structure, such as your blood vessels and nerve pathways.

Using the 3D Cone Beam Scanner for Dental Implants

3D cone beam CT scanners are often used for a variety of dental procedures that require a detailed understanding of the mouth, including tooth extractions, orthodontics, and TMJ dysfunction therapy. They are especially key for positioning dental implants within the jaw in a way that maximizes their chances of long-term success.

Using the detailed 3D image of your mouth from the 3D cone beam scanner, we can precisely determine the best possible location for your dental implant(s). Plus, it will help us anticipate any potential obstacles we may have otherwise missed and plan accordingly, ensuring your placement surgery goes smoothly and predictably.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implant care is essential if you want your new teeth to last as long as possible. Knowing how to properly take care of them over the years will ensure that they remain firmly in place and avoid complications or problems along the way. Although it might take some time to adjust to your new teeth, before you know it, you’ll be embracing the benefits only these prosthetics can provide while also committing to taking the best possible care of your smile.

Making Oral Hygiene a Priority

Dental implants may not decay, but if you have any remaining healthy teeth, they certainly can. This is why it’s crucial to maintain optimal oral hygiene while wearing these customized prosthetics. You’ve invested in your smile, so allowing decay to spread throughout your mouth will only cause problems that can lead to tooth and implant removal. Brushing, flossing, and rinsing regularly will be a practice you need to adopt when choosing dental implants.

Eating a Healthy Diet

Sugars and starches may be tempting and tasty, but they will wreak havoc on your dental implants. This is why you need to make sure that you are incorporating healthy, nutrient-rich foods like leafy greens, lean proteins, fruits, vegetables, and low-fat dairy into your diet. These foods will help to build up healthy tooth enamel among natural teeth while also working to combat gum disease, which can negatively impact your entire smile.

Breaking Bad Habits

Smoking, eating ice cubes, biting your fingernails, using your teeth to open containers and packages, and chewing on pens and pencils are all ways you can damage your dental implants. Should this happen, you’ll be on your way to seeing an emergency dentist who will need to survey the damage and determine if the implant needs to be removed. Giving up these bad habits, however, will ensure that your teeth remain in place and free of any damage for years to come.

Protecting Your Dental Implants

A customized mouthguard is one of the best investments you can make after receiving dental implants. The reason is that if you enjoy playing sports, you don’t want to run the risk of damaging your smile. A hard hit, a sudden fall, or a spiked volleyball to the face can cause your new teeth to be knocked out or damaged, resulting in more money being spent to try and fix your smile. These mouthguards are also helpful if you grind your teeth at night, as they serve to keep your teeth from touching and minimize jaw discomfort.

Scheduling Regular Dental Checkups

Your implant dentist is your ally and friend when it comes to keeping your new teeth in place. Seeing us every six months for regular checkups and cleanings is important, especially if you want to make sure that you’re taking care of your smile the right way. After a thorough examination, we’ll offer additional tips to ensure the longevity of your new smile while also recommending any treatment options that might be necessary to fix minor damage.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Once your dental implant surgery is over, you will need to have someone escort you home from your appointment. The reason is that the local anesthesia you receive will take time to dissipate, so you will be unable to drive. Once you are home, it is best if you take an over-the-counter pain reliever or any prescribed antibiotics before the anesthesia fully wears off, as this will minimize any discomfort you might experience.

You will also need to be mindful of getting plenty of rest within the first 24 hours and avoid smoking, using tobacco of any kind, or engaging in any sort of physical or strenuous activity.
