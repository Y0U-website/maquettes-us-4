The dental implant process can take several months from start to finish because healing time is needed after implant placement. This allows the implant body to fuse with the surrounding bone before the final replacement tooth is placed.

There are different types of dental implants designed to replace a single tooth, multiple teeth, or a larger number of teeth. Your dentist will recommend the most common type of implant based on your oral health, bone density, and treatment plan.

Bone grafting or bone augmentation may be recommended to improve bone density. This helps create a stable foundation for implant placement.

As with any surgical procedure, dental implant surgery carries some risks, such as infection, delayed healing, or issues at the implant site. Following post-surgical instructions and maintaining good oral hygiene helps reduce these risks.

Yes, dental implants are widely used and approved by health authorities in the United States, including the FDA. When placed by trained dental professionals, they are considered a safe and effective tooth replacement option.
