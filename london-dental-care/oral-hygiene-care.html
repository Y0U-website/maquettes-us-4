Nutrition and Oral Health

A healthy diet supports both good dental health and overall oral health. Sugary foods and drinks feed bacteria that cause tooth decay. Limiting snacks between meals gives your teeth time to recover from acid attacks.

Eating foods rich in calcium and vitamins helps keep healthy teeth and strong gums. Drinking water after meals helps rinse away food particles and supports saliva production.

Tobacco Use and Oral Health Risks

Using tobacco products increases the risk of serious oral health problems, including gum disease and oral cancer. Tobacco use can also lead to bad breath, stained teeth, and delayed healing after dental treatment. Avoiding tobacco is one of the best ways to protect the care of your oral health.

Oral Hygiene for the Whole Family

Good oral hygiene habits should start early and continue throughout life. Teaching the whole family proper brushing and flossing habits promotes long-term health. Children benefit from learning good oral care routines that protect their teeth as they grow.

Adults should also stay consistent with their oral hygiene routine and schedule dental exams as recommended.

The following are indications of good oral hygiene:

  • Your teeth are clean and free of debris
  • Gums are pink and do not hurt or bleed when you brush or floss
  • Bad breath is not a constant problem
