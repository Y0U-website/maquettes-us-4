Emergency Dentistry

  • Swollen or bloody gums, lips, cheeks, or tongue

  • A pimple-like, painful bump on the gums (dental abscess)

What should I do in a dental emergency?

First, remain calm, as panicking can make matters worse. Then, assess the situation. If there is excessive bleeding or extreme pain, then go to the nearest emergency room. If the situation does not warrant a trip to the ER, then contact our emergency dentist in Villa Rica as soon as possible.

While waiting to see Dr. Stacey, patients can prepare for urgent care by following these simple instructions for the following emergencies:

Avulsed (Knocked-out) Tooth

If you have a knocked-out tooth, be sure to keep the tooth safe and clean. Dr. Stacey recommends trying to put the tooth back into the empty socket while you wait to be seen. If that is too painful, then keep the tooth safely tucked away in the side of your cheek. Alternatively, patients can keep the tooth safe and moist by storing it in a glass of milk.

Broken or Cracked Tooth

Broken or cracked teeth can be extremely sharp, so protect your tongue and cheeks by covering the tooth with dental wax or a piece of sugar-free chewing gum. If there are any fragments of tooth, then bring those with you to our office.

Dental Infections or Abscesses

An untreated dental infection can spread to surrounding tissue or even the bloodstream, especially if a pus-filled abscess is present. While it may be tempting to pop an abscess, patients should refrain from touching it. Our skilled team can drain the abscess safely to prevent spreading the infection.

Damaged Soft Tissue

Soft, oral tissues are prone to bleeding when injured. Patients should try to control the bleeding while waiting to see Dr. Stacey by rinsing with a saltwater solution and applying light pressure with gauze pads or clean tea bags.

Extreme, Unexplained Toothache

Patients can reduce dental pain and soft-tissue swelling by taking over-the-counter painkillers (like ibuprofen or acetaminophen) and using ice packs. Rinsing with a saltwater solution also keeps the mouth clean and prevents oral infections.

Loose or Lost Dental Restorations

If a dental restoration (like a dental crown or filling) is loose, then schedule an emergency dentistry appointment right away. If the restoration is missing, then keep the tooth safe by covering it with dental wax or a piece of sugar-free gum.
