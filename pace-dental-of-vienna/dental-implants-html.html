What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a complex tooth replacement that is broken down into three components. This includes the implant post that acts as the replacement root, the porcelain restoration that’s affixed to the implant, and the abutment that connects the two components and provides long-term stabilization. Dental implants can be used to replace single teeth, but they can also be placed throughout the mouth to hold a larger implant-retained denture.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants continue to gain popularity as an ideal way to replace missing teeth because of their unique advantages. They’re the only restoration embedded directly into and eventually fuse with your jawbone. As a result, they’re stronger, more reliable, and have a longer lifespan than alternative options like dental bridges and dentures.

But that’s not all they do! Getting dental implants can increase your daily quality of life and contribute to your health for years to come. Continue reading to learn more about the many benefits of this one-of-a-kind solution, and feel free to contact us if you have additional questions.

Day-to-Day Benefits

Tooth loss can greatly detract from your usual standard of living because tasks that once felt like second nature, like smiling, eating, and speaking, are much more difficult. Thankfully, these prosthetics can help by:

  • Restore your ability to grind food . Once your prosthetics are placed, you can enjoy anywhere from 70 % up to 100 % of your original chewing power.
  • Boost your confidence . Your artificial teeth are made from lifelike materials that can be customized to match your natural grin to enhance your appearance.
  • Maintain dental hygiene . Because they’re a permanent fixture, keeping them clean is as simple as brushing and flossing twice daily.

Health Benefits

Tooth loss can also impact your health in several different ways. Not only can you develop gastrointestinal issues or malnutrition because it’s harder to chew, but you could also develop sunken cheeks and saggy skin as your jawbone thins.

Thankfully, dental implants also offer many perks that are less immediately obvious to improve both your dental and general well-being, such as:

  • Prevent dental drifting . Your natural teeth will likely shift out of place to fill in any gaps, which can wear down their enamel and cause damage or decay. Refurbishing your grin in this way helps keep them in their proper positions.
  • Preserve your jawbone . Without a root in place to stimulate new bone growth when you bite down, your jaw starts to erode. The titanium rod of your restoration fulfills this purpose to keep it active and healthy.
  • Fewer dental concerns . Because they’re easy to clean, you’re less likely to develop oral issues like cavities or gum disease.

Long-Term Benefits

Many patients who restore their smile with implants enjoy the benefits for the remainder of their natural lives. These prosthetics are known to last 20+ years, which means you can continue to enjoy many long-term advantages like:

  • They have an incredibly high success rate of 95 %, even up to 10 years after being placed, which can provide some well-deserved peace of mind.
  • These tend to be stronger than options like dentures or dental bridges, and require fewer repairs over the years.
  • Save money . Though they tend to be more costly initially, you might spend less in the long run because you’re less likely to need to fix or replace them.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

Once the implants are placed in your jaw, they start the process of osseointegration, where they gradually fuse with the surrounding bone and tissue. This typically takes three to six months and is crucial for creating a stable foundation for your new teeth, ensuring they remain secure without slipping or falling out.

After confirming that osseointegration is complete, your dentist will attach abutments to the implants. These small metal connectors are essential for securing your restorations in place, providing a strong and reliable connection for your new teeth.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Your new dental implants may be able to keep your smile strong, healthy, and complete for a lifetime if you give them excellent care while practicing proper oral hygiene. However, certain bad habits, such as neglecting routine dental appointments, can make implant failure much more likely due to increased risks of damage to the appliance and infections of the gums. Here’s a brief guide to how you can set your implants up to last for as long as possible, but please contact the team at Pace Dental of Vienna if you have any questions.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While dental implants are impervious to cavities, poor oral hygiene can lead to gum infections or jawbone erosion that can cause them to fail. Luckily, implants only require the same care regimen as real teeth to stay healthy, so stick to a daily routine that includes brushing, flossing, and using antibacterial mouthwash to keep your new teeth strong, gorgeous, and infection-free.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants allow patients to eat whatever they like, meaning they can enjoy a much more nutritious and diverse diet than they could with traditional restorations like dentures and dental bridges. While overindulging in foods that are sugary, sticky, or hard can cause problems for your implants, items that are rich in calcium, protein, and vitamin C can help your gums and jawbones stay healthy and strong.

Break Bad Habits

Tobacco use is a leading cause of implant failure because nicotine impedes the gums’ ability to heal and fight infections, so kicking a smoking or chewing habit can be the best thing you can do for your smile. Grinding your teeth out of frustration or using your smile to open packages or bottles or to chew on non-food items can also lead to severe damage to your implants and natural pearly whites, but being mindful of these habits can help you break them.

Protect Your Dental Implants

While dental implants are incredibly resistant to injury, they are not invincible and can be damaged by trauma. When you play sports or ride a bike, wear an athletic mouthguard to protect your smile against blows to the face. If you grind your teeth at night, wearing a nightguard can help prevent unnecessary wear and tear.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

It’s still important to see your dentist regularly, even if you have lost all of your natural teeth. Visiting our Vienna office for routine exams and cleanings every six months allows our dentists to monitor your oral health, correct any issues in their early stages, and advise you about better oral hygiene practices at home.
