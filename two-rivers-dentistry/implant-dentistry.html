The Implant Dentistry Process

A thorough exam, imaging, and medical review confirm candidacy. This visit clarifies options such as single implants, an implant-supported bridge, or a full-arch solution, and covers dental implant procedure steps.

Step 2: Tooth Preparation and Grafting (If Needed)

Damaged teeth may be removed. Where bone is thin, grafting can help support future implants. Healing times vary based on the site and overall health.

The implant is placed under local anesthesia. Many patients describe mild pressure rather than pain. A healing cap or cover screw is positioned, and the area is allowed to rest.

Bone bonds to the implant over several months. Your dental implant recovery timeline depends on the location of the implant, whether grafting was needed, and personal healing factors.

After integration, an abutment is attached and impressions are taken. A custom crown, bridge, or denture is then secured for a natural look and feel.

What to Expect

Most patients experience minor swelling and soreness for a few days. Plan for a soft-food diet and careful cleaning around the site. Avoid smoking and follow home-care instructions to protect healing tissues. Long term, maintain routine checkups and daily brushing and flossing to reduce risks such as peri-implantitis. If you are weighing dental implant vs bridge, implants help preserve bone and avoid reshaping neighboring teeth, while a bridge may be faster if the adjacent teeth also need crowns.
