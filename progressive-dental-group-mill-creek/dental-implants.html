Why Patients Choose Dental Implants

Dental implants are often selected because they function similarly to natural teeth.

Unlike bridges, implants do not require adjacent teeth to be reshaped. Unlike dentures, they do not shift during normal daily activities.

  • Strong biting ability
  • Improved speech stability
  • A natural appearance
  • Preservation of nearby healthy teeth
  • Support for long-term jawbone health

Types of Dental Implants

Single Tooth Implant

A single implant supports one custom crown. This option replaces one missing tooth without affecting neighboring teeth.

Implant-Supported Bridge

When several teeth in a row are missing, implants can anchor a bridge. This may reduce the number of implants required.

Full Mouth Dental Implants

Full arch treatment uses multiple implants to support an entire row of replacement teeth. This option provides more stability than traditional removable dentures.

Many adults are candidates for dental implants, but a consultation is necessary to confirm suitability.

Certain medical conditions or tobacco use may affect healing. Your dentist will review your medical history and perform imaging to determine the safest approach.

  • Have one or more missing teeth

The Dental Implant Process

Dental implant treatment takes place in stages to allow proper healing.

Consultation and Evaluation

Your dentist will perform a comprehensive exam, take digital images, and evaluate bone levels. A customized treatment plan will be developed.

Implant Placement

The implant is placed into the jawbone during a minor surgical procedure. Most patients return to normal daily activities shortly afterward.

Healing Phase

Over several months, the implant integrates with the bone. Temporary restorations may be used if needed.

Final Restoration

Once healing is complete, a custom-designed crown, bridge, or denture is attached. The final restoration is crafted to blend with your natural teeth.

Do You Need a Bone Graft Before Dental Implants?

Bone loss can occur after a tooth is lost. If there is insufficient bone to support an implant, a bone graft may be recommended.

A bone graft helps rebuild and strengthen the jawbone. After healing, implant placement can proceed safely.

Your dentist will determine whether grafting is necessary based on digital imaging and clinical evaluation.

Recovery and Long-Term Care

Most patients experience mild swelling or tenderness for a few days after implant placement. Following post-operative instructions supports healing.

Dental implants require the same care as natural teeth. With consistent hygiene and regular checkups, implants can function reliably for many years.

  • Daily brushing and flossing
  • Routine dental visits
  • Avoiding tobacco use
  • Maintaining overall health

Understanding Risks and Considerations

Dental implants are widely used and have a strong history of success. However, as with any surgical procedure, risks exist.

Careful planning, proper technique, and patient compliance help reduce these risks. Your dentist will discuss any concerns during your consultation.

  • Infection
  • Delayed healing
  • Implant failure
  • Nerve sensitivity
