What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants consist of three parts: the restoration (crown, bridge, or denture), the abutment (metal connector), and the implant post (root). These titanium posts deliver continuous stimulation to the jaw to minimize additional bone and tooth loss in the future. When surgically placed within the jaw, they will fuse with the surrounding tissues to create a foundation of support for the new crown, bridge, or denture that will be secured on top.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

  • Initial Dental Implant Consultation You will meet with Dr. Kiesel to determine if you are a good candidate for dental implants. In most cases, patients need additional treatment to address problems such as gum disease, tooth decay, or bone loss before being able to receive dental implants.
  • Dental Implant Surgery Once you are cleared for surgery, we will refer you to an outside specialist who will place your implants into the appropriate positions along the jawbone. This will require the use of anesthesia to ensure you are numb and comfortable throughout the procedure.
  • Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment After your implants are put into place, you will spend 4-6 months allowing osseointegration to occur, which is the fusing of the implants with the bone. Once you’re fully healed, you’ll come back to receive the metal abutments, which will help to hold your restoration in place.
  • Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s) The final step is to receive your crown, bridge, or denture. Using impressions taken of your smile, we will work with local labs to create a natural-looking restoration that looks and feels great when put into place.

Dental Implant FAQs

Does getting dental implants hurt?

You will receive local anesthesia before the surgery begins, so you should feel no pain throughout the procedure. Since the jawbone does not have many nerve endings, you should expect to remain free of any discomfort. If necessary, you can inquire about dental sedation, which can also lower your body’s ability to register pain. After surgery, you can expect mild soreness in the days following your procedure; however, prescribed or over-the-counter pain relievers can help minimize this. Also, a cold compress can help.

How successful are dental implants?

As long as your dental implants are placed with a skilled professional who is expertly trained in this type of dentistry, you can expect your placement to have a success rate of over 95 %. Of course, this also depends on the materials used to craft your prosthetics and how well you take care of them once they are in place. This means maintaining routine checkups, practicing good oral hygiene, avoiding harmful habits, and wearing proper mouth protection.

The success of your dental implants can also be determined by their location in the mouth. Molars may wear down faster than teeth toward the front simply because they experience greater pressure from chewing and eating.

Am I too young to get dental implants?

Young adulthood marks the point when most people’s jaws are fully developed. If dental implants are placed before this happens, it can interfere with jaw and bone development, resulting in serious complications. This is why most dentists do not recommend this type of procedure until a person reaches 18. Of course, it can take longer for the jaw and bone to develop, as many individuals do not see this completion until their mid-20s.

A thorough evaluation of your oral and facial anatomy is required before receiving dental implants. At this time, your dentist can tell you whether you can receive these prosthetics or if you need to wait.

Will I have to take off work for dental implant surgery?

Yes, you will need to take time off from work for dental implant surgery; however, the number of days you will need depends on the number of dental implants in Coppell you receive and how well you heal. In most cases, 1-2 days off are common when receiving these permanent prosthetics. Each case differs, so you can expect your dentist to explain how much time you will need to spend at home before returning to work.

Also, the type of job you have can determine when you can return. If your work is physically demanding, you may need to take 3-4 days off, as strenuous exercise or activity can increase bleeding and delay healing.
