Implant-Supported Dentures

  • Brush Gently: Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and a non-abrasive denture cleaner to clean all surfaces of your dentures gently. Avoid using regular toothpaste, as it can be too harsh and scratch the denture material.
  • Clean Implants: Pay special attention to cleaning around the implant attachments. Use a soft interdental brush or floss threader to remove plaque and food particles from these areas.
  • Soak (if removable): If your dentures are removable, soak them overnight in a denture cleansing solution to kill bacteria and freshen your dentures.
  • Brush and Floss: Even though you have dentures, it s crucial to brush your gums, tongue, and any remaining natural teeth at least twice a day with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste. This helps to maintain healthy gum tissue and prevent infection.
  • Rinse Regularly: Use an antibacterial mouthwash to rinse your mouth after brushing and after meals to help reduce bacteria and freshen your breath.
  • Professional Cleaning: Visit your oral surgeon or dentist for regular checkups and professional cleanings. They can remove any stubborn plaque or tartar buildup and ensure your implants and dentures are in good condition.
  • Address Concerns Promptly: If you notice any signs of irritation, discomfort, or loosening of your dentures, contact your oral surgeon immediately. Early intervention can prevent more serious problems from developing.
  • Avoid Hard Foods: While implant-supported dentures are strong, it s best to avoid extremely hard or sticky foods that could damage your dentures or implants.
  • Don t Smoke: Smoking can increase the risk of implant failure and other oral health problems.
  • Protect Your Dentures: When not wearing your dentures, store them in a safe place to prevent damage.
