Fluoride Treatments for Kids

Fluoride is one of the most well-supported tools in pediatric preventative dentistry, but it often comes with more questions than parents expect. Our team takes the time to walk through what fluoride does, why we recommend it, and how it fits into your child’s overall care.

The Benefits of Dental Sealants

Sealants are among the most straightforward preventative tools available, and they tend to be underused simply because parents aren’t always aware of how much they help. The back molars, which come in around age six and again around age twelve, have deep grooves where food and bacteria can hide even after brushing. Sealants fill in those grooves with a thin protective coating, making those surfaces much easier to clean.

Dr. Basilicato often mentions sealants when talking about what parents most commonly overlook. The procedure is quick, non-invasive, and can meaningfully reduce a child’s risk of cavities in those hard-to-clean areas. If your child is approaching the age when permanent molars come in, it’s worth asking about sealants at your next visit.

Why You Should Start Your Child’s Preventative Care Early

The recommendation is clear: bring your child in by their first birthday, or within six months of their first tooth, whichever comes first. That first visit isn’t about drilling or complicated procedures. It’s about getting your child comfortable in the dental chair, giving parents practical guidance, and establishing a baseline we can track over time.

Frequently Asked Questions About Preventive Dentistry

  • How often should my child come in for a preventive dental visit? Most children benefit from checkups and cleanings every six months. However, children who are at higher risk for cavities, including those who frequently consume sugary drinks or have a history of decay, may benefit from more frequent visits. Dr. Basilicato assesses each child’s individual risk profile and recommends a schedule that fits their specific needs, not just a standard interval.
  • What does a preventive dental visit include? A typical preventive visit at Smile Care for Kids includes a thorough cleaning to remove plaque and tartar buildup, a comprehensive exam by Dr. Basilicato, and X-rays when indicated by your child’s age and history. We also apply fluoride varnish, discuss your child’s brushing and diet habits, and address any developmental concerns we spot during the exam.
  • Do baby teeth really need preventive care if they fall out anyway? Yes, absolutely. Baby teeth hold space for the permanent teeth that follow, and decay or early loss can cause misalignment, speech issues, and pain. Preventive care during the primary tooth years builds the habits and the oral environment that set your child up for healthy permanent teeth. Treating cavities in baby teeth is far less costly and involved than managing the problems that stem from neglecting them.
  • What is the best way to prevent cavities in children at home? The foundation is brushing twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste, flossing once daily, and limiting sugary drinks and snacks between meals. For younger children, a parent should assist with brushing until around age 7 to ensure all surfaces are cleaned. Dental sealants and fluoride treatments applied at our office add an extra layer of protection, especially on the molars where decay most commonly starts.
