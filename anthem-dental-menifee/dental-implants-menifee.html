Missing a tooth for years? Bone, grafting and what the scan shows

When a tooth is lost, the bone that held it gradually shrinks, because nothing is stimulating it any more. That is why people who have been missing a tooth for a long time often ask whether it is too late for an implant. Usually it is not — but it is the question the 3D scan exists to answer.

What the cone-beam scan measures

  • The height and width of the remaining bone where the implant would go.
  • The position of nerves in the lower jaw and the sinus floor in the upper jaw.
  • Bone density, which affects healing time.
  • The angle of neighboring teeth, which affects where the crown can sit.

When grafting comes in

If there is not enough bone, a bone graft can rebuild the area first. Sometimes the graft is placed at the same time as an extraction, to preserve bone for a later implant; sometimes it is a separate step with its own healing time. Grafting adds months to the timeline, and you will know whether you need it — and the cost — before you begin.

Who is a good candidate

Healthy gums, enough bone (or the ability to graft it), and general health that supports healing. Smoking and poorly controlled diabetes both slow bone healing, and are discussed openly at the consultation. Age alone is rarely the barrier.

The same way you care for a natural tooth — brushing, flossing, and routine exams and cleanings. Implants can’t get a cavity, but the gum and bone around them still need to stay healthy, so keeping up with your regular visits matters just as much as it does for your natural teeth.
