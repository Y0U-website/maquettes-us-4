1. Full-Mouth Dental Implants Are Stable and Work Like Real Teeth

Full-mouth dental implants are anchored directly in your jawbone. That means they don’t slide around or suddenly pop loose the way dentures can sometimes do. Once everything is completely healed, they feel completely solid when you bite down.

They work exactly like real teeth. And they’re not removable. So you don’t have to take them out and soak them in a little bowl in your bathroom.

2. You Can Go Back to Eating All Your Favorite Foods

If you’re missing several teeth, you’ve probably had to get used to a whole new way of eating. Once you get full mouth dental implants, you can go back to eating all of the stuff you used to love. With implant-supported dentures, you’ve got real bite strength.

3. Full-Mouth Restoration Looks Completely Natural

Dr. Han precisely matches the shape, shade, and size of your new teeth so that they look natural with your face. You don’t have to worry about fake-looking dentures. They’re built as a connected arch. That means there aren’t any obvious gaps.

4. What Full Mouth Dental Implants Can Fix

Full-mouth restoration is a good fix for people living with these issues:

  • Missing multiple teeth
  • Teeth that are failing
  • Damaged teeth
  • Loose and uncomfortable dentures
  • Bone loss

5. Dental Implants Can Last a Lifetime with Good Care

Dental implants are a long-term investment in your quality of life and health. They’re made with materials that don’t decay. Titanium and porcelain. That means they last decades if you take good care of your teeth. That means keeping up with your appointments every six months with Dr. Han. And of course, continuing to practice good oral hygiene at home.

6. What the Treatment for Full Arch Dental Implants Near Me Looks Like

Dr. Han uses the TeethXpress ® protocol. This is the final set of replacement teeth that are attached to your implants. Here’s what the process looks like:

  • Consultation and 3D CT scan to map out your mouth.
  • Review a personalized treatment plan.
  • Failing teeth removed.
  • Bone grafting done if needed.
  • Implant placement with local anesthesia or IV sedation.
  • Temporary teeth attached.
  • Implants heal and fuse with the jawbone.
  • Return to have your permanent replacement teeth placed.
