What are my dental implant options?

Dental implants are one of the most versatile teeth replacements. A dental implant can support a dental crown, bridge, or denture, making it possible to replace any number of missing teeth.

Here are the solutions available with dental implants.

Dr. Han is a renowned prosthodontist in the Schaumburg area. Whether you need an implant crown or an implant-supported denture, your restorations are created with premium materials and ultimate expertise. The results are so natural that people won’t realize you have replacement teeth!

  • Single-unit dental implants: The dentist surgically inserts a titanium post into the same bone that once supported your natural tooth. Then, the dentist caps off your implant with a customized, natural-looking crown. The result is a tooth that blends beautifully with your smile.
  • Implant bridges : Implant-supported bridges are excellent for replacing a few missing teeth in a row. Unlike traditional dentures anchored by natural adjacent teeth, implant bridges are secured directly in the jawbone. This provides ultimate stability without the need to modify otherwise healthy natural teeth.
  • Full mouth dental implants: Have you lost several teeth in one arch or both? Traditional dentures aren’t your only option. The dentist can use dental implants to secure a full-mouth denture. Patients can opt for removable implant-retained dentures (overdentures) secured in the mouth with at least two implants. However, if you want a more secure option, you can opt for fixed implant-supported dentures anchored by at least four implants.

Can I straighten my teeth while having dental implants?

Yes, but it’s not that straightforward. Dental implants differ from natural teeth because they are permanently fused to your jawbone. That means they don’t move like natural teeth do during orthodontic treatment. So, while you can straighten the surrounding natural teeth, the implant stays fixed.

Here’s how orthodontics works with dental implants.

It’s possible to straighten your teeth with dental implants. However, the implant itself is fixed, and treatment needs to be carefully planned. Consult with your dentist and orthodontist to ensure your plan is safe and effective.

  • Before getting an implant: If you know you’ll need orthodontic work, it’s often best to complete that first, then place the implant afterward—this way, everything lines up beautifully.
  • After getting an implant, your orthodontist can still straighten your remaining teeth around it. The doctor works around the implant, adjusting the bite and alignment as needed without trying to move the implant itself.
