What Exactly are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a very small titanium post that Dr. Han places in your jawbone. Basically, it’s a complete replacement for the root that you lost with your tooth. Once it fuses and becomes a part of the bone, Dr. Joon Han attaches a small abutment and places a custom porcelain crown on top.

What are The Benefits of Dental Implants?

There are lots of reasons why getting a dental implant to replace a tooth is a good idea:

  • Chew your food just as you did with your real tooth.
  • Keep your other teeth from moving and becoming crooked.
  • Make sure your speech doesn’t change.
  • Preserve the health of your jawbone.
  • Maintain the natural shape of your face.
  • No moving around like you get with dentures.
  • Lasts a lifetime with proper care.

What to Expect at Your Consultation?

At your first visit, Dr. Han wants to get a very solid overview of what’s going on in your mouth. He’ll give you a thorough examination and then take X-rays or 3D scans. After that, he’ll go over your options with you.

How Dental Implants for Multiple Teeth Work?

Replacing multiple teeth doesn’t always mean you have to get an implant for every missing tooth. A few implants can support a dental bridge or even a full set of dentures:

  • A bridge with two implants to support a prosthetic tooth
  • Multiple implants supporting a larger bridge
  • 4-6 implants for full arch replacement

How does Bone Health Determine if Implants Can Work for You?

Not everyone can jump right into dental implants. Dr. Han needs to assess your jawbone to ensure there’s enough bone to support the titanium post. If you’ve lost bone because your tooth has been gone for too long, he may be able to use a bone graft to rebuild that area.

Other issues can affect your ability to get an implant. Gum disease, smoking, and some health conditions, for example.

What is the Process for Getting Dental Implants in Schaumburg, IL?

On the day of your implant surgery, Dr. Han numbs the area using a local anesthesia. He then makes a small opening in your gum and carefully places the implant post into the bone. Everything gets closed up, and your healing can start.

After the implant is placed, the bone will fuse around it over a few months. This is the osseointegration process. Once the implant is solid, he attaches a small abutment and a permanent crown or bridge.
