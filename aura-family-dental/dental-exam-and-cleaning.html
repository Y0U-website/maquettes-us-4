General Teeth Cleaning vs. Dental Deep Cleaning

Keep in mind that general dental cleaning is different from deep cleaning services. If the dentist discovers pockets of gum disease during your checkup, they might recommend a follow-up deep cleaning appointment.

This deeper cleaning focuses on heavy tartar removal, especially around the gum line. When the gums are starting to recede, then it’s necessary to remove the tartar and plaque under the gum line. The faster you have a deep cleaning treatment, the higher likelihood of preventing serious gum infections and complications down the road.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a dental cleaning painful?

No, our dental hygienists gently remove the buildup on your teeth without hurting you. You might feel a bit of sensitivity during and after the appointment, but you shouldn’t experience any pain. On the other hand, a dental deep cleaning can be painful since the area of the tooth below the gums is being cleaned.

How often should I schedule a dental cleaning appointment?

The general recommendation is to visit the dentist for a dental cleaning every six months. But patients with a higher risk of complications or other health concerns might need more frequent appointments. For example, patients with diabetes or receiving cancer treatments have a high risk of tooth and gum complications .

How long will a dental cleaning appointment be?

Plan on spending about an hour during your dental cleaning and examination. But if you have other services, such as x-rays and fluoride treatments, then it might take a little longer. Also, it’s a good idea to show up a few minutes early to complete paperwork before your appointment.

How long do I need to wait to eat after my dental cleaning?

We recommend that you avoid food and drink for at least 30 minutes after your appointment. If topical fluoride treatment is used, then you need to allow time for the fluoride to bond to the tooth enamel.
