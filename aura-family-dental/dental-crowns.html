Personalized Treatments for Your Dental Crowns

Each patient is unique, which is why a personalized treatment plan is necessary. Modern dentistry provides many customized options for the crown design as well as the materials that are used. For example, crowns can be made with porcelain or ceramic, as well as zirconia or porcelain fused to metal.

The material selection depends on the location of the tooth and the durability that is needed. For example, it’s essential to use a natural color for a front tooth crown since it’s so visible. But if the crown is in the back of the mouth, then it might be more important to choose a stronger material that can withstand the pressure when biting down.

Depending on the treatment plan, you might need to use a temporary dental crown while the main crown is being made. Your tooth is prepared during the initial appointment, and this temporary tooth cap is placed to cover the tooth. Then, you will return for a second appointment have the permanent crown secured in your mouth.

Signs You May Need a Dental Crown

Frequently Asked Questions

What is recovery like after a dental crown?

The day after the treatment, you will feel tooth sensitivity and a little bit of tenderness. Avoid eating foods that are hard, crunchy, or sticky. Over-the-counter medication can be used to manage the symptoms if needed. These symptoms usually go away within a few days.

How long do dental crowns last?

A crown can last as long as 15 years or more, depending on the type of material. Keep in mind that crowns can break, so some patients need to use night guards if they grind their teeth while sleeping. Also, good oral health habits help to reduce the risk of more damage to the tooth and gums.

Will my insurance pay for a dental crown?

If the crown is a restorative treatment, then most dental insurance plans will pay for a portion of the crown. If it is a cosmetic treatment, then no insurance coverage is available. Talk to our office staff to estimate your out-of-pocket expenses for a dental crown.
