If your child&#x27;s permanent tooth is knocked out, you must hold it by the smooth crown (not the root), gently rinse it with water, try to slip it back into the socket, or place it in a container of cold milk and call us instantly. Time is the absolute single most critical factor here; permanent teeth re-implanted by a pediatric dentist within 30 to 60 minutes have the highest success rate of survival.

You should call a specialized pediatric dentist first for any isolated dental injury, as standard hospitals and ERs rarely have a dentist on staff or the specialized tools required to save teeth. However, if your child is experiencing severe facial swelling that compromises their breathing, uncontrollable bleeding, a jaw fracture, or signs of a concussion, you should head straight to the nearest emergency room or call 911 immediately

To manage a sudden toothache at home, have your child gently rinse with warm salt water, use dental floss to check for trapped food, and apply a cold compress to the outside of their cheek to minimize swelling. You can administer an age-appropriate dose of over-the-counter pediatric pain relievers (like ibuprofen or acetaminophen), but never place an aspirin tablet directly against the aching gum tissue, as this can cause a severe chemical burn.

If a baby tooth is knocked out prematurely, you should leave the tooth out of the mouth and call our office so we can examine the area for root fragments and assess the need for a space maintainer. Unlike permanent teeth, we never re-implant a knocked-out baby tooth because doing so can severely damage the delicate permanent adult tooth germ developing directly underneath the gumline.
