An initial check is often recommended around age seven, when adult molars and front teeth appear. This timing helps identify crowding or jaw concerns early, and orthodontic treatment in El Paso, Texas, can be planned to match your child&#x27;s growth.

Length varies by the complexity of the bite and how well appliances are worn. Limited phase treatments can take several months, while comprehensive braces or aligners may take one to two years. Consistent home care and keeping appointments help stay on schedule.

Both can be effective. Braces are fixed and work well for many bite issues. Aligners require responsibility because they must be worn as directed. Your child&#x27;s habits, growth stage, and needs guide which option is recommended.

Many children describe a feeling of pressure after adjustments that eases in a day or two. Over-the-counter pain relief and soft foods can help during this short adjustment period.

Brush around brackets and along the gumline after meals, and use floss threaders or interdental brushes to clean between teeth. Choose softer foods right after adjustments and avoid very sticky or hard snacks that can damage braces.
