What are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is not a prosthetic tooth, but more specifically a prosthetic tooth root . A tiny titanium post is placed into the jaw where a tooth used to reside, and there, it bonds with the bone through a process called osseointegration. Then, it is topped with a crown, bridge, or denture. Because the new tooth is based below the gum line just like natural ones, to the casual observer, all they see is a complete and healthy smile.

The 4 Step Dental Implant Process

Because they have to be rooted to the jawbone, the dental implant process involves a couple of different steps or four steps, to be precise. Thanks to Dr. Assadourian’s expertise, we’re able to complete the entire process in-house. That means you won’t be needing a referral to receive dental implants near you! You can learn more about the four steps below, and as always, please let us know if you have any questions.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

A consultation might seem simple, but it’s essential for establishing your dental implant treatment plan. Dr. Assadourian and the rest of our team will perform a thorough oral exam, complete with X-rays, and review your health history. This will help us determine whether preliminary treatments (such as gum disease therapy or bone grafting) would help promote the success of your dental implants.

Dental Implant Surgery

During dental implant surgery, Dr. Assadourian will make small incisions in your gumline so she can access your jawbone. She’ll carefully place your titanium implant posts along the bone, then finish the procedure by stitching your gums. Rest assured, you’ll be supplied with a local anesthetic for this part of the process!

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

After that comes the recovery period. While your mouth is healing, your jawbone and titanium implant posts will be busy with osseointegration. Basically, since titanium is biocompatible, your jawbone will fuse with the material until it becomes part of the bone. This can take anywhere from three to six months, and it’s important not to disturb your implants in the meantime.

Once osseointegration is complete, we’ll be able to attach connector pieces (a.k.a. abutments ) to your implant posts.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

Customizing a dental implant crown, bridge, or denture requires precise skill, so we leave this step to the highly qualified technicians at the dental lab. When your restoration is crafted to perfection, they’ll send it to our office, and we’ll use the abutments to connect the restoration to your implants. After making any necessary adjustments and confirming you’re satisfied with your new smile, you’re free to go and enjoy your dental implants!

Who Can Dental Implants Help?

Anyone with missing teeth can benefit from dental implants, whether their situation is due to neglect, an accident, or health issues. Dr. Smith can tailor the procedure based on each person’s needs so we can permanently close any gaps.

Missing One Tooth

An implant can be positioned between two healthy teeth and topped with a crown without disturbing them. The crown itself will be shaped and colored to match those around it, and the nearby teeth won’t need to be changed at all.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Rather than using your remaining teeth to support a bridge or partial denture, we can secure these prosthetics using just two dental implants. This not only makes them stronger, but it also allows us to preserve the rest of the teeth. Plus, this helps the prosthetics place less pressure on the sensitive gums.

Missing All Teeth

Six to eight implants will be positioned along the jaw, and a denture will simply snap onto them. Implant dentures are able to maintain their day-one fit for several years because the underlying implants actually keep the bone strong and healthy, so you won’t have to worry about your teeth starting to slip as time goes by.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants excel as tooth replacements, but they don t work all on their own. You need to look after these special posts to keep them stable and functional. Fortunately, we have you covered; Dental Health & Wellness Boston knows all about dental implant care in Boston. Our team will gladly share some great ways to protect your new prosthetics from harm. With all that said, simply keep reading or call us to hear more.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

The first thing to do is to make oral hygiene a big (or bigger) priority. That way, your replacement teeth will be less likely to fail or stop working.

Even though they’re artificial, dental implants need a healthy mouth to function. Cavities in nearby teeth, gum disease, and similar issues can destroy the oral tissues that support them. Should the latter occur, your implants will face an increased risk of failure. Unless you want that to happen, you’d do well to brush twice daily and floss once daily.

Eat a Healthy Diet

To ensure your implants work properly, follow a healthy diet from now on. Nutritious meals will help your treatment succeed in the long run.

The truth is that dental implants don’t handle junk food well. They’ll help you chew sugary snacks, but the latter will often wedge between your posts and your teeth. This wedging can cause tooth decay that upsets implants, resulting in treatment failure. You should thus avoid risky treats like chewy candy, caramel, milk chocolate, and so on. Instead, try meals rich in calcium and phosphorus; they’ll strengthen your jaw and steady your gums!

Break Bad Habits

Soon after you get dental implants, please quit any bad oral habits you have. Sticking with them will only put your restored smile in danger.

Several mouth-related habits could harm your posts. For instance, smoking tobacco can disrupt your recovery from placement surgery; that’d keep your implants from fusing. Another example is biting hard objects, as doing so can make your new teeth chip or crack. Such dental damage quickly leads to implant failure when left unchecked.

Protect Your Dental Implants

You’ll need to protect your dental implants, too; they can break from intense force. Unless they’re well-defended, an accident or injury could cause a treatment failure.

In particular, look into getting a custom mouthguard. A good one tailored for athletes (i.e., a sportsguard) would protect your teeth from collisions, tackles, and falls during games. Another for sleeping (i.e., a nightguard) can also help, as it’d stop you from chipping or cracking your posts while asleep.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Make sure to attend regular dental checkups as well. Through those visits, your dental provider would improve your implants’ lifespan.

A dental checkup is the best kind of preventive oral care. At each one, your dentist can diagnose and treat issues that’d potentially harm your implant posts. You’d just need to attend them every six months (at least) to maintain your treatment results.

Dental Implant FAQs

Will I have to take off work for dental implant surgery?

Dental implant surgery does require that you take time off work. How much you will need to miss is ultimately up to you. Everyone heals at different rates, and the number of implant posts that you need, as well as the type of work you do, can determine how soon you can return to life as normal.

Most patients who need one or two dental implants can typically return after a few days of healing. Those who need implant dentures may require up to a week of recovery before resuming work. It’s recommended that you plan your surgery toward the end of the week so that you have the weekend to recover. Any physical or strenuous activity should be avoided immediately following surgery, as this can delay the process and put your new smile at risk.

Does getting dental implants hurt?

Receiving dental implants is not a painful process. Dr. Assadourian ensures each patient’s comfort and safety from start to finish. Before the procedure begins, he administers local anesthesia to numb your mouth so that you do not feel any discomfort throughout the placement process.

Naturally, as the anesthesia wears off, there will likely be some soreness that follows. However, you can take an over-the-counter pain reliever to alleviate the problem and achieve relief. As the days pass, any discomfort you experience should begin to dissipate.

Will people be able to tell that I have dental implants?

If you have been without your natural teeth and now have dental implants to replace them, others may notice, depending on where the missing teeth were located inside your mouth. However, these new teeth look and feel just like regular ones. This is one of their many benefits they act like natural teeth so that you can easily eat, speak, and smile.

Also, the way they are made ensures they blend in with the rest of your smile, so all you and others see is a seamless appearance.

How successful are dental implants?

Dental implants have a success rate of 95 % upon initial placement. As long as they are properly maintained, they can continue to remain just as successful after 10 years. This is dependent on how well you choose to take care of your new smile, though.

Daily brushing and flossing, regular dental exams and cleanings, proper mouth protection, healthy eating, and giving up unhealthy lifestyle habits are all ways to ensure the longevity of your new teeth.

What can cause dental implants to fail?

  • Failed osseointegration (the implant never fused with the bone)
  • Improper placement
  • Facial trauma
  • Autoimmune disorders
