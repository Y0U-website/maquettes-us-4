Dental Implants

With proper care and regular dental checkups, implants can provide a durable long-term solution for missing teeth.

  • Improved chewing and speaking ability
  • Support for jawbone health
  • No need to alter neighboring healthy teeth
  • Simple care with regular brushing, flossing, and dental visits

The Dental Implant Process

  • Consultation & Planning Your treatment begins with an evaluation of your teeth, gums, dental history, and jawbone. X-rays or 3D imaging may be used to determine implant placement and whether additional procedures, such as bone grafting, may be needed.
  • Implant Placement The titanium implant is carefully placed into the jawbone. Local anesthesia is typically used, and sedation options may be available depending on your needs.
  • Healing & Integration Over the following months, the implant integrates with the surrounding bone to create a stable foundation for the replacement tooth. Healing time varies from patient to patient.
  • Final Restoration Once healing is complete, an abutment is attached to the implant and a custom crown, bridge, or other restoration is placed. The restoration is designed to complement the shape and color of your surrounding teeth.

Who Can Benefit From Dental Implants?

Dental implants may be an option if you are missing one tooth, several teeth, or a full arch of teeth. They can help restore areas affected by tooth loss while improving chewing ability and supporting a more complete smile.

Good candidates generally need healthy gums and enough jawbone to support an implant. If bone loss has occurred, additional treatment may sometimes be recommended before implant placement.

A dental evaluation is the best way to determine whether implants are appropriate for you .

Why Consider Dental Implants?

Unlike removable dentures, dental implants are anchored securely in the jawbone and do not rely on adhesives to stay in place. They also differ from traditional bridges because neighboring healthy teeth generally do not need to be reshaped to support the replacement tooth.

For many patients, this provides a comfortable and convenient way to replace missing teeth while preserving surrounding tooth structure.
