After the implant heals, our dentists evaluate your bite, gums, and nearby teeth to plan a comfortable final restoration. Dental implant restoration may help:

  • An abutment connecting the implant and restoration
  • A custom crown , bridge , or denture above the gumline
  • Replace one or more missing teeth
  • Improve biting and chewing
  • Fill visible spaces in your smile
  • Support a balanced bite
  • Restore a worn implant crown

Creating Your Implant Restoration

Your treatment begins with an examination of the implant and surrounding tissues. Precise impressions or a digital scan may be taken to plan a restoration based on the size, shape, shade, and position of your surrounding teeth.

Once the custom restoration is ready, your dentist attaches it to the implant and carefully checks its appearance, fit, and bite. Minor adjustments may be made to support comfort and balanced chewing.

Implant restorations require consistent care to remain healthy and functional. Brush thoroughly, clean around and between the restoration as directed, and attend regular dental examinations. Our team will monitor the restoration, implant, gums, and bite for signs of wear or inflammation.

If implant placement is still needed, we can evaluate your oral health and discuss the appropriate next steps, including coordination with another dental provider when necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about dental implant restoration at Palo Alto Central Dental.

  • What is a dental implant restoration? A dental implant restoration is the replacement tooth or teeth secured to one or more dental implants. It may take the form of a crown, bridge, or denture and is designed to restore appearance and chewing function.
  • When can the final implant restoration be placed? The final restoration is generally placed after the implant has healed and integrated with the jawbone. Healing time varies, so your dentist and implant provider will determine when the implant is ready to support the restoration.
  • Does dental implant restoration require surgery? Attaching the final crown, bridge, or denture usually does not involve the same surgical procedure used to place the implant. Your dentist will explain the process based on the implant system and your individual treatment needs.
  • How long can an implant restoration last? Its lifespan depends on the restoration type, bite forces, oral hygiene, health habits, and routine dental care. Although the implant may remain stable, the attached restoration can eventually wear or require repair or replacement.
  • How should I care for my implant restoration? Brush the restoration and surrounding gumline carefully, clean between the teeth or beneath the restoration as instructed, and maintain regular dental visits. Proper care helps protect the gums and supporting implant.
