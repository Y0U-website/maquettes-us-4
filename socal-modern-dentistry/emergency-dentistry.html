Common Dental Emergencies We Treat

Knowing what qualifies as a dental emergency can help you determine when to seek immediate care. At SoCal Modern Dentistry, we offer emergency dentistry for a wide range of urgent situations. Here are some of the most common issues we treat:

1. Severe Toothaches and Pain

A severe toothache can signal various underlying issues, from cavities to infections or abscesses. If you’re experiencing sharp, throbbing pain that doesn’t go away, it’s time to visit us for emergency care. Our team will examine your tooth, identify the cause of the pain, and provide appropriate treatment, such as fillings, root canals, or extractions if necessary.

2. Broken, Chipped, or Cracked Teeth

Accidents happen, and teeth can crack or chip unexpectedly. Broken or cracked teeth can expose the inner layers of the tooth to bacteria, increasing the risk of infection. Our emergency dentistry in Torrance is equipped to repair damaged teeth, restore their structure, and prevent further complications. Whether it’s a small chip or a severe fracture, we’ll work to restore your tooth’s appearance and function.

3. Knocked-Out Teeth

If a tooth gets knocked out due to an accident, immediate action is essential. A knocked-out tooth has a high chance of being saved if you act quickly. Keep the tooth moist, avoid touching the root, and bring it with you to our office as soon as possible. We’ll assess the damage and may be able to reattach the tooth, saving it for the long term.

4. Lost or Damaged Fillings and Crowns

A lost filling or damaged crown leaves your tooth exposed and vulnerable. Without protection, the tooth is susceptible to decay and further damage. If you lose a filling or crown, contact our office right away. We’ll replace or repair the restoration, protecting your tooth and restoring your bite.

5. Abscesses and Infections

A dental abscess is a serious infection that can cause swelling, pain, and sensitivity. Left untreated, an abscess can spread to other areas of the body, posing a significant health risk. Signs of an abscess include a painful, swollen bump on the gums, a bad taste in the mouth, and fever. Our emergency team will drain the abscess, treat the infection, and may recommend further treatments like a root canal to ensure complete recovery.

6. Soft Tissue Injuries

Injuries to the lips, cheeks, or gums require gentle care and treatment to minimize pain and prevent infection. Our team provides emergency dental care for soft tissue injuries, cleaning wounds and helping you manage pain while the area heals.

What to Do in a Dental Emergency

Knowing what to do during a dental emergency can make a significant difference in the outcome. Here are some tips to help you manage different types of dental emergencies before you arrive at our office:

Remember, these are only temporary measures to alleviate pain and protect your teeth until you receive professional care.

  • For Toothaches : Rinse your mouth with warm water, floss gently to remove any debris, and apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek to reduce swelling.
  • For Knocked-Out Teeth : Handle the tooth by the crown (the top part) without touching the root. Rinse it gently if dirty, place it in a container of milk or saliva, and bring it to our office immediately.
  • For Broken Teeth : Rinse your mouth with warm water, and save any pieces of the broken tooth. Apply a cold compress to the area to manage swelling.
  • For Lost Fillings or Crowns : If possible, keep the crown or filling safe and bring it to your appointment. Over-the-counter dental cement can temporarily protect the area if you cannot get to our office right away.

Frequently Asked Questions about Emergency Dentistry

How do I know if my situation is a dental emergency? Any situation involving severe pain, bleeding, or broken teeth usually qualifies as an emergency. If you’re unsure, call our office, and our team can help you determine whether you need immediate care.

What should I bring to an emergency dental appointment? Bring any pieces of broken or knocked-out teeth, previous dental records if possible, and any medication details. If you’re experiencing swelling or pain, consider using a cold compress on the way to our office.

Will my insurance cover emergency dental care? Many dental insurance plans cover emergency visits, but it depends on your provider and specific coverage. Our team will help you understand your options and file claims to make the process as seamless as possible.
