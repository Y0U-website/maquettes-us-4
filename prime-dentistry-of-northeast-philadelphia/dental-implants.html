Dental Implants Vs Dentures & Bridges: Quick Comparison of Your Options

Many patients in Northeast Philadelphia come to us unsure if the investment in implants is worth it compared to traditional methods. Here is how they compare in terms of longevity, health, and lifestyle.

Implants vs. Traditional Dentures

  • Bone Health: Dentures sit on top of the gums and do not stimulate the jawbone, leading to facial collapse (a shrunken jaw) over time. Implants preserve natural bone density.
  • Stability: Dentures can slip when eating tough foods or speaking. Implants are anchored in bone and remain 100 % stable, allowing you to eat steak, apples, and corn on the cob without worry.
  • Maintenance: Dentures require messy adhesives and must be removed for cleaning. Implants are brushed and flossed just like natural teeth.

Implants vs. Dental Bridges

  • Tooth Structure: A traditional bridge requires grinding down healthy adjacent teeth to act as anchors. Dental implants are self-supporting and leave your healthy neighboring teeth untouched.
  • Lifespan: The average bridge lasts 10–12 years before needing replacement. A dental implant post is designed to be a permanent, lifetime solution.

Am I a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

If you are missing one or more teeth, there is a very good chance you are a great candidate for dental implants. For an implant to last a lifetime, though, it needs a strong and healthy foundation.

When you come in for your consultation, Dr. Pathik Shah will use advanced 3D imaging to get a complete picture of your mouth. This high-tech scan lets us see right beneath your gums, making sure your treatment is safe, precise, and completely customized for you.

One of the most important things we look at is your jawbone. A dental implant acts just like a natural tooth root, which means it needs enough strong bone to hold it securely in place. If you have been missing teeth for a while and have lost some bone density, do not worry, you aren’t out of options. We can often perform a simple bone grafting procedure to rebuild that area and create a solid base for your new smile.

Along with healthy bone, you also need healthy gums. Your gums act as a protective seal around the implant, keeping bacteria out while the area heals.

If you have active gum disease, we will simply need to treat that first. We will also chat about your overall medical history. Certain conditions that affect your immune system or slow down healing like uncontrolled diabetes or autoimmune disorders. It is important for us to know about so we can plan the safest approach for your dental implants surgery.

We will also discuss your everyday habits like smoking to determine your candidacy for implants. For your implant to be successful, it has to physically fuse with your jawbone. While being a smoker doesn’t mean you can’t get implants, smoking does make it harder for your mouth to heal.

How to Maintain your Dental Implants?

One of the biggest advantages of choosing dental implants is how easily they fit into your normal daily routine. Because they look and function just like your natural teeth, caring for them requires very little special effort.

To keep your new smile healthy, simply brush twice a day using a soft-bristled toothbrush and a gentle, low-abrasive toothpaste. Flossing every day is also highly important, as it safely removes hidden plaque from around the base of your new implant crown.

Alongside your easy at-home routine, it is highly recommended to schedule your dental visits periodically.

If you tend to clench or grind your teeth while you sleep, just let us know. Dr. Shah can easily create a comfortable, custom-fit night guard to protect your beautiful new crowns, ensuring your investment stays safe and secure for years to come.
