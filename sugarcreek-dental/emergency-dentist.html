What Qualifies as a Dental Emergency?

Some emergencies, such as uncontrolled bleeding, facial bone trauma or swelling that affects a patient’s airway, can be life-threatening. Contact us immediately for the best treatment available for your dental emergencies.

  • Tooth infections or dental abscesses
  • Severe pain
  • Knocked-out tooth
  • Cracked tooth
  • Soft tissue injury
  • Broken dental restoration

What to Do in Different Types of Dental Emergencies

Dental emergencies require immediate assistance, but patients may be able to alleviate their pain with home remedies until they can get to the dentist. If you have a severe toothache, use over-the-counter pain relievers such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen after calling your dentist.

Some dental issues are more urgent, because they can have a lasting effect on your oral health or even your overall health. For instance, if you had a tooth knocked out, make sure to visit the dentist within the hour for a higher chance of saving the tooth. If you have a dental abscess, rinse your mouth with warm water or a salt-water solution and ensure you visit the dentist immediately. The infection can spread to the surrounding teeth if not treated right away. Patients with broken restorations should keep the crown or bridge because the dentist may be able to save it.

Ways to Prevent Dental Injuries

Preventing dental injuries starts with good oral health, so your teeth are healthy and strong. Avoid consuming sugary food and drinks, and regularly brush and floss your teeth. If you play a sport, consider wearing a protective mouth guard while playing. Also, if you notice pain or discomfort in your teeth or gums, see a dentist immediately so the problem doesn’t become serious. It’s also important to regularly see an experienced dentist for checkups and professional cleaning. During your visit, a professional can check for any dental problems and offer a treatment that works.
