Signs Your Dental Implant Restoration May Need Attention

You should schedule an evaluation if you notice:

Prompt treatment can help preserve the longevity of your dental implant and support surrounding oral structures.

  • A loose implant crown
  • Difficulty chewing comfortably
  • Visible damage to the restoration
  • Changes in your bite
  • Increased sensitivity around the implant area
  • Food becoming trapped around the restoration
  • Cosmetic concerns affecting your smile

The Dental Implant Restoration Process

Every implant restoration case is unique, which is why we begin with a comprehensive examination of the implant, restoration, and surrounding tissues.

Using advanced technology such as Digital X-Rays and 3D CT Scanning , we can carefully evaluate implant-supported restorations and develop personalized treatment recommendations.

  • Evaluating the condition of the implant and restoration.
  • Taking digital images or scans to assess fit and function.
  • Removing damaged or worn restorative components when necessary.
  • Designing a custom restoration that matches your natural teeth.
  • Securing the new crown, bridge, or denture to the implant.
  • Adjusting the restoration for comfort, appearance, and proper bite alignment.

Benefits of Dental Implant Restoration

Restoring a damaged or worn implant restoration offers several advantages:

Improved Appearance

A custom restoration is designed to blend naturally with your surrounding teeth, helping maintain a balanced and attractive smile.

Restored Function

A properly fitted restoration can help improve chewing, speaking, and everyday comfort.

Long-Term Protection

Replacing damaged restorative components may help protect the implant and surrounding oral structures from unnecessary stress.

Customized Results

Each restoration is crafted to complement the shape, size, and color of your natural teeth for a seamless appearance.

Confidence in Your Smile

Patients often appreciate the renewed comfort and aesthetics that come with a properly functioning implant restoration.

Caring for Your Restored Dental Implant

Good oral hygiene and routine dental care play an important role in maintaining implant-supported restorations.

Regular visits for Dental Cleanings help us monitor the health of both your implant and surrounding tissues.

  • Brush twice daily using a soft-bristled toothbrush.
  • Floss carefully around implant-supported restorations.
  • Attend regular dental examinations and cleanings.
  • Avoid chewing on hard objects such as ice.
  • Wear a recommended Occlusal & Sports Guard if you grind your teeth or participate in sports.
  • Seek prompt evaluation if you notice looseness, discomfort, or visible damage.
