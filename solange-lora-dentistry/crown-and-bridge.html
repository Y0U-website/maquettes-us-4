Crown And Bridge

When one of the dentists at Solange Lora Dentistry recommends a dental crown, the first step is to come in for X-rays and impressions. Your dentist uses X-rays to determine the precise location of the tooth needing a crown to aid in the placement and planning of your dental crown. You will need to sit with dental putty in your mouth for a few minutes to allow your dentist to obtain an impression of the tooth.

During the appointment to receive your crown, your dentist will pare down your tooth with a special dental instrument. This process is to ensure that the crown will fit over your tooth without protruding further than the rest of your teeth. You receive a temporary crown until our laboratory creates a permanent one. The typical wait for a permanent crown is two weeks, and our scheduling staff will contact you to schedule another appointment once the permanent crown is available.

What is a Dental Bridge?

The sole purpose of a dental bridge is to replace a missing tooth. The bridge contains wires that adhere to the back of the tooth to the left and right of the missing tooth to secure it in place. Our dentists may also choose to adhere a bridge to a dental implant. Your new bridge will contain a replacement tooth but doesn’t replace the roots of the missing tooth as dental implants do. The two main components of a dental bridge are abutments, which are the natural or implant teeth that support the bridge, and the pontic, which is the official name for the replacement tooth.

You may receive either a cantilever, conventional, Maryland or implant-supported dental bridge based on your oral health needs and the recommendation of your dentist. We will provide you with information about each of the different bridge types during your initial consultation.
