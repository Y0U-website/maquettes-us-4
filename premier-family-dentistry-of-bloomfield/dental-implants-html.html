What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small posts that can be crafted from a few different types of biocompatible materials, which means they can fuse with the jawbone, including titanium, ceramic, and zirconia. The implants are then surgically placed within the bone to restore the foundational root structure of the missing teeth, allowing them to act as a strong support system for any type of restoration to be attached to, including a free-standing crown, bridge, or denture.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

The process of getting dental implants can often be categorized into four distinct steps, with each one contributing to the lasting, future success of the treatment. Remember, this process typically spans several months and requires a firm commitment to taking care of your smile. That said, everyone’s journey looks a little different, but we’ll be sure you know exactly what to expect. Here’s a closer look at how the process normally plays out.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The first step on your journey to receiving dental implants is coming by our office for a consultation with Dr. Abboushi. During this visit, he’ll carefully examine your mouth and take diagnostic images of your orofacial structures, to determine if your gum and bone tissue is healthy enough to support dental implant posts. Some patients may require a preparatory procedure before they can receive implants, such as a bone graft or periodontal therapy. In any case, we’ll be sure you’re an ideal candidate before moving forward with scheduling your procedure.

Dental Implant Surgery

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Following the actual surgery comes perhaps the most important phase of the whole process: osseointegration. This refers to the natural fusion between the biocompatible implant posts and the jawbone tissue. This is how dental implants can replace the root of a lost tooth; the posts will become fully integrated and sturdier as they heal over the course of several months. After this has happened, the abutment pieces are attached on top of the posts via a small, second procedure. These connector pieces will eventually house your custom-made restoration(s).

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

The final step of your dental implant journey is receiving your custom-made restoration(s)! These are designed specifically for your smile and can replace any number of missing teeth no matter where they were located. A crown can be used, as can a bridge or even a personalized denture. We’ll be sure they’re polished and adjusted as needed before sending you off into the world to enjoy your newly rebuilt smile!

Benefits of Dental Implants

Unlike alternatives like dental bridges and dentures, implants include a titanium rod that’s embedded in your jaw, which provides many unique advantages. As a result, they continue to gain popularity as a beautiful, functional, and reliable way to replace missing teeth near Bloomfield.

Continue reading to learn more about the different benefits of getting dental implants, and feel free to contact us for additional information or to schedule your consultation!

Day-to-Day Benefits

Tooth loss can detract from your daily quality of life because once-basic tasks like chewing, speaking, and smiling confidently suddenly become more challenging. Dental implants can help by:

  • Improving your bite force. They return an estimated 80 % or more of your original bite force so you can enjoy your favorite foods.
  • Enhancing enunciation. Gaps in your grin can lead to a slur, lisp, or other changes in your speech patterns that can be overcome once your restorations are placed.
  • Boosting confidence. They’re customized to match the shape, size, and tone of your natural teeth for seamless results.
  • Easy cleaning. Brushing and flossing twice daily, along with routine checkups and cleanings every six months with our team near Bloomfield, are easy and effective ways to keep them in great shape.

Health Benefits

There are additional advantages to having dental implants that might be less outwardly obvious. They can also improve your oral health by:

  • Preserving your jawbone. Many patients don’t realize their jawbone starts thinning without roots to stimulate new bone growth. The titanium rod of your implant fulfills this function to maintain a strong foundation for your teeth.
  • Prevent drifting. Neighboring teeth are likely to shift out of alignment to fill in spaces in your smile, which can wear down your enamel and increase their vulnerability to damage and decay. Your implants ensure that everything stays in its rightful position.
  • Improving dental condition. These restorations are easier to clean than dentures or dental bridges, so you’re less likely to develop gum disease, tooth decay, or other concerns.

Long-Term Benefits

These prosthetics are also known to last longer than the alternatives, so you can enjoy the following long-term advantages:

  • High success rate. They have a success rate of 90 % or more, meaning you’re not likely to develop complications or experience failure.
  • Resilience . Your jawbone fuses with the support rod, making it a permanent part of your smile that can last potentially the remainder of your natural life.
  • Save on expenses. They may cost more initially than other replacement teeth, but they usually require fewer repairs, replacements, adhesives, or accessories, meaning you save money over time.

Understanding the Cost of Dental Implants

Preliminary Treatments & Dental Implant Surgery

You may need preliminary treatments like gum disease therapy, tooth extractions, or bone grafting before you can receive dental implants, and the costs of these procedures will be noted on your final bill. Many of these treatments typically receive at least partial coverage from dental insurance policies.

The cost of your implant placement surgery depends largely on where the devices are being placed in the jaw and the anesthesia used. The Parts of Your Dental Implant

The cost of your dental implants will be determined by several factors such as:

  • The number of implants being placed: Each individual implant will be noted on your bill.
  • The type of restoration used: You may receive a crown, denture, or bridge, and larger appliances cost more.
  • Size and material: Implants can be made from a variety of materials with different costs, and some implants are longer than others, which makes them more expensive.
  • Brand: The brands of some dental implant manufacturers are more costly than others. Feel free to ask us if you’d like to know more about the company producing your appliances.
