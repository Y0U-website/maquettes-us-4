What Happens During Your Dental Exam

A dental exam is more than a quick glance at your teeth. Dr. Divis or Dr. Savage conducts a thorough evaluation of your entire oral environment, looking for the kinds of early-stage changes that are far easier to address now than later.

What Happens During Your Professional Cleaning

Even the most diligent brusher and flosser leaves behind some buildup. A professional cleaning removes hardened deposits that home care simply cannot address. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention , nearly half of American adults aged 30 and older show signs of gum disease — much of which stems from untreated plaque and tartar accumulation that regular brushing alone cannot prevent.

How Often Should You Schedule Visits

For most patients, twice-yearly visits are ideal. Some patients — particularly those managing active gum disease, dry mouth, or a history of frequent cavities — may benefit from visits every three to four months. During your exam, Dr. Divis or Dr. Savage will assess your specific risk factors and recommend a schedule that fits your needs.

A consistent schedule also gives our team a longitudinal view of your oral health. Changes that appear subtle in a single visit become meaningful trends when charted over time, allowing for earlier and more targeted intervention.

Why Regular Visits Matter More Than You May Think

Oral health does not exist in a vacuum. Research has long connected chronic gum disease to conditions including diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and respiratory illness. Staying current with your exams and cleanings addresses all of the following.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Exams & Cleanings

A routine visit typically takes 45 to 60 minutes for patients with healthy gums and no significant buildup. If it has been a while since your last cleaning, or if you have gum disease that requires more thorough scaling, the appointment may run longer. We will let you know in advance if we expect to need extra time.

Most patients find a professional cleaning comfortable. There may be mild sensitivity or pressure during scaling, especially if tartar has built up or your gums are inflamed — but that is temporary and resolves quickly after the appointment. If you have sensitive areas or dental anxiety, let us know before we start and we will adjust our approach. Sedation options are also available.

You will not be lectured or made to feel bad about the gap. Our job is to assess where your mouth is right now, address anything that needs attention, and get you back on a schedule that works. If significant buildup has occurred, we may recommend splitting the cleaning across two visits for your comfort. Either way, we handle it in whatever way makes the most sense for your specific situation.

A regular cleaning (prophylaxis) addresses the visible surfaces of your teeth and the area just at the gumline. A deep cleaning, or scaling and root planing, is performed when gum disease has caused pockets to develop deeper below the gumline. If we identify that level of buildup during your exam, we will explain what we found and walk you through the recommended next steps before proceeding with anything.
