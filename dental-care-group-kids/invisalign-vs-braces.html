How Traditional Braces Work

Traditional braces deliver steady force to guide teeth into place gradually, while encouraging jawbone changes for lasting stability. Main parts include brackets bonded to teeth and an archwire linking them. Adjustments to the wire’s tension during visits spark the movement, with steady progress over months or years to refine alignment and bites.

The Braces Process

  • Initial Placement : Brackets are bonded to each tooth with a safe adhesive, and an archwire is threaded through them to start applying gentle pressure
  • Biological Response : The wire’s tension compresses tissue on one side of the tooth roots, triggering cells to reshape bone, allowing teeth to move while new bone forms for support
  • Periodic Adjustments : Every four to six weeks, we tweak the archwire or add elements like elastics to maintain momentum and refine positioning
  • Component Variations : Options include metal brackets for durability, ceramic for subtlety, or lingual (behind teeth) for hidden placement, chosen based on your child’s needs
  • Treatment Completion : Spanning 18-24 months on average, with retainers afterward to secure the aligned smile long-term
