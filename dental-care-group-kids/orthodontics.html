Dental Care Group Kids Orthodontic Options at a Glance

Braces for Kids

Traditional braces remain one of the most trusted and effective ways to correct alignment issues in children and teens. Using brackets bonded to each tooth and connected by archwires, braces apply consistent, gentle pressure to gradually move teeth into their ideal positions. Modern braces are significantly smaller and more comfortable than older designs, and kids can even personalize them with colored elastic bands.

Most children wear braces for 18 24 months , with adjustment appointments every four to eight weeks.

  • Severe crowding or overlapping teeth
  • Significant bite problems (overbite, underbite, crossbite, open bite)
  • Complex rotations or jaw alignment concerns
  • Younger patients who may not yet be candidates for aligners

Invisalign for Kids & Teens

Invisalign clear aligners offer a virtually invisible alternative to braces, perfect for image-conscious tweens and teens. Custom-fit trays are worn 20–22 hours per day and swapped every one to two weeks, gradually shifting teeth without metal brackets or wires.

Invisalign is a great fit for kids and teens who:

Treatment typically achieves results in 12 18 months , often with fewer office visits than traditional braces. As a Diamond Invisalign Provider, Dr. Mars brings an exceptional level of expertise to every case.

  • Are approximately 12 or older, with most permanent teeth
  • Have mild to moderate alignment issues
  • Play sports or play instruments
  • Want the freedom to eat without restrictions
  • Prefer a discreet orthodontic option

Adult Braces & Invisalign

Orthodontic treatment isn’t just for kids. Adult patients now make up a significant portion of orthodontic cases, many treating crowding or shifting that has worsened over time, or for correcting alignment they never addressed in childhood.

We offer both traditional braces for adults and Invisalign clear aligners for adults seeking effective, discreet correction. Invisalign is especially popular among working adults who prefer a less noticeable option with minimal lifestyle disruption.

Common reasons adults seek orthodontic care:

  • Teeth that have shifted after previous treatment
  • Increased crowding from years of jaw pressure
  • Desire for improved aesthetics and oral health
  • Difficulty cleaning due to overlapping or crowded teeth

Signs Your Child (or You) May Need Orthodontic Treatment

Not every alignment issue is obvious. The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a child’s first orthodontic evaluation by age seven , even if their teeth appear mostly straight, so any developing issues can be monitored or addressed early.

Consider scheduling a consultation if you notice:

Early evaluation can prevent more complex and more costly problems later. And for adults, there’s truly no too late : the bones of adult jaws respond well to orthodontic pressure, and modern treatment options make correction more accessible than ever.

  • Crowded, overlapping, or twisted teeth
  • Gaps or spaces between teeth
  • Difficulty biting or chewing comfortably
  • Overbite, underbite, open bite, or crossbite
  • Jaw pain, clicking, or uneven jaw development
  • Speech issues that may be linked to misalignment
  • Mouth breathing or habits like thumb-sucking in younger children
  • Baby teeth falling out very early or very late

Why Orthodontics Is About More Than a Straight Smile

Orthodontic treatment has real, lasting health benefits that go far beyond aesthetics:

  • Easier oral hygiene: Properly aligned teeth have fewer tight spaces where plaque and bacteria accumulate, reducing the risk of cavities and gum disease
  • Better chewing and digestion: Correcting bite issues improves how food is broken down
  • Reduced tooth wear: Misaligned teeth wear unevenly over time; orthodontic correction prevents long-term damage
  • Decreased jaw strain: Bite problems can contribute to jaw joint disorders (TMD) and chronic discomfort
  • Improved speech: Misaligned teeth can cause speech impediments that improve with correction
  • Greater confidence: A smile your child feels proud of can positively impact their social interactions and self-esteem for life
