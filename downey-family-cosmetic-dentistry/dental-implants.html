Dental Implants

04 — Is it right for you Most adults are candidates for implants. Dental implants work for most patients, even those who have been told elsewhere that they are not candidates. Modern implant techniques and bone grafting have made implants possible for far more patients than even five years ago.

05 — The Process Your implant journey, step by step. Most implant cases follow this five-stage timeline from consultation to final smile.

  • Multiple Implants & Bridges Replace multiple missing teeth with a series of implants or an implant-supported bridge. We can replace two, three, or more teeth in a single arch with strategic implant placement that maximizes function and minimizes cost.
  • All-on-4 Full-Arch Replace an entire upper or lower arch of teeth with just four strategically placed implants supporting a fixed bridge. Patients walk in with failing or missing teeth and leave with a permanent, beautiful smile in as little as one day.
  • Adults with one or more missing teeth or failing teeth
  • Patients currently wearing partial or full dentures who want a permanent solution
  • Patients with adequate bone density, or willingness to have bone grafting if needed
  • Non-smokers, or patients willing to stop smoking during healing
  • Patients with overall good oral health and stable gum tissue
  • Patients who have completed any necessary periodontal or orthodontic treatment
  • Patients of any age, since implants work just as well at 70 as at 30
  • 01 Consultation & 3D Imaging Free initial consultation with Dr. Ben Reyhani. We discuss your goals, evaluate your bone density and oral health, and capture detailed 3D imaging to plan your implant placement with precision. You leave with a clear understanding of your options.
  • 02 Treatment Planning Using your 3D scans, we map exactly where each implant will be placed, what prosthetic will go on top, and what your timeline and investment will be. You see a digital preview of your final result before any work begins.
  • 03 Implant Placement The implant is surgically placed into the jawbone during a comfortable in-office procedure. Most patients return to normal activities the next day. We use sedation options for patients who prefer to be relaxed during placement.
  • 04 Healing & Integration Over 3 to 6 months, the implant fuses with your bone, a process called osseointegration that creates a foundation as strong as a natural tooth root. During this time, you wear a temporary crown or restoration if needed.
  • 05 Final Crown Placement Once the implant is fully integrated, we attach the permanent crown, bridge, or denture. The final result looks, feels, and functions like a natural tooth, and is designed to last a lifetime with proper care.

Questions about dental implants?

Implants are a significant investment in your long-term health. We hear these questions often and answer every one honestly.

How long do dental implants last? With proper care, dental implants are designed to last a lifetime. The crown attached to the implant typically lasts 15 to 25 years before it may need replacement, but the implant itself is permanent. Implants have the highest long-term success rate of any tooth replacement option, with documented success rates above 95 percent at 10 years and beyond.

  • Does getting a dental implant hurt? The implant placement itself is performed under local anesthesia, so you do not feel pain during the procedure. Most patients describe post-surgical discomfort as similar to a tooth extraction, manageable with over-the-counter pain relievers and resolved within 3 to 5 days. We offer sedation options for patients who prefer to be deeply relaxed during placement.
  • How long does the entire implant process take? Most single-tooth implant cases take 3 to 6 months from initial placement to final crown. The longest part is the healing period, when the implant integrates with your bone. Some patients qualify for same-day implants where a temporary crown is placed immediately after surgery, though the final permanent crown still requires healing time before placement.
  • How much do dental implants cost in Downey? Implant cost varies based on whether you need bone grafting, the type of crown or restoration on top, and how many implants you need. We offer flexible financing through CareCredit and accept most dental insurance plans that cover implants. Many patients are surprised that their insurance covers a meaningful portion. We provide a clear written cost breakdown before any work begins.
  • Are dental implants covered by insurance? Many dental insurance plans cover at least part of implant treatment, typically the crown portion and sometimes the implant itself. Coverage varies significantly by plan. We verify your specific benefits before treatment begins so you have a clear understanding of what your insurance covers and what your out-of-pocket cost will be. We accept most PPO plans including Delta Dental, MetLife, Cigna, Aetna, and Anthem.
  • What is All-on-4 and how is it different from regular implants? All-on-4 is a technique where a full arch of teeth (upper or lower) is supported by just four strategically placed implants. It is ideal for patients missing most or all of their teeth in an arch, or wearing dentures and ready for a permanent solution. The procedure is often completed in a single day, with patients leaving with a fixed temporary set of teeth that day, and a final permanent set after healing.
  • Can I get an implant if I have been missing a tooth for years? Often yes. When teeth are missing for extended periods, the jawbone in that area can shrink, a process called bone resorption. If significant bone loss has occurred, we may recommend bone grafting to rebuild the foundation before implant placement. Our 3D imaging shows exactly how much bone you have and what would be needed for successful placement.
  • What is the difference between a dental bridge and an implant? A traditional bridge replaces a missing tooth by anchoring to the two adjacent teeth, which must be ground down to receive crowns. An implant replaces the missing tooth without affecting any other teeth. Bridges typically last 10 to 15 years before needing replacement; implants are designed to last a lifetime. Bridges are typically less expensive upfront, but implants often cost less over the long term because they do not require periodic replacement.
