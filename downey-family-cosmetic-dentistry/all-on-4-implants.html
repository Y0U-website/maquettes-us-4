All-on-4 Implants

  • Eat what you want With All-on-4, patients can eat the foods they have given up on with dentures, steaks, apples, corn on the cob, hard breads. Chewing function returns to nearly natural levels.
  • Preserves jaw bone Implants stimulate the jaw bone the way natural tooth roots do, preventing the bone loss that occurs when teeth are missing or replaced with dentures alone.
  • Looks completely natural All-on-4 teeth are custom-designed to match facial structure, lip support, and aesthetic goals. Most people cannot tell that the teeth are not natural.
  • Long-term durability Properly placed implants typically last 25+ years, often a lifetime. The replacement teeth (prosthesis) may need refurbishing every 10-15 years, but the implants themselves rarely need replacement.
  • Better than individual implants Replacing every missing tooth with an individual implant would require 10-12 implants per arch and significantly higher cost. All-on-4 achieves comparable results with just four implants per arch, far more efficient.

Who is a candidate for All-on-4.

All-on-4 is designed for patients with specific situations. Here is when it typically makes sense.

Patients with severely insufficient bone, uncontrolled diabetes, recent radiation therapy in the jaw area, certain medications affecting bone metabolism, or significant medical conditions may not be candidates. Heavy smokers face higher implant failure rates and are typically counseled to quit before treatment. We evaluate each case carefully.

05 — The Process What to expect from All-on-4 treatment. Most cases follow this six-stage process from initial consultation to final restoration.

  • Missing all or most teeth in your upper or lower jaw
  • Remaining teeth cannot be saved due to advanced gum disease, severe decay, or fractures
  • Currently wearing traditional dentures and dissatisfied with fit, function, or comfort
  • Adequate bone density to support implant placement (or candidate for bone grafting)
  • In good general health and able to undergo a multi-hour procedure with sedation
  • Committed to long-term maintenance including routine cleanings and annual implant evaluations
  • Financially prepared for the investment in permanent tooth replacement
  • 01 Consultation & 3D Imaging Free consultation with Dr. Ben Reyhani, the practice&#x27;s implant specialist. 3D cone beam imaging captures detailed views of your jaw, bone density, and existing dental anatomy. We discuss treatment goals, timeline, and cost.
  • 02 Treatment Planning Detailed treatment plan including implant positioning, prosthetic design, and any preliminary procedures (extractions, bone grafting, or sinus lifts when needed). Plan is finalized with you before scheduling.
  • 03 Surgical Day In a single appointment (typically 4-6 hours), any remaining teeth are extracted, the four implants are placed, and a temporary prosthesis is attached the same day. You leave with teeth, often called teeth in a day.
  • 04 Healing Period Over the next 4-6 months, the implants integrate with the jaw bone (osseointegration). You wear the temporary prosthesis during this period. Soft food diet for the first weeks, gradually returning to normal eating.
  • 05 Final Prosthesis Design Once healing is complete, impressions are taken to design the permanent prosthesis. Material choice (acrylic, zirconia, or hybrid) is based on aesthetic priorities, durability needs, and cost considerations.
  • 06 Final Placement The temporary prosthesis is removed and the permanent prosthesis is attached to the implants. The transformation is permanent, you walk out with the final result.

Differences between upper and lower All-on-4.

Upper jaw considerations

  • Often requires sinus lift or bone grafting due to thinner bone naturally present in the upper jaw
  • Cosmetic visibility is higher, lip support and smile aesthetics are key planning factors
  • Implant angulation is critical for avoiding the sinus cavity and nasal floor
  • Typically takes slightly longer to plan and execute than lower arch

Lower jaw considerations

  • Generally has denser bone, often allowing direct implant placement without grafting
  • Functional priorities (chewing strength) often outweigh aesthetic priorities
  • Faster healing and earlier loading possible in many cases
  • Often less complex surgically than upper arch
