What Are Dental Implants?

Unlike traditional tooth replacement, dental implants recreate the entire structure of a lost tooth, including the section that is hidden underneath the gums. A dental implant contains three main parts: a titanium post that is surgically inserted into the jawbone, a metal connector piece called an abutment, and a custom-made restoration (crown/bridge/denture) on top.

Because dental implants are the only solution that replicates a lost tooth’s roots as well as its crown, they are able to look and feel perfectly natural while restoring more of your biting power. Those are just a few of the many reasons why most dentists, including ours , will agree that dental implants are the most beneficial way to replace missing teeth.

During your initial implant consultation, our dentists will examine your smile in its entirety and look at digital x-rays to get a better idea of your bone density. Dental Implant Surgery

Our team is equipped to place your dental implants and see that you receive a customized, beautiful restoration all under one roof. When arriving for your dental implant placement, we will administer local anesthesia to ensure you are comfortable throughout the procedure. Next, depending on the number of implants you’ll be receiving, we will make one or more small incisions in your gums before carefully positioning each implant. Once they are put into place, your surgical sites will be closed, and you’ll be sent home to spend the next several months healing.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

While at home, your implants will need to fuse to the bone and surrounding tissue in a process known as osseointegration. Within three to six months, you’ll be fully healed, and you can return to our office to have your abutments put into place. These small metal appliances are placed on top of your implants and will connect to your restorations.

Placement of Dental Implant Restorations

The final step in receiving dental implants is the placement of your customized restorations. Whether it is a dental crown, implant bridge, or implant dentures, your prosthetic will match the rest of your teeth and fit comfortably for a longer-lasting, beautiful smile.

Bone Grafting

Your bones tend to break down or resorb in areas where they’re no longer in use, which is why the jaw loses some of its density when teeth are missing. To reverse the damage, we can transplant new bone tissue to the damaged area; this causes the jaw to start developing tissue of its own. Given enough time (which can be anywhere from three months to a year), the site of the graft will have rebuilt itself to the point where it can successfully join with and support dental implants.

Typically, we’ll use bone tissue taken from your own body, likely from the back of your jaw. However, grafting material can also come from another human donor, and in some instances, animal bone might be used. There’s even a type of synthetic graft called an alloplast.

In some instances, we can perform implant surgery right after grafting. In general, though, you should plan on allowing several months for your mouth to recover. You’ll need to take care of your mouth for a few days after surgery; plan on eating only soft foods for a few days as well as using painkillers and ice packs to control any discomfort or swelling.

Sinus Lift

On the surface, a sinus lift is very similar to a bone graft in that new tissue is added to the jaw and helps it regain any height it has lost. There are two main differences between these procedures: first, a sinus lift is only performed on the upper jaw, and second, it moves the sinuses away from the mouth, which might be required if you’re getting implants to replace certain specific teeth.

After opening the gums, we’ll expose the thin membrane separating the sinuses from the mouth. We shall then very gently push the membrane up and away from the space where we need to work. The grafting material is added to this area in layers. After four or nine months, your mouth should fully heal, and we’ll be ready to move on with the implant process. (Note that the exact healing time will vary from person to person.)

Remember that while it’s very common for bone grafts and sinus lifts to be part of the dental implant process, they’re not always required; we may be able to simply schedule the implant surgery right away. The initial consultation at the beginning of the implant process is important for determining which procedures are necessary.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

The most important thing to remember after your surgery is complete is to not bother your surgical sites. It is necessary for blood clots to form within the sockets, as this encourages proper healing. Failing to do so can increase your risk of a dry socket, which can be painful and problematic. To avoid this, make sure that you:

  • Avoid spitting or sipping through a straw
  • Do not smoke for several days (it’s best if you can quit before surgery)
  • Do not poke or prod your surgical sites with your tongue or finger
  • Use tissues to remove excessive saliva from your mouth

Common Side Effects

Although it may not seem normal, there are certain side effects that you do not need to be worried about. For the first few days after implant placement, you can expect:

  • Minimal bleeding that will come and go. You can reduce the amount by applying gentle pressure and using clean gauze.
  • Some swelling for a few days or up to one week. A cold compress can help reduce inflammation.
  • Mild discomfort that can be managed with an OTC pain medication.

Maintaining & Caring For Your Dental Implants

Even after your dental implants have successfully fused with the jawbone, you still have to make sure that you’re taking good care of them. While implant failure is rare, it can very easily occur if you suffer an oral injury or infection. The good news is that there’s no real trick to implant maintenance you can clean and protect them in much the same way you would your natural teeth. Use the tips below to be proactive about keeping your implants safe so that you can rely on them for decades.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

You’re probably already well aware of how important it is to brush and floss every day to get rid of harmful bacteria and food particles. Some people mistakenly believe they don’t have to worry about brushing implant restorations because they don’t get cavities. But while tooth decay can’t affect implants, gum disease certainly can; an infection of the gums leads to bone loss and can cause the implant posts to come loose. In other words, a good oral hygiene routine is more important than ever once you’ve made an investment in implants.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Sugar and starch are often prime culprits when it comes to gum disease. Bacteria eat these substances and turn them into acids that can destroy gum and bone tissue. A sweet treat every once in a while shouldn’t be a problem, but if you want to actively create an oral environment where implants can thrive, your diet should mainly consist of fruits and vegetables that contain high amounts of vitamin C or fat-free dairy products that are rich in calcium.

Break Bad Habits

There are many things you should not do if you don’t want to break your implants or lose them to an infection:

  • Do not smoke or use tobacco products at all, as this makes the mouth more vulnerable to infection.
  • Do not bite on your nails or chew on pen tips, as this can gradually wear down your implants and the restoration(s) they support.
  • Do not use your teeth as tools to open packages or jars. This can very easily lead to dental injury.
  • Do not chew ice, popcorn kernels, or other hard foods that could damage implants.

Protect Your Dental Implants

You put your teeth and implants at risk whenever you head out onto the field to play your favorite sport without any oral protection. Make sure you have a custom-fitted mouthguard for such activities so that you’re protected in case of an accident. You can also wear a mouthguard at night so that you don’t damage your implant with unconscious clenching or grinding.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

A professional eye can check for signs of implant failure in the earliest stages. Don’t forget to visit our office every six months for your regular dental checkups and cleanings; a little prevention can save you time and money later.
