What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants stand out when it comes to tooth replacement because they are the only treatment that replaces the roots of teeth and their crown, while other options only address the visible portion above the gumline. The dental implant itself is a small titanium post that Dr. Johnson surgically places into your jaw. Once there, it mimics the function of your natural tooth root by merging with the surrounding jawbone and tissue in a process called osseointegration. Then, the implant can support a dental crown, bridge, or denture via a metal abutment fixture placed on top of it.

The result is a tooth replacement that feels as natural as it looks, lets you chew all of your favorite foods, and has the potential to last for decades! That’s why, when it comes to replacing missing teeth, dental implants are usually Dr. Johnson’s first recommendation.

Dental Implant Consultation

The first step of every dental implant treatment is a thorough consultation with Dr. Johnson. During your appointment, we will take a cone beam CT scan of your facial anatomy to show us the density of your jawbone, as well as other important details we’ll need to plan your surgery. We will also speak with you about what to expect during the process and inform you of all of your treatment options.

Dr. Johnson will put together a detailed treatment plan designed just for you, walking you through your journey and answering any questions you may have about the process. If you’re a good candidate and you wish to move forward with our proposed treatment plan, our team will schedule your surgery next.

Surgical Placement of Your Dental Implants

With Dr. Johnson’s extensive experience in dental sedation and dental implants, he’s able to complete the surgical placement of your dental implants himself. This means you won’t have to worry about traveling to a different office to see a doctor that you don’t know for this important part of your journey.

Healing, Osseointegration & Abutment Attachment

After your dental implants have been placed below your gumline to restore the missing root structure of your teeth, you will undergo a lengthy healing and osseointegration process. ( Osseointegration just means to fuse with the bone, involving your dental implants integrating with your jawbone like the natural roots of your teeth once were.) This typically takes between three and six months and provides a strong foundation for your restoration to be attached to.

After osseointegration and healing is complete, our team will have you come back to the office for another small surgical procedure. We will attach metal connector pieces to the top of each of your implants, called abutments. They will secure your restoration within your mouth. Once this is complete, we will take an impression of your gumline to send to the dental laboratory to craft your restoration.

Attaching Your Final Restoration

After our team receives your customized dental restoration from our laboratory, it will be time for the final step! We’ll make sure the replacement teeth match any existing teeth and are a perfect fit before permanently securing them to your dental implants and completing your smile.

Day-to-Day Benefits

We use our teeth every day to eat, speak, and smile. Because of that, it only makes sense that you’d want something that’ll allow you to do all of those things freely and confidently. Well, dental implants offer a range of daily benefits, including:

  • Versatility Implants can be used to replace one or more missing teeth. They can also bond and retain almost any restoration (i.e., crown, bridge, denture) on top.
  • Lower Maintenance Taking care of your dental implants is as easy as caring for natural teeth. All you have to do is brush, floss, and rinse as you normally would to keep your new smile happy and healthy!
  • Increased Confidence Since implants stay firmly in place, you don’t have to worry about them moving around in your mouth, allowing you to feel more confident in your day-to-day life.

Health Benefits

Although dental implants can certainly make you look good, they offer many health advantages as well, including:

  • Unmatched Stability Because dental implants are inserted into the jawbone, they can handle chewing almost any food.
  • Improved Oral Health You can more easily maintain a nutritious diet, which helps your smile stay healthy and functional.
  • Jawbone Preservation Implants are one of the only options that keep the jawbone healthy because they stimulate it, preventing its deterioration.

Long-Term Benefits

Dental implants in Midlothian can help you for years to come with the following advantages:

  • High Success Rate Dental implants have an impressive success rate of about 95 percent even 20 years after they’ve been placed!
  • Youthful Appearance Since implants stimulate the blood flow in the jaw, they help prevent the sunken, aged look that often comes with tooth loss.
  • Long Lifespan Unlike dentures and dental bridges, implants can last several decades without needing replacement.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

The many restorative benefits of dental implants aren’t the only promise you can expect. Their versatility is unmatched in the world of tooth replacement. Learn how you can restore your smile, regardless of the number of teeth you may be missing!

Missing One Tooth

A single tooth is replaced via one titanium post and one porcelain crown. An abutment attaches the restoration to the implant and provides long-term stability. Our dental office uses porcelain because of its incredibly natural characteristics.

Missing Multiple Teeth

If you have consecutive missing teeth, an implant-supported bridge can easily fill the gap in your smile. Instead of relying on natural teeth to anchor the restoration, two implants hold the bridge, one on either end with pontics sitting in between them. This option is ideal if you don’t want to remove the existing tooth structure.

Missing All Teeth

Are you missing most or all of your teeth in a single arch? Full and partial dentures can not only be held in place via dental implants but can be customized to be removable or fixed depending on your preference. Even better, you don’t need an implant dedicated to each tooth you intend on replacing, helping you cut down on costs.

Understanding the Cost of Dental Implants

While dental implants can be expensive, your treatment costs will likely differ from others. The planning and restoration stages of treatment are highly customizable; placing one implant is going to cost less than placing several throughout the jaw, and a single implant-supported crown will cost less than a three-unit bridge or implant denture. Furthermore, those who do not need preparatory treatments like bone grafts, periodontal therapy, or sinus lifts save more money on treatment overall.

It’s important to acknowledge that dental implants are a long-term investment. Additionally, they are considered to be the most cost-effective because of their longevity, which does not exist in the same way as traditional bridges or dentures.

Types of Dental Implants

How many teeth you’re missing and where they’re located will determine what type of dental implant Dr. Johnson will recommend for you.

  • Single Tooth : A single missing tooth can be replaced with one dental implant topped off with a customized dental crown.
  • Consecutive Missing Teeth : For two or more missing teeth in a row, an implant bridge may be the best solution. Unlike a traditional bridge that’s secured to two healthy existing teeth, one that is held in place by implants won’t alter your surrounding teeth. On either side of the gap in your smile will be a dental implant, which will hold the bridge in place.
  • An Entire Arch of Teeth : An entire arch of missing teeth can be remedied by a denture retained by between four to eight implants.
  • Full Mouth Replacement : If you’re missing all of your teeth, you can have two entire dentures retained by implants.

Dental Implant FAQs

How Long Have Dental Implants Been Around?

Although the oldest evidence of dental implants dates back to around 600 A.D., it wasn’t until 1952 that a Swedish orthopedic surgeon stumbled upon osseointegration. He was studying the bone healing process when he discovered the small titanium cylinder that he placed in a jawbone couldn’t be removed. Since then, advancements in dentistry and innovations in technology have made dental implants more precise, resulting in the state-of-the-art tooth replacement we know today!

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

Dental implants can last for 30+ years with proper care! Fortunately, you can keep yours in pristine condition throughout the years with the same best practices you use to preserve your natural teeth. This starts with a solid at-home oral hygiene routine, which includes brushing for a full two minutes at least twice a day, flossing daily, and using mouthwash regularly. Next, avoid unhealthy dental habits, like smoking, chewing on ice, and using your teeth as tools. Lastly, prioritize your six-month dental checkups and cleanings.

What Can Cause Dental Implants to Fail?

Although dental implants hold an impressive 95 % success rate, they can fail. The two leading causes are peri-implantitis (a form of gum disease) and failed osseointegration (the unsuccessful integration of the implant post to the jawbone). Other common causes include chronic teeth grinding, the use of tobacco products, and certain health conditions. That’s why our implant dentist in Midlothian will consider all of these factors when determining your candidacy. Furthermore, they will provide you with extensive guidelines both before and after your procedure to minimize your risk.

Is There Anything I Shouldn’t Eat with Dental Implants?

One major benefit of dental implants is that they can restore your bite force. With that being said, you should still exercise caution with extremely hard or sticky foods that can break or pull on them. For that reason, avoid peanut brittle, nuts, beef jerky, and the like. Quitting unhealthy dental habits, like opening packages with your teeth and chewing on ice, is also essential.
