What to Expect: Your Dental Implant Process in Orange, CA

Getting dental implants is a multi-step process, but each stage is straightforward when you know what’s coming. Here’s how it typically works at Glassell Dental.

Consultation and Treatment Planning

Your journey begins with a comprehensive exam. Using advanced digital imaging and treatment planning tools, we assess your bone structure, gum health, and overall oral health to determine whether implants are the right fit and to map out your personalized plan. We’ll walk you through every step, answer your questions, and make sure you feel genuinely prepared — not rushed.

Implant Placement and Healing

Once your treatment plan is finalized, the implant post is placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia. Most patients are comfortable throughout the procedure and manage any post-placement tenderness with over-the-counter pain relief. Over the following weeks to months, the implant fuses with the bone — a healing phase called osseointegration. We monitor your progress closely during this time.

Restoration: Your Final Crown

When healing is complete, a custom crown is attached to the implant. This is the visible part of your new tooth, crafted to blend seamlessly with your smile. The result is a tooth that looks, feels, and functions naturally — and one that’s built to last.

Dental Implants vs. Your Other Options

Understanding how implants compare to other tooth-replacement solutions helps you make the choice that’s right for your life and your budget.

Dental Implants vs. Dental Bridges

A dental bridge fills the gap left by a missing tooth by anchoring an artificial tooth to the two healthy teeth on either side. Bridges are a reliable and often more affordable option upfront. However, they require reshaping the adjacent teeth, and they don’t address bone loss beneath the gap. Implants preserve bone, don’t affect neighboring teeth, and typically last longer — making them the stronger long-term investment for many patients.

Dental Implants vs. Dentures

Dentures and partial dentures are removable appliances that replace one or more missing teeth. They tend to have lower initial costs and may suit patients who are not candidates for implants. However, dentures can shift during eating or speaking and don’t stimulate the jawbone, which means bone loss continues over time. Implants provide a fixed, stable solution that integrates fully with your anatomy. For patients replacing a single tooth, a single tooth implant in Orange, CA is often the most functional and comfortable long-term choice.

The Cost of Waiting: Why Replacing a Missing Tooth Matters

It’s easy to put off replacing a missing tooth, especially if it’s not immediately visible or causing pain. But understanding what happens over time can help you make a more informed decision — on your own timeline.

When a tooth is lost, the jawbone beneath it no longer receives the stimulation it needs to stay dense. Within the first year, measurable bone loss can begin. Over time, this can affect the shape of your face, alter your bite, and make future implant placement more complex or require additional preparatory procedures. Neighboring teeth may also gradually shift toward the gap, changing your bite alignment and increasing the risk of further dental issues. These are not meant to alarm you — they’re simply changes worth knowing about so you can weigh your options with a full picture.
