What Is the Dental Implant Process?

Dental implant treatment at Dental Co. of Knoxville is a simple, predictable process completed in clear steps. Here is what you can expect during your visits:

  • Consultation and digital imaging: Dr. Holt evaluates your teeth, gums, and bone structure using clear digital imaging to design your customized treatment plan.
  • Implant placement: Dr. Holt gently places the titanium post into your jawbone under local anesthesia to keep you completely comfortable and pain-free.
  • Healing and bonding: Over 3 to 6 months, your jawbone naturally fuses around the titanium post, creating a sturdy foundation for your new tooth.
  • Attaching the connector piece: Once healed, your dentist attaches a small connecting piece that holds your new custom tooth securely in place.
  • Placing your custom crown: Dr. Holt places a custom-crafted replacement tooth designed to match the exact shade, size, and shape of your natural smile.

How do I care for my dental implants?

Dental implants are the strongest and most reliable solution for tooth loss. Of course, just like natural teeth, implants require care and maintenance to remain fully functional for years to come. If you’ve recently had implants placed in Knoxville, here are three essential tips for optimal results.

Daily Oral Hygiene

After implant placement, you must commit to brushing twice daily with a soft-bristled brush and non-abrasive toothpaste to prevent plaque and tartar buildup, just as you would with a natural tooth. Daily flossing, whether with traditional floss or a water flosser, is equally important for cleaning between teeth and under the implant crown.

Watch What You Eat and Drink

While implants are notably durable, it’s best to avoid chewing hard items like ice or pens and take care when eating popcorn or unpitted olives, which can damage the restoration. Limiting sugary drinks and sticky foods also helps reduce bacteria and protect the gum tissue supporting your implant.

Visit Your Dentist

Of course, even with great at-home care, professional dental checkups and cleanings are vital. These regular cleanings (ideally every six months) enable your dentist to monitor the implant site, polish the restoration, and ensure that your remaining teeth and surrounding gum tissue remain healthy.

Can smokers get dental implants?

The answer is yes; however, it s essential to understand that tobacco use can significantly impact the success or failure of dental implants . Here s what you should know.

Why Smoking Negatively Impacts Dental Implants

Smoking reduces your blood flow and weakens your immune system, making it more difficult for dental implants to successfully integrate with your bone (osseointegration). This increases the risk of potential implant failure.

A weakened immune system also increases your susceptibility to infections. A dental infection can be the downfall of a tooth implant, causing it to fail.

You may also experience a slower recovery process due to smoking or tobacco use.

What Should Smokers Do If They re Considering Dental Implants?

Consult your dentist to confirm your candidacy for dental implants and discuss your smoking habits. You might want to consider quitting tobacco use, which improves your oral health and overall well-being.

Why Choose Dental Implants?

Dental implants offer distinct practical and aesthetic advantages over traditional bridges or removable dentures . Key benefits include:

  • Permanent solution: Built to last 25 years to a lifetime with basic brushing, flossing, and routine dental checkups.
  • Restored bite force: Restores up to 100 % of your natural chewing power so you can eat all your favorite foods with total confidence.
  • Jawbone preservation: Helps prevent the estimated 25 % bone density loss that typically happens during the first year after losing a tooth.
  • Natural look and feel: Custom-made to fit seamlessly along your gumline and blend right in with your surrounding teeth.
  • Protects neighboring teeth: Keeps your surrounding healthy teeth intact, unlike traditional bridges that require grinding down neighboring teeth.
