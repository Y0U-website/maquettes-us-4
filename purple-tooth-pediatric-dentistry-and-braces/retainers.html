Retainers

Choosing between these options usually comes down to how much daily responsibility feels realistic for your child and what their bite needs long-term. We help families weigh those factors so the retainer fits into real life instead of becoming another thing to forget.

  • Fixed retainers, a thin wire bonded behind the front teeth that stays in place without daily removal
  • Combination approaches, where a fixed retainer supports the front teeth while a removable retainer covers the rest of the arch

What to Expect With Retainer Care

Once your child gets a retainer, a little routine goes a long way. Removable retainers need to be rinsed and brushed gently, stored in their case when not in use, and kept away from pets, heat, and pockets where they tend to disappear. Fixed retainers need extra care during brushing and flossing because food and plaque can build up around the wire.

We check retainers at regular visits to make sure they still fit well and are doing their job. Teeth and jaws continue to change as kids grow, so a retainer that fit perfectly six months ago might need an adjustment now. Catching that early is much easier than dealing with a full relapse later.

Comfort matters too. A retainer that feels tight or causes soreness for more than a couple of days is worth a call, because that usually means an adjustment is needed rather than something to push through. Our Happy Room and low-pressure approach carry through to every part of care, including these follow-up visits.

Retainers as Part of the Bigger Picture

Braces and retainers are only one part of keeping a child’s smile healthy long term. Regular cleanings, exams, and attention to habits like thumb sucking or mouth breathing all play a role in whether orthodontic results hold steady. That is part of why we built our practice around combining pediatric dentistry and braces into fewer total visits instead of sending families back and forth between separate offices.

Some kids also benefit from evaluating other factors that affect bite and alignment, such as tongue tie, which can influence how teeth and jaws develop over time. Addressing those pieces alongside orthodontic treatment gives retainers a better foundation to work from, because the forces acting on the teeth are more balanced from the start.

Families who stick with a full care plan, not just the retainer alone, tend to see the most stable results. It is a team effort between what happens at home and what we track at each visit.
