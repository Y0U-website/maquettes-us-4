How Do Implant-Retained Dentures Work?

Implant-retained dentures rely on a small number of dental implants placed strategically in the jawbone, which then serve as anchor points for your denture. Rather than replacing every missing tooth individually, a handful of implants can support an entire arch, which keeps the procedure more efficient than it might sound. Once the implants have fused with the bone in a process called osseointegration, the denture snaps or clips onto the implants, giving you a secure fit you can rely on.

Is an Implant-Retained Denture Right for You?

Implant-retained dentures aren’t the only path forward if you’re missing most or all of your teeth, and the right choice depends on your goals, your bone health, and your budget. Patients missing only some teeth, rather than a full arch, may be better suited to partial dentures instead, which don’t require the same implant placement process. Patients who want maximum stability and are comfortable with a longer treatment timeline often find implant-retained dentures worth the investment, especially if they’ve struggled with a loose or shifting denture for years.

How Common Is Complete Tooth Loss?

Complete tooth loss becomes more common with age, and the numbers are more significant than many people expect. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention , about 1 in 10 adults aged 65 to 74 had lost all their natural teeth as of the most recent national survey data, and that figure rises to roughly 1 in 5 among adults 75 and older. For patients facing that reality, a few common frustrations with traditional dentures tend to come up again and again:
