How Does an Implant Crown Differ From a Regular Crown?

A regular dental crown caps a tooth that’s still present but damaged or weakened, protecting what’s left of the natural structure underneath. An implant crown is different because there’s no natural tooth left to protect. Instead, a titanium implant post is placed directly in the jawbone to act as an artificial root, and once it has fused with the bone through a process called osseointegration, the crown attaches to that post rather than to a tooth.

What If the Jawbone Needs Extra Support?

Not every patient is ready for an implant post right away. If a tooth has been missing for a while, the jawbone in that area may have thinned to the point where it can’t support an implant on its own, because bone tends to resorb once it’s no longer holding a tooth root in place. When that happens, a bone grafting procedure can rebuild the density needed before the implant is placed, adding a step to the timeline but giving the implant a much stronger foundation to fuse with.

We’ll evaluate your bone structure honestly before recommending a path forward, because starting implant placement too early can compromise the long-term result. Patients who need grafting shouldn’t see it as a setback so much as a necessary step toward a more predictable outcome down the road, and the extra healing time is a worthwhile tradeoff for an implant that holds up well over the years.
