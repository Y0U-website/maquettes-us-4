What the Process Looks Like

Placing a single tooth implant starts with a titanium post being positioned in the jawbone, where it’s given time to fuse with the surrounding bone in a process called osseointegration. That healing period allows the implant to become a stable, long-term anchor rather than something that shifts or loosens with normal use. Once that foundation is secure, we attach an abutment and then a custom implant crown that’s shaped and shaded to match your surrounding teeth.

Some patients need a little extra preparation before an implant can be placed, and that’s normal. If the jawbone in the area is too thin to support an implant on its own, a sinus lift or other bone grafting procedure can rebuild the foundation an implant needs to stay secure over time. If a damaged tooth needs to come out first, we can typically handle the extraction and implant planning together, so you’re not juggling separate appointments with separate providers or waiting longer than necessary between steps.

Options Beyond a Single Tooth

Not every patient is missing just one tooth, and implants can support more than a single crown. Implant-supported dentures anchor a full or partial denture to a small number of implants, which keeps the denture far more stable than one that rests on the gums alone. That stability tends to make a real difference in daily comfort, from eating a wider range of foods to speaking without worrying about slippage, and it also helps preserve jawbone that would otherwise continue to shrink under a traditional denture.

If you’ve already lost several teeth and you’re deciding between an implant-supported option and a traditional denture, it helps to talk through your specific case with someone who can look at your bone structure and bite before recommending a direction. You can also review our implant-retained dentures if a full arch replacement is closer to what you need than a single tooth solution.
