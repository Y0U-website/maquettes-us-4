Dental implants

A dental implant replaces the whole tooth — root and crown — so it looks, feels, and works like your own. It’s the closest modern dentistry comes to growing a tooth back.

How implants work

A small titanium post takes the place of the missing root and integrates with your jawbone over a few months. A custom crown is then attached, matched to your surrounding teeth in shape and shade. Unlike a bridge, an implant doesn’t rely on grinding down healthy neighboring teeth, and unlike a denture, it doesn’t move.

Are you a candidate?

Most adults with a missing or failing tooth are. Candidacy comes down to gum health and available bone, which we assess with CBCT 3D imaging at your consultation — the same scan we use to plan exactly where the implant will sit. If an implant isn’t your best option, you’ll hear that honestly, along with the alternatives — that consultation is a diagnosis, not a sales pitch.

With good hygiene and regular checkups, the implant post itself is designed to last decades — it integrates with your jawbone and becomes part of your mouth. The crown attached to it may need replacing after many years of normal wear, much like any other restoration.

Cost depends on how many teeth are being replaced, whether any preparatory work like bone grafting is needed, and the type of restoration. You’ll receive complete pricing in writing before treatment begins, insurance benefits are verified up front, and financing is available through Cherry and CareCredit.

The placement itself is done under local anesthetic, and most patients report it feeling more straightforward than a tooth extraction. Mild soreness for a few days afterward is normal and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief.
