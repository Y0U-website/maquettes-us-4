What Are Dental Implants?

The 4 Step Dental Implant Process

After tooth loss, dental implants are a wonderful investment in your oral health that can last a lifetime. The procedure is usually done in multiple phases that are spread out over 3-6 months and provide a big payoff: A beautifully restored smile that looks and feels like your own. And, although the process takes time, our patients are often surprised at how much easier it is than they expected.

Initial Consultation

Our first step is making sure we understand your needs and goals so we can create the absolute best treatment plan for your smile. Then we’ll use state-of-the-art cone beam technology to take 3D images that help us evaluate your teeth and jaws. We’ll also assess your overall health as well as your gums to ensure the long-term success of your implants.

Finally, we’ll answer any questions you have and discuss any treatment you may need beforehand, such as a bone graft procedure, extractions, or gum therapy. This allows us to give you a tailored estimate of your cost and a more specific timeline of how long your treatment will take.

Dental Implant Surgery

Here at Advanced Dental Treatment Center, Dr. Farr and Dr. Carini provide outstanding care through every part of the implant process, from the initial surgery to the final restoration.

Next, we’ll give you instructions for how to care for the area during the initial healing that takes place over the next several days.

Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

Dental implants provide unmatched strength and stability due to a process called osseointegration in which they fuse with the surrounding jawbone. In most cases, this takes between 3-6 months, during which we’ll put a healing cap over the area so you can eat, drink, and function normally.

Then you’ll return so we can attach an abutment to the implant, which is a type of adaptor that connects it to its dental restoration. Depending on your needs, this restoration might be an implant crown, bridge or denture.

Delivery of Final Restoration

Restoring an implant simply refers to completing it with a prosthetic tooth so you can eat, speak, and smile comfortably. The procedure is similar to a restoration for a natural tooth and will be precisely matched in size, shape, and color to the rest of your smile. One of our experienced dentists will make sure that your final result feels incredibly natural and also looks great as well. Benefits of Dental Implants

Day-to-Day Benefits

  • Eat whatever you like comfortably: Dental implants are strong, stable, and have chewing power that’s similar to natural teeth. They can help you eat steak, nuts, apples, and more easily and comfortably.
  • Enjoy a smile that looks and feels natural: Since your new replacement teeth will be anchored into your jawbone and emerge from the gums, they will look and feel just like home-grown pearly whites.
  • Brush and floss without hassle: Dental implants don’t require any special techniques or products to be cleaned. Just brush and floss like normal to keep them strong and sparkling!
  • Speak, smile, and eat with complete confidence: Your dental implants will essentially become part of your jaw, so they will never feel loose, click, or shift out of place as you go about your day.

Health Benefits

  • Keep your gums and remaining teeth healthy: Thanks to their unique placement, dental implants won’t wear down your remaining teeth or irritate your gums, like bridges and dentures do.
  • Preserve your jawbone: Replacing the roots of missing teeth with dental implants is the only way to stop the jawbone deterioration and sunken, aged facial appearance that typically accompanies tooth loss.
  • Prevent future tooth loss: Maintaining a strong and healthy jawbone will also go a tremendously long way in keeping your remaining teeth securely in place.
  • Lower your risk of systemic health issues: Dental implants naturally promote better oral health, and studies show that a healthy mouth lowers your risk of developing health conditions like diabetes, heart disease, and arthritis.

Long-Term Benefits

  • Rely on your smile for decades: Dental implants boast an astonishing success rate of over 95 %, even 10 years after placement. With the proper care, most patients can expect their dental implants to last a lifetime.
  • Extend your lifespan: Studies suggest that in the later years of life, the number of teeth you have correlates with your life expectancy. Dental implants could help you live longer.
  • Save money in the long run: While dental implants may be more expensive than other options initially, they will never need to be replaced. In comparison, dentures and dental bridges need to be replaced every 10 years or so. Dental implants could save you thousands of dollars on replacements alone! Pairing that with saving you money on extra cleaning supplies, adhesives, and trips to the dentist for repairs or adjustments, dental implants can save you a considerable amount of time and money over the years.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Dental implants remain the most versatile method of tooth replacement solely because of the number of teeth they are capable of replacing at any given time. Whether you want to replace a single tooth or replace an old denture that simply doesn’t meet your needs anymore, dental implants can get the job done quite effectively. Of course, our team will need to first evaluate your smile and confirm your needs before we can begin planning your procedure.

Who is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Generally, if you suffer from tooth loss and have good oral health, you are a candidate for the treatment. However, to ensure its long-term success, your dentist will need to go over your medical and lifestyle history to eliminate potential factors that may hinder your body’s ability to heal or fight infections. In addition, you must be committed to practicing good oral hygiene at home while also maintaining your regular dental appointments.

Once we’ve ensured you’re a good candidate for dental implants, we’ll help you get started in the early planning stages of your procedure.

Missing One Tooth

A single implant post is placed into the jawbone to support the dental crown. Over the next several months, it will begin to integrate with your bone tissue to create a lasting bond. Once an abutment and ceramic crown are placed on top, your replacement tooth will blend in seamlessly with your remaining teeth for a flawless smile. No one but you and our office will even know you had the procedure completed (that is, unless you tell them!)

Missing Multiple Teeth

As an alternative to a traditional dental bridge, several missing teeth can be replaced with dental implants. To replace a few consecutive missing teeth in a row, two dental implant posts can support a bridge to fill the gap. Keep in mind that unlike traditional bridges, implant bridges do not require the removal of existing tooth enamel just to keep them stable. This means you can maintain more of your teeth’s natural structure throughout the tooth replacement process.

Missing All Teeth

To replace several nonconsecutive teeth, dental implant posts can be used to anchor a partial denture in place. Additionally, when complete tooth loss has occurred on an arch, they can be replaced with the use of dental implants. A gum-colored base is made to feature the replacement teeth. Instead of using suction or an adhesive to hold the arch in place, an average of four to six dental implant posts are used for maximum stability.

Our office is also happy to offer the All-on-4 procedure, a treatment that enables you to restore an entire arch of teeth using just four dental implants. These posts are strategically placed throughout the jaw in order to provide lasting stability for the denture. Because of the nature of this procedure, you can actually receive your new restoration the same day the implants are placed.

Understanding the Cost of Dental Implants

The Price of Dental Implant Posts

The vast majority of the price of your implant tooth replacement plan is the cost of the dental implant itself. An individual dental implant post can cost $500 to $3000 or more in some cases. Luckily, most manufacturers offer discounts on additional implant posts, and when your implants are placed on the same day, you are less likely to have additional surgical costs.

Factors That Affect the Cost of Dental Implants

In addition to the cost of dental implants and their surgical placement, the following factors can also potentially impact the price of your tooth replacement plan:

  • Need for preparatory services like tooth extraction or tissue grafts
  • The number and location of missing teeth
  • The use of dental sedation to ensure comfort
  • The type of dental restoration needed (dental crown, bridge, partial, or full dentures)

Dental Insurance & Implant Tooth Replacement

Unfortunately, most dental insurance providers do not yet cover the cost of dental implants, but the other parts of your dental implant plan may receive some coverage. Our team will carefully review your policy and help you to maximize any available benefits to offset the costs of your tooth replacement plan.

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

After any oral surgery, including dental implant placement, a proper healing period is essential. At Advanced Dental Treatment Center, our team is dedicated to guiding you through every step of the process. Right after your procedure, you’ll receive clear, easy-to-follow post-op instructions designed to support a smooth and comfortable recovery. Below, you’ll find a few general tips to give you a better picture of what the healing process may look like.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Getting your dental implant placed can be one of the most demanding steps in the whole treatment, but with good aftercare and smart habits, your healing can be smooth and quick. In the early days, one of your top priorities should be protecting the blood clots that develop over the surgical areas these act like a natural bandage, allowing your gums to recover and bond with the implant(s).

For proper healing, make sure to refrain from:

  • Spitting (swallow your saliva or use a tissue instead)
  • Sipping through straws
  • Smoking or using tobacco products
  • Poking or touching the area with your tongue or fingers

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

If oral surgery is new to you, it can be tricky to tell which side effects are normal and which ones are not. It’s fairly typical to notice light, occasional bleeding, puffiness in the area, and some soreness these are all part of your body’s normal recovery response and should fade as the days go by. But if your symptoms linger or grow more intense, reach out to us right away for guidance.

While you heal, here are a few simple ways to ease your recovery:

  • For bleeding, place a clean piece of gauze over the spot and apply steady, gentle pressure.
  • To help with swelling, press a cold pack against the outside of your cheek on the affected side.
  • Take prescribed or over-the-counter pain relief to help manage discomfort.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

Right after your surgery, it’s important to stick to gentle, easy-to-eat foods. Within a few days, you can gradually reintroduce firmer foods just be careful not to chew on the area where the implant was placed. To make those first few days easier, consider keeping these soft, nourishing options on hand:

  • Applesauce
  • Pudding
  • Warm broth or soup (not hot)
  • Mashed potatoes
  • Smooth yogurt
  • Pasta
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Smoothies or protein shakes
  • Ice cream

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Maintaining excellent oral hygiene after surgery is essential for helping your dental implants fuse properly with your jawbone. Keep these tips in mind:

  • You can resume brushing your teeth the day after surgery, but be gentle and mindful around the area where the procedure was performed.
  • Rinse your mouth with a warm saltwater solution two to three times daily, ideally after meals. If recommended, a prescription mouthwash works just as well.
  • Steer clear of any mouthwashes that contain alcohol, as they can irritate the healing tissues.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

After your implants have fully integrated with your jawbone and your mouth has recovered, the most challenging part is behind you now it’s just a matter of placing your restoration(s)! You might feel some slight tenderness in your gums, but over-the-counter pain relief usually keeps it comfortable. Reach out to us right away if you notice any unusual swelling or bleeding after this stage, as these are not common reactions and could signal an issue that needs attention.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants can last for more than three decades or even the rest of your life. However, it’s crucial that you take all of the proper steps to care for them. If you aren’t taking the time to protect your dental implants from becoming damaged, they could end up failing and needing to be replaced with a new implant or another tooth replacement option. Fortunately, with a few simple precautions, you can keep your implants healthy and enjoy their benefits for numerous years down the road.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Dental implants aren’t able to get cavities themselves, but you still need to practice excellent oral hygiene. In addition to keeping your surrounding teeth in good shape, sticking to a great dental hygiene routine can prevent other dental problems, like gum disease, that can cause your implants to fail. Remember to brush, floss, and rinse with mouthwash each day.

Eat a Healthy Diet

One of the many benefits of dental implants is that you won’t need to deal with any eating restrictions. You can easily enjoy a nutritious meal without struggling to chew. Just remember that having too many items that are hard, sticky, or sugary can be problematic. Eat foods that have plenty of calcium and vitamin C to keep your jawbone and gums in good shape.

Break Bad Habits

Bad habits can cause your dental implants to fail over time. Refrain from smoking or using tobacco products immediately after your procedure, as they can slow down healing and increase your risk of developing an infection. Don’t use your teeth as tools either, as you could accidentally end up damaging your dental implant or a surrounding tooth. Instead, find scissors, a bottle opener, or another tool that can help you complete your task safely.

Protect Your Dental Implants

As strong as your dental implants are, they aren’t indestructible. You should always wear a mouthguard to protect your dental implants and natural teeth when participating in sports. If you grind your teeth at night, ask about a nightguard to protect your implants from additional wear and tear.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

You should always attend your regular cleanings and checkups every six months. This way, we can monitor your dental implants and spot any issues as early as possible. By catching potential problems now, you can prevent them from becoming more serious and leading to additional consequences, like a dental implant failure.

Do I need to have a tooth extraction or bone graft to get a dental implant?

When you visit Dr. Farr for your consultation, he’ll take images of your teeth and facial structure to determine whether you need any preparatory procedures. These can include:

Because dental implants need to naturally fuse with your jawbone to be successful, it’s important that your oral health is in good shape before the procedure begins. That means taking care of any possible risk factors, allowing you to enjoy your new smile for decades to come.

  • Gum disease therapy
  • Sinus lifts
  • Bone grafts
  • Tooth extractions

If my teeth have been missing for a while, can I still get dental implants?

If you’ve been missing your teeth for a while but still want to get dental implants, there’s no need to fret. The roots of your teeth are connected to your jawbone and provide it with stimulation throughout your life. When they fall out or get knocked out, your bone is no longer going to be getting the proper blood flow to it, causing it to slowly deteriorate.

Dental implants require proper bone mass to successfully integrate with the jawbone. However, the situation is usually nothing that a bone graft can’t handle. So, even if you don’t have a dense enough jaw at the moment, you can still be a good candidate for implants.

Do I need a consultation to get dental implants?

Consultations are required to get the implant process moving. They play a vital role in reducing any risk of complications or implant failure and allow Dr. Farr to strategically plan the treatment for quality and long-lasting results.
