How Does the All-on-4 Procedure Work?

Step 1: Consultation and Evaluation

Dr. Rivera evaluates your teeth, gums, bite, medical history, and treatment goals. If teeth remain, their condition and long-term prognosis are also considered.

Step 2: 3D Implant Planning

Accurate planning is essential for full-arch implant treatment. A CBCT Scanner can provide detailed three-dimensional information about the jawbone and surrounding anatomy to help plan potential implant positions.

Step 3: Preparing Your Mouth

Teeth that cannot be predictably saved may need to be removed before or during implant treatment. When necessary, Tooth Extractions become part of the overall treatment plan.

Step 4: Implant Placement

Dental implants are strategically placed in the jaw to support the planned full-arch restoration. The exact number, position, and angle depend on the patient&#x27;s anatomy and treatment plan.

Step 5: Temporary Teeth When Appropriate

Some patients may qualify for a provisional set of teeth during the healing period. Immediate temporary teeth cannot be guaranteed because sufficient implant stability and other clinical conditions are required.

Step 6: Healing and Final Teeth

The implants need time to integrate with the surrounding bone. Once healing has progressed appropriately, the final restoration can be designed and placed.

Do All-on-4 Dental Implants Require Bone Grafting?

Strategic implant positioning can sometimes make full-arch treatment possible without extensive grafting. However, All-on-4 does not eliminate the need for bone grafting in every patient.

If bone volume or anatomy is inadequate, Bone Grafting may be considered as part of the treatment plan. Imaging and a clinical evaluation are needed to determine what is appropriate.

How Long Does All-on-4 Treatment Take?

The timeline varies from patient to patient.

Treatment generally includes consultation and planning, implant placement, healing, and delivery of the final restoration. Patients requiring extractions, grafting, or other procedures may have a different timeline.

Healing is especially important because the implants need time to integrate with the jawbone before they can predictably support the final restoration.

Are All-on-4 Dental Implants Painful?

Local anesthesia is used during implant placement to help keep treatment comfortable. Temporary swelling, soreness, and tenderness can occur afterward as the tissues heal.

All-on-4 vs. Traditional Dentures

Traditional dentures are removable and receive their primary support from the gums and underlying tissues. They can be an effective and more affordable option for many patients.

All-on-4 uses dental implants to support a full arch of replacement teeth, offering greater stability and a different chewing experience. However, implant treatment requires surgery, sufficient supporting anatomy, ongoing maintenance, and typically a greater initial investment.

Patients considering removable tooth replacement can learn more about Full and Partial Dentures .

All-on-4 vs. Implant-Supported Dentures

Not every implant-supported full-arch restoration works the same way.

Some implant-supported dentures are designed to be removable by the patient, while fixed full-arch restorations remain attached and are typically removed only by a dental professional when necessary.

Implant-Supported Dentures may be an alternative depending on your anatomy, budget, preferences, and treatment goals.

Risks and Limitations of All-on-4

Like any surgical dental procedure, All-on-4 has potential risks and limitations.

These may include infection, swelling, discomfort, implant failure, bone or gum complications, and damage or wear to the prosthetic teeth. Implants and full-arch restorations also require ongoing professional and at-home maintenance.

Smoking, certain medical conditions, oral hygiene, bone quality, and other factors can influence healing and long-term outcomes.

Who May Not Be a Good Candidate?

All-on-4 may not be appropriate for patients with uncontrolled oral disease, certain uncontrolled medical conditions, unsuitable anatomy, or an inability to maintain the restoration properly.

Some patients may also have enough healthy teeth remaining that preserving them is preferable to full-arch replacement.

Dr. Rivera will evaluate your individual situation before recommending treatment.

Caring for All-on-4 Dental Implants

All-on-4 teeth still require daily care. Although replacement teeth cannot develop cavities, plaque and bacteria can accumulate around the implants and surrounding gums.

The restoration itself can also wear or require repair over time.

  • Daily brushing
  • Cleaning underneath the restoration
  • Using recommended floss or interdental cleaning tools
  • Regular dental examinations
  • Professional maintenance
  • Monitoring the implants and surrounding tissues
