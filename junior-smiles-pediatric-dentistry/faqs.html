GENERAL TOPICS

How often should my children get a dental cleaning?

Every child is unique. At Junior Smiles, we evaluate each patient’s oral health and well-being to create a personalized cleaning schedule. We recommend cleanings as often as every six weeks, and no less than twice a year.

When should my child see the dentist for the first time?

The American Academy of Dentistry recommends that children see the dentist before their first birthday or when their first tooth comes in — whichever comes first. If you notice any pain or discomfort in your child’s mouth, don’t wait — bring them to Junior Smiles!

What does a pediatric dentist do?

Pediatric dentists complete two to three additional years of training beyond dental school, focusing on oral health from infancy through adolescence. They tailor behavior guidance and care to each developmental stage to help prevent future dental issues. At Junior Smiles , Dr. Popper provides this specialized care with a gentle, child-centered approach.

Children’s laser dentistry

Laser dentistry enables dental professionals to treat cavities, canker sores, fever blisters, and perform frenectomies more gently, all without the use of needles, drills, or noise. Dr. Popper utilizes this advanced technology at Junior Smiles to help children feel more at ease during their treatment.

Why are primary teeth important?

Primary (“baby”) teeth play a critical role in chewing, guiding the eruption of permanent teeth, promoting jaw growth, supporting speech development, and influencing facial appearance. They usually last until about age 6–7 for front teeth and 10–13 for back teeth. Early dental care is encouraged by age one.

Eruption of your child’s teeth

Baby teeth begin to emerge around 4 months; all 20 typically appear by the age of 3. Permanent teeth start around age six and continue until roughly age 21, including wisdom teeth.

Dental emergencies

If your child experiences a dental emergency, Junior Smiles is here to help. Whether it’s a toothache, a chipped tooth, or an accident involving facial injury, call us right away for prompt guidance. For knocked-out permanent teeth, act quickly—time is critical.

Dental radiographs (X‑rays)

X‑rays reveal issues undetectable by clinical exam: erupting teeth, injuries, or bone disease. Most pediatric dentists recommend annual X‑rays, and high‑risk children may need them every six months. Junior Smiles utilizes digital X-rays to minimize radiation exposure.

What’s the best toothpaste for my child?

Choose a toothpaste with the American Dental Association seal. Use only a very small amount (or none) for young children who can’t spit to avoid excess fluoride ingestion and fluorosis.

Thumb sucking

Normal in infants, but if it persists beyond the age when permanent teeth emerge, it may impact alignment. Dr. Popper can help evaluate habits like thumb sucking and offer gentle guidance or intervention if needed.

EARLY INFANT ORAL CARE

Perinatal & infant oral health

Pregnant women with untreated oral disease may pass cavity-causing bacteria to infants. To prevent this, Dr. Popper encourages expectant mothers to maintain regular dental visits, use fluoridated toothpaste, avoid sharing utensils with babies, and consider xylitol gum. At Junior Smiles , we believe prevention starts before the first tooth erupts.

Establishing a dental home

Dental professionals recommend establishing a “dental home” by age one. At Junior Smiles , we strive to create a warm and welcoming space where your child becomes familiar with the dentist from an early age, helping to reduce fear and encouraging lifelong oral health habits.

When will my baby start getting teeth?

Teething typically begins between 6-8 months (with the lower front teeth first) and continues until around age 3. Dr. Popper can guide you through what to expect during teething and provide effective ways to soothe discomfort.

Baby bottle tooth decay (early childhood caries)

Prolonged exposure to sugary liquids in bottles—especially at bedtime—can lead to decay. Always wipe your baby’s gums after feeding and avoid letting them sleep with a bottle unless it’s filled with water.

Sippy cups

While convenient, sippy cups should be used only temporarily—ideally until the child’s first birthday. At Junior Smiles, we recommend limiting sugary drinks and encouraging water between meals.

PREVENTION

Good diet = healthy teeth

A balanced diet supports dental and overall health. Encourage crunchy fruits, vegetables, and dairy over sticky, sugary snacks. Dr. Popper often shares practical tips with families to help make smart food choices a part of their daily routines.

How do I prevent cavities?

Brush at least twice daily, clean infants’ gums with gauze, and avoid sugary bedtime bottles. Schedule regular check-ups with a pediatric dentist, such as Dr. Popper, who can monitor your child’s development and identify early signs of decay.

Seal out decay

Sealants are a great option to protect your child’s molars from cavities. At Junior Smiles, we offer this preventive treatment to keep chewing surfaces smooth and easier to clean.

Mouth guards

If your child plays sports, a properly fitted mouth guard can help prevent dental injuries. Junior Smiles offers custom-fit options to ensure safety and comfort.

Xylitol – reducing cavities

Xylitol—found in some gums and mints—can significantly reduce cavity-causing bacteria. Dr. Popper may suggest xylitol-based products as part of a preventive plan, especially for higher-risk children.

Beware of sports drinks

These drinks are often acidic and high in sugar. Encourage water instead, especially during games and practice. If your child consumes sports drinks, have them rinse their mouth with water afterward and avoid sipping them over extended periods.
