How Are Dental Implants Placed?

Our dental implant process begins with a consultation, including digital X-rays and 3D scans to assess your oral and jawbone health. If needed, preparatory procedures like tooth extraction or bone grafting are performed to ensure your implants have a strong foundation.

During implant placement, Dr. Salim uses X-Guide™ Dynamic 3D Navigation, an advanced technology that provides real-time guidance for precise implant positioning.

After placement, the implant fuses with your jawbone over several months in a process called osseointegration.

Once healing is complete, a small connector piece called an abutment is attached to the implant. The abutment sits just above the gum line and serves as the link between the implant and the final restoration.

Finally, a custom crown, bridge, or denture is attached to complete your new smile.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

With consistent care, dental implants can remain functional for 20 years or longer. The titanium implant post is designed to provide long-term support within the jawbone, while the restoration attached above it may have a different lifespan.

Crowns, bridges, and implant-supported dentures experience daily pressure from biting and chewing, so they may wear or require replacement sooner than the implant post. Routine dental examinations allow the dental team to assess both components and identify changes in the restoration, implant area, or surrounding tissues.

What Are the Benefits of Dental Implants?

Dental implants replace missing teeth while supporting several aspects of oral function. Because the implant post is placed in the jawbone, it provides a stable foundation for a crown, bridge, or implant-supported denture. The benefits can vary according to the type of restoration and the number of teeth being replaced.

How Do You Care for Dental Implants?

Although dental implants cannot develop cavities, plaque can still collect around the restoration and gumline. Maintaining the surrounding gums and nearby natural teeth remains important after implant placement.

Your cleaning routine may vary according to the type of restoration attached to the implant. The Faircourt Dental team can demonstrate how to reach areas that may be difficult to clean and provide instructions suited to your implant restoration. Consistent home care and professional examinations help maintain comfortable function over time.

  • Brush your teeth twice a day, paying close attention to the gumline around the implant.
  • Floss daily to clear plaque and food particles between teeth and around the restoration.
  • Clean beneath an implant-supported bridge or denture using the method recommended by your dental team.
  • Use any additional cleaning tools suggested for your specific crown, bridge, or denture.
  • Attend routine dental appointments so the team can examine the restoration and surrounding tissues.

A Natural-Looking Tooth Replacement

Implant restorations are custom-made to complement the surrounding teeth and gumline. A crown may replace a single tooth, while a bridge or implant-supported denture can address several missing teeth. The finished restoration is designed to restore the visible portion of the smile without drawing attention to the missing tooth.

Greater Stability While Eating and Speaking

The implant post anchors the restoration within the jawbone. This stability can help patients chew and speak without the movement that may occur with a conventional removable denture. Implant-supported dentures are secured to four to six implants and may snap into place or remain attached, depending on their design.

Support for the Jawbone

Tooth roots stimulate the jawbone during chewing. After a tooth is lost, the bone in that area no longer receives the same stimulation. A dental implant acts as an artificial tooth root and helps maintain the bone surrounding the implant site. The amount of available bone will be assessed through digital X-rays and 3D scans before placement.

Options for Different Degrees of Tooth Loss

Dental implants can support several types of restorations. One implant and crown may replace a single missing tooth. An implant-supported bridge may replace several adjacent teeth, while implant-supported dentures may be considered when many or all teeth in an arch are missing. This flexibility allows the implant plan to reflect the patient’s tooth replacement needs.
