Why patients choose us for root canals

Every root canal here follows the same protocol, whether it is a simple front tooth or a molar that was already treated somewhere else.

GentleWave multisonic cleaning

Files can only clean the main canal. GentleWave uses sound waves and fluid flow to reach the fins, side canals, and tubules where bacteria hide, and it removes debris instead of packing it.

Every step under a surgical microscope

Extra canals, cracks, and missed anatomy are the most common reasons a root canal fails. Working at high magnification lets us find them on the first visit instead of the retreatment visit.

Rubber dam isolation, always

A rubber dam keeps saliva and bacteria out of the tooth while it is open. It is a basic standard of care that is still skipped in many offices.

Two visits with calcium hydroxide

Rather than filling the tooth the same day, we place an antibacterial medication inside the canals for 10 to 14 days. GentleWave then removes it completely before the tooth is sealed. This is our standard for infected teeth.

Permanent crown the same day the tooth is sealed

A root canal tooth without a crown is at high risk of fracture. We design and mill a porcelain crown in the office (CEREC), so you leave the second visit with a finished tooth, not a temporary.

One doctor, both options

Dr. Regina performs root canals, retreatments, extractions, and robotic implant placement. When a tooth is borderline, you get an honest recommendation on saving versus replacing it, from someone who does both.

What to expect: two visits, one doctor

3D scan and diagnosis

A cone beam CT scan maps the tooth&#x27;s roots, canals, and surrounding bone. Dr. Regina confirms the tooth can be saved and explains the plan and the fee before treatment begins.

Visit 1: clean, medicate, protect

The tooth is numbed with local anesthesia, isolated with a rubber dam, and opened under the microscope. Canals are shaped and cleaned, calcium hydroxide medication is placed inside, and a core buildup with a sealed temporary protects the tooth. Nitrous oxide, oral sedation, or IV sedation can be used when appropriate.

10 to 14 days: the medication works

Calcium hydroxide keeps working inside the canals between visits, killing remaining bacteria and neutralizing their toxins. Most patients feel steady improvement during this period.

Visit 2: GentleWave and seal

The GentleWave procedure removes the medication and any remaining debris from the entire canal system. Each canal is then sealed under the microscope with a permanent filling material.

Same visit: permanent crown

A porcelain crown is designed and milled in the office while you wait, then cemented. You leave with a finished tooth.

Questions patients ask about root canals

Why root canals fail, and what we do differently

Published success rates for root canal treatment range widely, from roughly 65 % to 98 % depending on the study, the tooth, and how success is measured. The most common reason for failure is not a bad filling. It is bacteria that survived the cleaning. The inside of a root is not a single tube; it is a branching system of side canals, fins, and microscopic tubules, and a landmark 2002 study (Hoen and Pink, Journal of Endodontics ) found that 42 % of failing root canal teeth had at least one untreated canal.

Conventional treatment shapes the main canals with metal files and rinses them with a syringe. Files cannot enter the smaller spaces, and syringe irrigation reaches only a few millimeters beyond the needle tip. Whatever bacteria remain can regrow and re-establish infection, often within a few years.

Where Fotona SWEEPS fits

Mechanical files shape the main canals so they can be cleaned and sealed. Root canal anatomy is more complex than those main pathways: fins, lateral canals, and microscopic irregularities sit outside the reach of files.

Fotona SWEEPS (Shock Wave Enhanced Emission Photoacoustic Streaming) is another irrigation-activation technology available at Cedar Park Dental Wellness. It uses an Er:YAG laser to send synchronized ultra-short pulses into the irrigating solution inside the tooth. Those pulses generate photoacoustic shock waves that help move the solution through complex anatomy, including lateral spaces files cannot enter.

GentleWave G4 remains the standard advanced irrigation technology in the two-visit workflow. Depending on the tooth, anatomy, and treatment needs, Dr. Regina may also incorporate Fotona SWEEPS as an additional irrigation-activation technique. SWEEPS is not part of every root canal treatment.

Why we use two visits

For infected teeth, we place calcium hydroxide inside the canals between visits. It is highly alkaline, kills bacteria that survive the first cleaning, and neutralizes bacterial toxins that drive inflammation. The historical drawback of this approach is that residual calcium hydroxide is difficult to remove before sealing and can interfere with the seal. GentleWave removes it far more completely than conventional irrigation, which is what makes the combination practical.

The microscope and the rubber dam

Magnification is how missed canals get found. A surgical operating microscope provides far greater magnification and lighting than loupes, and we use it for every step, from locating canals to verifying the seal. The rubber dam keeps the tooth isolated from saliva during treatment. Both are considered standard of care in endodontics, and both are still routinely skipped.

Root canal or implant? An honest answer

A tooth with a solid root, enough remaining structure, and healthy bone is usually worth saving. A tooth with a vertical root fracture, severe bone loss, or repeated failed treatment usually is not. Because Dr. Regina performs both root canals and Yomi robot-guided implant placement in the same practice, and sees the long-term outcomes of both, her recommendation for a borderline tooth is not shaped by which procedure the office happens to offer.

Retreatment

If a previous root canal is failing, retreatment is often possible. The old filling material is removed, the canal system is cleaned again with GentleWave, missed anatomy is located under the microscope, and the tooth is resealed and restored.
