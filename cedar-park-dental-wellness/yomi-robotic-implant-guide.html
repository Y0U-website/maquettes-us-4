The robot guides. Your dentist performs the surgery.

Yomi S is not autonomous. It does not decide where an implant goes, and it does not complete the surgery on its own.

Dr. Regina creates the implant plan from your exam and 3D imaging. During surgery she controls the instruments. Yomi S provides robotic navigational and haptic guidance so she can follow that plan more closely than she could by eyesight and freehand positioning alone.

She can still evaluate the site as she works. If the clinical situation calls for a change, clinical judgment comes first. The technology supports that judgment. It does not replace it.

When robotic guidance can matter

Guidance is a planning and placement aid. It does not make every implant case simpler, and it does not guarantee a particular healing timeline or result.

  • Limited space next to neighboring teeth
  • Implants planned close to nerves, the sinus, or other anatomy
  • Front-tooth implant position, where small changes affect how the crown meets the gumline
  • Several implants that will support one bridge or prosthesis
  • Full-arch treatment, where implant number and position are planned around the final teeth
  • Implant placement planned around the final crown, bridge, or denture rather than around a surgical shortcut

How Yomi S differs from freehand placement and a static guide

Dentists use more than one way to place implants. These are broad differences, not a ranking of safety.

  • Freehand The dentist positions instruments using the plan, clinical landmarks, and experience, without robotic physical guidance during drilling.
  • Static surgical guide A pre-made guide, usually from the digital plan, constrains the drills along a preplanned path. It does not track the patient in real time.
  • Yomi S A digital plan, real-time patient tracking, and physical haptic robotic guidance, with Dr. Regina operating the instruments.
