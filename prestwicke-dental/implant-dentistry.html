From consultation to final restoration

The sequence and timeline are personalized. Some patients move directly to implant placement, while others need treatment to prepare the gums, bone, or extraction site first.

Consultation

Discuss your missing teeth, health history, priorities, concerns, and possible treatment alternatives.

Imaging and Planning

Clinical examination and appropriate imaging help evaluate the teeth, gums, bone, bite, and anatomy.

Site Preparation

Extraction, socket preservation, bone grafting, or gum treatment may be recommended before placement.

Implant Placement and Healing

The implant is placed and allowed time to integrate with the surrounding bone before final restoration.

Final Restoration

A custom crown, bridge, or denture is designed, delivered, checked, and maintained over time.

3D and digital implant planning Three-dimensional imaging can help evaluate bone dimensions, nearby teeth, sinus spaces, and other anatomy that may influence implant position. Digital planning supports diagnosis and communication, but it does not replace clinical judgment or guarantee a particular result.

When bone grafting may be needed Tooth loss, infection, anatomy, or prior extractions can leave insufficient bone for the preferred implant position. Grafting may help rebuild or preserve the site and may be completed before or at the time of implant placement, depending on the individual situation.

Healing and treatment timeline Integration between the implant and jawbone commonly requires several months. The complete timeline may be shorter or longer depending on extraction healing, grafting, implant stability, medical risk factors, and the type of temporary and final restoration being made.

Replacing one tooth versus multiple teeth

Implant treatment can be adapted to different patterns of tooth loss. The restoration—not simply the number of implants—must be planned around function, hygiene, anatomy, and long-term maintainability.

Implant-Supported Crown

A single implant can support an individual crown without relying on neighboring teeth for support. The final recommendation depends on bone, spacing, bite, and overall health.

Multiple Implants or an Implant Bridge

Several missing teeth may be replaced with individual implants or with a bridge supported by fewer implants. Location, available bone, hygiene access, and treatment goals guide the design.

Implant-Supported Full-Arch Care

Implants may stabilize a removable denture or support a fixed full-arch restoration. The number and position of implants must be planned for the individual patient.

Dental implant vs. dental bridge

Dental implant

  • Supported by the jawbone
  • Usually avoids preparing adjacent teeth solely for support
  • Requires surgery, healing, and implant-specific maintenance

Traditional bridge

  • Supported by neighboring teeth
  • May be useful when adjacent teeth already need crowns
  • Has different hygiene, support, and replacement considerations

Implant-supported vs. removable denture

Implant-supported

  • May improve stability and confidence during function
  • Requires implant surgery and healing
  • Needs careful cleaning around implant components

Conventional removable

Implant maintenance matters An implant cannot develop a cavity, but the surrounding gum and bone can become inflamed or diseased. Implant crowns, screws, attachments, and dentures can also wear or require repair.

Who may be a candidate for dental implants?

Many adults with missing teeth may be considered for implants. Candidacy depends on oral health, available bone, healing capacity, medications, tobacco use, medical history, and the ability to maintain the implant. A clinical examination and appropriate imaging are required before recommending treatment.

How long does the dental implant process take?

The timeline varies by case. After implant placement, integration with the jawbone commonly takes several months. Extraction healing, bone grafting, the number of implants, and the type of final restoration can change the overall schedule.

Will I need a bone graft before an implant?

Not every patient needs grafting. It may be recommended when available bone is insufficient in height, width, density, or contour. Depending on the site, grafting may be completed before implant placement or during the same procedure.

Is an implant better than a dental bridge?

Neither option is best for every patient. An implant can replace a tooth without using neighboring teeth for support, but it requires surgery and healing. A bridge may be appropriate when the adjacent teeth already need crowns or when implant treatment is not preferred or advisable.

Does dental insurance cover implants?

Coverage varies significantly by plan. Benefits may apply to some diagnostic, surgical, grafting, or restorative steps while other portions remain the patient&#x27;s responsibility. Our team can help review available benefit information, but the insurance company makes the final coverage determination.

How do I care for a dental implant?

Implants require consistent brushing, cleaning between and around the restoration, and professional examinations and maintenance. The recommended tools and visit interval depend on the implant design, gum health, dexterity, and individual risk factors.
