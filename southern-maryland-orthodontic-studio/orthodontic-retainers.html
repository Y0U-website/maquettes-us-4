Fixed Retainers (Permanent)

A fixed retainer is a thin wire bonded to the back of the teeth, most commonly on the lower front teeth (and sometimes the upper front teeth too). Why Dr. Hagan may recommend fixed retention:

A fixed retainer does require patients to practice proper oral hygiene, and be examined at regular dental cleanings.

  • Patient has high relapse risk. If the lower front teeth were crowded or rotated, fixed are often the best option.
  • Less compliance required. Patients don’t have to remember to wear it.
  • Long-term stability. Retainer acts as safety net, especially for the teeth prone to shifting long after treatment.

Caring For Retainers

For removable retainers, keep them in a case when not in use. No case leads to lost retainers.

Very hot water, dishwashers, car dashboards, and other warm areas can warp clear retainers.

Brush and floss regularly, as retainers work best when gums and teeth healthy. For fixed retainers, floss behind the wire or use a water flosser.

Orthodontic Retainers FAQ

What happens if I don’t wear my retainer?

Teeth drift. Sometimes slowly, sometimes faster than patients expect. The first few months after treatment are the highest-risk window, but shifting can happen years later too. If a retainer stops fitting, that’s usually a sign teeth already moved. In that case, call our office. If we catch it early, patients can often get back on track without restarting full treatment.

How do I get a replacement retainer?

Call our office and tell us what happened (lost, cracked, stopped fitting, fixed retainer feels loose). We’ll schedule a quick visit to evaluate and take a scan or impression if needed. Timing matters, so don’t wait. The longer a patient goes without a retainer, the more likely teeth will shift and the harder it is to make a replacement that fits comfortably.

How long do I have to wear retainers?

The general protocol is nightly forever. While the bone stabilizes after about a year, mild physiological drift happens to everyone as they age. Wearing retainers while sleeping is the only certain way to prevent this natural movement.
