What to Expect During the Dental Implant Process

The dental implant process usually requires multiple appointments. Every patient’s timeline is different, but treatment commonly includes consultation, planning, implant placement, healing, and restoration.

1. Consultation and Treatment Planning

Your dentist will evaluate your mouth, review your health history, and discuss your goals. This step helps determine whether you are a candidate for dental implants and whether a single implant, implant-supported denture, bridge, or another treatment may be appropriate.

2. Implant Placement

Once treatment begins, the implant is placed into the jawbone. The implant acts as an artificial tooth root and creates the foundation for the final restoration.

3. Healing and Osseointegration

After placement, the implant needs time to heal and fuse with the jawbone. This healing phase is important because it allows the implant to become stable enough to support a restoration.

4. Bone Support, If Needed

Some patients do not have enough healthy jawbone to support an implant right away. If additional bone support is needed, Midtown Smiles may recommend bone grafting before or during the implant process.

5. Abutment and Final Restoration

After healing, an abutment is placed on the implant. A custom crown, bridge, or denture is then designed to fit your bite and blend with your smile.

For patients who need added denture stability, implant-supported dentures may be recommended as an alternative to traditional removable dentures.

Dental Implants vs. Dentures in Nashville

Many patients search for the best choice between implants or dentures in Nashville, TN. The right option depends on your oral health, bone support, budget, timeline, and personal preferences.

Dental implants are fixed or implant-retained solutions that can provide added stability and support. Traditional dentures are removable appliances that replace multiple missing teeth and may be a better fit for patients who are not ready for implant treatment or who prefer a non-surgical option.

Patients considering removable tooth replacement can also review full and partial dentures as part of their treatment options.

  • Dental implants may offer greater stability, but they require surgery and healing time.
  • Dentures are usually completed faster, but they may feel less secure for some patients.
  • Implant-supported dentures may combine the coverage of dentures with added implant stability.
  • Some patients need bone grafting before implants can be placed.
  • Cost varies based on the number of missing teeth, imaging, restoration type, and additional procedures.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

Dental implants are designed to be a long-term tooth replacement option. With proper care, routine dental visits, and healthy gums, implants can last for many years. However, longevity varies by patient and depends on factors such as oral hygiene, bite pressure, bone health, tobacco use, medical conditions, and regular maintenance.

The crown, bridge, or denture attached to the implant may need repair or replacement over time due to normal wear.

FAQs About Dental Implants in Nashville

How do I know if dental implants are right for me?

Dental implants may be an option if you have missing teeth, healthy gums, and enough jawbone to support the implant. Some patients need gum treatment, bone grafting, or extractions first. A consultation at Midtown Smiles helps determine whether implants, dentures, bridges, or another solution is appropriate.

How long does the dental implant process take?

The dental implant process often takes several months because the implant needs time to heal and bond with the jawbone before the final restoration is placed. Your exact timeline depends on bone health, healing, the number of implants, and whether extractions or bone grafting are needed.

Are dental implants better than dentures?

Dental implants and dentures serve different needs. Implants can provide added stability and jawbone support, while dentures may be a faster or more affordable removable option for some patients. The right choice depends on your oral health, budget, treatment goals, and whether you are comfortable with surgery.
