FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is All-on-X the same as dentures? No. Traditional dentures generally rest on the gums and are removable. An All-on-X restoration is supported by dental implants and is designed to provide greater stability. Each option has different clinical, maintenance, and financial considerations.

Can I receive temporary teeth on the day of implant placement? Some qualifying patients may receive a temporary restoration during the surgical phase. Immediate temporary teeth are not appropriate for every case. Eligibility can only be determined after examination and diagnostic planning.

Can All-on-X be completed if I have bone loss? Possibly. Bone loss does not automatically exclude a patient, but it can affect implant positioning and the need for additional procedures. The dentist must evaluate your anatomy before making a recommendation.

Will I need bone grafting? Not every patient requires bone grafting. The need depends on the amount, quality, and location of the available bone and the proposed implant positions.

Is the procedure painful? Comfort measures and appropriate anesthesia are used during treatment. Temporary soreness, swelling, or bruising may occur afterward. Your dentist will provide personalized postoperative instructions and medication recommendations when appropriate.

How long does recovery take? Initial recovery and implant integration are different stages. Many patients experience improvement in initial discomfort over the first several days, while biological integration of the implants generally takes longer. Your personal timeline will depend on the procedures performed and your healing response.

What foods can I eat during recovery? A soft-food diet is commonly recommended during healing. The clinical team will provide instructions based on your treatment and the stability of your temporary restoration.

How do I clean All-on-X teeth? Daily cleaning remains essential. Patients may need specialized floss, interdental brushes, a water flosser, or other tools recommended by the dental team. Professional implant maintenance should also be completed at the intervals prescribed by the dentist.

How long can All-on-X dental implants last? Implant longevity depends on oral hygiene, professional maintenance, bite forces, smoking, general health, and other clinical factors. The prosthetic teeth may also require maintenance, adjustment, repair, or replacement over time. No restoration should be presented as permanent or guaranteed for life.

Does dental insurance cover All-on-X? Coverage varies by insurance plan. Some plans may contribute toward certain diagnostic, surgical, or restorative procedures, while others may provide limited or no implant coverage. Our team can help verify available benefits, but coverage is determined by the insurance carrier.

Do you accept Medicaid or HMO dental plans? No. Q Smiles Dentistry does not accept Medicaid or HMO dental plans. We accept select PPO networks, self-pay patients, and financing applications, subject to verification and approval.
