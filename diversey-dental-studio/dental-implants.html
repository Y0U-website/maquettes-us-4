What Dental Implants Are and Who They’re For

Dental implants are long-term tooth replacement solutions designed to replace both the visible part of a tooth and the root beneath it. A small biocompatible post is placed in the jawbone, where it can integrate with the surrounding bone over time. Once healed, the implant can support a custom restoration, such as a crown, bridge, or denture.

Because dental implants are anchored in the jaw, they offer excellent stability for eating, speaking, and smiling. They can also help support the health of the jawbone by providing stimulation in the area where a natural tooth root once was.

Dental implants may be right for patients who are missing one or more teeth, have loose or uncomfortable dentures, need a tooth extracted and replaced, or want a more stable alternative to traditional tooth replacement options. The best way to know if implants are right for you is to schedule a consultation with our Lincoln Park dental team.

Differentiator Heading

Single-Tooth Dental Implants

A single-tooth implant is used to replace one missing tooth. The implant post acts like an artificial tooth root, and a custom dental crown is attached once the area has healed. This option can restore the look and function of the missing tooth without relying on the surrounding teeth for support.

Implant-Supported Bridges

If you are missing multiple teeth in a row, an implant-supported bridge may be recommended. Instead of placing an implant for every missing tooth, strategically placed implants can support a custom bridge. This can provide strong, stable function while restoring a fuller smile.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Implant-supported dentures are designed to provide more stability than traditional removable dentures. By attaching a denture to dental implants, patients can experience a more secure fit, improved chewing ability, and greater confidence throughout the day.

All-on-4 Dental Implants

All-on-4 is a full-arch implant option for patients who need to replace an entire upper or lower set of teeth. This treatment uses a small number of strategically placed implants to support a full arch of replacement teeth. For the right candidate, All-on-4 can be a life-changing option for restoring comfort, function, and confidence with a more stable alternative to traditional dentures.

The Benefits of Dental Implants

Stable, Comfortable Function

Because implants are supported by the jawbone, they can feel more like natural teeth when you bite and chew. Many patients appreciate that they do not have to rely on removable appliances or adhesives for everyday comfort.

A Natural-Looking Smile

Your implant restoration is designed around your smile, bite, and facial features. Whether you need a single crown or a full-arch solution, the goal is to create a result that feels balanced, healthy, and true to you.

Support for Long-Term Oral Health

When a tooth is missing, the jawbone in that area can begin to change over time. Dental implants help stimulate the bone, supporting a healthier foundation for your smile.

Dental Implant Treatment Process

Consultation and Digital Planning

Your first visit begins with a conversation about your goals, concerns, and oral health history. We will evaluate your teeth, gums, bite, and jawbone, and may use digital scans or CBCT imaging to better understand your anatomy. This helps us plan your treatment with accuracy and explain your options in a way that feels clear.

Preparatory Treatment, If Needed

Some patients are ready for implant placement right away. Others may need a tooth extraction, bone graft, sinus lift, or periodontal care before moving forward. If any additional steps are recommended, we will explain why they matter and how they fit into your overall treatment plan.

Implant Placement

Once your smile is ready, the implant post is placed in the jawbone. Our team will make sure you are comfortable throughout the appointment and will provide detailed aftercare instructions to support healing.

Healing and Integration

After placement, the implant needs time to integrate with the jawbone. This healing phase is an important part of creating a strong foundation for your final restoration. We will monitor your progress and let you know when you are ready for the next step.

Custom Restoration

Once the implant has healed, we will design and place your final restoration. Depending on your treatment plan, this may be a crown, bridge, denture, or full-arch restoration. Your restoration will be crafted to support your bite, complement your smile, and help you feel confident again.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do dental implants cost?

The cost of dental implants depends on several factors, including how many teeth are being replaced, whether preparatory treatment is needed, and what type of restoration is recommended. At your consultation, we can provide a personalized treatment plan and walk you through your options.

What is the difference between dental implants and dentures?

Traditional dentures rest on the gums and are removable. Dental implants are placed in the jawbone and can support crowns, bridges, or dentures with added stability. For patients who struggle with loose dentures or want a more secure replacement option, implant-supported dentures or All-on-4 may be worth exploring.

Can dental implants replace all of my teeth?

Yes, dental implants can be used to replace a full arch of missing teeth. Options like All-on-4 use strategically placed implants to support a full set of replacement teeth, creating a more stable and natural-feeling alternative to traditional dentures.

How do I know if I need bone grafting before dental implants?

Bone grafting may be recommended if the jawbone is not strong or dense enough to support an implant. During your consultation, we can use advanced imaging to evaluate your bone structure and determine whether grafting or another preparatory procedure is needed.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper placement, good oral hygiene, and regular dental checkups, dental implants can last for many years. The implant post is designed to be a long-term replacement for the tooth root, while the crown, bridge, or denture attached to it may eventually need to be repaired or replaced due to normal wear.

Are dental implants painful?

Your comfort is always a priority. Implant placement is performed with local anesthesia, and our team will make sure you feel as comfortable as possible throughout the appointment. Some soreness or swelling is normal after the procedure, but most patients are able to manage recovery with detailed aftercare instructions.

Can I get dental implants if I have bone loss?

Possibly. Even if you have been told in the past that you do not have enough bone for dental implants, you may still have options. With advanced imaging and careful treatment planning, our team can evaluate whether a preparatory procedure, such as bone grafting or a sinus lift, may help create the support needed for implant placement.

Who is a good candidate for dental implants?

You may be a good candidate for dental implants if you are missing one or more teeth and want a stable, natural-looking replacement option. Ideal candidates typically have healthy gums, enough jawbone to support an implant, and a commitment to maintaining their oral health with regular dental visits and at-home care.
