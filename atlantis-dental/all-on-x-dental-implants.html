When All-on-X May Be Considered

All-on-X is intended for patients who are missing most or all teeth in an arch, have extensive failing teeth, or struggle with unstable dentures. It is not usually the treatment for one or two missing teeth.

Failing Upper Arch

Replace an extensively damaged upper dentition with a complete implant-supported restoration.

Failing Lower Arch

Rebuild a lower arch when many teeth cannot be predictably restored.

Transition from Dentures

Explore a more stable implant-supported option when conventional dentures move or hurt.

All-on-X Dental Implant Questions

What does All-on-X replace?

It supports a complete upper or lower set of teeth using strategically planned dental implants.

Can I receive teeth the same day?

Some clinically appropriate patients may receive a temporary restoration the day implants are placed. This cannot be confirmed until evaluation and planning.

Is All-on-X removable?

The treatment is generally designed as a fixed restoration removed by the dental team for maintenance when necessary. Your recommended design will be explained.

What if I have bone loss?

Bone availability is assessed with clinical evaluation and imaging. Different implant positions or supporting procedures may be considered.

How do I clean full-arch implants?

Daily cleaning under and around the restoration plus professional maintenance visits are essential. The team provides individualized instructions.

How much does All-on-X cost?

Investment varies by arch, diagnostics, surgical needs, materials, temporary teeth, and final restoration. Financing options are available.
