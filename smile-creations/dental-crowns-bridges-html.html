Dental Implant Crowns & Bridges: An Overview

While a traditional crown can help rebuild a broken or decayed tooth, implant crowns are the only kind that can bring back a missing tooth. The difference is that an implant crown is designed to fit over a prosthetic root, while a regular crown has to be shaped so it can replace the missing portions of a damaged tooth. Implant crowns are typically made of 100 % dental porcelain, giving them an extremely lifelike appearance.

Bridges are used to replace multiple consecutive missing teeth, and to place a traditional one, dentists typically have to shave down the healthy teeth surrounding the missing ones to fit crowns that will support the prosthetic. However, implant bridges allow us to give a patient this restoration and preserve their remaining teeth because the prosthetic is held in place using implant crowns. This makes them ideal for patients who do not have enough healthy dental structure to support a normal bridge.

Dental Implant Crowns and Bridges FAQs

How Many Teeth Can an Implant-Supported Bridge Replace?

An implant-supported bridge replaces a minimum of two teeth. It uses one crown and one pontic (artificial tooth) to do so. If two dental implants are used, one on each end of the gap, an implant-supported bridge may be able to replace up to five consecutive missing teeth.

Do I Have to Clean Beneath an Implant-Supported Bridge?

It is important to keep the space beneath your pontics clean. A floss threader or a water flosser can help. When you visit us for regular checkups, we might remove your implant-supported bridge so we can thoroughly clean the area around your implants.

Is the Dental Implant Bridge Process Painful?

Comfortable treatment is a cornerstone of high-quality dentistry. We partner with local specialists who are careful to ensure that patients feel little to nothing during their implant placement surgery. A bit of swelling and soreness is normal after the procedure, but it is usually mild and does not last very long.

What If My Multiple Missing Teeth Are Not Consecutive?

Implant-supported bridges are intended to replace consecutive missing teeth. If you have missing teeth at various places throughout a dental arch, we will recommend a treatment based on your unique circumstances. You might benefit from a few single-tooth implants (implant crowns) or an implant-supported partial denture.

Who Is a Candidate for Implant-Supported Crowns and Bridges?

Most patients who have suffered a degree of tooth loss are candidates for either implant-supported crowns or an implant-supported bridge. Your dentist will carefully examine your oral structures and ask you some questions about your overall health before he determines whether dental implants are right for you. If you are not a candidate at this time, Dr. Ariel may be able to design a treatment plan that will make you a candidate in the future.
