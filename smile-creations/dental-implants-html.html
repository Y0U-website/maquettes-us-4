Single-Tooth Dental Implants

Thankfully, dental implants provide the perfect solution to this problem. They are not only the most stable and reliable tooth replacement solution available today, but also the most natural-looking. Dental Implant Crowns & Bridges

To replace a single tooth, we’ll use what is called an implant crown. This will consist of the implant itself, a custom-made, all-porcelain crown, and a metal connector called an abutment. The crown will attach directly to the implant using the abutment, and once this is done, it will be virtually indistinguishable from your other teeth. We can even use implant crowns to form a bridge to replace multiple missing teeth in a row.

Dental Implant Dentures

Removable dentures, especially lower ones, tend to slip and click at the most inopportune times. An implant denture is a type of overdenture that is supported and attached to dental implants. They stabilize the denture and stimulate new bone growth, thereby preventing the bone loss that can cause dentures to become ill-fitting over time. We offer several types of implant dentures to Massapequa Park and the surrounding communities, and we can accommodate a variety of needs with excellent solutions chosen based on lifestyle, oral health, and ultimate goals.

Overdentures

As their name indicates, overdentures are smaller and thinner than traditional ones. This makes them an ideal solution for patients whose jawbone is too thin or brittle to support regular implants. With them, now even more people can benefit from the most complete dental restoration available today. Even though they are smaller, they can still be used to support a denture with a much more secure hold than a traditional removable prosthetic.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

To put it succinctly, the primary thing you should do on the day of your implant surgery is rest. You may feel a little groggy if sedation was used, and it’s important that you don’t move around too much so that the surgery site has a chance to clot. This will protect the tissues as they heal and give your posts the best environment for osseointegration to take place.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

You can expect to feel a little soreness and experience some minor bleeding and swelling following dental implant surgery. Thankfully, these tend to be relatively mild and brief. You may be prescribed pain medication, but often, over-the-counter options are enough to help someone stay comfortable. You should avoid taking acetaminophen (Tylenol) as this can thin the blood and make bleeding worse.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

Before your dental implant surgery, it’s wise to stock up on soft food items that don’t require a lot of chewing. This will save you from having to run to the store whilst recovering! Some of the items we suggest include:

  • Oatmeal
  • Sugar-free yogurt
  • Shakes of all varieties (but don’t drink them with a straw)
  • Eggs
  • Pasta
  • Soup
  • Pudding

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Depending on how many dental implants you had placed at one time, you may be able to brush your teeth the evening following surgery just make sure to avoid the treatment area. But, if you received multiple extractions and posts, it may be easier to just rinse your mouth with saltwater initially. This needs to be done very gently, and you shouldn’t spit let the toothpaste or water fall from your mouth, letting gravity do most of the work. Actively spitting can potentially disrupt the clotting process, which can dramatically slow down your recovery.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

You want your dental implants in Massapequa to last a lifetime, and while our team will do everything we can to make that happen, ongoing day-to-day maintenance is up to you. Thankfully, this won’t involve some elaborate routine. In many cases, caring for your dental implants is just like looking after your natural teeth, and these recommendations will ensure your restorations endure for decades to come.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Yes, while dental implants might be state-of-the-art, the most powerful tool you have in your arsenal when it comes to maintaining them is your toothbrush (and floss). Brushing at least twice a day and flossing in the evening can prevent the buildup of harmful bacteria that can weaken the gums and bone keeping your new teeth stable and strong. Just by taking a few minutes each day, you can negate the most common reason for implant failure.

Eat a Healthy Diet

A healthy diet can mean a lot of different things to different people, but for us, it simply means mostly consuming minimally processed foods with a low amount of added sugar. This means fruits, vegetables, and lean meats are your best bet. Chewing hearty foods stimulates the jawbone and keeps healthy blood flow to the area, and a diet light in sugar drastically lowers your risk of infection around your implants.

Break Bad Habits

While there are many bad habits in the world, the ones we’re talking about include behaviors like chewing on your nails, eating ice, or using your teeth to open various packages. While your implants are incredibly resilient, these actions can wear them down fairly quickly if you do them regularly. And for people who smoke or use tobacco, quitting is paramount, as it can drastically increase the chances of infection and failure. Of course, this is easier said than done, so be sure to reach out to us for resources and support in doing this.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Do you play sports? If so, then it’s worth investing in a custom mouthguard to ensure a stray ball or unexpected hit doesn’t damage your implants. And, if you grind your teeth while you sleep, we can provide you with a nightguard to wear to bed that will keep your entire smile protected and whole.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Most dental implant-related problems start as small issues, but they aren’t addressed for a long time until they lead to loose or hurting teeth. This can largely be avoided with the suggestions above, but maintaining your routine dental checkups is important as well. They enable your dentist to take a close look at your implants and spot any potential issues during their earliest stages, meaning they can intervene and save you from a much bigger headache down the road.
