What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are made with titanium posts that are placed into the jawbone through a surgical procedure. The tooth implant uses this post as an artificial tooth root and requires a few months to heal and integrate into the jawbone.

Once teeth implants are in place, they can support a variety of restorative dental treatments, including a crown, dental bridge , or dentures . This treatment fills in the gap and restores your smile, while also improving overall function at the same time.

At My Dental Hub, our implant dentist offers customized treatment plans for every patient. If you need to replace one missing tooth, then a single tooth implant with a crown is commonly used. If you are missing multiple teeth, then several implants can be used to support larger restorations, such as a bridge or dentures.

When Are Dental Implants Recommended?

In order to be a good candidate for dental implants, you need to have one or more missing teeth and sufficient jawbone density to hold the post in place. Dentists commonly recommend that patients replace missing teeth after extraction to prevent further complications, especially when tooth loss impacts the person’s ability to eat or speak.

If a patient is experiencing extensive tooth loss, then full mouth dental implants (commonly known as All-on-4 dental implants) can be a fast and effective solution to restore the entire smile. An entire arch can be replaced using All-on-Four implants.

Benefits of Dental Implants

The primary benefit of using dental implants for tooth replacement is that you can fill in the gaps and restore function after tooth removal. The result is a stable, long-lasting restoration that allows you to eat and talk normally. Additionally, the placement of dental implants can support the overall facial structure to minimize the appearance of sunken cheeks.

Another benefit is that placing the implant post into the jawbone can help to preserve bone density after tooth loss. The dental crown or bridge that is attached to the top of the implant fills in the gaps to prevent nearby teeth from shifting, which helps to reduce the need for more extensive restorative treatments in the future.

The Dental Implant Procedure Explained

This dental implant procedure is performed in our nearby office and requires multiple appointments to complete the full treatment. The first step is an in-depth clinical evaluation, including digital imaging, to ensure that your jawbone will be able to support the placement of a titanium post.

During the oral surgery, our implant dentist will carefully place the implant in the ideal location, using modern technology for ultimate precision. Then, you will need to wait for a few months so that the implant can integrate with the bone during your healing period. Finally, the restoration is attached to the top of the implant.

Typical steps of the dental implant procedure include:

  • A thorough examination, including digital imaging, is necessary to evaluate your oral health.
  • You will have an implant placement surgery to secure the titanium post into the jawbone.
  • The implant needs to heal for several months and integrate with the jawbone.
  • During the healing time, you can use a temporary restoration to fill in the gap.
  • When the implant is secure, the final restoration can be placed.
  • Make sure to maintain regular dental check-ups so that we can monitor the implant.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants can last for many years or even a lifetime with proper care. The timeline depends on your overall oral hygiene and bone health.

Is the dental implant procedure painful?

Our implant dentist uses local anesthesia to ensure that you feel no pain during the treatment. You can expect mild discomfort for a few days during recovery.

Can dental implants replace multiple missing teeth?

Yes, dental implants can be a foundation to support many types of dental prosthetics to replace one or multiple missing teeth: crowns, bridges, or even All-on-4 implants for full-arch restorations.

How do I know if I am a candidate for dental implants?
