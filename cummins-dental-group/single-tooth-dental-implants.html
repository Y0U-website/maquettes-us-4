What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants have become an increasingly popular option for missing teeth, but what exactly are they? Dental implants are small, screw-like posts surgically placed into the jawbone to act as artificial tooth roots.

These posts comprise biocompatible materials, such as titanium, which fuse with the jawbone to create a stable foundation for replacement teeth. Once we securely place the post, a custom-made crown is attached to the implant, providing a natural-looking and functional replacement tooth. Not only do dental implants offer a durable and permanent solution for missing teeth, but they also promote overall oral health by preventing bone loss and maintaining proper alignment of surrounding teeth. With good care and maintenance, dental implants can last a lifetime.

Improved Oral Health

Single-tooth dental implants improve oral health by filling the gap left by the missing tooth. The gap created by tooth loss can cause surrounding teeth to shift into the space, leading to misalignment of teeth, gum disease, and cavities. Also, dental implants are more resistant to decay, preventing further dental problems. A dental implant can preserve your oral health and prevent future dental problems.

Natural-Looking

Unlike dentures or bridges, dental implants look, feel, and function like natural teeth. The implant is placed in the jaw, which fuses with the natural bone, creating a permanent and stable fixture that blends in with the other teeth. Dental implants are custom-made to fit the patient’s mouth, ensuring a natural-looking result that complements the patient’s facial features.

Improved Comfort

Dentures and bridges can cause discomfort or pain for some patients, particularly when they don’t fit correctly. Dental implants are designed to comfortably fit the patient’s mouth, with no slipping or clicking. They improve the patient’s comfort and reduce the risk of mouth sores and discomfort.

Dental implants provide a long-lasting solution, unlike dentures or bridges, which may need replacement or refitting every seven to fifteen years.

What Are the Steps to Getting a Dental Implant?

At Cummins Dental Group, we understand that getting dental implants can seem overwhelming, so we’ve prepared this step-by-step guide to help you understand the process. We’ll walk you through the steps involved in single-tooth dental implant treatment so that you feel confident and informed before your visit.

The first step in getting a dental implant is to schedule a consultation with Dr. Cummins. During this appointment, he will examine your teeth and gums to determine if you’re a good candidate for dental implants. He’ll also discuss the risks and benefits of the treatment and answer any questions you may have.

If you’re deemed a good candidate for dental implants, he will start the process of planning and preparing for the procedure. This preparation may include taking X-rays or digital scans of your teeth and jaw and making impressions of your mouth to create a customized treatment plan.

Once your treatment plan is in place, implant placement is the next step. Dr. Cummins will surgically place the implant into your jawbone during this step. This placement utilizes local anesthesia, so you won’t feel any pain during the procedure.

The implant will be left to integrate with your jawbone for several months, during which time we may place a temporary crown on top of the implant to protect it while it heals.

We will place the abutment once the implant successfully integrates with your jawbone. An abutment is a small connector piece that sits on the implant and supports the crown. This step also uses local anesthesia.

After the abutment is in place, the final step is to attach the crown. A crown is a customized tooth-shaped cap placed on top of the abutment to look and function like your natural teeth. The crown is typically made of porcelain or ceramic and blends seamlessly with your surrounding teeth.

After your dental implant procedure is complete, it’s crucial to maintain proper care to ensure the longevity of your new tooth. Good oral health includes regular brushing and flossing, routine dental check-ups and professional cleanings, and avoiding habits that can damage your teeth, such as smoking and chewing hard foods. Proper aftercare can help your dental implant last for years to come.
