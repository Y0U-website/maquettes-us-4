Benefits of Deep Cleaning and Scaling & Root Planing

Choosing to undergo a deep cleaning is an investment in your overall health. The benefits extend far beyond just clean teeth.

For patients prone to periodontal issues, this treatment is essential. It helps reset your oral health and creates a fresh start for your gums.

  • Stops the progression of gum disease and prevents tooth loss
  • Reduces inflammation and eliminates infection in the gums
  • Restores the health of your gum tissue and bad breath

Who Needs Deep Teeth Cleaning?

You might be wondering if this treatment is right for you. Gum disease often progresses without pain in the early stages. However, there are clear signs that indicate a deep cleaning may be necessary.

If your dentist or periodontist has recommended this treatment after measuring your gum pockets, it is important to act. Ignoring these signs can lead to more advanced problems that require surgery later.

  • Persistent bad breath that does not go away with brushing
  • Swollen, red, or tender gums that bleed easily
  • Gums that are pulling away from your teeth making them look longer
  • Loose teeth or a change in the way your teeth fit together

The Procedure: What to Expect

We understand that visiting the dentist can cause potential anxiety. That is why we take extra time to explain every step. Your comfort is our priority.

Before we begin, we review your diagnosis and discuss your personalized plan. On the day of the procedure, we will numb the area using a local anesthetic. This ensures you feel no discomfort while we work. We use specialized tools to gently remove the buildup of plaque and tartar from above and below the gum line.

Depending on the extent of the infection, we may treat one side of your mouth at a time. This means you might need two visits to complete the full treatment.

Recovery is typically quick. Most patients return to their normal routine immediately. We will schedule a follow-up visit to check your healing and ensure your gums are responding well.

  • Your gums may feel tender for a day or two
  • Teeth might be sensitive to hot or cold temperatures
  • We will provide specific instructions on how to keep the area clean
