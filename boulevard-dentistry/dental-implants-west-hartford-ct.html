Comparing Dental Implants To Other Tooth Replacement Options

Dental implants offer unmatched stability compared to dentures or bridges. They preserve bone health while providing a natural appearance and function that feels like your own teeth. Implants are often considered the most effective long-term solution.

The Dangers Of Resorption After Tooth Loss When a tooth is lost, the jawbone begins to deteriorate due to lack of stimulation. Over time, this can lead to facial structure changes and complications for future restorative treatments. Preventing resorption is key to maintaining oral health.

  • Bone preservation: Prevents bone loss and maintains facial structure.
  • Independent support: Does not rely on adjacent teeth for stability.
  • Long-term value: Saves money over time compared to frequent replacements.
  • Loss of stimulation: Bone weakens without tooth roots to activate growth.
  • Jawbone shrinkage: Results in changes to facial shape.
  • Future complications: Makes restorative treatments more difficult.

Types Of Dental Implants

Dental implants can be personalized to address a wide range of tooth replacement needs. Each option is designed to restore function, improve comfort, and enhance your smile. Discover the implant solution that’s right for you.

All-On-4 implants replace an entire arch of teeth using just four implants. This option offers full functionality with minimal surgical intervention.

All-On-6 implants provide enhanced stability with six implants supporting a full arch. This option is ideal for patients needing added durability and support.

Single-tooth implants replace one missing tooth without affecting adjacent teeth. They are a seamless and natural-looking solution for isolated tooth loss.

Implant-supported bridges replace multiple missing teeth in a row. They provide better support and help maintain the health of the jawbone.

Implant-supported dentures provide a secure alternative to traditional dentures. They are stable, comfortable, and custom-fitted to your unique dental structure.
