How Bone Grafting and Preparatory Work Drive Up the Price

One of the biggest cost variables is whether you need bone grafting. When a tooth has been missing for a while, the surrounding jawbone tends to shrink. Implants need a solid bone foundation to integrate successfully.

Bone grafting adds time and cost to your treatment. The graft material must heal before the implant can be placed. This can extend your overall timeline by several months. It also requires additional appointments and sometimes additional imaging.

Other preparatory procedures that can affect your total cost include tooth extractions and sinus lifts. A sinus lift is sometimes needed for upper back tooth implants when the sinus cavity sits too close to the jaw. These are all procedures Dr. Sandhu will identify during your initial evaluation. Knowing about them upfront helps you plan financially without surprises.

Is the Higher Cost of Dental Implants Worth It?

This is the question most Mesa patients ask once they understand the pricing. The honest answer is that implants offer benefits that other options simply can’t match. They function, feel, and look like natural teeth. They also preserve your jawbone — something dentures and bridges do not do.

Consider what other tooth replacement options require over time. Dentures may need relining or replacement every several years. Bridges require grinding down healthy adjacent teeth. Both involve ongoing maintenance and eventual replacement costs. Implants, by contrast, are designed to be a permanent solution.

For patients managing their oral health long-term, implants often make the most clinical and financial sense.
