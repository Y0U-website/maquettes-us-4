Dental Implants

Dental implants are a long-term option for replacing missing teeth. An implant acts as a stable foundation for a crown, bridge, or other restoration, helping you eat and speak more comfortably and smile with confidence.

Your plan is based on your exam, imaging, and goals. If you are missing many teeth, full-arch options may be available as part of an implant plan, depending on your health and anatomy.

Learn more about dental implant treatment .

All-on-4® / Hybrid / Teeth-in-a-Day

If you’re missing many teeth, full-arch implant treatment may be an option to replace an entire arch with a secure, natural-looking smile. Approaches such as All-on-4® (sometimes called hybrid implants or Teeth-in-a-Day) use strategically placed implants to support a full set of teeth. Your plan is based on an exam and imaging, with a focus on long-term stability, comfort, and predictable healing.

Wisdom Teeth Removal

Wisdom teeth do not always come in normally. When they are impacted, hard to keep clean, or too crowded, they can lead to pain, swelling, infection, or pressure on nearby teeth.

We evaluate your wisdom teeth and explain the recommended timing, what to expect on procedure day, and what recovery typically looks like. If sedation is appropriate for your case, we will review those options during your visit.

Tooth Extractions

A tooth extraction may be recommended when a tooth is severely damaged, infected, impacted, or cannot be removed safely through routine care. Our team plans extractions carefully to protect surrounding teeth and bone and to support predictable healing.

If you are considering dental implants after an extraction, we can discuss timing and steps that may help preserve the site for future treatment.

Learn more about tooth extraction care .

Bone Grafting

Bone grafting can help rebuild or preserve the jawbone after tooth loss, infection, or extraction. It is also commonly recommended to support dental implant placement when additional bone is needed for stability.

If bone grafting is part of your plan, we will explain the goal of the procedure, what healing typically involves, and how it supports the next step in care.

Jaw Surgery

Jaw surgery may be recommended when jaw alignment affects bite function, comfort, or overall oral health. In some cases, surgery supports a broader treatment plan that also involves orthodontic care.

We use clinical evaluation and imaging to determine whether surgical treatment is appropriate, then coordinate recommendations with the referring provider when needed.
