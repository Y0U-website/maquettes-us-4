Understanding Dental Implants

What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants provide a durable cosmetic dental solution for replacing missing teeth, designed to mimic the look, feel, and function of natural teeth. Each implant consists of a titanium post (the implant), an abutment, and a custom-made crown designed to blend seamlessly with your other teeth.

How Do Dental Implants Work?

The titanium post is surgically placed into the jawbone, where it integrates with the bone over a few months in a process known as osseointegration. This integration forms a strong and stable foundation for the new tooth.

Dental Implant Restorations

Dental Crown

For single-tooth replacement, a crown is attached to an individual implant. The crown is custom-crafted to match the color and shape of your surrounding teeth, ensuring a natural look.

Dental Bridge

If you’re missing several teeth, an implant-supported bridge can restore the appearance and function of your teeth. This bridge uses implants as supports on either end and fills the gap with artificial teeth.

Implant-Supported Denture

For a full arch replacement, implant-supported dentures provide a secure, stable alternative to traditional dentures. With just a few implants, the denture snaps securely into place, providing improved comfort and functionality.

The Benefits of Dental Implants

Durability and Longevity

Dental implants are built to last and, with proper care, can continue to function like natural teeth for decades. They are a worthwhile investment in your long-term oral health.

Enhanced Appearance and Confidence

Implants look and feel like your natural teeth. They can dramatically enhance your smile and boost your self-esteem, allowing you to speak and eat with confidence.

Book Appointment Exploring Types of Dental Implants Endosteal Implants These are the most common type of implants and are placed directly into the jawbone. They serve as an artificial root to hold a replacement tooth or bridge.

Single-Tooth Implant

This type is used when only one tooth needs replacement. A single implant is placed in the jawbone, topped with a crown, effectively replacing the missing tooth without affecting adjacent teeth.

Mini Dental Implant (MDI)

Ideal for patients who prefer a less invasive option or have insufficient bone mass, MDIs are smaller than traditional implants. They are often used to secure dentures or as a temporary anchorage device in orthodontic treatments.

Subperiosteal Implants

These are placed under the gum but on, or above, the jawbone. This option is suitable for patients who do not have enough healthy jawbone and cannot, or do not wish to undergo a bone augmentation procedure to add bone.

Zygomatic Implants

In cases of severe jawbone loss where even bone grafting is not feasible, zygomatic implants are an alternative. These are longer than traditional implants and are anchored in the cheekbone.
