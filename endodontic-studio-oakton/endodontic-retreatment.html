Some teeth with persistent infection have few or no symptoms and are discovered during routine dental imaging.

Symptoms can also come from problems unrelated to the original root canal, including a cracked tooth , which is why careful diagnosis with the aid of 3D imaging is important before deciding on treatment.

At Endodontic Studio | Oakton, Dr. Abasin Safi provides evaluation and retreatment of previously root canal-treated teeth with a focus on careful diagnosis and preserving the natural tooth whenever possible.

If you have new pain, swelling, or concerns involving a previously treated tooth, we can evaluate the tooth, review your imaging and previous treatment, and discuss whether root canal retreatment, endodontic microsurgery , or another approach is most appropriate.

  • Does pain mean my root canal failed? Not necessarily. Pain in a previously treated tooth can have several causes, including problems unrelated to the original root canal. The tooth should be evaluated before determining whether retreatment or another treatment is necessary.
  • Signs a Previously Treated Tooth May Need Evaluation A previously root canal-treated tooth should be evaluated if you develop new or persistent symptoms such as: Pain or tenderness when biting or chewing
  • New or increasing sensitivity around the tooth
  • Swelling or tenderness in the gums
  • A small bump or “pimple” on the gum near the tooth
  • Persistent discomfort following previous root canal treatment
  • Changes seen on a dental X-ray around the roots of the tooth
  • Advanced Imaging for Previously Treated Teeth Previously treated teeth can be more complex to evaluate with traditional two-dimensional dental X-rays alone. When clinically appropriate, 3D CBCT imaging can provide additional information about the roots, surrounding bone, and previous treatment. CBCT imaging may help identify findings such as untreated anatomy, persistent areas of infection, root resorption, certain fractures, or other changes that may influence treatment planning.
  • Can a root canal-treated tooth become infected again years later? Yes. A previously treated tooth can occasionally develop a new or recurrent infection months or years later because of leakage, new decay, complex anatomy, a crack , or other changes involving the tooth. In many cases, the tooth may still be treatable.
