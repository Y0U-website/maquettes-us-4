Who may be a candidate for dental implants?

Implant candidacy depends on more than age. The dentist considers your overall and oral health, available bone, gum condition, smoking or nicotine use, medications, healing factors, bite forces, and ability to maintain the area. Certain medical conditions or medications can affect treatment planning, which is why a complete health history is important.

If a tooth is still present but has a poor prognosis, the dentist will also discuss whether saving the tooth is realistic before recommending extraction and implant replacement. When an implant is appropriate, the objective is a restoration that is cleansable, functional, stable, and compatible with the surrounding tissues.

How CBCT imaging supports implant planning.

Traditional dental X-rays provide valuable two-dimensional information. For selected implant cases, cone-beam computed tomography, or CBCT, provides a three-dimensional view of the jaws and surrounding anatomy. This can help the dentist evaluate bone dimensions and important structures before determining implant position and size.

Our Chicago office also uses digital imaging and intraoral scanning. Depending on the case, these tools can support restorative planning and help patients better understand the proposed treatment.

When bone grafting may be part of the plan.

Bone naturally changes after a tooth is lost or removed. In some areas, the remaining ridge may not have enough width or height for the preferred implant position. Bone grafting may be recommended to preserve or rebuild support before or during implant treatment.

The type and timing of grafting vary. Some grafts are performed at the time of extraction, while others are completed as a separate procedure and allowed to heal before implant placement. Your dentist will explain whether grafting is recommended and what it means for the overall timeline.

The implant is only one part of the finished tooth.

After integration, an implant typically supports a restorative component such as a crown. The shape, bite, contact with neighboring teeth, and cleansability of that restoration are important to long-term function. Implant-supported bridges or other prosthetic designs may be considered when multiple teeth are missing.

Implants are not immune to inflammation or bone loss. Daily plaque control, professional maintenance, periodic examinations, and monitoring of the bite remain important after treatment. Patients who grind or clench may also be advised to use a protective appliance depending on the case.
