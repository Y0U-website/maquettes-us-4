The stages, and how long each takes

An implant is not a single appointment. The sequence at Shield Dental Care runs in four stages:

The total is usually measured in months rather than weeks. Where a tooth still has to be removed first, or a graft is needed, that adds a healing period of its own before the post can go in.

  • Consultation and planning. 3D imaging shows the bone available and the position of the nerve and sinus, so the post can be placed where the bone actually is rather than where it is assumed to be.
  • Placement. The post goes into the jaw under local anaesthetic, with sedation available if you would rather not be aware of it.
  • Healing. The osseointegration period. This is the long part, and it is biology rather than dentistry — nothing speeds it up meaningfully.
  • The crown. Impressions are taken once the post is solid, and the finished tooth is fitted.

  • All-on-4 , a full arch on four angled posts — typically $20,000 to $30,000.

Compared with a bridge or a denture

A conventional bridge is faster and cheaper up front, but it works by grinding down the healthy teeth on either side of the gap to carry the load. A denture is cheaper still and needs no surgery, but it rests on the gums rather than in the jaw. Neither replaces the root, which is why neither stops the bone beneath a missing tooth from shrinking over time.

That bone change is the argument for an implant, and it is also the argument against waiting: the ridge is easier to build on before it has resorbed than after.

How long they last, and what failure looks like

Implant survival is high. The cohort study cited above reported 97.4 % survival at two years , and survival rates above 95 % are consistently reported in the literature. That is a strong number, but it is not 100 %, and knowing what a problem looks like matters more than the statistic.

Get in touch promptly if you notice pain that persists beyond the first week, swelling or redness around the implant, any movement at all in a healed implant, or bleeding around it months or years after placement. The last of those usually points to peri-implantitis , a bacterial infection of the gum and bone around the post — treatable when it is caught early, and much harder once bone has been lost.

Looking after one

An implant does not decay, which occasionally leads people to relax about it. The tissue around it can still become infected, and that is what determines how long the implant lasts. Brush twice daily, clean around the implant with floss designed for the purpose or a water flosser, and keep to regular hygiene appointments so the gum around it is checked rather than assumed to be fine.
