How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

  • Regular checkups. Routine visits catch early gum trouble and let your dentist check the fit and the bite.
  • Managing your bite. Grinding or clenching puts heavy stress on the crown and post. A nightguard often helps.
  • Not smoking. Smoking slows healing and raises the risk of gum problems around the implant.

What can shorten an implant’s life?

The most common reason implants fail is a gum infection called peri-implantitis. It works a lot like gum disease around natural teeth — plaque builds up, the tissue gets inflamed, and over time the bone that holds the implant can shrink. That’s why the same brushing and flossing habits that protect your real teeth also protect your implant.

Uncontrolled health conditions and heavy grinding can also play a role. The good news is that most of these risks are things you and your dentist can stay ahead of.

Do implant crowns need replacing?

Sometimes. Crowns take years of biting and chewing, and materials do wear. If a crown chips, loosens, or wears down, it can usually be repaired or replaced without touching the post underneath. Think of it like changing tires while the car keeps running.
