Manage Orthodontic Issues at…

Recognizing these issues early on is key to managing them effectively until you can visit your orthodontist.

  • Lost aligners: Since aligners are removable, they can be easily misplaced.
  • Sharp spots causing sores: Both braces and aligners can cause irritation in the mouth, leading to painful sores.
  • Poking wires: Over time, wires may become longer and start poking into the cheeks or lips.

Step-by-Step Guide to Managing Orthodontic Issues at Home

Broken Brackets or Wires

If a bracket breaks or a wire comes loose, start by assessing the situation. If the broken part isn’t causing discomfort, cover it with orthodontic wax to prevent irritation and schedule a visit with your orthodontist.

If the loose bracket is causing discomfort, use a pliers to remove the bracket from the wire and then use nail clippers to trim the wire up to the next bracket that is still bonded to the teeth. Check out our video tutorial for guidance: Problem Solved : Removing Broken Bracket from Wire

Lost Aligners

If you have lost an aligner, simply move on to the next aligner set in your series. Wear this new set for a few extra days to give time for the teeth to catch up in movement. Most of our patients at True North will change their aligners every 7 days. So for example, if you lose aligner #3 on day 3, move to aligner #4 and stay in it for 11 days instead of the initially prescribed 7 days.

Sharp Spots Causing Sores

Sharp spots on braces can often be managed by applying orthodontic wax to the irritating area. This provides a smooth barrier between the appliance and your mouth’s soft tissue. Additionally, rinsing with warm salt water can help heal sores more quickly. Check out our video tutorial for guidance: Problem Solved : Sharp Spot on my Braces

For sharp spots on aligners, you can use a nail file or Emery board to file down and smooth out the spot that is causing irritation. Check out our video tutorial for guidance: Problem Solved : Sharp Spot on my Clear Aligner

Poking Wires

If a wire is poking into your cheek or lip, use a clean cotton swab or the eraser end of a pencil to gently push the wire into a more comfortable position. You can also cover the end with orthodontic wax for added comfort until you can see your orthodontist.

If you aren’t able to readjust or cover with wax, nail clippers can be used to trim the section of wire up to the back bracket. Check out our video tutorial for guidance: Problem Solved : Braces Wire is Poking

Risks of DIY Braces Repairs

While some issues can be temporarily managed at home, attempting DIY repairs on your orthodontic appliances can lead to further damage or prolong your treatment. Adjusting your braces or aligners without professional guidance could shift your teeth incorrectly, risking your oral health and treatment outcome.

After making any repairs at home, please let your orthodontist know and get an appointment scheduled as soon as possible.

The Importance of Professional Help

No matter how minor an orthodontic issue might seem, it’s always best to consult with your orthodontist. True North Orthodontics emphasizes the importance of maintaining open communication with their team. They provide instructions and tools necessary for dealing with emergencies and ensure you don’t have to handle these situations alone. Remember, timely professional assistance is crucial in preventing minor issues from becoming major complications.
