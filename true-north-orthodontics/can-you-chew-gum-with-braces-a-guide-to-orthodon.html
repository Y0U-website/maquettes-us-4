Can You Chew Gum with Braces?…

  • Difficulty Cleaning Chewing gum can stick to your wires, brackets, or teeth, making it harder to maintain good oral hygiene . This might increase the risk of plaque buildup and other dental issues.
  • Sugar and Gum Health For individuals with braces, sugary gum can contribute to tooth decay or gum irritation, compounding the challenges of keeping your teeth clean. If you are going to chew gum, best to make it the sugar-free variety.

The Case for Braces-Friendly Gum: Benefits to Chewing

Here’s the good news under the right conditions, certain types of gum can actually be safe for braces wearers. Sugar-free gum, especially those containing xylitol, offers some unique benefits:

  • Xylitol and Oral Health Sugar-free gums with xylitol have been shown to help reduce bacteria levels in the mouth. Xylitol can inhibit plaque formation and promote healthier gums.
  • Stimulates Saliva Production Chewing gum helps stimulate saliva flow, which neutralizes acids and washes away food particles. This is especially helpful for braces patients who want to reduce their risk of cavities.

Expert Recommendation:

If you want to enjoy gum occasionally and safely, always opt for sugar-free gum labeled as orthodontic-friendly or approved by dental professional organizations. Avoid sticky or bubble gum varieties at all costs!

Alternatives to Chewing Gum

If chewing gum seems too risky or uncomfortable with braces, there are plenty of other ways to maintain gum health and freshen your breath:

  • Mints Sugar-free mints provide a braces-friendly way to freshen your breath without sticking to your appliances. Just be sure to suck on the mints only. Chomping down a mint can definitely break a bracket!
  • Water Intake Drink plenty of water throughout the day to rinse your mouth and stay hydrated. It’s essential for gum health and overall oral hygiene.
  • Crunchy, Healthy Snacks Chewing raw vegetables like celery or carrots can give you a similar clean and refreshing feeling while being safe for braces. We recommend cutting these vegetables into bite size pieces before chomping into them to avoid bracket breakage.

Final Thoughts on Chewing Gum with Braces

While orthodontic care requires some adjustments, chewing gum doesn’t have to be entirely off-limits. The key lies in choosing the right type of gum and enjoying it in moderation. However, if there’s any doubt, your orthodontist is your best resource for personalized advice. Always consult them before making changes to your diet or routine.
