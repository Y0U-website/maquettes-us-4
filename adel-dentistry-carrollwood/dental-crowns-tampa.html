When does a tooth need a crown?

A filling repairs small damage; a crown rebuilds the whole tooth. You’ll likely need one if you have a large cavity too big for a filling, a cracked or fractured tooth, a tooth weakened after a root canal , a worn-down tooth from grinding, or a cosmetic flaw you want permanently corrected — or if you need an implant crown to top a dental implant . During your exam, Dr. Adel will show you exactly what’s going on — with digital x-rays and a comfortable digital scan — and give you an honest recommendation. If a filling will do, he’ll tell you that too.

Why same-day changes everything

No temporary crown.

Temporaries are the worst part of traditional crowns: they fall off, they feel wrong, and they need babying for weeks. With a same-day crown, there’s nothing temporary about your visit — you leave with the real thing.

No second appointment.

One visit, start to finish. No taking more time off work, no second round of numbing, no waiting two weeks chewing carefully on one side.

Digital precision.

Instead of goopy impressions, we take a comfortable digital scan. Your crown is designed on-screen and milled from a solid block of dental ceramic — shaped and shade-matched to blend with your natural teeth and your bite.

What to expect at your appointment

After numbing the area, Dr. Adel gently reshapes the tooth and takes a digital scan — no messy impressions. While you relax, your crown is designed and milled in-office. Dr. Adel then checks the fit, shape, and bite, bonds it permanently, and polishes it to a natural luster. Most patients are surprised by two things: how comfortable it was, and that they’re done in one visit.
