Risk Factors for Gum Disease

Some people are more susceptible to gum disease than others. Risk factors include:

  • Smoking or tobacco use, 1 of the most significant risk factors. Smoking reduces blood flow to the gums, making it harder for the body to fight infection.
  • Diabetes, which makes people more prone to infections. And gum disease, in turn, makes blood sugar harder to control.
  • Genetics, as some people are simply more predisposed to gum disease even with excellent oral hygiene.
  • Hormonal changes during pregnancy, menopause, and puberty, which can make gums more sensitive and vulnerable.
  • Dry mouth caused by certain medications, since saliva plays a crucial role in protecting the mouth from bacteria.
  • Infrequent dental visits, as without professional cleanings to remove tartar, the conditions for gum disease are always present.

How Vineland Dental Treats Gum Disease

Treatment depends on the stage of the disease.

Routine cleaning (for gingivitis). A thorough professional cleaning combined with improved home care is often all that&#x27;s needed. Gingivitis at this stage is fully reversible.

Scaling and root planing (deep cleaning). For more advanced gum disease, this involves carefully removing tartar deposits from below the gumline and smoothing the root surfaces so gum tissue can reattach. Done under local anesthetic and may be completed over 1 or 2 visits.

More frequent maintenance cleanings. Patients with a history of gum disease typically benefit from professional cleanings every 3 to 4 months rather than the standard every 6.

Monitoring and reassessment. After treatment, Dr. Hinman monitors your gum health at every visit, checking pocket depths, gum attachment, and bone levels over time.

Gum Disease and Your Overall Health

Untreated gum disease has been linked to:

This is why we take gum disease seriously even at its earliest stage. It&#x27;s not just a mouth issue.

  • Heart disease and stroke, as the bacteria responsible for gum disease can enter the bloodstream and contribute to arterial inflammation.
  • Diabetes, as gum disease makes blood sugar harder to control and diabetes makes gum disease harder to treat. The relationship goes both ways.
  • Respiratory disease, as bacteria from the mouth can be inhaled into the lungs.
  • Pregnancy complications, as pregnant patients with untreated gum disease have higher rates of preterm birth and low birth weight.

FAQs About Gum Disease Treatment in Vineland, NJ

Q: How do I know if I have gum disease?

A: The most reliable way is a professional exam. Dr. Hinman and Tara check for gum disease at every visit by measuring pocket depths, checking for bleeding, and reviewing X-rays. At home, watch for bleeding gums, persistent bad breath, gum recession, or sensitivity. If you notice any of these, call us rather than waiting for your next scheduled visit.

Q: Is gum disease reversible?

A: In its earliest stage (gingivitis), yes, completely reversible with professional treatment and consistent home care. Once it advances to periodontitis and bone loss has occurred, that damage can&#x27;t be undone, but the disease can be controlled. Early detection makes all the difference.

Q: How long does gum disease treatment take?

A: Gingivitis can often be addressed in a single cleaning appointment. Scaling and root planing is typically completed over 1 to 2 visits. Ongoing maintenance cleanings every 3 to 4 months are part of long-term management for most patients with a history of periodontitis.

Q: Can gum disease come back after treatment?

A: Yes, which is why maintenance is so important. Regular professional cleanings, consistent home care, and avoiding risk factors like smoking are the most effective ways to keep it under control.

Q: Does insurance cover gum disease treatment?

A: Most dental insurance plans cover some level of gum disease treatment. Routine cleanings are typically covered at 100 %. Deep cleanings are usually covered at 50 to 80 % depending on your plan. No insurance? Ask about our membership plan or financing options.
