How Do Full-Mouth Dental Implants Work?

Traditional dental implants require one implant for each missing tooth, which can mean eight to ten implants per arch and a lengthy, expensive treatment process. The full-mouth technique revolutionizes this approach by using just four implants per arch, strategically angled to maximize bone contact and support. Two implants are placed vertically at the front of your jaw where bone density is naturally higher, while two are positioned at an angle toward the back to anchor the prosthetic firmly in place.

This innovative design eliminates the need for bone grafting in most cases, making you a candidate for full-mouth dental implants even if you’ve been told you don’t have enough bone. The implants fuse with your jawbone through a process called osseointegration, creating a stable foundation that prevents the bone loss associated with traditional dentures. Your custom prosthetic attaches securely to these implants, providing the look and function of natural teeth.

What Are the Benefits of Implant-Supported Dentures?

One of the most significant advantages of implant-supported dentures is the ability to leave our office with a functional set of teeth on the same day as your implant placement. We attach a temporary prosthetic to your implants immediately after surgery, so you never have to go without teeth during the healing process. Once your implants fully integrate with your bone over the next few months, we’ll replace the temporary prosthetic with your permanent restoration.

Improved Function and Comfort

Unlike removable dentures that rest on your gums and can slip or cause sore spots, implant-supported restorations function like natural teeth. You’ll regain up to 70 % of your original bite force, allowing you to eat your favorite foods without restriction. According to the American Academy of Implant Dentistry , dental implants have a success rate of up to 95 %. They can last a lifetime with proper care, making them one of the most reliable long-term solutions for tooth replacement.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Full-Mouth Dental Implants in Carbondale?

You may be an excellent candidate for full-mouth dental implants if you’re missing most or all of your teeth, have failing teeth that need extraction, or are frustrated with traditional dentures. The technique works well for patients who have moderate bone loss, as the angled placement of the posterior implants maximizes the use of available bone. Even if you’ve been told you need extensive bone grafting for traditional implants, implant-supported dentures may offer a faster, more affordable alternative.

Good candidates should have adequate overall health to undergo oral surgery and be committed to maintaining excellent oral hygiene. If you smoke, Dr. Rivers will work with you to develop a plan to improve your healing potential. During your consultation, we’ll perform a thorough examination, including 3D imaging, to assess your bone structure and determine if full-mouth dental implants are right for you.
