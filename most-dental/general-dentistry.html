Preventative Dentistry

It is important to brush your teeth at least twice daily to help remove plaque and food particles that get stuck to your teeth. It is recommended that you brush your teeth for two minutes (or longer), though most people rush through this time. You need to make sure that you reach every side of each tooth, including the inside and outside. To help keep your breath fresh, you should also brush your tongue. Then, you can begin flossing.

Cleanings And Exams

As part of the patient’s preventive dental healthcare routine, dental professionals advise dental cleanings at least once in every six months. Professional dental cleanings are a great way to thoroughly cleanse the oral cavity from potential germs or bacteria which regular home-care routines cannot remove. This also allows the dentist to examine areas of the oral cavity that may need more care and attention.

As the patient arrives at the office for their regular dental cleaning session, Dr. Most and the hygienist would help them receive diagnostic and preventive dental services. During the appointment, the dentist would also advise or educate the patient on good oral hygiene habits that you can routinely follow at home.

  • Systematic review and analysis of the patient’s medical history to understand if any other healthcare problems may be directly impacting the patient’s oral health.
  • Setting up digital X-rays to examine the teeth (this helps to detect signs of decay or gum disease).
  • Inspect the patient’s gum tissue for signs of diseases.
  • Evaluate if the patient suffers from any problems while chewing or swallowing.
  • Professional dental cleanings help remove stains, plaque, and hard calculus, which regular cleaning cannot remove.
  • Hygiene visits also provide children with dental sealants. Sealants are a thin plastic coating applied on the grooves and chewing surfaces that act like an enamel-shield.
  • Recommend restorations such as crowns or fillings that are also part of the patient’s hygiene visit.
  • Hygiene visits would also allow the dentist to clean or re-adjust any existing restorations.

Oral Cancer Screenings

Oral cancer is among the most aggressive forms of cancer, affecting an estimated 43,250 men and women in America each year. Only a little more than half of those diagnosed with oral cancer will survive past the five-year point, but early detection can help to improve these statistics. Performed at your routine hygiene checkups every six months, oral cancer screening with Dr. Most may actually help save your life.

Oral Cancer: Why Early Detection Matters

Like all other forms of cancer, oral cancer is best treated before the disease has progressed to a more advanced stage. The goal is to diagnose and treat cancer before it spreads to other tissues in the body, including the lymph nodes. Because it is impossible to spot abnormal clusters of cells with the naked eye, your dentist utilizes advanced diagnostic technology to check for cancerous and pre-cancerous lesions. If a suspicious area is identified, a biopsy may be ordered to determine the presence of these dangerous and destructive cells.

Risk Factors for Oral Cancer

Though oral cancer may affect an individual who is otherwise completely healthy, there are some risk factors to take into consideration. These include:

  • Age : Men and women over the age of 40 are statistically more likely to be diagnosed with oral cancer.
  • HPV : Human papillomavirus, or HPV, is a sexually transmitted disease that increases patients likely to develop oral cancer.
  • Tobacco and alcohol : The majority of oral cancer cases can be directly linked with the use of cigarettes, excessive consumption of alcohol, or the two behaviors combined.
  • Excessive sun exposure : Cancerous lesions found on the lips may be tied to inadequate protection against the sun’s harmful rays.
  • Diet : Patients whose diets contain inadequate nutrients from fruits and vegetables are at a higher risk of developing cancer of the mouth or throat.

Symptoms of Oral Cancer

In its earliest stages, oral cancer does not always present with noticeable symptoms. A patient may be at Stage I or Stage II of oral cancer and still feel perfectly fine. This is why oral cancer screening at our office is so critical to successful treatment. If you notice any of the following symptoms between your routine dental visits, please contact our office immediately.

While these symptoms do not always necessarily indicate the presence of oral cancer, their existence does warrant serious consideration and close inspection.

  • A thickening of the soft tissues anywhere in the mouth
  • Red, white, or light pink patches on the gums, cheek lining, the roof of the mouth, or the floor of the mouth
  • Unexplained bleeding
  • Ulcers or sores that do not heal or tend to recur
  • Numbness, tingling, or any other abnormal sensations
  • A persistent cough, often coupled with hoarseness
  • Difficulty swallowing
  • Jaw pain
  • Trouble moving the tongue freely
  • Teeth that are loose or painful
  • A difference in the way dental prostheses (like dentures) fit on the gums

Sports Mouth Guards

As dentists, we are determined to make sure your mouth and teeth are well protected during sports activities, no matter what your passion is. To do this, we offer many different types of mouth guards, depending on the types of activities in which you are involved. There are some mouth guards for low-impact sports and others for high-impact sports.

Mouth guards not only protect your mouth and teeth, but also have other purposes. Mouth guards will help decrease the impact if you get hit in the jaw. They have also been shown to increase an athlete’s performance.

Many people clench their teeth while playing, which can give them temporomandibular joint syndrome (TMJ). Clenching your jaw the whole time can be quite exhausting. Mouth guards help to relax your jaw, allowing you to completely focus on the game!
