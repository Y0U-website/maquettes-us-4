CBCT Scanner

A CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography) scanner is a cutting-edge imaging tool used in our dental office to create detailed 3D images of the teeth, jaw, and surrounding structures. Unlike traditional X-rays, CBCT provides a clearer, more precise view, which helps dentists plan treatments like implants, extractions, and orthodontics. It offers a lower radiation dose than traditional CT scans and improves diagnostic accuracy, enabling better treatment planning and patient outcomes.

A CBCT scanner can identify tooth infections, fractures, and impacted teeth, assess bone structure for implants, and detect jaw problems like TMJ disorders. It also aids orthodontic planning, spots cysts or tumors, evaluates sinus health, and monitors periodontal disease. This advanced imaging improves treatment accuracy and patient care.

Potential Side Effects of the "Lip Flip" Procedure:

The "lip flip" procedure, involving Botox injections, may result in several temporary side effects. Patients may experience difficulty performing certain motions, such as sucking on straws, whistling, or producing other "sucking" actions. Additionally, there may be a temporary loss of upper lip control, affecting the ability to rub lips together or swish liquids in the mouth. These effects are generally temporary and should resolve as the Botox wears off, but it is important to be aware of these potential side effects before undergoing the procedure.

The Reality Bites Dental experts discuss your medical history, lifestyle, and goals. They examine your teeth, bite, gums, and jaw and discuss bothersome symptoms you may have before customizing a treatment plan.

Dental Bridges Q & A

Dental bridges are typically non-removable appliances used to replace missing permanent teeth.

Various bridges exist, but a traditional bridge is highly popular. It’s made of porcelain fused with metal and consists of two crowns that bond over the top of the teeth adjacent to a gap. The two crowns contain an artificial tooth between them, which is the replacement tooth.

Another type of bridge is an implant-supported bridge. It often contains three artificial teeth connected, anchored by dental implants (artificial metal tooth roots). Implant-supported bridges serve the same purpose as traditional bridges: replacing missing permanent teeth.

A permanent dental bridge is sturdy, strong, and long-lasting. It can last decades or even a lifetime when cared for properly. Bridges sometimes require replacement because of normal wear.

To determine if a dental bridge is right for you, the Reality Bites Dental team examines your mouth, gums, and teeth. They discuss your oral health history, take dental X-rays if needed, and ask about your goals and treatment preferences. Your dentist customizes a plan that best matches your individualized needs.

Before receiving a dental bridge, you might need a tooth extraction or a bone grafting procedure, depending on the type of bridge you require and whether you have enough jawbone to anchor an implant-supported bridge.

Your dentist digitally scans your mouth, which allows a dental lab to create a custom-fitted traditional or implant-supported bridge that fits your mouth perfectly. They may insert dental implants into your jaw and allow for a period of healing.

When your bridge is ready, simply return to the Reality Bites Dental office. The dental team bonds your new bridge to adjacent teeth or dental implants and ensures it fits properly and comfortably.

Take good care of your new bridge by brushing your teeth daily, flossing every day, seeing your dentist at least every six months, and not chewing on hard objects.
