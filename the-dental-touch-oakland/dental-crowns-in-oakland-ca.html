Crowns are cemented permanently onto your prepared tooth, becoming the new outer surface. They’re made from durable materials like porcelain, ceramic, or porcelain-fused-to-metal, designed to withstand normal chewing forces while looking like a natural tooth. Crowns protect compromised teeth from further damage and can last many years with proper care.

You may need a dental crown in several situations:

Large Cavities or Decay : When a tooth has extensive decay that’s too large for a filling, a crown restores structure and prevents the tooth from breaking.

Cracked or Broken Teeth : Crowns hold together cracked teeth and restore broken teeth to their original shape and function.

After Root Canal Treatment : Teeth become brittle after root canal therapy and need crown protection to prevent fracture during normal chewing.

Worn Teeth : Severe wear from grinding or acidic erosion can be restored with crowns that rebuild lost tooth structure.

Dental Implant Restoration : Crowns complete dental implants, providing the visible "tooth" portion that attaches to the implant post.

Cosmetic Improvement : Crowns can cover severely discolored or misshapen teeth that can’t be corrected with other treatments.

During your exam, we’ll evaluate your tooth and recommend a crown if it’s the best solution for your situation.

After the anesthesia wears off, you might experience mild sensitivity or soreness for a few days, especially when biting down. This is normal and resolves quickly. Your temporary crown may feel slightly different than your natural tooth, but your permanent crown should feel comfortable and natural once placed. If you experience persistent pain or discomfort after crown placement, contact us immediately so we can make any necessary adjustments.

Caring for a dental crown is simple and similar to caring for natural teeth. Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, floss daily around the crown, and visit us for regular checkups and cleanings.

To protect your crown and extend its lifespan, avoid chewing ice, hard candy, or other very hard objects. Don’t use your teeth as tools to open packages. If you grind your teeth at night, wear a nightguard to prevent crown damage. While the crown itself can’t decay, the tooth underneath it can, so maintaining good oral hygiene is essential. With proper care, your crown can last 10-15 years or longer, protecting your tooth and keeping your smile beautiful.
