Where implant quotes usually go wrong

Implant treatment is commonly quoted in pieces. You are given a figure for the post, then later a figure for the abutment that connects it, then a figure for the crown on top, then separate charges for each surgical stage. Each number on its own sounds reasonable. The total only becomes clear once you are several appointments in and no longer comparing practices.

The $3,350 here is the finished result: consultation, the implant, the second-stage surgery, and the permanent crown. There is no separate abutment charge and no per-stage surgical fee arriving later.

What is quoted separately

Bone grafting, where the jaw does not have enough volume to hold an implant. This is common when a tooth has been missing for years, because bone resorbs once the root is gone and there is nothing transmitting chewing forces into it. Grafting is assessed with imaging at your consultation and quoted before anything begins, so you know the full figure before you commit rather than after.

Extraction, if the tooth is still in place and needs removing first. Some cases allow the implant to be placed at the same appointment as the extraction, which shortens the overall timeline considerably. Dr. Mathew will tell you at consultation whether yours is one of them.

What makes someone a good candidate

  • Enough healthy jawbone to hold the implant, or willingness to have a graft first
  • Healthy gums, since gum disease attacks the bone around an implant much as it does around a natural tooth
  • Reasonable general healing capacity
  • Not currently smoking, or willing to stop, since smoking measurably raises implant failure rates
  • Diabetes that is reasonably well controlled, because blood sugar directly affects healing
  • Willingness to maintain it, because an implant still needs brushing, flossing and regular checkups

Why it takes months, and what happens in between

The appointments themselves are short. The waiting between them is the bone doing its work, and it cannot be rushed.

Consultation and imaging

Dr. Mathew assesses bone volume and density and plans the implant position. If grafting is needed, it is identified and quoted here.

Placement

The implant is placed under local anaesthetic. Most patients describe it as more comfortable than an extraction. Sedation is available if the prospect makes you anxious.

Integration

Three to six months while bone fuses to the implant surface. You are not walking around with a gap; a temporary is provided wherever appearance matters.

Crown fitted

The abutment is attached and the permanent crown is made, shaded and fitted to match the teeth either side of it.
