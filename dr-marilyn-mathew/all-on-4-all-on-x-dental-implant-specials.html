What Teeth in a Day actually involves

One appointment, several hours, and you leave with fixed teeth in place.

Planning

Imaging determines bone volume and where the four implants can be angled to find the densest bone. This is done before the day, not on it.

Extractions

Any remaining teeth in the arch are removed. This happens under the same anaesthetic, so it is not a separate ordeal on a separate day.

Implants placed

Four implants are positioned, the rear pair angled to engage bone toward the front of the jaw where it is typically denser.

Temporary arch fitted

A fixed provisional arch is secured the same day. You leave with teeth, not a gap and not a plate.

The months between temporary and permanent

The temporary arch is fixed, functional and looks like teeth, but it is not the final result. It is deliberately made in a material that can be adjusted as the tissue settles, because the gum reshapes considerably during healing.

During this period you eat a softer diet at first and build back up. Most patients describe the first fortnight as the adjustment, then it stops being something they think about. You will have review appointments so the fit can be checked as the swelling reduces.

Once the implants have integrated with the bone, usually after several months, the permanent zirconia arch is made and fitted. Zirconia is used because it is strong enough for full chewing force and does not stain the way acrylic does.

Living with it

An All-On-4 arch does not come out. You clean it in the mouth rather than in a glass, using a water flosser and interdental brushes to keep the area underneath the arch clear. That cleaning routine is the single biggest factor in how long the result lasts, and we will show you exactly how to do it before you leave.

You will still need regular checkups. Implants do not decay, but the gum and bone around them can develop the same inflammatory problems that affect natural teeth, and that is what causes implant failure years down the line.
