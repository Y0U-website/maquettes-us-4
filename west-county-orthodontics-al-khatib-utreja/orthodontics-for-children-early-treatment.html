Orthodontics for Children

While not all children need early treatment, those who do can benefit greatly from a head start.

  • Guide jaw growth and tooth eruption
  • Create space for incoming permanent teeth
  • Improve bite alignment and facial symmetry
  • Reduce or eliminate the need for extractions or surgery later

SIGNS YOUR CHILD MAY NEED EARLY TREATMENT

What Is Phase I Treatment?

Phase I orthodontic treatment, also known as interceptive treatment, is designed for younger children (typically ages 6–10) while they still have a mix of baby and adult teeth. It may involve:

The goal is to correct problems early, laying the groundwork for a healthier, easier Phase II (teen) treatment, or sometimes eliminating the need for it altogether.

  • Limited braces or aligners
  • Expanders
  • Space maintainers
  • Habit appliances (for thumb-sucking or tongue thrust)

FAQs: Children’s Orthodontics & Early Treatment

Why age 7? Isn’t that too early?

Age 7 is the ideal time to evaluate growth patterns. It doesn’t mean your child needs braces right away, but early monitoring helps us time treatment perfectly if needed.

What happens during the first visit?

Is early treatment always necessary?

Not always! Many children are placed in our “growth observation” program and monitored until the time is right.

What’s the difference between Phase I and Phase II treatment?

Phase I happens during childhood to correct specific growth issues. Phase II (usually during the teen years) completes alignment with full braces or aligners.

Are early orthodontics covered by insurance?

Many plans offer partial coverage. We’ll help you review your benefits and provide clear pricing and payment options.
