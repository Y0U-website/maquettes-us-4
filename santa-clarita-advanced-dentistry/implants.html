Dental Implant Options Available

Every situation is different. Here are the three main approaches we use to help patients restore their teeth — each tailored to your specific needs.

Single-Tooth Implants

A single implant replaces one tooth without affecting neighboring teeth. It looks, feels, and functions like your natural tooth — no special care needed.

  • Preserves neighboring healthy teeth
  • Restores full chewing ability
  • Helps maintain jawbone health

Multiple Implants

When you are missing multiple teeth, implants can support individual crowns or small bridges — providing stability and comfort.

  • Individual crowns for each tooth
  • Small bridges for adjacent gaps
  • No removable partial dentures needed

Full-Arch / All-on-4

If you need to replace all your teeth, full-arch implants provide a permanent set of fixed teeth supported by just 4-6 implants.

  • Fixed teeth that stay in place
  • Eat steak, apples, and corn on the cob
  • Natural look and feel

A Fixed Smile for Full-Arch Restoration

When you need to replace all your teeth

Whether from decay, injury, or other causes, losing multiple teeth can feel overwhelming. All-on-4 offers a path to a complete, natural-looking smile.

How All-on-4 works

Four strategically placed implants support a full arch of fixed, non-removable teeth. They look natural, feel secure, and stay in place.

Even with bone loss

The All-on-4 approach is often possible even when other implants are not, because of the way the implants are angled to maximize existing bone.

Is This Right for Me?

These are the concerns we hear every day. Here is what we want you to know.

Am I too old for dental implants?

Age alone is rarely a barrier to dental implants. As long as your jawbone has finished developing and you are in reasonably good health, implants can be placed successfully at any age. In fact, many of our patients are in their 60s, 70s, and beyond. We evaluate each patient individually — bone quality, gum health, and overall medical history matter far more than birthdate.

What if I have significant bone loss?

Bone loss is common among patients who have been missing teeth for years, but it does not automatically disqualify you from implants. Depending on the extent of the loss, we may recommend a bone grafting procedure to rebuild the foundation before placing the implant. Many patients complete grafting and implant placement with excellent long-term results. We will take a 3D cone-beam scan to assess your bone volume and present all available options during your consultation.

Will the procedure be painful?

The implant placement surgery is performed under local anesthesia, so you will not feel pain during the procedure. Most patients are surprised by how comfortable the experience is. Afterward, some mild soreness and swelling for a few days is normal and managed well with over-the-counter pain relievers. We also offer sedation dentistry for patients who prefer a more relaxed experience from start to finish.

I have had bad dental experiences before.

We hear this often, and we take it seriously. Our entire team is committed to making your care gentle, transparent, and judgment-free. We will explain every step before we do it, go at your pace, and never pressure you. Sedation options are available if anxiety is a concern. Many of our most grateful implant patients came to us carrying years of dental fear — and they now say implants were one of the best decisions they ever made.

How long does the whole process take?

The timeline varies depending on your individual situation. For a straightforward single implant with sufficient bone, the process typically spans four to six months from placement to final crown — allowing time for the implant to integrate with the bone (osseointegration). If bone grafting is needed, add a few additional months. During your consultation we will map out a personalized timeline so you know exactly what to expect at each stage.
