What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a titanium post that is surgically placed in the jaw to take the place of a missing tooth root. Then, the implant takes about three to six months to fuse with the surrounding jawbone in a process called osseointegration. Once this step is complete, Dr. Freyne can install metal abutment fixtures on top of the implant, which can be used to support a lifelike dental crown that brings back the natural appearance of your smile. Multiple dental implant posts can also be placed to support a dental bridge or denture.

No matter how many teeth you’ve lost, dental implants can create a solution that delivers unmatched strength, function, and beauty. Plus, they have the potential to last a lifetime!

The Dental Implant Process

Unlike traditional treatments, the process of rebuilding your smile with dental implants will take several months to complete because it’s performed in multiple phases. Each step is necessary to replicate the entire structure of your missing tooth. Although it might take a bit longer, it’s time well spent to ensure your new smile has the foundation it needs to last for a lifetime.

Initial Consultation

Generally, anyone who has good oral and overall health is a candidate for dental implants; however, you’ll still need a consultation with your dentist in Chevy Chase to ensure they are right for you. Besides a visual examination, Dr. Freyne will need to review a CT scan of your mouth to assess your jawbone’s density. He will also take time to discuss your oral and lifestyle habits to look for any red flags that can cause a problem, like smoking.

Based on the results of the examination, you may need additional treatments to become a candidate for dental implants, like periodontal therapy, bone grafting, or a sinus lift. Although it adds more time to your treatment plan, it creates the healthy, strong foundation your dental implants need to thrive.

Surgical Implant Placement

After you’ve healed from any previous treatments, you’re scheduled for your implant placement surgery. If you’re only having 1 post placed, a local anesthetic may be all that’s needed; however, if you have a more complex case, you may require a sedative or general anesthesia.

Once you’re numb, your gum tissue is opened to expose pre-determined areas of your jawbone. A small hole is drilled into it to insert the post. Your gum tissue is stitched closed, and you’ll spend the next few weeks healing.

The post acts as a new root which stimulates growth in your jawbone. Through a process called osseointegration, your bone will fuse to the post, allowing it to stay in place forever with the right aftercare.

Abutments & Restorations

After your jawbone has healed, you’ll enter the final phase of your treatment. You’ll need a second minor surgery to attach an abutment to your dental implant. This is a special fixture between the post and the restoration.

It will take about two weeks for your gum tissue to heal. During this time, the dental lab is hard at work carefully crafting your new restoration. It will be made to match your natural teeth to look seamless when you smile.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Dental implants are one of the most versatile tooth replacement options out there. It doesn’t matter if you’re replacing one tooth or several, they can be designed with your needs in mind.

Missing One Tooth

To replace a single tooth, a titanium implant post will be placed inside your jaw. This is followed by an all-porcelain crown, which is meant to reflect light exactly like natural tooth enamel.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Replacing multiple consecutive teeth is done via an implant-supported dental bridge. Unlike traditional bridges, which use modified teeth adjacent to the gap to anchor a restoration, implant bridges use an implant on either side of the bridge. Keep in mind that you do not need a dedicated implant for each tooth you intend to replace.

Missing All Teeth

Whether you need to replace your top or bottom denture , dental implants are the perfect solution. It doesn’t matter if you need a partial or full denture, both can be custom-made to fit on top of dental implants. You can also opt to have the implant denture fixed or removable, depending on your preference.

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

Most patients report that the recovery period after receiving dental implants is quite mild and significantly easier than what would be expected after a tooth extraction. By following your aftercare instructions, you can help ensure a smooth healing process and set yourself up for many years with functional implants. Here’s a quick guide to what to expect while recovering after receiving dental implants as well as when to contact our office in Chevy Chase for help.

What to Do Immediately After Dental Implant Surgery

One of the most important parts of keeping your recovery process complication-free is protecting the blood clot that will form over the surgical area. To do this, stick to the following guidelines:

  • Refrain from spitting. Instead, use tissues or swallow.
  • Do not drink through a straw.
  • Do not smoke for at least a full day.
  • Do not probe at the surgical area with your fingers or tongue.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

You can expect a few mild side effects after receiving dental implants, but these should go away on their own after several days. They may include:

If you find that your symptoms are getting worse instead of better or notice signs of infection such as fever, fatigue, chills, or a foul taste in your mouth, contact our office.

  • Intermittent bleeding: While this can go on for several days, it can be addressed by applying light pressure through clean gauze.
  • Swelling: This can last for more than a week, but it can be alleviated by applying a cold or warm compress.
  • General discomfort: You can manage this with prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

It’s best to stick to a diet of soft, mild foods that are not very hot for a few days after receiving dental implants. These can include:

You can resume your normal diet when you feel comfortable doing so, but it’s best to be careful when chewing around the implant site.

  • Mashed potatoes
  • Ice cream
  • Pudding
  • Yogurt
  • Eggs
  • Pasta

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

You’ll be able to brush your teeth normally the day after surgery, but you’ll need to take special care when cleaning around the surgical sites. It’s also best to rinse your mouth twice or thrice daily with salt water, ideally after meals. While we may recommend a prescription mouthwash, avoid products that contain high levels of alcohol like Scope or Listerine.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

You may experience some temporary sensitivity after you receive your restoration, but this should be mild and go away on its own as you get used to wearing your device. If necessary, you can treat this discomfort with pain medication. You’ll be ready to begin using your smile normally the day you receive your restoration, but contact our office if you experience issues like swelling, bleeding, or signs of infection like fever or a foul taste in your mouth.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Your new dental implants may be able to keep your smile complete, healthy, and strong for a lifetime if you take excellent care of them. Neglecting to protect your artificial teeth from damage and the gums supporting them from infection can lead to implant failure and the need for costly replacements. Here’s a brief overview of a few excellent precautions to take to ensure that your new teeth stay in excellent shape.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While dental implants cannot develop cavities, they can still fail due to infections that compromise the gum and bone tissues supporting them. To keep your implants safe, be sure to brush, floss, and use antibacterial mouthwash every day to eliminate food debris, sugary residues, and harmful bacteria.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants give patients the power to eat whatever they like, but it’s important to eat a healthy diet to prevent oral and overall health issues that can compromise your restorations. Be sure to enjoy plenty of foods that are rich in calcium, phosphorus, protein, and vitamins C and D to keep your gums and implants in good shape. It’s also best to only enjoy sugary, crunchy, or sticky foods in moderation since they can stress your implants or feed harmful oral bacteria.

Break Bad Habits

Bad oral habits can damage your dental implants or compromise the gum tissue supporting them. Tobacco use and excessive alcohol consumption are particularly bad since they can inhibit the gums’ ability to heal and fight infections. Chewing on ice or inedible objects or using your teeth to open packages or bottles are great ways to damage your natural and artificial teeth, so be sure to kick these destructive habits.

Protect Your Dental Implants

While dental implants are incredibly strong, they are not invincible. It’s best to wear an athletic mouthguard to protect your teeth from injury when playing sports or riding a bike. It’s also a great idea to wear a customized nightguard to prevent unnecessary enamel wear if you grind your teeth at night. We can provide you with one of these appliances at our office.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Visit our office every six months for routine checkups and cleanings. This way, we can monitor your dental implants and oral health, address any issues in their early stages, and help you understand the best ways to keep your natural and artificial teeth in excellent condition for many years.

Dental Implant FAQs

How long does the dental implant process take?

Each patient’s situation is different, so a treatment protocol is always designed around his or her specific needs. This can also affect the amount of time needed to perform a particular procedure. After an implant is inserted, it can typically take three to six months for the fixture to fuse with the underlying jawbone, and for the jaw and gums to heal.

How long can I expect my dental implant to last?

One of the most attractive features of dental implant insertion is the longevity it offers. With proper care, which should include brushing and flossing at least two times a day and maintaining six-month dental visits, an implant can last up to a lifetime.

Will I be able to eat the foods I love?

After having a dental implant inserted, you’ll be able to maintain a normal diet. However, hard items like certain nuts should be avoided, as they could cause the final restoration to break or the implant to dislodge.

Will I have a natural-looking smile?

Because a dental implant is attached to the jawbone and mimics the function of a tooth root, it provides a natural-looking and aesthetically pleasing smile that will boost your confidence and make for more favorable first impressions.
