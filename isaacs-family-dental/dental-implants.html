How Dental Implants Work

At Isaacs Family Dental, we understand the importance of restoring both the function and aesthetics of your smile. That’s why we offer comprehensive dental implant solutions to address the challenges associated with tooth loss. But how exactly do dental implants work?

When a tooth is lost, it can lead to various complications, including rapid bone loss and changes in speech and chewing patterns. Dental implants offer a permanent solution by replacing the missing tooth root and providing a stable foundation for artificial teeth.

Our dental implant systems consist of two main components: the dental implant body and the dental implant abutment. The implant body is surgically inserted into the jawbone, where it acts as a replacement for the natural tooth root. The abutment is then attached to the implant body, extending through the gums into the mouth to support the artificial teeth.

By mimicking the structure and function of natural teeth, dental implants not only restore your smile but also prevent further bone loss and improve overall oral health. What Are Dental Implants Made Of?

Dental implants have revolutionized the field of dentistry, offering a durable and long-lasting solution for tooth replacement. But what are dental implants made of, and how do they compare? At Isaacs Family Dental, we believe in providing you with the information you need to make informed decisions about your oral health.

Traditionally, dental implants have been primarily made from titanium and titanium alloy, and for good reason. Titanium is biocompatible, meaning it interacts well with the human body and encourages the process of osseointegration–the fusion of the implant with the jawbone. With a success rate of 95 % and the ability to last a lifetime with proper care, titanium implants are the gold standard in dental implant materials.

However, recent advancements in technology have introduced alternative materials, such as zirconia, into the world of dental implants. Zirconia implants offer a promising option for patients allergic to titanium and are often recommended for their biocompatibility and aesthetic appeal. While they may be slightly more challenging to insert compared to titanium implants, zirconia implants are a one-piece solution that can be completed in a single session.

In addition to titanium and zirconia, other materials, such as polymers, ceramics, and alternative metals, are sometimes used in dental implants. While these materials offer potential benefits, such as biodegradability or aesthetic customization, they can come with drawbacks such as lower durability or success rates.

Ultimately, the choice of dental implant material depends on your specific needs and preferences. Common Questions About Dental Implants

What are dental implants?

Dental implants are small titanium posts that Dr. Isaacs will insert in your gums where you have missing teeth. They serve as replacement teeth roots, and after a healing period where they fuse with your jawbone, they will support new artificial teeth. Implants also help preserve your jawbone, making them an ideal choice for teeth replacement.

How much do dental implants cost?

The cost for dental implants can vary greatly from patient to patient. The ultimate cost depends on how many implants are needed, where they are located in the mouth, and the type of replacement teeth used. A consultation will be needed so we can examine your mouth, take a set of images, and discuss your options. Afterward, we can give you an accurate estimate of your implant treatment.

Can dental implants be removed?

In theory, implants could be removed from the jaw if needed. However, there is usually not a need to ever do so. Once they are placed in your jaw and have had a chance to bond with your bone, they will last for decades, and maybe your entire life. The artificial teeth we attach to them will often last for at least 10 years, but the implants themselves rarely need replacement.

When should I get dental implants?

If you are missing any number of teeth, you owe it to yourself to consider getting implants. They are designed to function as artificial roots for your replacement teeth. Implants make crowns, bridges, and dentures more secure. They prevent bone loss by stimulating your jaw, and they restore your biting power so you can eat anything you want.
