Preventative Care

The whole point is the visit where nothing is wrong.

Prevention is the quiet star of pediatric dentistry: a handful of easy visits and small habits that make the dramatic appointments mostly unnecessary. Here is the whole family of preventative care under one roof.

Prevention is a philosophy here, not a service

The idea behind everything on this page is simple: prevent dental disease from ever happening, so visits stay positive and kids grow up with healthy habits and a healthy outlook on the dentist. Unlike a lot of health conditions, dental disease is largely preventable, which makes childhood the single best time to get ahead of it.

Every service in the prevention family works toward that same end. The rest of our services exist for the times prevention was not enough, and the better this page does its job, the less you will need the others.

The six-month rhythm

The spine of preventative care is the routine checkup: a comprehensive exam, a professional cleaning, and a growth check, twice a year. Checkups every six months keep little problems little, because children’s mouths change quickly enough that a lot can happen between visits.

Our cleanings and exams guide walks through a visit start to finish, including what we watch for at every age, from the first tooth to the teenage years.

The shield layer: fluoride and sealants

Two small treatments do a large share of the work.

Fluoride treatments strengthen enamel while it is still forming. Children’s enamel is still developing, and a quick fluoride application at a checkup helps it harden.

BPA-free dental sealants protect the deep grooves of the back teeth, which is where childhood cavities most like to start. A sealant flows into those grooves and hardens into a thin shield, done in minutes at a regular visit, no numbing needed.

Whether either one belongs in your child’s plan is a conversation with your pediatric dentist, based on your child’s teeth rather than a default.

Prevention starts younger than most parents think

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends a first dental visit by the first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth. Those earliest visits are pure prevention: a gentle lap exam, teething guidance, thumb and pacifier support, and nutrition habits set while they are still easy to set. Our infant dental care guide covers that first visit minute by minute.
