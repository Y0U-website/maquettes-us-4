What Preventive Care Includes at Glendale Dental Wellness

Preventive dentistry is more than a six-month cleaning. Our prevention program covers the full range of services that keep cavities, gum disease, and bigger problems from taking root:

  • Professional cleanings : scaling, polishing, and flossing every six months for most healthy adults; more frequent for patients with active gum disease maintenance needs
  • Comprehensive checkups and exams : full review of every tooth, bite, restoration, and soft tissue
  • Low-radiation digital X-rays : up to 80 % less radiation than traditional film, with instant on-screen review
  • Oral cancer screenings : quick, painless, and built into every comprehensive exam; early detection dramatically improves outcomes
  • Personalized cavity prevention coaching : risk assessment plus home-care recommendations tailored to your bite, diet, and history
  • Protective dental sealants : thin coatings that shield the deep grooves of molars from decay; especially valuable for kids but available for adults too
  • Topical fluoride treatments : for patients with high cavity risk or visible enamel weakness
  • Custom mouthguards and nightguards : sport guards for athletes and bite splints for patients who clench or grind

How Often Should You Be Seen?

The American Dental Association recommends that most adults see a dentist every six months. Your specific schedule depends on factors like:

At your first visit Dr. Chan will recommend the schedule that fits your situation. Many of our patients book their next two visits at the front desk before they leave, so the rhythm of preventive care simply becomes part of their year.

  • Cavity history : patients with frequent cavities benefit from cleanings every 3-4 months
  • Gum disease history : patients in active scaling and root planing maintenance often visit every 3 months
  • Existing dental work : crowns, bridges, implants, and veneers benefit from more frequent monitoring
  • Lifestyle factors : smokers, patients with diabetes, and those on certain medications may need more frequent visits
  • Recent treatment : for the first 6-12 months after major restorative work, we typically see patients more often to confirm everything is settling well

Keeping Your Smile Healthy Every Day

Brush for two full minutes twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, using gentle circular motions. Floss once daily to clean between teeth where your brush cannot reach. Finish with an antimicrobial mouthwash for added protection against bacteria.

Replace your toothbrush every three months or sooner if bristles fray. Consider an electric toothbrush for more consistent plaque removal. If you grind your teeth at night, ask Dr. Chan about a custom nightguard to protect your enamel.

Limit sugary snacks and acidic drinks that feed cavity-causing bacteria. Choose crunchy fruits and vegetables, cheese, and nuts as tooth-friendly alternatives. Drink plenty of water throughout the day to rinse away food particles and keep saliva flowing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Preventive care is the most cost-effective approach to oral health. Regular cleanings, fluoride treatments, and screenings help detect problems early when they’re easier and less expensive to treat. Prevention also protects against serious conditions like gum disease and oral cancer.

Most patients benefit from professional cleanings every six months. However, patients with gum disease or higher risk factors may need cleanings every three to four months. Dr. Chan will recommend the ideal schedule based on your individual oral health status.

Dental sealants are thin protective coatings applied to the chewing surfaces of back teeth. They create a barrier against decay-causing bacteria and acids. Sealants are especially beneficial for children and teens, but adults with cavity-prone teeth can benefit as well.

Fluoride strengthens tooth enamel and helps resist acid attacks that lead to cavities. Professional fluoride treatments provide a much higher concentration than toothpaste or mouthwash, offering superior protection against decay for both children and adults.

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, floss every day, use an antimicrobial mouthwash, and eat a balanced diet low in sugar. Avoid tobacco products and limit snacking between meals. These daily habits work together with professional care to keep your mouth healthy.
