What Constitutes a Dental Emergency?

Not every dental issue requires urgent attention, but certain symptoms demand immediate care. According to the Cleveland Clinic , dental emergencies involve severe pain, uncontrolled bleeding, or damage that threatens the survival of a tooth. Common examples include:

If you’re unsure whether your situation is urgent, MouthHealthy recommends contacting your dentist immediately for guidance.

  • Knocked-out teeth
  • Severe toothaches (indicating infection or abscess)
  • Cracked or fractured teeth
  • Lost fillings or crowns
  • Soft tissue injuries (e.g., cuts to gums, lips, or tongue)

What to Do in a Dental Emergency: First Steps

For detailed guides, visit MouthHealthy’s Dental Emergencies page .

  • Knocked-Out Tooth Handle the tooth by the crown (not the root).
  • Rinse gently with water, and try to reinsert it into the socket. If not possible, store it in milk or saline.
  • Call We Care Dental immediately—we’ll see you within the hour.
  • Severe Toothache Rinse with warm water and floss to dislodge debris.
  • Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling.
  • Avoid aspirin on gums (it can burn tissue).
  • Broken Crown or Filling Save the pieces and bring them to your appointment.
  • Use dental cement (available at pharmacies) as a temporary fix.

FAQ’s About Dental Emergencies

1. What qualifies as a dental emergency? A dental emergency includes severe toothaches, knocked-out teeth, cracked or fractured teeth, dental abscesses, and injuries causing uncontrolled bleeding or swelling. If you experience any of these, seek immediate dental care.

2. What should I do if I have a knocked-out tooth? Handle the tooth by the crown (not the root), rinse it gently with water, and try to place it back into the socket. If that’s not possible, store it in milk or saliva and visit a dentist within an hour for the best chance of saving the tooth.

3. Is tooth pain always a dental emergency? Not always. While mild discomfort can wait for a regular appointment, severe pain indicating an infection, abscess, or trauma requires immediate attention.

4. Should I go to the ER for a dental emergency? Most dental emergencies are best handled by a dentist. However, if you have severe facial swelling, uncontrollable bleeding, or broken facial bones, visit the emergency room immediately.

5. How can I temporarily manage a broken tooth or lost filling? Rinse your mouth with warm water and apply over-the-counter dental cement to protect the area until you see a dentist. Avoid using super glue or other non-dental adhesives.

6. Are dental emergencies more expensive than regular visits? Emergency dental care costs are often comparable to regular visits, especially if covered by insurance. It’s advisable to check your insurance policy for specific coverage details.

7. How can I prevent dental emergencies? Maintain good oral hygiene, schedule regular check-ups, wear protective gear during sports, and avoid chewing hard objects like ice or pens.

8. What should I bring to an emergency dental visit? Bring identification, insurance information, a list of medications you’re taking, and any relevant dental records if available.

9. What should I do if my crown or filling falls out? Try to keep the restoration and bring it to your dentist. You can temporarily reattach it using over-the-counter dental cement until you receive professional care.

10. What steps should I take during a severe toothache? Rinse your mouth with warm saltwater, floss gently to remove debris, and take over-the-counter pain relievers as directed. Contact your dentist immediately for further evaluation and treatment.
