Dental Implant Restorations…

You may already have the post in place from an oral surgeon, or you may be starting from scratch. Either way, our focus is the crown or set of teeth that completes the work.

  • The abutment, a connector that links the healed post to the tooth above it
  • The restoration, the crown, bridge, or denture you see, bite with, and care for

Who Is a Good Candidate for Implant Restorations ?

Most adults who are missing one or more teeth and have healthy gums make strong candidates. You need enough jawbone to hold the post steady and a willingness to keep up with brushing, flossing, and regular checkups. Before recommending anything, Dr. Mark Polle reviews your health history and uses digital and panoramic X-rays to see the bone clearly. If the bone is thin, he will talk through options honestly rather than push you toward a plan that will not hold up.

What Types of Restorations Can Go on an Implant?

Implants can hold several kinds of restorations, and the right one depends on how many teeth are missing:

Choosing among these is a conversation, not a sales pitch. We walk through what each option means for your daily life and budget so you can decide with confidence.

  • An implant crown replaces a single missing tooth without disturbing the teeth beside it
  • An implant-supported bridge fills a gap of several teeth in a row using two posts as anchors
  • Implant-supported dentures snap onto multiple posts to secure a full arch that will not slip

What Happens During the Restoration Process?

How Long Does It Take to Restore a Dental Implant ?

The restoration phase usually takes about two to three weeks from scan to final placement. That window covers the lab time needed to build a piece that fits precisely. If you are counting from the day the post was first placed, the full journey runs longer, often three to six months, because the bone needs time to bond with the implant before we add anything on top. We give you a clear timeline at your first visit so there are no surprises.

Do Restored Implants Look and Feel Natural?

Yes, a well-made implant crown looks and feels remarkably close to a real tooth. The porcelain is shaded to blend with the teeth on either side, and because the tooth is rooted in the jaw, it stays put while you eat and talk. There is no adhesive to fuss with and no clicking. Many patients tell us they forget which tooth was replaced, which is exactly the result we aim for. Because the restoration is shaped to fit your bite, it also supports the teeth around it and helps keep your jaw working the way it should.

How Much Does an Implant Restoration Cost?

Cost depends on the type of restoration and how many teeth you are rebuilding, so a single crown costs less than a full-arch set. We believe you deserve to know the number before treatment begins, not after. Our front desk gives you a clear written estimate, explains what your dental insurance is likely to cover, and files the claim for you. There are no hidden fees and no surprise bills, which is a promise we take seriously.

How Do You Care for a Restored Implant?

Caring for your new tooth is much like caring for the rest of your smile. A few simple habits keep the restoration and the gum around it healthy for years:

  • Brush twice a day and floss around the restored tooth to keep the gum line clean
  • Avoid using the tooth to open packages or chew on ice, hard candy, or pens
  • Keep your routine cleanings and exams so we can check the fit and the surrounding bone

How Long Do Implant Restorations Last?

With good care, the crown or bridge on top typically lasts ten to fifteen years, and the post beneath it can last a lifetime. Everyday wear, grinding, and skipped cleanings can shorten that lifespan, which is why we keep an eye on your restoration at each visit. If a crown ever wears down, it can usually be replaced without disturbing the healthy post underneath, making this one of the most durable forms of missing tooth replacement available today.
