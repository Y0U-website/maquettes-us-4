What Happens at a General Dentistry Visit

  • Check-in and health review. We review your health history and any concerns you want to discuss. New patients fill out intake forms in advance to save time.
  • X-rays when indicated. Diagnostic images help us identify decay, bone loss, or issues not visible to the naked eye. We keep radiation exposure minimal and purposeful.
  • Professional cleaning. Our hygienist removes plaque and tartar buildup, polishes your teeth, and checks gum health.
  • Comprehensive exam. Dr. Wyman examines every tooth, checks your bite and jaw, screens for oral cancer, and reviews your X-rays.
  • Treatment discussion. If anything needs attention, we walk you through the options, costs, and timing. No pressure. Just a clear picture of what’s going on.
  • Next appointment scheduled. Most patients come in every six months. We’ll set your next visit before you leave.

General Dentistry FAQs

How often should I come in for a checkup?

Every six months is the standard recommendation for most patients with healthy gums. If you have a history of gum disease, cavities, or other ongoing concerns, Dr. Wyman may recommend more frequent visits. For children, regular checkups starting around age one help build good habits early.

Do you take patients without insurance?

Yes. We offer a 5 % discount for patients without dental insurance. We also accept CareCredit, which lets you finance treatment over time. We’re upfront about costs before any work begins so you can make an informed decision.

What if I have dental anxiety?

It’s more common than most people realize. Our team moves at a pace that feels comfortable for you. Dr. Wyman takes time to explain what’s happening before and during any procedure. If you have specific concerns, just let us know when you call to book. We can talk through them ahead of your visit.

Do you treat kids?

Yes. We see patients of all ages, including young children. Starting dental visits early helps kids become comfortable with the process and reduces the likelihood of problems down the road. We keep it relaxed and positive for younger patients.

What counts as a dental emergency?

Severe tooth pain, a knocked-out or cracked tooth, a lost filling, or significant swelling are all emergencies. As a patient of record, you have access to 24-hour emergency care. Call the office number and you’ll get guidance on what to do next.
