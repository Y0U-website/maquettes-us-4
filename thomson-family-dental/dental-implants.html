Am I a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most adults in good general health are candidates for dental implants. The most important factor is having sufficient jawbone density to support the implant post. Patients with a history of gum disease, heavy smoking, or uncontrolled diabetes may need additional evaluation. Maintaining good preventive dental care before and after implant placement is important for long-term success.

The best way to determine whether you are a candidate is a consultation with one of our dentists. We will take X-rays and review your full oral health history before making any recommendation.

The Dental Implant Process at Thomson Family Dental

Getting a dental implant is a multi-step process that takes place over several months. Here is what to expect from start to finish.

1. Consultation and Evaluation

Your dentist will perform a comprehensive exam including X-rays and 3D imaging to assess your bone density, gum health, and the position of nearby teeth and nerves. This allows us to plan the exact placement of your implant with precision before any procedure begins.

2. Implant Placement

The titanium post is placed into the jawbone during a minor surgical procedure performed under local anesthesia. Most patients are surprised by how manageable the procedure is. You will be given detailed aftercare instructions and any necessary medication to support healing.

3. Osseointegration

Over the following three to six months, the implant fuses with the surrounding bone in a process called osseointegration. This creates the stable foundation that makes implants so durable. During this period you will have periodic checkups to monitor healing.

4. Abutment Placement

Once the implant has fully integrated, a small connector piece called an abutment is attached to the top of the post. This is what the crown will attach to.

5. Crown Placement

A custom dental crown is fabricated to match the color, shape, and size of your natural teeth and attached to the abutment. Once placed, your implant is fully restored and ready for normal use.

Dental Implants vs. Other Tooth Replacement Options

Implants vs. Dental Bridges

A dental bridge can replace one or more missing teeth without surgery, making it a faster and often less expensive option. However, a bridge requires crowning the healthy teeth on either side of the gap to anchor the restoration. Implants preserve those adjacent teeth and are generally the better long-term investment because they do not need to be replaced as frequently.

Implants vs. Dentures

Traditional dentures are removable and rest on the gum surface, which means they do not prevent the bone loss that occurs after tooth extraction. Implant-supported dentures are an option for patients missing many or all teeth, combining the stability of implants with the broader coverage of a denture. Your dentist will help you determine which approach fits your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

How much do dental implants cost in Thomson, GA?

The cost of a dental implant depends on the number of teeth being replaced, whether bone grafting is needed, and the type of restoration placed on top. Thomson Family Dental accepts most major dental insurance plans and can discuss financing options at your consultation. We recommend scheduling a visit so we can give you an accurate estimate based on your specific situation.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, the implant post itself can last a lifetime. The crown placed on top typically lasts 15 to 25 years before it may need to be replaced due to normal wear. Consistent oral hygiene and regular dental visits are the most important factors in implant longevity.

Is the dental implant procedure painful?

Implant placement is performed under local anesthesia, so most patients feel pressure but no pain during the procedure. Soreness and mild swelling in the days following surgery are normal and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief. Most patients return to their normal routine within a day or two.

How long does the full implant process take?

From the initial consultation to final crown placement, the process typically takes four to nine months. Most of that time is the osseointegration phase where the implant fuses with the bone. The actual in-office appointments are relatively short, and many patients find the process more manageable than they expected.

What happens if I lost a tooth due to an injury?

Traumatic tooth loss from an accident or sports injury is one of the most common reasons patients seek implants. If the tooth is still intact, visiting an emergency dentist immediately gives the best chance of saving it. If the tooth cannot be saved, an implant is typically the most natural and durable long-term replacement option.

Can I get other cosmetic work done alongside my implant?

Yes. Many patients choose to complete additional cosmetic work such as clear aligners or whitening around the same time as their implant, particularly if they want to ensure the implant crown matches an improved overall smile. Your dentist will help you sequence treatments to get the best outcome.
