  • Step 3: Healing and Osseointegration– Over the next few months, the implant naturally bonds with your bone in a process called osseointegration. This creates a strong, stable foundation for your new tooth.

Dental Implant Aftercare

  • Rest for the remainder of the day following surgery.
  • Apply ice packs intermittently for the first 24–48 hours to help reduce swelling.
  • Take all prescribed medications as directed.
  • Avoid vigorous rinsing, spitting, or drinking through a straw for the first 24 hours.
  • Expect mild swelling, soreness, and slight bleeding or oozing during the first few days.
  • Stay hydrated and eat soft foods such as yogurt, eggs, mashed potatoes, smoothies, and soup. Avoid hard, crunchy, spicy, or sticky foods while healing.
  • Maintain excellent oral hygiene to support healing and long-term implant success.
  • Avoid strenuous exercise and heavy lifting for several days after surgery.
  • Refrain from smoking, vaping, and using tobacco products during healing.
  • Limit alcohol consumption while healing and taking medications.

Concerns Addressed

Bone loss in the jaw

Dental implants help stimulate the jawbone like natural tooth roots, reducing bone loss and helping maintain the strength, density, and structure of the jaw over time.

Loss of tooth function

Dental implants function just like natural teeth, allowing patients to eat, speak, and smile comfortably and confidently again.

Missing teeth

Dental implants replace missing teeth with secure, natural-looking restorations that restore the appearance, function, and confidence of a complete smile.

Technology

Digital X-Rays

Digital X-rays help evaluate bone health, identify underlying issues, and monitor healing throughout the dental implant process while using less radiation than traditional X-rays.

Intraoral Camera

An intraoral camera captures high-resolution images inside the mouth, allowing patients to better understand their treatment needs and implant planning recommendations.

CBCT

CBCT provides detailed 3D images of the jawbone, nerves, and surrounding structures, allowing for precise implant planning and safer placement.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What types of dental implants exist? Dental implants can replace a single tooth, multiple teeth, or an entire arch. Common options include single dental implants, implant-supported bridges, implant-supported dentures, and full-mouth dental implants such as All-on-X.
  • What are the benefits of dental implants? Dental implants look, feel, and function like natural teeth while providing a long-lasting solution for tooth loss. They help restore chewing ability, improve confidence, preserve jawbone health, maintain facial structure, and offer exceptional stability compared to traditional tooth replacement options.
  • Can your bite affect dental implant placement? Yes, your bite plays an important role in dental implant planning and placement. A properly aligned bite helps ensure that chewing forces are distributed evenly across your teeth and implants, reducing the risk of excessive wear or complications. During your consultation, we carefully evaluate your bite to help ensure that your dental implants are placed for optimal function, comfort, and long-term success.
