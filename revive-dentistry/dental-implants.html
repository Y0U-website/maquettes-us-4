When One Tooth Becomes a Bigger Problem

A missing tooth doesn’t stay a one-tooth problem. The jawbone underneath starts to resorb, neighboring teeth drift into the gap, and what could’ve been a straightforward single implant becomes a more involved — and pricier — case a year or two later. A single-tooth implant replaces both the root and the crown: a titanium post integrated with the jawbone, topped with a custom-matched crown, with no impact on the teeth next to it.

Options Beyond a Single Tooth

Missing several teeth in a row calls for an implant-supported bridge — stable, with no removable pieces. Missing most or all of your teeth opens up full-arch options: fixed bridges or implant-supported overdentures, both far more secure than traditional dentures.
