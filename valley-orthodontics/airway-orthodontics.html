Understanding Airway-Focused Care

Airway orthodontics begins with careful evaluation. These common questions explain what we consider and how treatment may fit into a broader care plan.

Airway orthodontics considers the relationship between orthodontic development and breathing. In addition to evaluating the teeth and bite, Dr. Rocha looks at the dental arches, jaw growth, facial balance, and breathing patterns to understand the full picture before recommending treatment.

Possible signs include frequent mouth breathing, snoring, restless sleep, a narrow palate, crowded teeth, or concerns about jaw and facial development. These signs do not confirm an airway condition, but they may be worth discussing during an orthodontic assessment.

Depending on a patient’s age and needs, orthodontic treatment may guide jaw development, create appropriate space for the teeth, or broaden a narrow dental arch. The goal is a personalized plan that supports healthy oral function while creating a balanced, confident smile.

Children, teens, and adults may benefit from an airway-focused evaluation. Early assessment can be especially useful while a child’s jaws and face are still developing, but it is never too late to discuss breathing, sleep, bite, or alignment concerns with Dr. Rocha.
