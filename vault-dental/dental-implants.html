The Implant Treatment Process Placement The process begins once the personalized treatment plan is ready. During the procedure, a small opening is made in the gum and the implant is gently placed into the jawbone where the missing tooth once was. The area is then cleaned and sutured so healing can begin. This creates a strong foundation for the new tooth that will be added later.

Healing & Osseointegration

Healing starts right away. The gum tissue usually settles within a week or two, while the implant bonds with the bone over the next few months. This bonding period is important because it allows the implant to feel secure and stable. Follow-up visits keep the healing process on track and ensure everything is progressing smoothly.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Check out these frequently asked questions, or call us to speak with our team.

What is a single tooth implant?

A single tooth implant is a long-lasting way to replace a missing tooth without relying on removable appliances. It includes a titanium post placed into the jawbone, which acts like a new tooth root, and a custom crown that looks and feels like a natural tooth. Because the implant becomes part of the bone, it stays firmly in place during eating and talking. This option is ideal for anyone who wants a replacement tooth that looks realistic, feels comfortable, and does not rely on adhesives or hooks.

How are dental implants made?

Dental implants are created through a combination of precise engineering and personalized design. The implant itself is made from medical-grade titanium, a material that works well with the body and encourages strong bonding with the bone. The restoration on top, usually a porcelain crown, is crafted specifically for the individual so the color and shape match naturally. Some offices design the crown digitally on-site, while others work with highly skilled labs. Once the crown is ready, it is attached to the implant to complete the new tooth.

How long will my dental implant last?

Dental implants are known for their longevity, and many patients enjoy them for several decades. The implant post integrates directly with the jawbone, creating a stable and long-lasting foundation. Although the crown may need to be replaced eventually due to normal wear, the implant itself often lasts for life when cared for properly. Regular brushing, flossing, and routine visits help keep the surrounding tissues healthy and protect the implant over the long term.

Can my dental implant become infected?

While infection around an implant is not common, it can occur when plaque builds up around the area or when post-surgery care is not followed closely. This condition is known as peri-implantitis. Keeping the area clean, following aftercare instructions, and attending recommended follow-up visits greatly lowers the risk. If early signs of irritation or discomfort appear, addressing them promptly can help protect the implant and keep the tissue healthy.

Do dental implants stop bone loss?

Yes, dental implants help prevent bone loss by stimulating the jawbone just the way a natural tooth root would. When a tooth is missing, that area of the bone stops receiving pressure from chewing and slowly begins to shrink. An implant restores that stimulation, helping maintain bone density and supporting the natural shape of the face. This makes implants not only a cosmetic improvement but also a valuable long-term health investment.

Can I get an implant in one day?

In some situations, it is possible to place an implant and attach a temporary replacement tooth on the same day. This depends on bone health, the location of the tooth, and overall treatment needs. A same-day option allows you to leave the office with a complete smile while the implant heals underneath. After the healing period, a custom permanent crown is created and fitted. A consultation helps determine whether this approach is appropriate and safe.
