Dental Implant Surgery

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Once your implant is in place, it can take several weeks for your bone to fuse to it through a process called osseointegration. With the post acting as a new root, it will stimulate bone growth, allowing your jaw to fuse to the implant. Your dentist will monitor your recovery to determine the best time to move forward with the next step in the process.

After adequate healing, you’ll need a second minor surgery to place an abutment on the implant post. It is a special connector piece to secure your restoration to the implant. Your gums should be healed around the fixture in about 2 weeks.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

The final step to a beautiful smile is receiving your restoration from Dr. Morton. It will be crafted of high-quality materials and customized to match the color, size, and shape of your natural teeth. It will blend in seamlessly, so only a trained professional will know you have dental implants. Your new smile can last for 30 years or longer. Your dental implants in Wylie will serve you for many years with good oral hygiene at home and regular care from a dentist. You will never have to worry about gaps in your smile ever again after just a few short months.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are very strong and durable, but they aren’t completely indestructible. It’s crucial that you take all the proper steps to care for them and prevent dental implant failure. By investing your time and effort into your smile now, you can enjoy it for a lifetime! Here are some healthy habits to implement to keep your replacement teeth in good shape.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Your dental implants can’t get cavities, but your natural teeth are still at risk. It’s also still possible to develop gum disease. To keep your replacement teeth (and natural teeth) healthy, you should continue to maintain excellent oral hygiene. This means brushing twice, flossing, and rinsing with mouthwash every day.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants don’t require any eating restrictions, so you can enjoy a full nutritious diet. Just be careful not to overindulge in hard, sticky, and sugary foods. You should enjoy plenty of foods that contain calcium and vitamin C. These nutrients help to keep your jawbone and gums in excellent health.

Break Bad Habits

Bad habits can cause a variety of issues for your dental implants and natural teeth. It is always best to refrain from smoking cigarettes and using other tobacco products. These can slow healing and make it more challenging for your body to fight off infections. You should also avoid using your teeth as tools. Instead, look for scissors, a bottle opener, pliers, or another item that can help you complete your task without risking the integrity of your smile.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are very strong, but they aren’t completely indestructible. You should always wear a mouthguard when participating in contact sports. If you have a tendency to grind your teeth at night, you should ask your dentist about getting a nightguard so you can protect your replacement teeth from additional wear and tear.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

You should continue to attend regular cleanings and checkups every six months. This way, we can monitor your dental implants, natural teeth, gum tissue, and more! By finding problems early, we can address them quickly and may even be able to prevent issues from arising in the future.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

Dental implants are one of the most long-lasting solutions for missing teeth, designed to last over 30 years with proper care and maintenance. Sometimes, they can even last patients a lifetime! And the best part? They don’t require special care beyond what’s already recommended to keep your natural teeth in good condition. That means you can help your new smile lasts as long as possible by practicing good oral hygiene, visiting your dentist every six months, and kicking bad habits to the curb (i.e., smoking, chewing on ice, using your teeth as tools).

Does Getting Dental Implants Hurt?

Before the procedure begins, your mouth will be completely numbed with a local anesthetic. Additionally, since the jawbone doesn’t have many nerve endings, you shouldn’t feel any discomfort during your surgery. Your mouth will feel slightly sore for the first few days following your procedure. Luckily, you can manage these symptoms by taking prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication and using cold compresses. If your pain persists or worsens after a few days, you should contact Dr. Morton for assistance.

How Successful Are Dental Implants?

If placed by a skilled dental professional, dental implants have an impressive success rate of over 95 %. Of course, their success depends heavily on how well you take care of them after your procedure. Practicing good oral hygiene, visiting your dentist every six months, and following a healthy diet are just a few ways you can ensure your new smile lasts as long as possible. The success of your dental implants can also vary based on their location in the mouth. For instance, since molars receive more strain from chewing than front teeth, they’re a bit more likely to fail.

What Can Cause Dental Implants to Fail?

Dental implant failure is relatively uncommon. However, it can most likely occur for one of two reasons: peri-implantitis or failed osseointegration. Peri-implantitis is a type of gum disease that results from poor oral hygiene, damaging the bone and connective tissues supporting the implant. Failed osseointegration happens when the titanium post doesn’t integrate with the jawbone. A few other causes of dental implant failure include chronic teeth grinding, tobacco use, poor oral hygiene, and more. At your consultation, Dr. Morton will explain how you can minimize your risk of implant failure.
