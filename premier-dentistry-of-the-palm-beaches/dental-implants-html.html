What are Dental Implants?

Traditional dentures and dental bridges only sit above the gumline, restoring the visible portion of your smile. However, dental implants extend beneath the gums, all the way to the jawbone, delivering complete root-to-crown restoration. As titanium posts that fit within the jawbone, they stimulate the area to prevent facial collapse while also supporting newly designed crowns, bridges, and dentures. As long as they are properly maintained, they can often last a lifetime.

The 4 Step Dental Implant Process

Replacing your missing teeth with a dental implant restoration can take between three and six months, so it’s a good idea to know what you should expect. The entire process can be broken down into four main steps, which your implant dentist in West Palm Beach will be sure to discuss in more detail during your consultation. In the meantime, our team will be happy to address any questions or concerns you may have, so don’t hesitate to give us a call!

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The first step to restoring your smile with dental implants is to make sure our team thoroughly understands your treatment goals. That means meeting with her for a consultation, where you’ll have time to explain your needs, talk about your schedule, and voice questions or concerns. Our team will also examine your mouth to evaluate whether you’ll need preliminary treatments like bone grafting or gum disease therapy.

Dental Implant Surgery

Since we can complete the entire dental implant process in-house, you’ll return to our office for your implant surgery. Our dentist will numb your mouth with a local anesthetic, and if requested, will administer dental sedation before getting started. Once you’re comfortable, she’ll make a small incision in your gumline so she can place the implant posts at strategic locations along your jawbone.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

It’s important for your mouth to undergo a full recovery after dental implant surgery, and it will take a couple of months for the implant posts to fuse with your jawbone. But when our team is confident the posts are secure and your jaw is ready, she’ll promptly fix the small connector piece (or the abutment ) to the posts. This will enable her to attach your crown, bridge, or denture to your implants in the future.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

While we’re getting your smile ready for your customized restoration, our partners at the dental lab will be busy fabricating the piece. Rest assured, our team will work closely with them to help ensure your restoration fits well and looks great. As soon as it’s delivered to our office, we’ll let you know that it’s time for your final appointment! If you determine you’d like a few minor adjustments, don’t worry. We’ll be able to complete them before you leave.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Many experts consider dental implants the ideal way to replace missing teeth because of their unique placement in your jaw. Unlike dentures and bridges, they eventually fuse with the bone material, which allows them to provide certain advantages over the alternatives.

Continue reading to learn more about the benefits of choosing this treatment, so you can make an informed decision regarding the future of your smile!

Day-to-Day Benefits

Gaps in your grin can potentially detract from your daily quality of life. For instance, many patients feel self-conscious about the sudden changes to their appearance that accompany tooth loss, while others develop challenges with eating and speaking normally.

  • Restore bite force. This procedure can return up to 80 % or more of your initial biting power, so you can bite and chew more easily.
  • Improve enunciation. They function like artificial teeth that you can press your tongue against to form different sounds needed to clearly articulate various words.
  • Boost confidence . They’re capped with a lifelike prosthetic that’s carefully customized to match your natural teeth for beautiful results that enhance your self-esteem.

Health Benefits

This treatment can also support your oral and overall health in ways that might be less immediately noticeable, but are just as significant, including:

  • Maintain jawbone. The support rod placed in your jawbone during this procedure functions like a root, stimulating new bone growth with each bite to keep it active and healthy, which helps avoid additional tooth loss.
  • Preserve facial structure. Supporting your jawbone keeps it from thinning, which can otherwise result in sunken or hollowed-looking cheeks and additional facial wrinkles.
  • Prevent oral issues. Maintaining your new tooth can be as easy as continuing to brush and floss twice daily and visiting our team for a checkup and cleaning every six months to avoid dental problems, like gum disease or tooth decay.

Long-Term Benefits

Dental implants can last 20+ years if cared for correctly, allowing patients to enjoy the following long-term advantages:

  • High success rate. This procedure has an incredibly high success rate of over 90 %, meaning you’re unlikely to develop complications if it’s performed by a qualified provider, like Dr. Kadiyala.
  • Resilient materials . The biocompatible rod placed in your jaw eventually becomes a more permanent part of your mouth, while the durable ceramic cover resists potential chips, cracks, and stains.
  • Reduced expenses . There’s no need to invest in special accessories, adhesives, soaking solutions, and special cleaners to maintain your restorations, which can save money over time.

Preliminary Treatments & Dental Implant Surgery

Preliminary treatments and dental implant surgery are considered to be separate costs but what’s the difference between the two?

Preliminary treatments are any procedures you might need before starting the dental implant process. Since conditions like gum disease and oral infection can result in dental implant failure, it’s best to take care of these issues and restore your health first.

The dental implant surgery , meanwhile, is the process of actually placing your implants along your jawbone.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

Dental implants are made up of the titanium post that will fuse with your jawbone, the crown, denture, or bridge that will replace your missing teeth, and the abutment that will connect these two pieces. Generally speaking, an implant denture is more expensive than an implant bridge or crown, but can still be a cost-effective way to replace multiple missing teeth at once.

How Dental Implants Can Save You Money

Many patients find that their dental implants end up saving them money in the long-run. Compared to those who wear traditional restorations, patients with dental implants spend less time and money on repairs, replacements, and adjustments. They’re also less likely to need costly jawbone treatments.

Does My Dental Insurance Cover Dental Implants?

Yes and no. That is to say that your dental insurance plan will likely cover some of your treatment expenses. Most plans categorize dental implants as a voluntary treatment, meaning its excluded from the usual benefits. However, your plan could very well pay for other aspects, such as preliminary treatments. It’s still worth reviewing your policy to see if it could save you money.
