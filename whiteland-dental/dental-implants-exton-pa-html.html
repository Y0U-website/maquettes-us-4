What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that replaces the root of a missing tooth. It fuses with your jawbone over 3–6 months in a biological process called osseointegration, then supports a custom-crafted crown, bridge, or denture. Unlike traditional bridges, implants don&#x27;t require grinding down adjacent healthy teeth, and unlike removable dentures, they never slip, never need adhesive, and let you eat anything you used to enjoy. Modern implants have a documented 98 % success rate when placed by experienced dentists using premium materials, and with proper care they can last 30+ years.

Common Questions

With proper oral hygiene and regular checkups, implants are designed to last a lifetime. The titanium post itself integrates permanently with your jawbone. The crown on top typically lasts 15–25 years before potentially needing replacement, similar to a natural-tooth crown.

Many dental insurance plans now offer partial coverage for implants, typically 20–50 % of the cost up to your annual maximum. We verify your specific benefits at no charge before treatment begins, and we work with all major PPO plans. Medicaid is not accepted.

Most adults with good general health are candidates. Even patients with bone loss can often qualify after a bone graft. The only common disqualifiers are uncontrolled diabetes, active gum disease, or recent radiation to the jaw. Your free 3D consultation determines candidacy in one visit.

In some cases we can place an implant the same day as the extraction (immediate placement). More commonly, we wait 8–12 weeks for the socket to heal before placement. Your specific timeline depends on bone quality and any infection at the site, which we evaluate during your consultation.
