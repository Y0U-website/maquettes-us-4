Is Dental Work Tax Deductible?…

Mini dental implant treatment is an incredible innovation in tooth replacement. While many dental insurance plans don’t cover dental implants, there are other ways to make your care cost-effective. At Fine Dental Care, we offer flexible financing options that split your costs into manageable monthly payments. And for qualifying patients, dental procedures (such as dental implants and other major restorative work) may be tax deductible!

  • Faster healing periods: Mini dental implants only need a few weeks to integrate with your jawbone. While traditional implants require months of waiting, mini implants typically secure in less than a month.
  • Better jawbone health: The titanium used in dental implants is biocompatible, so it easily integrates with your jawbone. It also replaces missing tooth roots, unlike other options like bridges and dentures. With dental implants in place, you can keep your smile secure and your jawbone healthy.
  • Lower risk of infection: Mini implants have a unique one-piece design. Without multiple parts, mini implants help protect against bacteria and lower your risk of infections like peri-implantitis.

Making Your Dental Care Tax Deductible

IRS Topic no. 502, Medical and dental expenses states that certain dental expenses are eligible for deduction. Dental implants, or artificial teeth, may be a covered expense. However, you must meet certain conditions.

  • First, you’ll itemize eligible expenses when filing your taxes . If you traditionally take the standard deduction when filing your taxes and aren’t confident about itemizing, we highly suggest meeting with a tax professional.
  • Remember, you don’t have to do this alone! You can contact a tax professional to assist you throughout the deduction process. Next, you need to figure out your adjusted gross income , or AGI . To calculate this figure, take your total gross income, including wages, dividends, and capital gains , and subtract certain adjustments, like retirement contributions or interest paid on student loans. The formula is: Gross income Adjustments = AGI.
  • Once you’ve done those calculations, you’ll need to figure out what 7.5 percent of your AGI is. This is the minimum threshold for the deduction. As an example, let’s say a patient has an AGI of $55,000. 7.5 % of their AGI is $4,125, which is their minimum threshold for deduction. If their qualifying dental expenses exceed the minimum threshold, they could deduct their treatment costs. However, these qualifying dental expenses generally can’t include what insurance covered or what you paid with tax-free Health Savings Account (HSA) distributions.
  • Finally, you’ll need to determine your deduction . Using the same example from before, our patient has a minimum deduction threshold of $4,125. If their implant costs (minus any expenses covered by insurance or an HSA) are $20,000, they would subtract $4,125 from $20,000. The result is their eligible deduction amount.

Breaking Down The Deduction Formula

Claiming a qualifying dental-expense deduction may lower your taxable income and save you money. But it’s important to get the numbers right. The following chart includes the formulas you need to figure out your deduction properly, using the example from above.
