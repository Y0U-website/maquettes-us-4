Wayne J. Yee, D.D.S., P.C.

How can a Dentist truly meet your needs without understanding your concerns? Without good communication Dentistry becomes a mechanical process. Communication between Doctor and Patient is everything! Your needs, expectations, and goals play a large role in the sucess of your dental care. One of the greatest causes for failure of a good Treatment Plan is lack of communication between the Doctor and Patient.

I always strive to listen to my patients so that together we develop a treatment plan that best meets your desires while still addressing all areas of concern. Your Treatment Plan is then determined out of our mutual decision, and therefore budgeted within your expectations.

I believe that it is best to clearly explain all of the possible benefits and potential complications of treatment so that all of your questions will be answered before we begin. I never force patients to accept treatment that they are not ready for. I believe that patients have rights when properly informed to decide their own course of action.

I also will not discourage a patient from realising their Cosmetic Dental dreams despite the difficulty of the case, but I will be honest in explaining the limits of today’s available technology. I will make every effort possible to give my patients the results that they want. I will endeavor to give you the very best quality and value for your money. I make this promise whether you are asking for the Mecedes-Benz of Dentistry, or only a simple fix that safely stabilizes a problem in a cost effective manner.

Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center (Post Graduate Fellowship 2nd Year)

Experienced in Reconstructive Treatment of Facial Trauma: Dr. Wayne Yee completed his 1st year of Post Graduate Residency at Our Lady of Mercy Medical Center (A University Hospital Campus of New York Medical College). There he studied a comprehensive Residency in General Dentistry, and Hospital Dentistry. Working in the Emergency Room helped him to developed his skills in the primary closure of Facial Lacerations and Fixation of Broken Jaws.

His 52-76 hours/week Residency Program also provided practical experience in General and Concious Sedation, Operatory Room Surgery, Day Surgery Unit, Control of Infecious Diseases, Pain Management, Oncology, Emergency Room Medicine, Reconstructive Oral and Maxilliofacial Surgery.

Even before Dental School Dr. Wayne Yee showed an interest in Dental Research. He served Marquette’s Psychology Department as a research assistant, while an undergraduate in Experimental Psychology . His project was Dental related in the study of the Auditory Response in Hypophosphatemic Mice.

Hypophosphatemia is a rare inherited disorder characterized by impaired transport of phosphate and diminished Vitamin-D metabolism in the kidneys. Quite often this affects the way the bones in the ear transmit sounds.

The study’s goal was to determine if different frequencies of sound could be used as a diagnostic tool in identifying patients with Hypophosphatemia. This in turn could help identify dental pathology caused by the disease. Unfortunately, Dr.Yee had to graduate and transfer his project to another student research assistant before completion of the work.

University of Michigan School of Dentistry - Ann Arbor

Former Dental Research Assistant (1983-1986) under William O’Brien, PhD; Dr. Wayne Yee co-authored a Research Paper published in the Journal of the American Dental Association on transillumination of blue light through hard and soft tissues of the oral cavity. Early research contributed to a practical use in dental diagnostics 10 years later.

Want to learn more on Transillumination? Devices using white light instead of X-rays are now available in the Dental Profession exclusively for the use of detecting caries and other pathology. An excellent example of this has been developed by Electro-Optical Sciences, Inc. Their product combines this diagnostic technique with video and computer technology.
