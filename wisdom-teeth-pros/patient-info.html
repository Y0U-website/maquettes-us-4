Pre-Surgery & Pre-Sedation Instructions

Please review and follow all of the instructions below carefully. Your safety depends on it.

Nothing to Eat or Drink If you are receiving IV sedation, you must have absolutely nothing to eat or drink (including water) for at least 8 hours before your procedure. This is a strict safety requirement.

Arrange a Driver You must have a responsible adult drive you home after surgery. You will NOT be permitted to drive yourself or take a rideshare alone if you received IV sedation. Your driver must remain at the office during the procedure.

Medications Continue taking any regularly prescribed medications with a small sip of water unless instructed otherwise. Inform our office of all medications and supplements you are taking, including blood thinners.

Arrive on Time Please arrive 10–15 minutes early to complete any remaining paperwork. Late arrivals may result in rescheduling to protect the safety of all patients.

Wear Comfortable Clothing Wear loose, comfortable clothing with short sleeves or sleeves that can easily be rolled up. Avoid wearing jewelry, contact lenses, or nail polish on the day of surgery.

Contact Us With Questions If you are feeling ill, have a fever, or developed any new medical conditions in the days leading up to your appointment, please call our office immediately so we can advise you.

Post-Op Recovery Instructions

Proper aftercare is essential for a fast and complication-free recovery. Follow these guidelines closely.

Control Bleeding Bite firmly on the gauze pads placed over the surgical site for 30–45 minutes. If bleeding continues, replace with fresh gauze and maintain firm pressure. Some oozing is normal for the first 24 hours. Avoid spitting.

Swelling & Bruising Swelling typically peaks around 48–72 hours after surgery. Apply an ice pack to the outside of your face in 20-minute intervals for the first 24 hours. After 24 hours, switch to warm compresses to help reduce swelling.

Pain Management Take prescribed pain medications as directed, beginning before the local anesthetic wears off. Over-the-counter ibuprofen (Advil/Motrin) alternated with Tylenol can be very effective. Do not take medications on an empty stomach.

Diet Stick to soft, cool foods for the first 24–48 hours: smoothies, yogurt, applesauce, mashed potatoes, soup (not hot). Avoid hard, crunchy, chewy, or spicy foods. Do not use a straw — the suction can dislodge blood clots.

Oral Hygiene Do not rinse or spit on the day of surgery. Starting the next day, gently rinse with warm salt water (1/2 teaspoon of salt in 8 oz of water) after every meal. Brush your teeth gently, avoiding the surgical sites.

Activity Restrictions

Rest for the remainder of the day. Avoid strenuous physical activity for at least 3–5 days. Keep your head elevated when lying down. Smoking is strictly prohibited — it significantly increases the risk of dry socket and infection.

Warning Signs — Call Us If You Experience Severe or worsening pain after 3–4 days (possible dry socket), fever above 101°F, difficulty swallowing or breathing, excessive uncontrolled bleeding, or pus/foul odor from the extraction site.
