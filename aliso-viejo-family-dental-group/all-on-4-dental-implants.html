What the 3D scan is actually measuring

Bone volume and density

How much bone is there, where it sits, and whether it can hold a fixture at the angle the plan needs.

Sinus and nerve position

In the upper jaw the sinus floor sets the limit; in the lower, the nerve canal does. Both are mapped before anything is placed.

How your teeth meet

Your bite pattern, and any history of clenching or grinding, change how the load has to be spread.

How the number of implants is decided

All-on-4 and All-on-6 are the same idea — one fixed bridge carried by implants — separated by how many anchor points the arch is given. The count follows your anatomy and your bite, not a package you pick from a menu.

Bone in the back of the arch

All-on-4 tilts the two rear implants to reach denser bone at the front, which is what lets many patients avoid grafting. Six fixtures need usable bone further back.

Upper jaw or lower

Bone in the upper jaw is naturally softer than in the lower, and the sinus sits above it. That difference often drives the count on its own.

How hard you bite

A history of clenching or grinding spreads more force through the bridge, and more anchor points spread that force across more of the jaw.

The size of the arch

A longer arch has more span between supports, and span is what a full-arch bridge has to be engineered around.

All-on-4

Four implants per arch, with the two rear fixtures angled to use the denser bone at the front of the jaw. Most patients avoid sinus lifts and major grafting.

All-on-6

Six implants per arch, spreading the load across two additional anchor points. Chosen where the bone supports it and where the bite calls for a wider distribution.

Both plans finish the same way: a fixed bridge you clean in your mouth, not a denture you take out.

What maintenance actually involves

A fixed bridge is cleaned in place, and the parts that hold it are checked on a schedule. Knowing that before you start is part of choosing it honestly.

Every day, at home

  • A water flosser under the bridge, which is where most of the cleaning happens.
  • Super floss or an interdental brush threaded along the underside.
  • A non-abrasive toothpaste — abrasives dull the surface over time.

At the practice

  • Professional hygiene around the implants, on the interval your plan sets.
  • Bite verification, because how the bridge meets the opposing arch changes over time.
  • Screw and fitting checks, with the bridge removed by the dentist when that is needed.

Tell us early about

  • Any movement, clicking or a change in how the bridge feels under pressure.
  • Gums that bleed, swell or become sore around a fixture.
  • A chip, a crack, or a change in how your teeth meet.
