If a Tooth is Knocked Out

Handle the tooth ONLY by the crown (the biting surface), never the root. Do not scrub it, and do not use soap or disinfectant. If dirty, rinse briefly with milk or saline. For an adult tooth, try to place it back in the socket. If you can&#x27;t, store it in milk or the patient&#x27;s own saliva — never in tap water — and come in immediately. Never try to reinsert a child&#x27;s baby tooth — bring the child and the tooth in instead.

If the Tooth is Fractured

Rinse your mouth gently with warm saltwater. Save any broken tooth fragments. Apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek to minimize facial swelling, and avoid biting down on that area.

If You Have Gum Swelling

Visible swelling near a tooth root indicates a dental abscess or deep infection. Apply cold compresses in 20-minute intervals. Do not apply hot packs or place aspirin directly on the gums, as it can burn the tissue. If the swelling is spreading, see the red-flag list above and seek emergency care.

If You Have Bleeding That Won&#x27;t Stop

Fold clean gauze into a pad, place it over the site, and bite down with firm, steady pressure for 15–20 minutes without lifting it to check. If it soaks through after two full cycles — or you take a blood thinner, or the bleeding followed a blow to the head — seek emergency care right away.
